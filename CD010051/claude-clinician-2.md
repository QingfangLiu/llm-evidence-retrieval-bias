Here's a structured set of primary studies (RCTs and controlled clinical trials only — no reviews, meta-analyses, or guidelines) on topical cyclosporine A (CsA) for dry eye disease (DED), organized by formulation, with a synthesis of benefit/harm signals.

## CsA 0.05% emulsion (Restasis and generics)

- **Sall et al. 2000 (Ophthalmology)** — the pivotal FDA trials: two identical 6-month, multicenter, double-masked, vehicle-controlled RCTs (combined n=877). CsA 0.05% and 0.1% produced significantly greater improvement than vehicle in corneal staining and Schirmer scores, and 0.05% also improved blurred vision, need for artificial tears, and physician-rated global response; there was no dose-response benefit of the higher concentration.
- **Stevenson, Tauber, Reis 2000 (Ophthalmology)** — dose-ranging phase 2 RCT (n=162) testing 0.05–0.4% CsA vs vehicle over 12 weeks; established that efficacy plateaus around 0.05–0.1% with no added benefit at higher doses.
- **Barber et al. 2005 (Ophthalmology)** — open-label, non-randomized 3-year safety extension of the two phase 3 trials (n=412), examining long-term ocular and systemic safety of twice-daily CsA 0.1%.
- **Chen et al. 2010 (J Ocul Pharmacol Ther)** — 8-week, multicenter, double-blind, parallel-group RCT of 0.05% CsA vs vehicle in Chinese patients with moderate-to-severe DED.
- Chinese phase III RCT, 12-week, multicenter, double-masked, placebo-controlled study of 0.05% CsA emulsion (PMC6709181).
- **Feng, Wang, Gong, Lin 2025 (Curr Eye Res)** — RCT of topical CsA combined with punctal plugs vs CsA alone for DED and inflammation.
- 2024–2025 RCT (First Affiliated Hospital of Gannan Medical University, n=100) — CsA drops vs sodium hyaluronate for post-cataract-surgery dry eye.

## CsA 0.09% nanomicellar solution (Cequa/OTX-101)

- **Tauber, Schechter, Bacharach et al. 2018 (Clin Ophthalmol)** — phase 2b/3, randomized, double-masked, vehicle-controlled, dose-ranging RCT of OTX-101.
- **Goldberg, Malhotra, Schechter et al. 2019 (Ophthalmology)** — phase 3, multicenter, vehicle-controlled, double-masked RCT (744 patients) with a primary endpoint of ≥10 mm Schirmer test improvement at day 84; the treatment group achieved this significantly more often than vehicle (16.6% vs 9.2%, P<0.001), with secondary improvements in corneal and conjunctival staining.
- **Malhotra, Devries, Luchs et al. 2019 (Cornea)** — pooled analysis of the phase 2b/3 and phase 3 OTX-101 trials focused on corneal staining outcomes.

## CsA 0.1% cationic emulsion (Ikervis)

- **Baudouin, Figueiredo, Messmer et al. 2017 (Eur J Ophthalmol) — SICCANOVE trial** — RCT in moderate-to-severe DED.
- **Leonardi, Van Setten, et al. 2016 (Br J Ophthalmol) — SANSIKA trial** — multicenter, randomized, double-masked, 6-month phase III RCT (246 patients) in severe DED with corneal staining; the primary composite responder endpoint was not significantly different between CsA and vehicle (28.6% vs 23.1%, P=0.326), but mean corneal staining improvement was significantly greater with CsA.
- **Leonardi, Messmer, Labetoulle et al. 2019 (Br J Ophthalmol)** — pooled analysis of SICCANOVE and SANSIKA individual patient data across subgroups (age, sex, Sjögren's status).
- **Labetoulle, Leonardi, Amrane et al. 2018 (Clin Ther)** and **Leonardi et al. 2017 (PMC6380090) — 1-year SANSIKA open-label extension** — nonrandomized but reports on the randomized cohort's continued follow-up.

## CsA 0.1% water-free solution (Vevye/ESSENCE)

- **Sheppard, Wirta, McLaurin et al. 2021 (Cornea) — ESSENCE phase 2b/3 RCT.**
- **Wirta, Sheppard, et al. — ESSENCE-2 (JAMA Ophthalmol 2023)** — multicenter, double-blind, vehicle-controlled phase 3 RCT (March 2021–July 2022) in adults with moderate-to-severe DED across 12 Chinese hospitals, randomizing patients 1:1 to water-free cyclosporine or vehicle after a 14-day artificial-tear run-in.
- Wirta, Galor, Aune et al. — ESSENCE-2 open-label extension (long-term safety follow-up of the randomized cohort).

## CsA 0.05% gel and other novel formulations

- **COSMO trial (Dove/DDDT, 2022)** — multicenter, randomized, double-masked phase III RCT of CyclASol/CyclAGel 0.05% CsA gel vs vehicle.
- **Kang, Kim, Chou et al.** — RCT of a novel 0.05% CsA topical nanoemulsion in primary Sjögren's syndrome dry eye.

## Comparator/head-to-head RCTs

- **Park et al. 2019 (BMC Ophthalmol)** — RCT comparing 0.05% CsA vs 3% diquafosol — **note: this article was subsequently retracted** (2020) due to incomplete/overlapping reporting; flagging so you don't cite it.
- **Park et al. 2019 (Korean J Ophthalmol)** — related RCT comparing CsA nanoemulsion, CsA emulsion, and diquafosol 3%.

## Subgroup/special population RCTs

- Small, Acheampong, Reis et al. 2002 (J Ocul Pharmacol Ther) — pharmacokinetic sub-study of blood CsA levels during long-term treatment (drawn from the phase 3 RCT population), relevant to systemic-absorption safety.

## Synthesis of benefits and harms across these trials

**Benefits:** Across formulations, CsA consistently outperforms vehicle on objective signs — corneal/conjunctival fluorescein staining and Schirmer wetting — with more variable and often smaller effects on patient-reported symptoms (OSDI, global symptom scores), and effects that typically take 4–12 weeks to emerge. Trials directly comparing concentrations (0.05% vs 0.1% vs higher) generally show a plateau, with no added benefit above 0.05–0.1%. Newer delivery formulations (nanomicellar, cationic emulsion, water-free solution) were developed largely to improve tolerability and bioavailability rather than to demonstrate superior efficacy over the original emulsion.

**Harms:** The most consistent adverse effects across trials are instillation-site burning/stinging and conjunctival hyperemia, occurring in a meaningful minority of patients (rates vary by formulation, generally higher with older emulsion vehicles and lower with newer solutions/nanomicellar formulations). Serious systemic adverse events are rare, and long-term extension studies (e.g., the 3-year Barber et al. follow-up) have not identified significant systemic CsA absorption or new safety signals with continued use.

**Important caveat:** Several pivotal trials (SANSIKA, and parts of the FDA phase 3 program) did not meet their pre-specified primary composite efficacy endpoint despite showing benefit on individual objective signs — this is a recurring theme worth flagging to patients regarding expectation-setting.
