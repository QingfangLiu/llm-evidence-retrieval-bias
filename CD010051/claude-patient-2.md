## Cyclosporine A eye drops for dry eye disease — Primary studies found

I searched specifically for original RCTs and other primary research (excluding systematic reviews/meta-analyses/narrative reviews/guidelines/opinions). Here's what these trials generally show, organized by theme, with citations to specific primary studies.

**Efficacy vs. vehicle/placebo (pivotal trials):** The original FDA registration trials randomized 877 patients with moderate-to-severe dry eye to CsA 0.05%, 0.1%, or vehicle for 6 months and found cyclosporin A 0.05% and 0.1% ophthalmic emulsions compared to vehicle in patients with moderate to severe dry eye disease improved both objective signs (corneal staining, Schirmer scores, tear break-up time) and symptoms (OSDI), concluding that topical CsA represents a new pharmacologically based treatment for dry eye disease that may provide significant patient benefits.

**Newer formulations vs. vehicle:** More recent trials tested reformulated CsA. SICCANOVE compared 0.1% cyclosporine A cationic emulsion versus vehicle in patients with moderate to severe dry eye disease over 6 months. SANSIKA, in patients with more severe disease (corneal staining grade 4), found the primary composite endpoint wasn't significantly different between arms, though assessment of corneal damage showed greater improvement with CsA cationic emulsion over vehicle in mean adjusted corneal fluorescein staining change from baseline to month 6. A newer water-free 0.1% solution was tested in the ESSENCE-2 trial, described as investigating the effectiveness of a water-free cyclosporine eye drop, 0.1%, in the treatment of dry eye disease.

**Head-to-head / active comparator trials:** Other trials compared CsA against other active agents rather than vehicle, e.g., hyaluronic acid 0.15% vs. cyclosporine 0.05% (NCT04127851), and a Phase II trial comparing a novel cyclosporine gel formulation against Restasis as an open-label comparator in 240 patients with moderate-to-severe disease.

**Harms/safety-focused primary studies:** One study specifically measured blood cyclosporin A concentrations during treatment with CsA topical ophthalmic emulsions in patients with moderate to severe dry eye disease, collecting blood from 128 patients enrolled in the Phase 3 program to assess systemic absorption.

**Special populations:** A crossover trial in vernal keratoconjunctivitis, and studies in Sjögren's-syndrome-associated dry eye and post-cataract-surgery dry eye, extend findings beyond the general dry-eye population.
