# Evidence map: topical cyclosporine A for dry eye disease

I searched for human primary clinical studies of topical cyclosporine/ciclosporin A through **July 25, 2026**, focusing on randomized trials and longer-term safety cohorts. I excluded systematic reviews, meta-analyses, narrative reviews, guidelines, consensus documents, editorials, and pooled or post hoc analyses when an original trial report was available.

Because CsA concentration alone does not characterize the intervention, I kept the following formulations separate:

* Conventional oil-in-water emulsion: usually 0.05% or 0.1%
* 0.1% cationic emulsion
* 0.09% nanomicellar solution
* 0.1% water-free solution
* 0.05% ophthalmic gel

## Overall interpretation

### Benefits

**Objective ocular-surface signs improve more consistently than symptoms.** Across the larger vehicle-controlled trials, CsA generally produced statistically greater improvements in corneal or conjunctival staining than vehicle. The absolute between-group differences were usually modest, however, and their clinical importance varies with the staining scale used. ([PubMed][1])

**Tear production improves in some, but not all, studies.** In the phase III 0.09% nanomicellar trial, for example, a ≥10-mm Schirmer response occurred in 16.6% with CsA versus 9.2% with vehicle. The conventional emulsion trials also reported favorable categorized Schirmer outcomes, while SANSIKA did not show a significant between-group Schirmer difference. ([PubMed][2])

**Patient-reported symptoms are inconsistent.** Several major studies did not meet their principal symptom or combined sign-symptom endpoint:

* SANSIKA: OSDI change was similar with 0.1% cationic CsA and vehicle.
* The 0.09% nanomicellar trials: symptoms improved considerably in both groups without clear separation.
* ESSENCE-1: the prespecified OSDI endpoint was not met, although eye-dryness VAS favored CsA.
* ESSENCE-2 and the corresponding Chinese water-free trial: staining improved, but the principal dryness-symptom effect was not clearly significant. ([Sage Journals][3])

**Onset may be faster with some newer formulations.** Water-free CsA and the 0.05% gel showed corneal-staining separation as early as approximately day 14 in their trials. This does not establish that they are superior to conventional emulsion, because the products were not compared in adequately masked, head-to-head equivalence or superiority trials. ([PubMed][4])

### Harms

The dominant harms are **local administration-site effects**: burning, instillation pain, stinging, irritation, foreign-body sensation, and occasionally eyelid edema or hyperemia.

Representative rates illustrate the formulation dependence:

* SANSIKA 0.1% cationic emulsion: instillation-site pain 29.2% versus 8.9% with vehicle, mostly mild.
* OTX-101 0.09% phase II/III: instillation pain 15.1% versus 3.3% with vehicle.
* COSMO 0.05% gel: treatment-related eye pain 8.1% versus 2.2%.
* Long-term conventional emulsion cohort: burning 10.9%, stinging 3.9%, and conjunctival hyperemia 3.4%. ([Sage Journals][3])

Despite local discomfort, **discontinuation because of adverse events was usually uncommon**, and treatment-related serious ocular or systemic adverse events were rare in the trials. Long-term studies reported negligible or undetectable systemic CsA exposure and did not identify a systemic toxicity signal. Nevertheless, most extensions were uncontrolled, so they are better for detecting common tolerability problems than rare harms. ([ScienceDirect][5])

## Findings from active-comparator and special-population trials

These studies are clinically informative but give less direct evidence of the incremental effect over vehicle:

* **Kim et al., 2009:** randomized comparison of 0.05% CsA, topical vitamin A, and neither active agent in 150 patients. Both active treatments improved several DED findings. ([PubMed][14])
* **Yang et al., 2015:** topical CsA versus diquafosol in DED; both were active treatments, limiting conclusions about absolute efficacy. ([PubMed][15])
* **CsA versus fluorometholone:** small six-month randomized comparison in mild DED not responding to artificial tears. ([PubMed][16])
* **Gao et al., 2023:** 60 patients with Sjögren-associated DED assigned to CsA, artificial tears, or the combination; CsA-containing groups generally performed better on Schirmer, staining, and TBUT than artificial tears alone. ([PubMed][17])
* **SAHARA, Ayres et al., 2023/2024:** 345 participants with DED and meibomian-gland dysfunction randomized to TearCare or 0.05% CsA. Both improved; TearCare was superior for TBUT and meibomian-gland measures but not consistently for OSDI or staining. Treatment-related AEs occurred more often in the CsA arm and were mainly mild. ([PMC][18])
* **Mu et al., published online 2026:** 0.05% CsA versus 0.1% sodium hyaluronate in mild short-break-up-time DED; both improved symptoms and tear stability, with a more sustained FBUT effect and less asthenopia in the CsA group at eight weeks. ([PubMed][19])
* Small randomized studies after cataract or refractive surgery suggest possible tear-stability, staining, or optical-quality benefits, but these postoperative populations and cointerventions limit generalizability to ordinary chronic DED. ([PubMed][20])

## Important evidence limitations

1. **Vehicle response is substantial.** Emulsion, cationic, nanomicellar, gel, and semifluorinated-alkane vehicles can themselves lubricate the ocular surface, reducing the apparent between-group symptom difference.

2. **Signs and symptoms correlate poorly.** Improvement in staining should not automatically be interpreted as improvement in discomfort, visual fluctuation, or quality of life.

3. **Outcome heterogeneity is considerable.** Studies used different staining scales, eye-selection rules, Schirmer definitions, symptom instruments, responder thresholds, treatment durations, and concomitant artificial-tear policies.

4. **No reliable formulation ranking is possible.** Cross-trial comparisons of 0.05%, 0.09%, and 0.1% products are confounded by their delivery vehicles, dosing frequency, eligibility criteria, and endpoints.

5. **Most pivotal trials were manufacturer-sponsored.** Several primary endpoints were negative even when secondary staining endpoints favored CsA, so emphasis on secondary or post hoc outcomes can overstate the overall benefit.
