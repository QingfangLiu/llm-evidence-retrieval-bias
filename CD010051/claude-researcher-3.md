## Overview of the evidence base

Primary studies on topical cyclosporine A (CsA) for dry eye disease (DED) span roughly 1993–2025 and include:

- **Pivotal FDA trials** establishing CsA 0.05% (Restasis) as an approved therapy, showing improvements in Schirmer scores, corneal/conjunctival staining, and symptoms, with **ocular burning/stinging as the most common adverse event** (no significant systemic toxicity or vision-threatening AEs reported).
- **Dose-ranging and newer-formulation trials** (0.05%, 0.09% OTX-101/Cequa, 0.1% water-free, gel formulations) testing whether higher concentrations or novel vehicles improve efficacy or tolerability.
- **Comparative trials** against vehicle, artificial tears, punctal plugs, and other CsA products, in general DED populations as well as subgroups (post-cataract surgery, post-glaucoma surgery, contact lens wearers, Sjögren's-associated dry eye).
- **Long-term safety/extension studies** (up to 3 years, and one 10-year follow-up) addressing durability of benefit and continued tolerability.

Consistently reported harms across studies: transient ocular burning/stinging (~10–17% of patients, the leading cause of discontinuation, ~2–3%), and less commonly conjunctival hyperemia; serious or systemic adverse events are rare.
