## Benefits

**Objective signs improve modestly but consistently.** The original phase 3 program combined two identical 6-month, vehicle-controlled RCTs in 877 patients with moderate-to-severe DED comparing CsA 0.05% and 0.1% emulsion twice daily against vehicle, assessing corneal/interpalpebral staining, Schirmer test, tear break-up time (TBUT), OSDI, and investigator global responseand treatment success and daily use of artificial tears were assessed as efficacy outcomes across corneal and interpalpebral dye staining, Schirmer tear test, tear break-up time, OSDI, and investigator's global response. The earlier dose-ranging phase 2 RCT (162 patients, doses 0.05–0.4% vs vehicle) established that 0.05% and 0.1% concentrations achieve similar efficacy, with higher concentrations offering no obvious additional benefit.

**Newer formulations show similar or improved results.** The once-daily 0.1% cationic emulsion (Ikervis) was tested in two phase 3 RCTs:
- **SICCANOVE** (495 moderate-to-severe DED patients, 6 months): the mean change in corneal fluorescein staining from baseline to month 6 was significantly greater with CsA cationic emulsion than vehicle (-1.05 vs -0.82; p=0.009), and the drug was well-tolerated and effectively improved signs and symptoms, especially in patients with severe disease at risk of irreversible corneal damage.
- **SANSIKA** (246 patients with severe DED, 6-month double-masked phase + 6-month open-label extension): the composite responder endpoint (≥2 grade improvement in corneal staining and ≥30% improvement in OSDI) was 28.6% with CsA vs 23.1% with vehicle, not statistically significant, but mean corneal staining improvement was significantly greater with CsA (-1.76 vs -1.42; p=0.037).

The water-free 0.1% cyclosporine solution has two notable RCTs: the phase 2b/3 **ESSENCE** study, and **ESSENCE-2**, a multicenter, double-blind, vehicle-controlled phase 3 trial in adults with moderate-to-severe DED recruited from 12 hospitals in China, using a 14-day artificial-tear run-in before randomization 1:1 to water-free cyclosporine or vehicle, with an option for open-label extension for longer-term safety.

CEQUA (nanomicellar 0.09% solution) has two pivotal phase 2b/3 and phase 3 RCTs (Study 1, n=455; Study 2, n=744): co-primary endpoints were reduction in conjunctival staining and global symptom score in Study 1, and the primary endpoint in Study 2 was percentage of eyes with ≥10 mm improvement in Schirmer score at day 84, with efficacy demonstrated in a statistically significant, higher percentage of eyes achieving clinically significant increases in tear production versus vehicle.

A Chinese multicenter phase 3 RCT of 0.05% CsA (240 patients, 3-month double-masked, vehicle-controlled) found significant improvement in total ocular surface score, symptom score, and OSDI versus vehicle plus lubricant alone.

**Long-term safety/efficacy data**: A 1-to-3-year open-label extension in 412 patients previously treated in the phase 3 trials found that continued twice-daily 0.1% cyclosporine produced modest improvements in objective measures of dry eye disease with adverse events tracked over 3 years of extended use.

## Harms

Across these trials, the adverse-event profile is consistent and predominantly local/ocular rather than systemic:

- **Instillation-site burning/stinging/pain** is the most frequent adverse event across virtually every formulation. For CEQUA, the most common adverse reaction was instillation site pain (22%) and conjunctival hyperemia (6%), confirmed in the FDA review: the most common adverse reactions, reported in over 5% of subjects, were pain on instillation (22%), and overall regulators concluded the potential benefits through increased tear production outweighed the identified risks in the 769 subjects who received at least one dose across trials.
- In the CEQUA phase 3 study, mild adverse events were more frequent with active drug than vehicle: 111 patients on OTX-101 0.09% experienced a mild adverse event compared with 74 on vehicle, mostly burning or stinging, with no abnormal changes in visual acuity.
- For the cationic emulsion (Ikervis), systemic absorption is negligible, and there have been no significant safety findings associated with the conjunctiva in the two randomized controlled trials; the majority of treatment-emergent adverse events were ocular, with eye irritation being most common, and reported systemic events were mostly mild to moderate.
- No formulation in these trials has shown clinically meaningful vision loss or serious systemic toxicity attributable to the drug; discontinuations are usually driven by instillation discomfort.

**Dose-response on tolerability**: the original phase 2 dose-ranging trial found higher concentrations (0.2%, 0.4%) did not add efficacy but did increase local irritation, supporting the eventual settling on 0.05–0.1% (and later 0.09% nanomicellar) as the clinically used range.
