## Benefits (efficacy)

**Original US emulsion (Restasis, CsA 0.05%/0.1%)**
- The pivotal **Sall et al. 2000** trial (two identical, 6-month, vehicle-controlled RCTs pooled, n=877) found CsA superior to vehicle for increasing Schirmer wetting and reducing corneal staining in patients with more severe baseline keratitis, though many symptom endpoints did not separate strongly from vehicle. This compared CsA 0.05% and 0.1% ophthalmic emulsions to vehicle in patients with moderate to severe dry eye disease across 877 patients in two identical 6-month trials, with results combined for analysis.
- The dose-ranging **Stevenson/Tauber/Reis 2000** Phase 2 RCT (n=162) tested 0.05–0.4% concentrations and found CsA safe, well tolerated, and effective at improving signs, symptoms, and vision-related quality of life, without dose-response above 0.05%. The formulations were safe and well tolerated, significantly improved the ocular signs and symptoms of moderate-to-severe dry eye disease, and decreased the effect of the disease on vision-related functioning.
- **Chen et al. 2019** (12-week, placebo-controlled Chinese Phase III RCT) replicated efficacy of 0.05% CsA on top of artificial tears in an Asian population. Patients with moderate to severe dry eye disease were randomized to receive twice-daily 0.05% CsA ophthalmic emulsion or its vehicle, along with unpreserved hypromellose eye drops three times per day, with follow-up at multiple time points.

**Cationic emulsion 0.1% (Ikervis)**
- **SICCANOVE** and **SANSIKA** (both double-masked, vehicle-controlled Phase III RCTs) and their **pooled analysis (Leonardi 2019)** showed benefit concentrated in patients with more severe keratitis/Sjögren's-associated disease. Pooled data from these two similar phase III studies, in which patients aged 18 years or older received cationic emulsion 0.1% or vehicle once daily for 6 months, suggested a favourable safety and tolerability profile with efficacy in improving signs and symptoms, particularly in more severely affected subgroups.
- The **SANSIKA open-label extension (Labetoulle 2018)** examined durability after stopping treatment. Of patients who achieved a low corneal staining score by the end of the study, a substantial minority relapsed during a 24-month period after treatment discontinuation, with relapse being more common in those treated for only 6 months versus 12 months.

**Newer water-free formulation 0.1% (CyclASol/Vevye)**
- **ESSENCE-1 (Sheppard 2021)** and **ESSENCE-2 (Akpek 2023, JAMA Ophthalmology)** were the two pivotal RCTs supporting FDA approval, showing rapid onset (as early as 2–4 weeks) of corneal/conjunctival staining improvement. These randomized and controlled clinical studies consistently demonstrated significant improvements in corneal and conjunctival staining with an onset of effect as early as 2 and 4 weeks.
- A **Chinese bridging Phase III RCT (SHR8028)** replicated the water-free formulation's benefit on corneal staining in a non-US population. This water-free cyclosporine ophthalmic solution exhibited superiority over vehicle in improving total corneal fluorescein staining, though not dryness score, at day 29, with efficacy differences noted between US and Chinese populations.

**Novel/investigational formulations**
- A micellar nanoparticle CsA emulsion RCT compared two dosing regimens against Restasis directly (active-comparator design) rather than vehicle.
- Voclosporin (AUDREY trial) and other next-generation calcineurin-inhibitor drops have completed dose-ranging RCTs.

## Harms (safety/tolerability)

- Across trials, the dominant adverse effect is **local instillation-site burning/stinging**, generally mild-moderate; the pooled Sall data reported roughly 2% discontinuation for this reason. In two multicenter studies of cyclosporine 0.05% for moderate to severe dry eye, about 2.2% of subjects discontinued treatment due to burning and stinging.
- **Long-term safety** was addressed by a dedicated non-randomized open-label extension: this open-label extension study evaluated cyclosporine 0.1% ophthalmic emulsion over 1 to 3 years in moderate to severe dry eye patients previously dosed for 6–12 months in prior phase III trials, monitoring corneal staining, Schirmer scores, symptoms, and periodic biomicroscopy, visual acuity, and intraocular pressure.
- Newer formulations report better tolerability: pooled studies of the water-free cyclosporine 0.1% solution found that 99.8% of patients experienced no or mild instillation site irritation. The China bridging RCT similarly reported low rates of treatment-related adverse events (14.6% CsA vs 10.7% vehicle, mostly mild).
- Systemic toxicity has not been a signal in RCTs, consistent with minimal systemic absorption after topical dosing, though this is based on trial-level monitoring rather than dedicated long-term pharmacovigilance studies.

Note: I focused on dry eye disease trials; there is a separate, well-developed RCT literature on CsA for vernal keratoconjunctivitis (VKTIS, NOVATIVE) in children, which is a different indication — let me know if you want those included too.
