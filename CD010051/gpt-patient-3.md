## Bottom line

For **moderate-to-severe dry eye disease**, topical cyclosporine A is more consistently effective at improving **objective ocular-surface signs**—particularly corneal/conjunctival staining and, in some studies, Schirmer tear production—than at improving how patients feel. Symptom scores frequently improve substantially in both the cyclosporine and vehicle groups, leaving little or no statistically significant between-group difference.

The main harm is **transient burning, stinging, or instillation-site pain**. Its frequency varies considerably by formulation: approximately 24% with cyclosporine 0.09% nanomicellar solution in one pivotal trial, versus rates close to vehicle with the newer water-free 0.1% formulation. Serious ocular or systemic drug-related adverse events were uncommon in these studies, and systemic cyclosporine exposure was generally undetectable or negligible. ([AAO Journal][1])

## How to interpret the evidence

**Most likely benefit:** reduced epithelial staining and ocular-surface inflammation. Some patients also experience increased tear production.

**Less certain benefit:** noticeable improvement in burning, dryness, discomfort or vision-related quality of life beyond that produced by the formulation’s vehicle and concurrent lubricants. This does not mean individual patients cannot experience substantial symptomatic benefit; it means the average additional benefit was inconsistent in controlled trials.

**Most likely harm:** short-lived burning or pain immediately after application. The risk appears formulation-dependent rather than simply concentration-dependent.

**Rare or uncertain harms:** the trials did not show a signal for clinically important IOP changes, visual-acuity loss, ocular infection or systemic cyclosporine toxicity. However, most controlled trials lasted only one to six months and were not large enough to exclude very rare harms. Longer studies were principally open-label. ([AAO Journal][1])

There is no reliable head-to-head evidence establishing that one currently marketed formulation is universally most effective. Cross-trial comparisons are limited by different vehicles, dosing schedules, entry criteria and outcome scales. Many pivotal trials were manufacturer-sponsored or included manufacturer employees, which should be considered when assessing certainty. ([AAO Journal][11])
