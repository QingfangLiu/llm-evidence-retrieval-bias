# Evidence map: topical cyclosporine A for dry eye disease

**Search date:** July 25, 2026. I searched PubMed and publisher/full-text records using combinations of *dry eye*, *keratoconjunctivitis sicca*, *cyclosporine/ciclosporin*, individual formulation names, and trial names. The synthesis below uses individual human primary studies only. It is a broad evidence map rather than a PRISMA-complete database search.

## Overall interpretation

The most reproducible benefit of topical cyclosporine A is a **modest improvement in objective ocular-surface signs**, particularly corneal or conjunctival staining and, in some trials, Schirmer tear production. Effects on **patient-reported dryness, discomfort, or OSDI scores are less consistent**: symptoms frequently improve from baseline in both CsA and vehicle groups, without a statistically significant between-group difference. This sign–symptom discordance appears across 0.05% emulsion, 0.09% nanomicellar solution, 0.1% cationic emulsion, and 0.1% water-free solution programs. ([PubMed][1])

Benefits generally become detectable after **four to twelve weeks**. Continued treatment can produce additional improvement through six months or longer, but most controlled studies last three to six months; longer studies are mainly extensions or open-label cohorts. ([PubMed][2])

The principal harm is **transient instillation-site pain, burning, stinging, irritation, or hyperemia**. Rates vary markedly by formulation and vehicle. Serious ocular complications and treatment-related systemic adverse events have been uncommon in the published studies, and measured systemic exposure has generally been negligible. ([PubMed][1])

## Benefits by outcome

### Ocular-surface staining

This is the **most consistently positive outcome**. Large trials of 0.05% emulsion, 0.09% OTX-101, 0.1% cationic emulsion, and water-free 0.1% solution generally report statistically better corneal or conjunctival staining than vehicle. Absolute differences are often small, and the clinical importance depends on the staining scale and baseline severity. ([PubMed][3])

### Tear production

Schirmer improvement is less universal but appears in several pivotal programs. In the OTX-101 phase III trial, the absolute increase in patients achieving a ≥10-mm response was approximately **7.4 percentage points**, corresponding to an unadjusted number needed to treat of about **14**. ([PubMed][5])

### Symptoms and quality of life

Symptomatic benefit is heterogeneous. The Chinese 0.05% emulsion trial and several smaller active-comparator or open-label studies found OSDI or symptom improvements, whereas SANSIKA, OTX-101, and ESSENCE trials frequently found little or no symptom advantage over vehicle. ([PubMed][2])

### Tear-film stability and meibomian-gland dysfunction

Smaller studies suggest improved TBUT and meibomian-gland signs, but this evidence is less robust than the pivotal staining evidence. In a 70-person MGD trial, CsA 0.05% improved invasive and noninvasive TBUT and several lid-margin outcomes more than carboxymethylcellulose. ([PubMed][11])

## Harms

The clinically important tolerability issue is **drop discomfort**, not systemic immunosuppression:

* SANSIKA: instillation pain **29.2% with CsA versus 8.9% with vehicle**. ([Sage Journals][6])
* OTX-101 0.09% phase III: instillation pain **24.2% versus 4.3%**; 2.4% discontinued because of pain. ([AAO Journal][12])
* Water-free 0.1% ESSENCE-2: overall treatment-emergent adverse-event and discontinuation rates were similar to vehicle, with mild instillation reactions in about one in ten participants. ([JAMA Network][13])
* In the older dose-ranging study, blood CsA was generally undetectable or extremely low; the maximum reported value was 0.16 ng/mL. ([PubMed][1])
* A 52-week water-free CsA extension reported mostly mild ocular events; instillation pain occurred in 6.5%, no treatment-related serious event was reported, and three participants discontinued because of adverse events. ([PubMed][14])

Because many efficacy trials excluded active infection, major ocular comorbidity, recent surgery, or substantial concomitant treatment, uncommon harms and tolerability in less-selected clinical populations remain less certain.

## Important applicability issues

**Formulations should not be treated as interchangeable.** Concentration, oil-in-water emulsion, cationic emulsion, nanomicellar delivery, and water-free semifluorinated-alkane vehicles influence ocular exposure and tolerability. A 2025 randomized trial of aqueous versus oil-emulsion CsA 0.05% found improvement with both but differences in several tear-film and lipid-layer measures. ([PubMed][15])

**Special-population evidence is inconsistent.** A 17-person contact-lens pilot favored CsA over rewetting drops, whereas a later 44-person contact-lens trial found no significant objective or subjective advantage. ([PubMed][16])

**Open-label improvements cannot establish comparative efficacy.** For example, the IMPACT study found substantial six-month improvements in staining, visual function, Schirmer scores, TBUT, and symptoms, but every participant received CsA and there was no concurrent control. ([PubMed][17])
