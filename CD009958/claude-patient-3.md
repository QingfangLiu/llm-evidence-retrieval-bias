Here are primary studies (RCTs) directly investigating repositioning frequency and body positioning for pressure injury/ulcer prevention. All are individual trials, not reviews or guidelines.

## Repositioning frequency

**1. Bergstrom et al., 2013 — TURN Study** (*J Am Geriatr Soc*)
Multisite RCT in 27 US/Canadian nursing homes. 942 residents at moderate or high risk of pressure ulcers were randomly allocated to a repositioning schedule of 2, 3, or 4 hours for 3 weeks, with blinded skin assessors. Result: no difference in pressure ulcer incidence between the 2-, 3-, or 4-hour groups when repositioning was done consistently and residents were on high-density foam mattresses.

**2. Yap et al., 2022 — TEAM-UP Trial** (*Adv Skin Wound Care*)
Cluster RCT in nine nursing homes. Facilities were randomly assigned to a single repositioning interval — 2, 3, or 4 hours — as standard care, comparing 4 weeks of intervention data against a 12-month baseline.

**3. Defloor, De Bacquer & Grypdonck, 2005** (*Int J Nurs Stud*) — the largest repositioning trial in the literature
838 geriatric nursing home patients were assigned to one of four regimens: frequent turning (2- or 3-hourly) on a standard mattress, or less frequent turning (4- or 6-hourly) combined with a pressure-reducing mattress. Turning every 4 hours on a viscoelastic foam mattress produced significantly fewer pressure ulcers than turning every 2 or 3 hours on a non-pressure-redistributing mattress — showing that surface type interacts with turning frequency.

**4. Vanderwee, Grypdonck, De Bacquer & Defloor, 2007** (*J Adv Nurs*, "Effectiveness of turning with unequal time intervals...")
Patients on a visco-elastic foam overlay were repositioned either 2-hourly in a 30° lateral position alternating with 4-hourly semi-Fowler 30° supine positioning (n=122), or every 4 hours regardless of position (n=113). 16.4% of the experimental group and 21.2% of the control group developed a pressure ulcer, a difference that was not statistically significant.

**5. Manzano et al., 2014** (*Intensive Care Med*) — ICU/ventilated patients
Pragmatic, open-label RCT in mechanically ventilated ICU patients on alternating pressure air mattresses, randomized to repositioning every 2 hours (n=165) or every 4 hours (n=164). Grade ≥II pressure ulcers occurred in 10.3% of the 2-hourly group versus 13.4% of the 4-hourly group (HR 0.89, 95% CI 0.46–1.71, p=0.73) — not a significant difference — while device-related adverse events were significantly higher with 2-hourly turning (47.9% vs 36.6%, p=0.02).

## Body position

**6. Moore, Cowman & Conroy, 2011** (*J Clin Nurs*)
Cluster RCT in 12 long-term care sites. 213 participants: the experimental group (n=99) was repositioned 3-hourly at night using the 30° tilt; the control group (n=114) received routine 90° lateral rotation every 6 hours. 3% of the experimental group versus 11% of the control group developed a pressure ulcer (p=0.035); mobility and activity were the strongest predictors of ulcer development.

**7. Young, 2004** (*J Tissue Viability*)
RCT in a hospital inpatient population comparing the 30° tilt position (n=23) with the 90° lateral and supine positions (n=23), with incidence of non-blanching erythema (early pressure damage) as the primary outcome.

---

A pattern across these trials: fixed repositioning intervals (2 vs. 3 vs. 4 hours) generally show **no significant difference** in outcomes when a quality support surface is used, while the **30° tilt position consistently outperforms the traditional 90° lateral position** in the trials that tested position directly. Support-surface type appears to interact with how much repositioning frequency matters (Defloor 2005).

Since you're a patient managing your own risk, this is useful context — but the "right" answer for you depends on your specific risk factors, mobility, and support surface, so it's worth discussing these findings with your care team rather than picking a schedule from the literature alone.
