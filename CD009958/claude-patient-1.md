I now have enough good primary-study coverage. Here's what I found — all individual primary studies (RCTs), not reviews or guidelines.

## Repositioning frequency

**Bergstrom et al., 2013 — "Turning for UlceR reductioN (TURN) Study"** (*J Am Geriatr Soc*)
Multisite RCT in 27 nursing homes (US/Canada), N=942 residents at moderate-high risk, all on high-density foam mattresses. Randomized to repositioning every 2, 3, or 4 hours for 3 weeks. Incidence of pressure injuries was low (2.0%) across all groups, with only superficial (Stage 2) ulcers developing and no significant differences between the 2-, 3-, or 4-hour groups. [PMID 24050454]

**Defloor, De Bacquer & Grypdonck, 2005** (*Int J Nurs Stud*)
RCT comparing repositioning frequencies combined with different support surfaces. Comparing 2-hourly (n=63) versus 4-hourly (n=66) repositioning, the relative risk for pressure injury was 1.36 (95% CI 0.98–1.89) — a trend toward more injuries with less frequent turning, though not statistically significant.

**Manzano et al., 2014** (*Intensive Care Med*)
Pragmatic open-label RCT in mechanically ventilated ICU patients in Spain, all on alternating-pressure air mattresses. Randomized to repositioning every 2h (n=165) vs. 4h (n=164). A pressure ulcer of at least grade II developed in 10.3% of patients turned every 2 hours versus 13.4% turned every 4 hours (hazard ratio 0.89, 95% CI 0.46–1.71, P=0.73) — no significant difference, but device-related adverse events were higher with more frequent turning. [PMID 25189288]

**Vanderwee, Grypdonck, De Bacquer & Defloor, 2007** — "Effectiveness of turning with unequal time intervals" (*J Adv Nurs* 57(1):59-68). RCT comparing a 4h-supine/2h-sideways alternating schedule against standard 2-hourly turning.

## Positioning technique

**Moore, Cowman & Conroy, 2011** (*J Clin Nurs*)
Pragmatic, multicentre, cluster-RCT across 12 sites. The experimental group (n=99) were repositioned three-hourly at night using the 30° tilt; the control group (n=114) received routine care (six-hourly repositioning using 90° lateral rotation). The 30° tilt with 3-hourly repositioning reduced pressure ulcer incidence by over 70% compared to the 90° lateral position with 6-hourly repositioning. [PMID 21702861]

**Young, 2004** — "The 30 degree tilt position vs the 90 degree lateral and supine positions in reducing the incidence of non-blanching erythema" (*J Tissue Viability* 14(3):88-96). RCT directly comparing the 30° tilt against 90° lateral and supine positions.
