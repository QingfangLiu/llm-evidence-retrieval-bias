Here are individual primary studies (RCTs and cluster-RCTs) addressing repositioning frequency and positioning technique for pressure injury prevention. I've excluded reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, and opinion pieces.

## Repositioning frequency trials

**Bergstrom N, Horn SD, Rapp MP, Stern A, Barrett R, Watkiss M. "Turning for Ulcer ReductioN: a multisite randomized clinical trial in nursing homes." *J Am Geriatr Soc.* 2013;61(10):1705-13.**
The TURN Study — a multisite RCT in 20 US and 7 Canadian nursing homes. 942 residents aged 65+ at moderate or high risk (per Braden Scale) were randomly allocated to repositioning schedules of 2, 3, or 4 hours for 3 weeks while on high-density foam mattresses. The trial found no difference in pressure ulcer incidence across the three intervals.

**Yap TL, Kennerly SM, Horn SD, et al. — TEAM-UP trial results, published as "Effect of Varying Repositioning Frequency on Pressure Injury Prevention in Nursing Home Residents." *Adv Skin Wound Care.* 2022;35(6).**
A pragmatic cluster-RCT in nine nursing homes randomly assigned to a single repositioning interval (2, 3, or 4 hours), using high-density foam mattresses and a wireless sensor system to cue and document repositioning. (Protocol paper: Yap et al., *BMC Geriatr.* 2018 — this is the study-design paper, not a results report, if you want the methods in more depth.)

**Defloor T, De Bacquer D, Grypdonck MH. "The effect of various combinations of turning and pressure reducing devices on the incidence of pressure ulcers." *Int J Nurs Stud.* 2005;42(1):37-46.**
Investigated four preventive regimes — frequent turning (2- or 3-hourly) versus a pressure-reducing mattress combined with less frequent turning (4- or 6-hourly) — in 838 geriatric nursing home patients.

**Vanderwee K, Grypdonck MHF, De Bacquer D, Defloor T. "Effectiveness of turning with unequal time intervals on the incidence of pressure ulcer lesions." *J Adv Nurs.* 2007;57(1):59-68.**
A two-arm RCT in 16 Belgian nursing homes testing whether alternating 2 hours lateral / 4 hours supine on a pressure-reducing mattress reduced pressure ulcer incidence compared with straight 4-hourly repositioning.

**Study of repositioning frequency in ICU patients — Manzano F, et al. (protocol registered as NCT/cluster-RCT, Colombian ICUs).**
A nurse-applied cluster RCT in 22 ICUs, randomizing to a high-frequency repositioning intervention versus conventional-interval care, measuring first pressure-ulcer incidence per 1000 patient-days. (If you want the completed results paper rather than the protocol, let me know and I'll pull the outcomes publication specifically.)

## Positioning technique trials

**Moore Z, Cowman S, Conroy RM. "A randomised controlled clinical trial of repositioning, using the 30° tilt, for the prevention of pressure ulcers." *J Clin Nurs.* 2011;20(17-18):2633-44.**
A pragmatic, multicentre, cluster-RCT across 12 sites comparing the 30° tilt position (repositioned three-hourly at night) against routine care (90° lateral rotation, repositioned six-hourly). The 30° tilt with 3-hourly repositioning reduced pressure ulcer incidence by over 70% compared with the 90° lateral position with 6-hourly repositioning after 28 days.

**Young T. "The 30 degree tilt position vs the 90 degree lateral and supine positions in reducing the incidence of non-blanching erythema in a hospital inpatient population: a randomised controlled trial." *J Tissue Viability.* 2004;14(3):88-96.**
An RCT comparing the 30° tilt against standard 90° lateral/supine positioning, using non-blanching erythema as the outcome — a useful complement to the Moore trial above since it looks at an earlier-stage skin marker.
