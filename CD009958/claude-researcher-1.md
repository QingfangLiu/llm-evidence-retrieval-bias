Here are individual primary studies (RCTs and cluster-RCTs) on repositioning frequency and body positioning for pressure injury prevention in adults — reviews, guidelines, and commentaries excluded.

## Repositioning frequency trials

**Bergstrom N, Horn SD, Rapp MP, Stern A, Barrett R, Watkiss M (2013).** *Turning for Ulcer ReductioN: A Multisite Randomized Clinical Trial in Nursing Homes.* J Am Geriatr Soc 61(10):1705-13.
942 nursing home residents at moderate/high risk, on high-density foam mattresses, randomized to 2-, 3-, or 4-hour turning schedules for 3 weeks; no difference in pressure ulcer incidence between the three intervals.

**Yap TL, Kennerly SM, Horn SD, Bergstrom N, et al. (2022).** *Effect of Varying Repositioning Frequency on Pressure Injury Prevention in Nursing Home Residents: The TEAM-UP Randomized Clinical Trial Results.* Adv Skin Wound Care 35:315-25.
Embedded pragmatic cluster-RCT in nine nursing homes randomized to one of three repositioning intervals (2, 3, or 4 hours), using wireless sensors to cue staff on repositioning need. Protocol paper: Yap TL et al., PMC5820803 (2018) — tests whether visco-elastic high-density foam mattresses permit extending intervals to 3-4 hours without increasing incidence.

**Defloor T, De Bacquer D, Grypdonck MH (2005).** *The effect of various combinations of turning and pressure reducing devices on the incidence of pressure ulcers.* Int J Nurs Stud 42(1):37-46. Two-arm RCT in 16 Belgian nursing homes; compared repositioning alternating 2-hour lateral/4-hour supine on a pressure-reducing mattress against 4-hourly repositioning alone.

**Manzano F, Colmenero M, Pérez-Pérez AM, et al. (2014).** *Comparison of two repositioning schedules for the prevention of pressure ulcers in patients on mechanical ventilation with alternating pressure air mattresses.* Intensive Care Med 40:1679-87. Pragmatic open-label RCT of mechanically ventilated ICU patients on alternating pressure air mattresses, randomized to repositioning every 2 h (n=165) vs every 4 h (n=164); grade ≥II ulcers developed in 10.3% vs 13.4% (HR 0.89, 95% CI 0.46–1.71, not significant).

**Cortés OL, et al.** *Frequency of repositioning for preventing pressure ulcers in patients hospitalized in ICU* — protocol/trial, BMC Nursing (2021). Multicentric, double-blind cluster-RCT across Colombian ICUs comparing repositioning ≤2 hours vs conventional care.

## Positioning (tilt/lateral vs supine) trials

**Moore Z, Cowman S, Conroy RM (2011).** *A randomised controlled clinical trial of repositioning, using the 30° tilt, for the prevention of pressure ulcers.* J Clin Nurs 20(17-18):2633-44. Pragmatic multi-centre, open-label, cluster-RCT across 12 sites comparing two repositioning regimens in older persons; experimental group repositioned three-hourly overnight using the 30° tilt.

**Young T (2004).** *The 30 degree tilt position vs the 90 degree lateral and supine positions in reducing the incidence of non-blanching erythema in a hospital inpatient population: A randomised controlled trial.* J Tissue Viability 14:88-96.

**Ghezeljeh T, Kalhor L, Moghadam O, Lahiji M, Haghani H.** *The comparison of the effect of the head of bed elevation to 30 and 45 degrees on the incidence of ventilator associated pneumonia and the risk for pressure ulcers: a controlled randomised clinical trial.* (per citation in ftrdergisi.com source)

**Vanderwee K, Grypdonck MH, De Bacquer D, Defloor T (2007).** *Effectiveness of turning with unequal time intervals on the incidence of pressure ulcer lesions.* J Adv Nurs. RCT testing alternating 2-hour lateral/4-hour supine positioning vs standard 4-hourly repositioning; found pressure is higher in the lateral than the supine position.
