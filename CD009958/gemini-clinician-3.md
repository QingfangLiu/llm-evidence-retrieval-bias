As a clinician, looking past standard "routine" practices and directly at the primary trial data is the best way to optimize patient care without placing unnecessary burden on nursing staff.

While the "2-hour turning rule" has been historical doctrine since Florence Nightingale, major randomized controlled trials (RCTs) over the last two decades have demonstrated that the optimal frequency depends heavily on the support surface being used, and that positioning angles matter just as much as frequency.

Here is what the primary clinical trials reveal about repositioning frequencies and body positions for pressure injury (PI) prevention.

## Optimal Repositioning Frequency

The primary literature indicates that extending the turning interval from 2 hours to 3 or 4 hours is safe and effective—**provided the patient is on a high-density or viscoelastic foam mattress**.

* **The TURN Study (Bergstrom et al., 2013):** This multisite RCT investigated nursing home residents at moderate to high risk of developing PIs. They compared 2-hour, 3-hour, and 4-hour turning intervals for patients placed on high-density foam mattresses.
* **Finding:** There was **no statistically significant difference** in PI incidence among the three groups. The study concluded that 4-hour repositioning is just as effective as 2-hour repositioning when using appropriate pressure-redistributing mattresses, which has major implications for reducing sleep disruption and nursing staff workload.


* **The Defloor Trial (Defloor et al., 2005):** This highly cited multicenter RCT evaluated four different turning and mattress combinations: 2-hour and 3-hour turning on standard institutional mattresses, versus 4-hour and 6-hour turning on viscoelastic foam mattresses.
* **Finding:** Turning every 4 hours on a viscoelastic foam mattress resulted in a **significant reduction** in the number of pressure ulcer lesions compared to 2-hour turning on a standard mattress. The 4-hour interval on advanced foam was the most effective and feasible preventative method tested.



## Optimal Body Position

When repositioning patients, avoiding direct pressure on the sacrum, coccyx, and trochanter (hip bone) is critical. The primary evidence strongly supports the **30-degree tilt** over the traditional 90-degree lateral (full side-lying) position.

* **The 30° Tilt Trial (Moore et al., 2011):** This cluster-randomized controlled clinical trial compared two different repositioning regimens in older adults at risk for PIs. The experimental group received repositioning using a 30-degree tilt every 3 hours overnight, while the control group received standard care (a 90-degree lateral rotation every 6 hours).
* **Finding:** The 30-degree tilt combined with 3-hourly repositioning **significantly reduced** the incidence of pressure ulcers compared to the 90-degree standard care group. The 30-degree angle effectively shifts weight off the bony prominences of the pelvis without transferring the ischemic load directly onto the greater trochanter.



## Key Clinical Takeaway

Based on these primary trials, the traditional "turn every 2 hours" mandate can safely be updated. If your patients are equipped with high-density or viscoelastic foam mattresses, repositioning them **every 3 to 4 hours using a 30-degree lateral tilt** provides an optimal balance of superior pressure injury prevention, better patient sleep quality, and reduced manual handling strain for your clinical team.
