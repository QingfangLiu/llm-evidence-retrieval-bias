## Repositioning frequency

**1. Bergstrom N, Horn SD, Rapp MP, Stern A, Barrett R, Watkiss M (2013).** *Turning for Ulcer ReductioN (TURN Study): a multisite randomized clinical trial in nursing homes.* J Am Geriatr Soc. 61(10):1705-1713.
- N=942 residents, moderate/high risk (Braden 10–14), high-density foam mattresses, randomized to turning every 2, 3, or 4 hours for 3 weeks. No difference in pressure ulcer incidence over 3 weeks of observation between those turned at 2-, 3-, or 4-hour intervals when they were repositioned consistently and skin was monitored.

**2. Yap TL, Horn SD, Sharkey PD, Zheng T, Bergstrom N, Colon-Emeric C, Sabol VK, Alderden J, Yap W, Kennerly SM (2022).** *Effect of Varying Repositioning Frequency on Pressure Injury Prevention in Nursing Home Residents: TEAM-UP Trial Results.* Adv Skin Wound Care. 35(6):315-325.
- Cluster RCT across nine nursing homes randomized to 2-, 3-, or 4-hour repositioning intervals, using a wearable sensor system to cue staff, in residents without current pressure injuries at Braden ≥10 on high-density foam mattresses, followed for 4 weeks after 12 months of baseline data.

**3. Vanderwee K, Grypdonck MHF, De Bacquer D, Defloor T (2007).** *Effectiveness of turning with unequal time intervals on the incidence of pressure ulcer lesions.* J Adv Nurs. 57(1):59-68.
- Experimental group (n=122) was repositioned 2-hourly in the 30° lateral position, alternated with 4-hourly semi-Fowler 30° supine positioning, compared with a control group (n=113) nursed on the same viscoelastic foam overlay but positioned every 4 hours. 16.4% of the experimental group developed a pressure ulcer versus 21.2% of controls, a difference that was not statistically significant.

**4. Defloor T, De Bacquer D, Grypdonck MHF (2005).** *The effect of various combinations of turning and pressure reducing devices on the incidence of pressure ulcers.* Int J Nurs Stud. 42(1):37-46. (PMID 15582638)
- Factorial RCT comparing turning schedules combined with different mattress types; described elsewhere in the literature as one of the largest repositioning trials.

## Positioning technique (30° tilt vs. 90° lateral/other)

**5. Moore Z, Cowman S, Conroy RM (2011).** *A randomised controlled clinical trial of repositioning, using the 30° tilt, for the prevention of pressure ulcers.* J Clin Nurs. 20(17-18):2633-2644.
- Pragmatic, multi-centre, cluster-RCT. Experimental group (n=99) repositioned three-hourly at night using the 30° tilt; control group (n=114) received routine six-hourly repositioning using 90° lateral rotation. 3% in the experimental group and 11% in the control group developed a pressure ulcer (p=0.035).

**6. Young T (2004).** *The 30 degree tilt position vs the 90 degree lateral and supine positions in reducing the incidence of non-blanching erythema in a hospital inpatient population: a randomised controlled trial.* J Tissue Viability. 14(3):88-96.
- RCT examining the effects of the 30-degree tilt position, which in laboratory settings reduced contact pressure between patient and support surface, in reducing non-blanching erythema in a small hospital sample compared with 90° lateral and supine positions.

## Additional condition-specific trial

**7. (Stroke population)** *Comparison of the Effectiveness of Lateral Position 30 Degrees and 90 Degrees on Decubitus Events in Stroke Patients.*
- RCT in stroke patients comparing 30° versus 90° lateral tilt position on risk of pressure ulcer development, with the control group tilted 90 degrees and the intervention group tilted 30 degrees.
