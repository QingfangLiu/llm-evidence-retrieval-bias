## Primary studies on repositioning frequency

**Bergstrom et al. (2013), "Turning for Ulcer ReductioN" (TURN Study)** — Multisite RCT, 942 nursing home residents (US/Canada), moderate/high risk (Braden 10–14), on high-density foam mattresses, randomized to 2-, 3-, or 4-hourly turning for 3 weeks. Participants were randomly allocated using risk stratification to a repositioning schedule (2, 3, or 4 hour) for 3 weeks with blinded weekly skin assessment. Found no difference in pressure ulcer incidence between the three turning intervals when residents were on high-density foam mattresses.
*J Am Geriatr Soc. 2013;61(10):1705-13.*

**Defloor, De Bacquer & Grypdonck (2005)** — RCT in 11 Belgian nursing homes comparing 2-, 3-, 4-, and 6-hourly turning combined with either a standard institutional mattress or a viscoelastic foam mattress. Participants receiving 2-hourly and 3-hourly repositioning were on the standard institutional mattress, while those receiving 4-hourly and 6-hourly repositioning were on viscoelastic foam mattresses; 4-hourly turning on the foam mattress produced significantly fewer pressure ulcers than the more frequent turning schedules on the standard mattress.
*Int J Nurs Stud. 2005;42(1):37-46.*

**Vanderwee, Grypdonck, De Bacquer & Defloor (2007)** — Two-arm RCT, 16 Belgian nursing homes, patients with non-blanchable erythema, comparing alternating 2h-lateral/4h-supine positioning (on a pressure-reducing overlay) vs. routine 4-hourly repositioning. In the experimental group (n=122), patients were repositioned alternately 2 hours in a lateral position and 4 hours in a supine position; in the control group (n=113), patients were repositioned every 4 hours. Pressure ulcer incidence (grade 2-4) was 16±4% in the experimental group vs 21±2% in the control group, not statistically significant.
*J Adv Nurs. 2007;57(1):59-68.*

**Manzano et al. (2014)** — RCT, mechanically ventilated ICU patients on alternating-pressure air mattresses, comparing 2-hourly vs 4-hourly repositioning. A pressure ulcer of at least grade II developed in 10.3% (17/165) of patients turned every 2h versus 13.4% (22/164) of those turned every 4h (HR 0.89, 95% CI 0.46-1.71, P=0.73); device-related adverse events were higher in the 2-hourly group (47.9% vs 36.6%, P=0.02).
*Intensive Care Med. 2014;40(11):1679-87.*

**Darvall, Mesfin & Gorelik (2018)** — Pre-post intervention study, single tertiary ICU, comparing 5-hourly vs 3-hourly turning schedules. A pre-post intervention evaluation compared a 6-month period with 5-hourly turns to a 6-month period with 3-hourly turns, separated by a 3-week wash-in period, in a metropolitan tertiary referral ICU, aiming to determine the impact on pressure injury incidence. The 3-hourly schedule was associated with roughly a 50% reduction in pressure injury incidence.
*Crit Care Resusc. 2018;20(3):217-22.*

**Yap et al. (2022), "TEAM-UP" trial** — Cluster-randomized study, 9 US nursing homes, residents at low/moderate/high risk on viscoelastic support surfaces, randomized by facility to 2-, 3-, or 4-hourly repositioning intervals over 4 weeks (N=951).
*Adv Skin Wound Care. 2022;35(6):315-25.*

## Primary studies on positioning technique (30° tilt vs. lateral/supine)

**Moore, Cowman & Conroy (2011)** — Cluster-RCT, 12 Irish long-term care sites, 213 participants, comparing 3-hourly 30° tilt positioning vs. routine 6-hourly 90° lateral rotation. The experimental group (n=99) were repositioned three hourly at night, using the 30° tilt; the control group (n=114) received routine prevention (six-hourly repositioning, using 90° lateral rotation). Three patients (3%) in the experimental group and 13 patients (11%) in the control group developed a pressure ulcer (p=0.035).
*J Clin Nurs. 2011;20(17-18):2633-44.*

**Young (2004)** — RCT comparing the 30° tilt position against 90° lateral and supine positions for reducing non-blanching erythema in hospital inpatients.
*J Tissue Viability. 2004;14(3):88-96.*

## Related primary study (support surface, included for context on repositioning interaction)

**Vanderwee, Grypdonck & Defloor (2005)** — RCT, 19 wards across 7 Belgian hospitals, comparing alternating pressure air mattresses vs. standard prevention (repositioning-based).

A few notes for your synthesis:
- The **Bergstrom/TURN**, **Defloor**, **Vanderwee 2007**, and **Manzano** trials are the core RCTs on repositioning *frequency* and are the ones most syntheses build from — worth checking each for risk-of-bias, since several confound frequency with mattress type (especially Defloor).
- **Darvall 2018** is a pre-post design, not an RCT — flag this if your inclusion criteria are strict about randomization.
- I excluded the Cochrane review, RNAO/NPIAP guidelines, and narrative/scoping reviews that surfaced in search, per your instructions — though they're useful as citation-trail sources if you want to hand-search their reference lists for additional primary studies I may have missed (e.g., older/non-English trials).
