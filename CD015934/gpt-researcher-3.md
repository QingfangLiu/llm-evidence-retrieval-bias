## Scope

I treated an eligible programme as one in which smoking-cessation treatment was **first delivered during an adult psychiatric hospitalization**, even when most treatment continued after discharge. I included randomized, controlled and uncontrolled intervention studies reporting smoking abstinence or consumption outcomes. I excluded protocols, secondary analyses, outpatient-only studies, adolescent studies and nonintervention observational research.

The search was updated through **July 25, 2026**. Studies conducted in psychiatric day-care rather than inpatient wards are separated below.

## Overall finding

**Quit-smoking programmes begun during psychiatric hospitalization probably help some patients quit, particularly when treatment combines counseling, nicotine replacement therapy and active support after discharge.** The most informative trials reported:

* Prochaska: 6-month abstinence **14.4% versus 6.5%**.
* Metse: 6-month abstinence **15.8% versus 9.3%**.
* Brown: biochemically verified 6-month abstinence **8.9% versus 3.5%**.

Thus, the absolute improvement in the positive larger trials was approximately **5–8 additional quitters per 100 patients**. ([PMC][1])

The result was not completely consistent. Stockings found a significant advantage at four months but not six months, and the small Hickman feasibility trial produced numerically higher quit rates without providing definitive evidence of efficacy. ([OUP Academic][2])

Two qualifications matter:

1. Most successful programmes contained substantial **postdischarge treatment**, so their findings do not isolate the effect of the inpatient component itself.
2. Effects were clearer for **7-day point-prevalence abstinence** than for prolonged or continuous abstinence, and were not always maintained at 12 months.

## Study-level findings

### Multicomponent programmes compared with usual care

| Primary study                   | Population and intervention                                                                                                                                                           | Main smoking result                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Prochaska 2014**              | 224 smokers on a locked acute psychiatric unit; motivationally tailored counseling plus posthospital nicotine replacement versus usual care                                           | Verified 7-day abstinence was 14.4% versus 6.5% at 6 months and 20.0% versus 7.7% at 18 months. This is the clearest evidence of a sustained effect. ([PMC][1])                                                                                                          |
| **Stockings 2014**              | 205 Australian psychiatric inpatients; psychosocial and pharmacological support extending four months after discharge versus usual care                                               | Abstinence at four months was 11.5% versus 2.0%, but neither point-prevalence nor continuous abstinence differed at six months. Cigarette consumption, dependence and quit attempts nevertheless favored intervention. ([OUP Academic][2])                               |
| **Hickman 2015**                | 100 uninsured, socioeconomically disadvantaged psychiatric inpatients; tailored computer/manual intervention, counseling and 10 weeks of posthospital NRT versus enhanced usual care  | Abstinence was numerically higher at 6 months, 17.5% versus 8.5%, and at 12 months, 26.2% versus 16.7%. This was explicitly a small feasibility/replication study rather than a definitive efficacy trial. ([PMC][3])                                                    |
| **Metse 2017**                  | 754 patients across four acute psychiatric facilities; brief inpatient motivational intervention followed by four months of pharmacological and psychosocial support                  | Seven-day abstinence was higher at 6 months, 15.8% versus 9.3%, but not at 12 months, 13.4% versus 10.0%. There was no significant effect on one-month prolonged abstinence. ([Sage Journals][4])                                                                        |
| **Brown 2021 — Helping HAND 3** | 353 randomized adults with serious mental illness; inpatient motivational counseling, discharge nicotine patches, quitline/text/web support and automated follow-up versus usual care | Biochemically verified 6-month abstinence was 8.9% versus 3.5%; adjusted OR 2.95, 95% CI 1.24–6.99. Treatment engagement was also substantially higher. ([PMC][5])                                                                                                       |
| **Bennett 2026**                | 58 psychiatric inpatients; hospital orientation to an adapted digital cessation programme with postdischarge digital resources versus brief counseling                                | At one month, 6 digital-intervention participants versus none receiving brief counseling reported 7-day abstinence, p=0.04. This was a feasibility pilot; the published abstract does not report a convincing longer-term comparative estimate. ([Psychiatry Online][6]) |

### Medication or behavioral comparisons

| Primary study              | Intervention comparison                                                                                                | Main smoking result                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Akbarpour 2010**         | 32 hospitalized patients with schizophrenia; eight weeks of bupropion versus placebo                                   | The authors reported greater smoking abstinence/discontinuation with bupropion at the end of treatment, but the accessible primary abstract provides no event counts or longer-term abstinence result. The estimate is therefore difficult to appraise or use quantitatively. ([Minerva Medica][7]) |
| **Gelkopf 2012**           | 53 long-term hospitalized patients with schizophrenia; five-session behavioral smoking-reduction group versus waitlist | Cigarettes smoked decreased relative to waitlist at three months, without clinical worsening or weight gain. The study demonstrated **reduction**, not smoking cessation. ([PubMed][8])                                                                                                             |
| **Chen 2013**              | 184 long-term hospitalized patients with schizophrenia; higher-dose versus lower-dose nicotine patches                 | Only 5 of 184 participants, 2.7%, achieved 7-day abstinence. Smoking cessation and reduction were not meaningfully related to patch dose; higher-dose treatment was not superior. ([NYMU Academic Hub][9])                                                                                          |
| **Tavakoli-Ardakani 2024** | 47 psychiatric inpatients motivated to quit; cytisine for 25 days versus 2-mg nicotine gum for eight weeks             | At six months, complete cessation occurred in 3/17 cytisine participants versus 2/30 NRT participants. The trial was open-label and extremely small, so it provides only a preliminary signal and cannot establish superiority. ([Taylor & Francis Online][10])                                     |

### Uncontrolled inpatient pilot

**Kacmarek 2026 — StayQuit:** Twenty-six Veterans were enrolled in an inpatient-plus-telephone counseling programme; only 15 completed counseling and follow-up. Among completers, cigarettes per day decreased and abstinence self-efficacy increased, but there was no control group and the study did not establish a cessation effect. ([PMC][11])

## Interpretation for evidence synthesis

The evidence supports a cautious conclusion:

> **Multicomponent cessation care started on a psychiatric ward and actively continued after discharge can produce a modest increase in short- to medium-term abstinence compared with usual care.**

Confidence is limited by low quit-event numbers, substantial heterogeneity, single-site studies, weak evidence for prolonged abstinence, and inability to distinguish the effect of inpatient initiation from the effect of continued medication and counseling after discharge. Evidence for behavioral reduction alone, bupropion, different NRT doses, cytisine or digital treatment is currently insufficient for a firm comparative conclusion.
