## Findings

I focused on **randomized or cluster-randomized primary studies involving adults**, where the intervention began during admission to a psychiatric inpatient unit. I excluded reviews, protocols, secondary analyses, adolescent studies, outpatient-only trials, and studies evaluating only smoke-free policies or withdrawal management.

**Overall, quit-smoking treatment started during psychiatric hospitalization can help, especially when it includes counseling, nicotine-replacement therapy, and proactive treatment after discharge.** Results are less convincing for ward-only interventions, smoking-reduction programs, or comparisons between medications/NRT formulations.

### Multicomponent programs continued after discharge

| Study                         | Design and intervention                                                                                                                                                                                  | Smoking outcome                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Prochaska 2014**            | RCT, 224 acute psychiatric inpatients. Motivationally tailored computer/counseling intervention plus up to 10 weeks of postdischarge NRT versus inpatient NRT and written advice.                        | Verified 7-day abstinence favored intervention at 6 months, **14.4% vs 6.5%**, and at 18 months, **20.0% vs 7.7%**. | **Hickman 2015**              | Feasibility RCT, 100 predominantly uninsured and socioeconomically disadvantaged inpatients. Hospital orientation to a digital cessation program with web, text, and online-community components versus brief counseling.                                | At one month, **6 digital-intervention participants versus none receiving brief counseling** reported 7-day abstinence. | Study                      | Design and intervention                                                                                                                   | Smoking outcome                                                                                                                                                                                                         |

| **Akbarpour 2010**         | Double-blind RCT, 32 hospitalized patients with schizophrenia. Eight weeks of bupropion versus placebo.                                   | The authors reported greater smoking discontinuation/reduction with bupropion at treatment end, but numerical abstinence results were inadequately reported and there was no long-term follow-up. ## Clinical interpretation

The most transferable model for an inpatient psychiatry service is:

**Begin motivational counseling and NRT during admission, arrange medication at discharge, and provide proactive follow-up for several months.**

Three substantial trials demonstrated higher point-prevalence abstinence at six months, although absolute quit rates remained modest—approximately **9%–16% with intervention versus 3%–9% with usual care**. ([PMC][1]) Durability is uncertain: Prochaska’s benefit persisted to 18 months, whereas the large Metse trial’s abstinence difference was no longer significant at 12 months. ([PMC][1])

Medication-only and NRT-formulation studies are generally small, short, or focused on smoking reduction rather than sustained abstinence. They do not yet identify a clearly superior inpatient pharmacotherapy strategy. ([Minerva Medica][7])
