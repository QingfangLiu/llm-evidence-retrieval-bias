Here's what I found. I searched PubMed and the web for individual primary studies (trials and other original research) — not reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, or opinion pieces — evaluating smoking-cessation programmes **initiated on psychiatric inpatient wards** for adults with mental illness.

## Summary of findings

The primary evidence base is dominated by randomized controlled trials, mostly combining brief motivational/behavioral counseling started during the admission with nicotine replacement therapy (NRT) and post-discharge follow-up support. Consistent themes across trials:

- Interventions starting in-hospital and continuing after discharge tend to raise short/medium-term abstinence (often 6 months), with effects sometimes fading by 12–18 months without continued support.
- Post-discharge support (calls, NRT continuation, counseling) appears critical — inpatient-only intervention without post-discharge follow-through is less durable.
- Uptake of NRT and counseling during and after admission is generally high when proactively offered.
- Newer pilot work is testing digital/app-based extensions of these "sustained care" models to bridge the discharge gap.

## Primary studies

1. **Prochaska JJ, Hall SE, Delucchi K, Hall SM.** Efficacy of initiating tobacco dependence treatment in inpatient psychiatry: a randomized controlled trial. *Am J Public Health*. 2014;104(8):1557–1565.

2. **Stockings EA, Bowman JA, Baker AL, et al.** Impact of a postdischarge smoking cessation intervention for smokers admitted to an inpatient psychiatric facility: a randomized controlled trial. *Nicotine Tob Res*. 2014;16(11):1417–1428.

3. **Metse AP, Wye P, Wiggers J, et al.** Efficacy of a universal smoking cessation intervention initiated in inpatient psychiatry and continued post-discharge: a randomised controlled trial. *Aust N Z J Psychiatry*. 2017.

4. **Rigotti NA, Regan S, Levy DE, et al.** Sustained care intervention and postdischarge smoking cessation among hospitalized adults: a randomized clinical trial. (Includes psychiatric inpatients as a subgroup analyzed for treatment effect.) 2014.

5. **Japuntich SJ, Hammett PJ, Rogers ES, et al.** Sustained Care Smoking Cessation Intervention for Individuals Hospitalized for Psychiatric Disorders: The Helping HAND 3 Randomized Clinical Trial. *JAMA Psychiatry*. 2021.

6. **White SA, et al.** Bridging the Hospital Discharge Gap: Pilot Trial of a Digital Smoking Cessation Program for Psychiatric Inpatients. *Psychiatric Services*. 2026 (pilot RCT, n=58).

7. **Barnett PG, Wong W, Jeffers A, Hall SM, Prochaska JJ.** Cost-effectiveness of smoking cessation treatment initiated during psychiatric hospitalization: analysis from a randomized, controlled trial. *J Clin Psychiatry*. 2015 (secondary economic analysis of the trial in #1, using the same primary trial data).

8. **Ganhao I, Trigo M, Paixao A.** Evaluation of tobacco use and willingness to accept nicotine replacement therapy during stay in an acute inpatient psychiatric ward. *BJPsych Open*. 2021 (small observational feasibility study, n=40).

A few notes on scope: I excluded trials whose intervention was recruited/delivered in **outpatient** psychiatric settings (e.g., Baker et al. 2006 on psychotic disorder, Hall et al. depressed outpatients, SCIMITAR+), since your question specifically concerns programmes *started on the inpatient ward*, even though these are often cited alongside inpatient trials in reviews. I also left out several published **study protocols** without outcome data (e.g., the Stockings/Baker 2011 and 2014 BMC Public Health protocols) since they don't yet report primary results — I flagged them only because their eventual results papers (#2 and #3 above) are what you want.
