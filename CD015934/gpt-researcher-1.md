## Bottom line

**Quit-smoking programmes begun during psychiatric hospitalization can improve cessation, particularly when inpatient counseling and nicotine-replacement therapy are continued after discharge.** Across the larger randomized trials, the absolute improvement in 6-month point-prevalence abstinence was generally about **5–8 percentage points**. However, quit rates remained low, effects beyond 6 months were inconsistent, and most interventions bundled inpatient initiation with substantial post-discharge treatment—so the independent effect of starting treatment on the ward cannot be isolated.

I found **nine primary intervention trials and one primary companion cohort report** meeting the setting criterion. All studies cited below are primary reports, not reviews, guidelines, editorials, or committee statements.

## What the primary studies collectively suggest

The most reproducible benefit comes from **multicomponent care**: counseling begun during admission, provision of NRT, and active continuity after discharge. Three substantive RCTs found higher 6-month point-prevalence abstinence:

* Prochaska: absolute difference **+7.9 percentage points**.
* Metse: **+6.5 percentage points**.
* Brown/Helping HAND 3: **+5.4 percentage points**. ([PMC][1])

Stockings found an effect at 4 months but not at 6 months, while Hickman’s smaller feasibility trial showed favorable numerical differences without providing a definitive comparative estimate. 

Longer-term cessation is less certain. Prochaska retained a clear advantage through 18 months, whereas Metse found no significant difference at 12 months and no effect on prolonged abstinence. ([PMC][1])

The stand-alone pharmacotherapy and reduction studies are too small or methodologically limited to identify an optimal drug or regimen. In particular, higher-dose patching did not outperform lower-dose patching, the cytisine comparison had only five total quitters, and the bupropion inpatient trial was inadequately reported. ([NYMU Academic Hub][6])

### Important synthesis limitations

Most positive studies measured **7-day point-prevalence abstinence**, rather than sustained or prolonged abstinence. The tested packages also continued for weeks or months after discharge; their results therefore support an **inpatient-initiated continuity-of-care model**, not a brief ward-only intervention. Finally, the Taiwanese programme reports may contain overlapping participants, so they should be checked carefully before inclusion as independent datasets.
