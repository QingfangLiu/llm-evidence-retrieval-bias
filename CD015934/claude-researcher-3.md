## Primary Studies: Smoking Cessation Programmes Initiated in Psychiatric Inpatient Wards

Below are individual primary studies (trials, cohort/feasibility studies) evaluating smoking-cessation interventions **initiated while adults were hospitalized on a psychiatric ward**. I excluded systematic reviews, meta-analyses, narrative reviews, guidelines, and editorials.

**Randomised controlled trials (efficacy/effectiveness)**

1. Prochaska JJ, Hall SE, Delucchi K, Hall SM. Efficacy of initiating tobacco dependence treatment in inpatient psychiatry: a randomized controlled trial. Participants were recruited from a locked acute psychiatry unit with a 100% smoking ban; verified abstinence was significantly higher for the motivational/NRT intervention than usual care across follow-ups to 18 months, and the usual-care group had greater likelihood of psychiatric rehospitalization. *Am J Public Health* 2014;104(8):1557–65.

2. Stockings EA, Bowman JA, Baker AL, et al. Impact of a postdischarge smoking cessation intervention for smokers admitted to an inpatient psychiatric facility: a randomized controlled trial, conducted at an Australian inpatient psychiatric facility with 205 patient smokers; postdischarge cessation support was effective in encouraging quit attempts and reducing cigarette consumption up to 6 months postdischarge. *Nicotine Tob Res* 2014;16(11):1417–28.

3. Metse AP, Wiggers J, Wye P, et al. Efficacy of a universal smoking cessation intervention initiated in inpatient psychiatry and continued post-discharge: a randomised controlled trial across four Australian psychiatric inpatient facilities (N=754); the intervention increased 7-day point prevalence cessation and quitting behaviours at 6 months, with sustained effects on quitting behaviour at 12 months. *Aust N Z J Psychiatry* 2017;51(4):366–381.

4. Brown RA, Minami H, Hecht J, et al. Sustained Care Smoking Cessation Intervention for Individuals Hospitalized for Psychiatric Disorders: The Helping HAND 3 Randomized Clinical Trial. Using data from Helping HAND 3 (NCT02204956), sustained care — inpatient motivational counseling, free nicotine patches at discharge, and offers of postdischarge quitline/text/web counseling plus automated calls/texts — was compared with usual care among 342 patients hospitalized for psychiatric disorders; findings supported the effectiveness of this brief, multicomponent intervention for promoting cessation-medication/counseling use and abstinence after discharge. *JAMA Psychiatry* 2021;78(9):1016–1025.

5. Hickman NJ 3rd, Delucchi KL, Prochaska JJ. Treating Tobacco Dependence at the Intersection of Diversity, Poverty, and Mental Illness: A Randomized Feasibility and Replication Trial, pooling data from two randomized tobacco-treatment trials conducted in inpatient psychiatry (San Francisco, recruited April 2009–April 2010), examining outcomes among patients with co-occurring substance use disorders. *Nicotine Tob Res* 2015;17(8):1012–1021.

6. Prochaska JJ, et al. Smokers with serious mental illness and requests for nicotine replacement therapy post-hospitalisation. This study counselled smokers hospitalised in smoke-free inpatient psychiatry units to quit and offered free NRT at discharge; receiving NRT right at discharge was related to reported abstinence and quit attempts at 1-week post-hospitalisation. *Tob Control* 2016.

7. "Bridging the Hospital Discharge Gap" pilot trial. A randomized pilot trial evaluating feasibility, acceptability, and preliminary outcomes of a digital smoking cessation intervention adapted for psychiatric inpatients to bridge the postdischarge period. *Psychiatric Services* 2026.

**Secondary/process analyses of the above RCT cohorts (also primary data, not reviews)**

8. Metse AP, et al. Uptake of smoking cessation aids by smokers with a mental illness — analysis of RCT data (N=378) on use of cessation counselling, NRT, and Quitline support among psychiatric inpatients offered these aids at discharge. *Prev Med Rep* 2016.

**Other primary (non-RCT) studies conducted in psychiatric wards**

9. Hasnaoui S, Ramachandran V. Evaluating appropriate use of nicotine replacement therapy on acute adult psychiatric units and adverse events related to smoking bans on wards — a retrospective review of electronic records of inpatients admitted to two acute adult units over a three-month period (65 patients). *BJPsych Open* 2021.

10. Evaluation of tobacco use and willingness to accept nicotine replacement therapy during stay in an acute inpatient psychiatric ward — a prospective evaluation of 40 of 44 patients in a general adult psychiatric inpatient unit regarding smoking habits and willingness to accept NRT and a cessation programme. *BJPsych Open* 2021.
