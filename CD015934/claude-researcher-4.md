## Summary

I searched for original research (excluding systematic reviews, meta-analyses, narrative reviews, guidelines, and editorials) on smoking-cessation interventions **initiated during adult psychiatric inpatient admission**. The available literature is dominated by a cluster of RCTs from Australia, the US, and the UK, plus a couple of pre-post/pilot implementation studies and a nurse-led service evaluation. Findings across these trials are broadly consistent: interventions begun on the ward and continued with pharmacological/behavioural support after discharge modestly increase short-term abstinence (e.g., 7-day point prevalence) and quit attempts relative to usual care, but effects tend to attenuate by 12–18 months, and one trial additionally found a reduction in psychiatric rehospitalization in the intervention arm. Pure pilot/implementation studies (Parker et al.; the acute-unit pre-post study) report on feasibility and process change rather than long-term abstinence.

I excluded protocol-only papers that describe planned trials without reported outcomes (e.g., the Clancy et al. 2011 and Baker et al. 2014 study protocols, and the Spanish telephone-intervention protocol), since these are not primary studies reporting results, and excluded SCIMITAR+/other trials that recruit from community mental health teams rather than initiating treatment on an inpatient ward.
