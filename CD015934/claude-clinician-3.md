Let me also check the older classic studies (e.g., an earlier trial on smoke-free units) with one more search.Based on my searches, here are individual primary studies (RCTs and other primary research, not reviews) that specifically test smoking-cessation interventions **initiated on a psychiatric inpatient ward**:

**Larger RCTs with efficacy/effectiveness outcomes**

A randomised controlled trial across four psychiatric inpatient facilities in Australia (N=754) tested a brief motivational interview and self-help material in hospital, followed by a 4-month pharmacological and psychosocial intervention after discharge, against usual care. The intervention increased 7-day point-prevalence smoking cessation and quitting behaviours at 6 months, with sustained effects on quitting behaviour at 12 months (Metse et al., Aust N Z J Psychiatry, 2017).

A related RCT at an Australian inpatient psychiatric facility randomised 205 patient smokers to treatment as usual or a smoking-cessation intervention with psychosocial and pharmacological support for 4 months postdischarge, finding postdischarge support was effective in encouraging quit attempts and reducing cigarette consumption up to 6 months (Stockings et al., Nicotine Tob Res, 2014).

A US RCT randomised 224 smokers recruited from a locked acute psychiatry unit with a 100% smoking ban to a motivational tobacco cessation treatment combined with nicotine replacement, or usual care, initiated in inpatient psychiatry. Verified abstinence was significantly higher for intervention than usual care at 3, 6, 12 and 18 months, and cessation treatment was associated with lower psychiatric rehospitalization (Prochaska et al., Am J Public Health, 2014).

The Helping HAND 3 trial compared a multicomponent sustained-care smoking cessation intervention with usual care among individuals with serious mental illness who smoked daily and were receiving inpatient psychiatric care in a single hospital in Austin, Texas (Brown et al., JAMA Psychiatry, 2021; a statistical correction was later published for Table 1).

**Pilot/feasibility trials**

A pilot trial of a digital smoking-cessation program bridging the hospital discharge gap for psychiatric inpatients examined feasibility (enrollment rates), acceptability (engagement/satisfaction), and preliminary abstinence outcomes at 1 and 3 months post-discharge, enrolling 58 of 218 eligible patients (White et al., Psychiatric Services, 2026).

**Smaller observational/quasi-experimental study**

A study evaluating tobacco use and willingness to accept nicotine replacement therapy during a stay in an acute inpatient psychiatric ward assessed 40 patients over four weeks, offering nicotine lozenges/patches and finding most smokers accepted NRT during their inpatient stay (PMC8770221).

**Related descriptive/secondary studies from the same Australian trial cohort** (useful context, not standalone efficacy trials, but genuine primary data):
- A descriptive study of smoking-cessation care receipt across four Australian acute psychiatric facilities, using data from the same RCT cohort (Metse et al., Int J Ment Health Nurs, 2018).
- A cross-sectional study of smoking and environmental characteristics of smokers with mental illness, conducted within the same trial's inpatient psychiatric facilities (PMC4832515).
- A descriptive study of factors associated with retention in this same smoking-cessation trial for people with mental illness (PMC6307187).
- An implementation trial testing a clinical-practice-change intervention to increase provision of nicotine dependence treatment in inpatient psychiatric facilities (Wye et al., BMC Psychiatry, 2017) — this targets staff practice change rather than direct patient quitting, but is relevant primary evidence on hospital-initiated smoking care systems.
