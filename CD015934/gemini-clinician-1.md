Yes, primary clinical trials demonstrate that smoking cessation programs initiated during an acute psychiatric hospitalization are effective at helping adults quit smoking. Importantly, these interventions do not harm mental health recovery and can even reduce the risk of future psychiatric readmissions.

## Key Insights from Primary Trials

* **Inpatient Initiation Works:** Starting the intervention while the patient is admitted (often taking advantage of a smoke-free locked unit) capitalizes on a period of forced abstinence. Patients randomized to motivationally tailored tobacco treatment during acute psychiatric hospitalization achieve significantly higher long-term abstinence rates over 18 months compared to those receiving usual care (Prochaska et al., 2014).
* **The Crucial Role of Sustained Care:** Success heavily depends on bridging the gap between the inpatient ward and the community. Multicomponent programs that initiate treatment in the hospital and sustain it post-discharge—using continuous counseling, automated follow-ups, and extended nicotine replacement therapy (NRT)—yield much better engagement and sustained abstinence up to 6 months post-discharge (Brown et al., 2021; Stockings et al., 2014).
* **Safety and Psychiatric Stability:** A common clinical hesitation is that quitting smoking or managing nicotine withdrawal might exacerbate psychiatric symptoms. However, evidence shows no adverse effects on mental health recovery. In fact, patients receiving inpatient smoking cessation treatment actually demonstrated a significantly lower likelihood of psychiatric rehospitalization compared to the control group (Prochaska et al., 2014).
* **Harm Reduction Benefits:** Even for patients who do not achieve complete abstinence, initiating cessation support during their inpatient stay leads to significantly more subsequent quit attempts, lower daily cigarette consumption, and decreased nicotine dependence post-discharge (Stockings et al., 2014).

Initiating tobacco dependence treatment in your ward is a strongly evidence-based approach, provided your team can coordinate a framework to sustain that care as patients transition to the outpatient setting.
