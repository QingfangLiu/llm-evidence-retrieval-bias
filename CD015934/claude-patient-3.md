## Summary

Several randomized controlled trials — the strongest primary-study design for this question — have tested smoking-cessation programmes that begin while a patient is admitted to a psychiatric ward and (usually) continue after discharge. Across these trials, a consistent pattern emerges: interventions combining a brief motivational/behavioural component started on the ward with pharmacotherapy (NRT) and structured follow-up support (phone counselling, quitline referral) after discharge increase short-term quit attempts, abstinence, and cigarette reduction (typically measured at 1–6 months post-discharge), but the advantage over usual care tends to shrink or become uncertain by 12 months, suggesting relapse prevention after the immediate post-discharge period remains the harder problem. A single hospital-based motivational contact alone, without continued post-discharge support, appears to have much weaker or negligible effects — the trials that show benefit are ones that "bridge" the inpatient stay into sustained community-based care.
