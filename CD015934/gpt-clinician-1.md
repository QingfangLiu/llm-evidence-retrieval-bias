## Bottom line

**Quit-smoking treatment started on an adult psychiatric ward can improve cessation, particularly when treatment continues actively after discharge.** The most consistently successful model combines:

* motivational or behavioral counseling during admission;
* nicotine-replacement therapy during admission and/or supplied at discharge; and
* proactive counseling, medication support, telephone calls, texts, or other follow-up for several months after discharge.

Three larger randomized trials found higher abstinence with this approach:

* **Prochaska 2014:** verified abstinence was 14.4% versus 6.5% at six months and 20.0% versus 7.7% at 18 months.
* **Metse 2017:** 15.8% versus 9.3% at six months, although the difference was no longer significant at 12 months.
* **Brown 2021:** biochemically verified abstinence was 8.9% versus 3.5% at six months; number needed to treat was approximately 19. ([PMC][1])

Thus, in the larger positive trials, the **absolute six-month improvement was approximately 5–8 percentage points**. Quit rates remained modest, but the intervention groups were approximately twice as likely to be abstinent in several studies.

Results were not uniformly positive. Stockings and colleagues found higher abstinence at four months—11.5% versus 2.0%—but no significant difference at six months. The smaller Hickman replication produced favorable but imprecise rates: 17.5% versus 8.5% at six months and 26.2% versus 16.7% at 12 months. ([PubMed][2])

### Interpretation for inpatient practice

The primary studies support **using hospitalization as the starting point, rather than treating the admission itself as the entire intervention**. The likely effective element is the bridge from mandatory inpatient abstinence to treatment after discharge: medication access, planned follow-up, and repeated patient contact. This is an inference from the intervention components and the timing of outcomes across the trials. ([PMC][1])

Evidence for choosing a particular medication is much weaker:

* A small bupropion trial reported better cessation than placebo but included only 32 participants.
* Cytisine produced numerically more six-month quitters than 2-mg nicotine gum—3/17 versus 2/30—but the open-label trial was far too small for a reliable comparative conclusion.
* Higher-dose nicotine patches did not outperform standard-dose patches in long-stay inpatients; only 2.7% of all participants quit.
* One earlier nicotine-patch study reported promising three-month abstinence, but the accessible report does not provide enough comparator data to estimate a dependable treatment effect. ([Minerva Medica][3])

Two newly published 2026 pilots add feasibility information but not strong long-term efficacy evidence. A randomized digital program produced six self-reported one-month quitters versus none after brief counseling, but enrolled only 58 people. The single-arm StayQuit study reduced cigarettes from 15 to eight per day among 15 follow-up completers, but it cannot establish a cessation effect because there was no control group and substantial attrition. ([Psychiatry Online][4])

## Primary studies

I treated separate participant cohorts as separate studies and did not count protocols, cost-effectiveness reports, trial registrations, or secondary subgroup analyses as additional studies.

1. **Chou KR, Chen R, Lee JF, Ku CH, Lu RB.** The effectiveness of nicotine-patch therapy for smoking cessation in patients with schizophrenia. *International Journal of Nursing Studies.* 2004;41(3):321–330. doi:10.1016/j.ijnurstu.2003.07.001.
   Controlled study of 68 people recruited from a psychiatric-hospital day-care ward. Eight-week patch treatment produced 26.9% point-prevalence abstinence and 23.1% continuous abstinence at three months in the patch group. ([PubMed][5])

2. **Akbarpour F, Rezaei O, Khodaie-Ardakani MR, Sheikhvatan M, Goodarzi H, Dolatshahi B.** A double-blind placebo-controlled trial of bupropion for smoking abstinence and cognition improvement in schizophrenia. *Minerva Psichiatrica.* 2010;51(4):263–269.
   Thirty-two hospitalized patients with schizophrenia were randomized to bupropion or placebo for eight weeks. The authors reported greater cessation with bupropion, but the publication provides limited numerical cessation data. ([Minerva Medica][3])

3. **Gelkopf M, Noam S, Rudinski D, et al.** Nonmedication smoking reduction program for inpatients with chronic schizophrenia: a randomized control design study. *Journal of Nervous and Mental Disease.* 2012;200(2):142–146. doi:10.1097/NMD.0b013e3182438e92.
   A five-session behavioral group program reduced cigarette consumption relative to a waiting list in 53 chronic inpatients. It did **not establish smoking cessation**. ([PubMed][6])

4. **Chen HK, Lan TH, Wu BJ.** A double-blind randomized clinical trial of different doses of transdermal nicotine patch for smoking reduction and cessation in long-term hospitalized schizophrenic patients. *European Archives of Psychiatry and Clinical Neuroscience.* 2013;263(1):75–82. doi:10.1007/s00406-012-0338-3.
   In 184 long-stay inpatients, 31.2-mg followed by 20.8-mg patches did not outperform 20.8-mg patches. Overall seven-day abstinence was 2.7%. ([Springer][7])

5. **Prochaska JJ, Hall SE, Delucchi K, Hall SM.** Efficacy of initiating tobacco dependence treatment in inpatient psychiatry: a randomized controlled trial. *American Journal of Public Health.* 2014;104(8):1557–1565. doi:10.2105/AJPH.2013.301403.
   In 224 acute psychiatric inpatients, motivationally tailored treatment plus postdischarge NRT increased verified abstinence at 3, 6, 12, and 18 months. ([PMC][1])

6. **Stockings EA, Bowman JA, Baker AL, et al.** Impact of a postdischarge smoking cessation intervention for smokers admitted to an inpatient psychiatric facility: a randomized controlled trial. *Nicotine & Tobacco Research.* 2014;16(11):1417–1428. doi:10.1093/ntr/ntu097.
   Among 205 participants, psychosocial and pharmacologic support improved four-month abstinence, quit attempts, consumption, and dependence, but not six-month abstinence. ([PubMed][2])

7. **Hickman NJ III, Delucchi KL, Prochaska JJ.** Treating tobacco dependence at the intersection of diversity, poverty, and mental illness: a randomized feasibility and replication trial. *Nicotine & Tobacco Research.* 2015;17(8):1012–1021. doi:10.1093/ntr/ntv034.
   A 100-person feasibility trial found consistently higher verified abstinence with tailored counseling and ten weeks of postdischarge NRT, although it was not powered for definitive efficacy testing. ([PubMed][8])

8. **Metse AP, Wiggers J, Wye P, et al.** Efficacy of a universal smoking cessation intervention initiated in inpatient psychiatry and continued post-discharge: a randomised controlled trial. *Australian & New Zealand Journal of Psychiatry.* 2017;51(4):366–381. doi:10.1177/0004867417692424.
   In 754 participants across four facilities, the intervention improved seven-day abstinence at six months but not at 12 months; prolonged abstinence did not differ significantly. ([Sage Journals][9])

9. **Brown RA, Minami H, Hecht J, et al.** Sustained care smoking cessation intervention for individuals hospitalized for psychiatric disorders: the Helping HAND 3 randomized clinical trial. *JAMA Psychiatry.* 2021;78(8):839–847. doi:10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2021.0707.
   In 353 randomized participants, inpatient counseling, discharge patches, cessation-service linkage, and automated follow-up increased biochemically verified six-month abstinence. ([PubMed][10])

10. **Tavakoli-Ardakani M, Gholamzadeh Sani Z, Beyraghi N, Najarimoghadam S, Kheradmand A.** Comparison between cytisine and nicotine replacement therapy in smoking cessation among inpatient psychiatric patients. *Journal of Addictive Diseases.* 2024;42(4):352–359. doi:10.1080/10550887.2023.2237395.
    Open-label randomized comparison of cytisine and nicotine gum in 47 inpatients; six-month complete cessation occurred in 17.6% and 6.7%, respectively. ([Taylor & Francis Online][11])

11. **Bennett ME, Dickerson F, Cha S, et al.** Bridging the hospital discharge gap: pilot trial of a digital smoking cessation program for psychiatric inpatients. *Psychiatric Services.* 2026;77(5):421–428. doi:10.1176/appi.ps.20250326.
    Randomized pilot involving 58 participants. At one month, six digital-program participants versus no brief-counseling participants reported seven-day abstinence; durable efficacy remains uncertain. ([Psychiatry Online][4])

12. **Kacmarek CN, Smith HC, Lucksted A, et al.** Efficacy, acceptability, and feasibility of StayQuit for sustaining smoking abstinence after psychiatric hospitalization: a pilot study. *Journal of Patient-Centered Research and Reviews.* 2026;13(1):42–50. doi:10.17294/2330-0698.2184.
    Single-arm VA inpatient pilot of hospital counseling followed by telephone sessions. Cigarette consumption declined among follow-up completers, but the study did not demonstrate comparative cessation efficacy. ([PMC][12])
