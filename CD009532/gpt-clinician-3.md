## Bottom line

**Yes.** Individual primary studies consistently show that oral iron after blood donation improves ferritin, hemoglobin recovery, or both. Trials that specifically measured subsequent eligibility generally found fewer low-Hb deferrals or improved ability to donate again. The most applicable evidence comes from donors with ferritin ≤30 µg/L, iron-deficient female donors, frequent donors, and donors already deferred for low hemoglobin.

I limited the evidence below to **individual primary studies**—no reviews, guidelines, committee statements, or editorials.

## Clinical interpretation

The most defensible conclusions from these individual studies are:

* **Iron stores:** Oral iron reliably accelerates ferritin and total-body-iron recovery, particularly when baseline ferritin is ≤26–30 µg/L. Without supplementation, recovery commonly takes longer than the usual donation interval. ([PubMed][1])
* **Future eligibility:** Studies that directly assessed repeat donation found fewer deferrals or better Hb-based eligibility, including the Gordeuk randomized trial, the Marks randomized trial, and the recent Garg deferred-donor intervention. ([Wiley Online Library][10])
* **Dose and duration:** Benefit has been demonstrated with approximately 19–60 mg elemental iron daily for roughly 4–8 weeks, although older studies also used shorter, higher-dose regimens. FORTE suggests 60 mg daily for 56 days produced the most complete ferritin repletion among the tested regimens. ([PubMed][1])
* **Low Hb is not synonymous with donation-induced iron deficiency:** Particularly in men, postmenopausal women, donors with marked anemia, or those with concerning symptoms, evaluation for occult bleeding and other pathology remains important. ([PMC][4])
