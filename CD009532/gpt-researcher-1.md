## Bottom line

Individual primary studies consistently show that iron supplementation after blood donation improves ferritin, total-body iron, and hemoglobin recovery. Evidence for preventing **actual low-hemoglobin donation deferral** is less commonly reported, but several trials found improved donation eligibility or fewer repeat-donation deferrals. The strongest direct result is an older randomized trial in which repeat-donation deferral fell from **36% with placebo to 8% with carbonyl iron**. ([Wiley Online Library][1])

Below are primary studies only. I excluded reviews, meta-analyses, protocols, guidelines, committee reports, and commentaries.

## Nonrandomized implementation studies

* **Bryant et al., 2012:** During a 39-month blood-center program, 1,236 low-Hb-deferred donors and 400 nondeferred controls underwent iron testing; iron-deficient donors received ferrous sulfate 325 mg/day. Iron-related laboratory abnormalities normalized despite continued donation. Compliance was 68%, and gastrointestinal effects occurred in 21%. The study is also important because medical evaluation uncovered gastrointestinal bleeding and other serious conditions in some low-Hb donors. ([PubMed][17])

* **Pittori et al., 2011:** In 116 nonanemic women with ferritin ≤10 ng/mL, a 16-week iron-substitution program plus an extended donation interval increased mean ferritin from 7.12 to 25.2 ng/mL; no anemia developed, and 60% completed the program and donated again. Comparison data were retrospective. ([PubMed][18])

## Reports that share the same participants

For study-level synthesis, these should **not** be counted as independent trials:

* **Kiss et al., 2015 HEIRS** is the principal clinical report. ([PMC][3])
* **Cable et al., 2016** is a companion analysis of the same 215 randomized donors. It estimated that total-body iron increased by 281 mg with supplementation but declined by 74.1 mg without supplementation at 24 weeks; 88% of the measured supplementation benefit occurred during the first eight weeks. ([PubMed][19])

## Interpretation for your two outcomes

**Improvement in iron stores:** This is strongly and consistently supported across placebo-controlled oral-iron trials, pragmatic supplementation studies, and IV-iron trials. Ferritin, total-body iron, or related iron indices improved in nearly every relevant comparison. ([PubMed][19])

**Reduced inability to donate because of low iron/Hb:** The evidence is supportive but more heterogeneous. Actual repeat-donation deferral was clearly reduced in Gordeuk 1990; Marks 2014 demonstrated improved capillary-Hb eligibility; modern HEIRS and FORTE trials showed improved Hb-threshold outcomes rather than observed on-site deferral; and uncontrolled programs showed that many previously deferred donors could return. ([Wiley Online Library][1])

This was a targeted primary-study search rather than a fully reproducible exhaustive database search; older non-English and pre-electronic trials may require additional database and citation-index searching.
