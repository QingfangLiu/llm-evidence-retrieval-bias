Here are individual primary studies (RCTs and other original research — not reviews, meta-analyses, or guidelines) on iron supplementation and blood donor iron stores/deferral:

**Direct evidence on iron stores and deferral outcomes**

- **Kiss JE, Brambilla D, Glynn SA, et al. "Oral Iron Supplementation After Blood Donation: A Randomized Clinical Trial" (HEIRS study). JAMA. 2015;313(6):575–583.** 215 donors randomized to low-dose oral ferrous gluconate (37.5 mg/day) vs. no iron after donation; iron group had significantly faster recovery of hemoglobin and ferritin. (jamanetwork.com)

- **Bryant BJ, Yau YY, Arceo SM, et al. "Iron replacement or repeated phlebotomy — the STRIDE trial" — reported in Cable RG et al., "Iron deficiency in blood donors: the STRIDE study."** 692 frequent donors randomized to 19 mg iron, 38 mg iron, or an educational letter based on ferritin. Iron groups had more RBC donations and fewer hemoglobin deferrals than controls; the education-letter group also had fewer deferrals than controls. (PMC5612857)

- **Cable RG, Glynn SA, Kiss JE, et al. "Effect of iron supplementation on iron stores and total body iron after whole blood donation." Transfusion. 2016;56:1988-1996.** Ancillary analysis of the 215-donor HEIRS cohort (37.5 mg/day ferrous gluconate vs. none for 24 weeks), looking at total-body iron and iron stores rather than just hemoglobin. (Wiley Online Library)

- **Bialkowski W, Kiss JE, Wright DJ, et al. "Estimates of total body iron indicate 19 mg and 38 mg oral iron are equivalent for the mitigation of iron deficiency in individuals experiencing repeated phlebotomy." Am J Hematol. 2017;92:851-857.** Dose-comparison trial from the STRIDE program.

- **Karregat JHM, Meulenbeld A, Quee FA, et al. "Ferritin-guided iron supplementation as an alternative or complement to prolonged blood donation intervals (FORTE): a double-blind, randomised, controlled trial." Lancet Haematol. 2025;12:e694–e704** (also posted as a medRxiv preprint, 2025.01.03.25319940). 830 donors with ferritin ≤30 µg/L randomized to placebo, 30 mg, or 60 mg elemental iron (ferrous bisglycinate), daily or alternate-day, for 56 days; 60 mg daily was most effective at correcting iron deficiency, low ferritin, and low hemoglobin without increasing GI side effects or reducing donor return. (Lancet Haematology, medRxiv, ScienceDirect, Hematology Advisor)

**Symptom/wellbeing outcomes with iron repletion in donors (secondary but original RCT data)**

- **[Study referenced as "Iron Supplementation in Blood Donors" RCT, IV ferric carboxymaltose 800 mg]** — reported in Blood. 2022;140(25):2730 as "A randomized trial of blood donor iron repletion on red cell quality for transfusion and donor cognition and well-being." This is itself a primary RCT examining red cell quality plus donor cognition/wellbeing after IV iron repletion. (ASH Publications/Blood)

- **Radtke H, et al. / an Austrian group's "IronWoMan" RCT** — "The Effect of Parenteral or Oral Iron Supplementation on Fatigue, Sleep, Quality of Life and Restless Legs Syndrome in Iron-Deficient Blood Donors: A Secondary Analysis of the IronWoMan RCT." 176 donors with ferritin ≤30 ng/mL randomized to IV ferric carboxymaltose vs. oral ferrous fumarate; both raised iron stores. (PMC7284357)

- **"The effects of intravenous iron supplementation on fatigue and general health in non-anemic blood donors with iron deficiency: a randomized placebo-controlled superiority trial." Sci Rep. 2020.** 405 donors with ferritin ≤50 µg/L randomized to 800 mg IV iron-carboxymaltose vs. placebo; ferritin and hemoglobin rose significantly with iron, though fatigue/wellbeing scores did not differ. (Scientific Reports/PMC7449957)

**Related mechanistic/protocol trial**

- **Chiozza ML, et al. (Lausanne) — "The impact of iron supplementation efficiency in female blood donors with a decreased ferritin level and no anaemia" (study protocol for an RCT)**, PMC2637271 — this is a published protocol; if you want, I can look for the completed results paper from this specific trial separately, since a protocol alone isn't a completed primary study.

A few notes for your appraisal:
- The **FORTE trial** and **HEIRS trial** are the two most directly relevant, well-powered RCTs measuring ferritin/hemoglobin recovery and (for FORTE) donor return, in donors selected for low ferritin.
- The **STRIDE trial** is the one that most directly measured **deferral rates** as an outcome (rather than just ferritin/hemoglobin levels), which sounds closest to your practical question.
- Some sources (like the Blood 2022 IV-iron/cognition study) are less about deferral and more about downstream symptom/product-quality outcomes — still primary data, just answering a different question than "will iron supplements stop me being deferred."
