I have a solid set of primary (non-review) studies on iron supplementation in blood donors, covering effects on iron stores/ferritin and donation deferral. Here they are, organized by design:

**Randomized controlled trials — oral iron supplementation, ferritin/Hb outcomes**

1. **Kiss JE, Brambilla D, Glynn SA, et al.** "Oral Iron Supplementation After Blood Donation: A Randomized Clinical Trial" (HEIRS trial). *JAMA*. 2015;313(6):575–583. — 215 donors randomized to ferrous gluconate (37.5 mg) vs. no iron for 24 weeks; iron supplementation led to iron balance findings after donors were randomized to receive ferrous gluconate daily (37.5 mg iron) or no iron for 24 weeks post-donation.

2. **Cable RG, Glynn SA, Kiss JE, et al.** "Effect of iron supplementation on iron stores and total body iron after whole blood donation." *Transfusion*. 2016;56(4):1029–1034. — Companion analysis of the HEIRS cohort quantifying total-body-iron recovery.

3. **Bialkowski W, Kiss JE, Wright DJ, et al.** "Estimates of total body iron indicate 19 mg and 38 mg oral iron are equivalent for the mitigation of iron deficiency in individuals experiencing repeated phlebotomy." *Am J Hematol*. 2017;92(9):851–857. — Part of the STRIDE program comparing two oral iron doses.

4. **Kiss JE, et al.** "The operational implications of donor behaviors following enrollment in STRIDE (Strategies to Reduce Iron Deficiency in blood donors)." *Transfusion*. 2017 (PMID 28703859). — 692 frequent donors randomized to 19 mg or 38 mg iron for 60 days vs. educational letter vs. control; donors randomized to receive iron pills had increased red blood cell donations and decreased hemoglobin deferrals compared with controls or with pre-STRIDE donations, while donors randomized to receive educational letters had fewer hemoglobin deferrals compared with controls.

5. **Mast AE, Bialkowski W, Bryant BJ, et al.** "A randomized, blinded, placebo-controlled trial of education and iron supplementation for mitigation of iron deficiency in regular blood donors." *Transfusion*. 2016;56(6):1588-1597 (PMID 26813849).

**Randomized controlled trials — ferritin-guided/dose-frequency oral iron protocols**

6. **Karregat JHM, Meulenbeld A, Quee FA, et al.** "Ferritin-guided iron supplementation as an alternative or complement to prolonged blood donation intervals (FORTE): a double-blind, randomised, controlled trial." *Lancet Haematology*. 2025;12:e694–e704 (also medRxiv preprint 2025.01.03.25319940). — 830 donors with ferritin ≤30 µg/L randomized to placebo/30 mg/60 mg, daily or alternate-day, for 56 days; compared to placebo, all iron supplementation groups exhibited significantly lower odds of iron deficiency at 56 days, with odds ratios ranging from 0.60 to 0.65, and 60 mg iron supplements taken daily was the most effective strategy for mitigating iron deficiency, low hemoglobin, and especially low ferritin without gastrointestinal discomfort or changes in donors' intention to return.

**Randomized controlled trials — IV vs. oral iron, or IV vs. placebo**

7. **Radtke H, et al. / "IronWoMan" trial** — protocol paper: "High-dose intravenously administered iron versus orally administered iron in blood donors with iron deficiency: study protocol for a randomised, controlled trial." *Trials* (PMC5084417). Donors with ferritin ≤30 ng/mL randomized 1:1 to IV ferric carboxymaltose (1 g) vs. oral iron fumarate (10 g total).

8. **[IronWoMan secondary analysis]** "The Effect of Parenteral or Oral Iron Supplementation on Fatigue, Sleep, Quality of Life and Restless Legs Syndrome in Iron-Deficient Blood Donors: A Secondary Analysis of the IronWoMan RCT" (PMC7284357).

9. **Keller P, von Känel R, Hincapié CA, et al.** "The effects of intravenous iron supplementation on fatigue and general health in non-anemic blood donors with iron deficiency: a randomized placebo-controlled superiority trial." *Sci Rep*. 2020;10:14219. — 405 donors randomized to IV ferric carboxymaltose (800 mg) vs. placebo; mean differences were 114.2 µg/L for ferritin and 5.7 g/L for hemoglobin, with significantly higher values in the iron supplement group.

10. **[NHLBI/REDS-III team]** "A randomized trial of blood donor iron repletion on red cell quality for transfusion and donor cognition and well-being." *Blood*. 2022;140(25):2730–2739. — IV ferric carboxymaltose (800 mg) vs. placebo; iron repletion did not induce a clinically relevant decrease in self-described fatigue and general well-being, but improved ferritin/hemoglobin and, in post hoc analyses, female subjects in the placebo group were more likely to be deferred from allogeneic donation for not meeting minimum hemoglobin and hematocrit criteria.

11. **Waldvogel S, Pedrazzini B, Vaucher P, et al.** "Clinical evaluation of iron treatment efficiency among non-anemic but iron-deficient female blood donors: a randomized controlled trial." *BMC Medicine*. 2012;10:8.

**Related large RCTs on donation policy affecting iron stores/deferral (not supplementation per se, but often compared against it)**

12. **Di Angelantonio E, Thompson SG, Kaptoge S, et al. (INTERVAL trial).** "Efficiency and safety of varying the frequency of whole blood donation (INTERVAL): a randomised trial of 45,000 donors." *Lancet*. 2017;390:2360–2371.

13. **van den Hurk K, et al. (FIND'EM trial).** "Effectiveness of ferritin-guided donation intervals in whole-blood donors in the Netherlands (FIND'EM): a stepped-wedge cluster-randomised trial." *Lancet*. 2024. — Ferritin-guided donation intervals significantly improved haemoglobin and ferritin concentrations and significantly decreased iron deficiency over the study period; haemoglobin-based deferrals decreased significantly for male donors.

**Observational cohort study establishing baseline iron-deficiency/deferral risk (useful as background, not an intervention trial)**

14. **Cable RG, Glynn SA, Kiss JE, et al.** "Iron deficiency in blood donors: analysis of enrollment data from the REDS-II Donor Iron Status Evaluation (RISE) study." *Transfusion*. 2011;51:511–522, and the follow-up "Iron deficiency in blood donors: the REDS-II donor iron status evaluation (RISE) study." *Transfusion*. 2012;52:702–711.
