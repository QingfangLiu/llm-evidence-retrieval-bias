Here's a synthesis of the individual randomized controlled trials that have directly tested intensive versus standard blood pressure control after reperfusion therapy (thrombolysis and/or thrombectomy) for acute ischemic stroke. I've excluded reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, and commentaries as requested.

## Trials after thrombolysis (IV alteplase)

**ENCHANTED** tested lower BP targets after clot-dissolving drug treatment. it built on prior work involving intensive blood pressure reduction with intravenous thrombolysis therapy for acute ischaemic stroke, an international randomized, open-label, blinded-endpoint trial comparing intensive (target SBP <130–140) versus guideline-standard (<180) BP control in the first 24–72 hours after alteplase. It found no significant improvement in functional outcome with intensive lowering, though it did reduce hemorrhage rates.

## Trials after endovascular thrombectomy (EVT)

**BP-TARGET** (France, 2021): An intensive systolic blood pressure target of 100–129 mm Hg after successful endovascular therapy did not reduce radiographic intraparenchymal haemorrhage rates at 24–36 h as compared with a standard care systolic blood pressure target of 130–185 mm Hg. This trial was underpowered for functional outcomes.

**ENCHANTED2/MT** (China, 2022): an investigator-initiated, pragmatic, multicentre, open-label, blinded-endpoint randomised controlled phase 3 trial, done at 44 tertiary-level hospitals in China, randomizing patients with persistently elevated BP after successful thrombectomy to a target <120 mm Hg versus 140–180 mm Hg. Intensive treatment also worsened the composite outcome of death or early neurological deterioration, and of major disability at 90 days, with no differences in symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage, serious adverse events, or death alone. The trial was stopped early for harm.

**OPTIMAL-BP** (South Korea, 2023): a multicenter, randomized, open-label trial with a blinded end-point evaluation, conducted across 19 stroke centers in South Korea, comparing an intensive target (SBP <140) with a conventional target (140–180) for 24 hours post-reperfusion. The intensive management group had a lower proportion achieving functional independence (39.4%) than the conventional management group (54.4%), leading to early trial termination for safety concerns.

**BEST-II** (United States, 2023): a prospective, randomized, open-label, blinded-endpoint clinical trial at three U.S. centers, randomizing patients 1:1:1 to SBP targets of <140, <160, or ≤180 mm Hg (guideline standard) for 24 hours. It was designed as a phase 2 futility trial; researchers couldn't conclude that treating to lower BP goals would benefit patients, though by design they could not rule out potential harm of lower blood pressure targets.

**IDENTIFY** (China, 2025): a randomised, multicentre, open-label, blinded-endpoint clinical trial conducted at 63 stroke centres in China, randomizing patients treated within 6 hours to intensive (SBP <130) versus standard (<180) targets. Unfavourable functional outcome occurred in 71.0% of the intensive-management group and 67.5% of the standard-management group, with no significant difference between groups; intensive BP management to <130 mm Hg did not improve outcomes, and the trial stopped early after neutral interim results.

## Bottom line across these trials

Across BP-TARGET, ENCHANTED2/MT, OPTIMAL-BP, BEST-II, and IDENTIFY, intensive BP lowering after successful reperfusion has generally not improved—and in two trials (ENCHANTED2/MT, OPTIMAL-BP) appeared to worsen—functional recovery, without a consistent reduction in symptomatic hemorrhage. This is why current practice generally favors a less aggressive BP target (closer to guideline-standard ranges) in the first 24 hours after successful reperfusion, though the exact optimal threshold remains unsettled.
