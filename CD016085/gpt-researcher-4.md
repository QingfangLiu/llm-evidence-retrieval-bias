## Scope

I searched for completed **comparative randomized primary studies** in adults with acute ischemic stroke who received:

* intravenous thrombolysis, or
* successful endovascular thrombectomy/reperfusion,

and were then assigned to a more intensive versus standard/less intensive blood-pressure strategy.

Search updated through **July 25, 2026**. I excluded protocols, observational cohorts, intraprocedural anesthesia/BP studies, reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, editorials, and comments. Secondary publications from the same randomized cohort were not counted as separate studies.

## Overall interpretation

### After intravenous thrombolysis

The ENCHANTED trial found that targeting systolic blood pressure, or SBP, of **130–140 mm Hg** rather than **<180 mm Hg** reduced radiographic intracranial hemorrhage but **did not improve 90-day functional outcome**. The achieved difference between groups was modest—approximately 6 mm Hg—so the trial does not establish whether a larger but still safe separation would have produced a different result. ([PubMed][1])

### After successful endovascular thrombectomy

The trials of a **uniform lower SBP target for all successfully reperfused patients** generally found no benefit and, in the larger trials, evidence of harm:

* SBP **<120 mm Hg** caused worse functional recovery in ENCHANTED2/MT.
* SBP **<140 mm Hg** caused worse 90-day recovery in OPTIMAL-BP, accompanied by more episodes of SBP <100 mm Hg and more malignant cerebral edema.
* SBP **<130 mm Hg** was neutral for the primary outcome in IDENTIFY but produced substantially more hypotension and concerning adjusted secondary disability results.
* BP-TARGET, BEST-II, and DETECT found no convincing functional or hemorrhagic benefit from lower uniform targets. ([ScienceDirect][2])

A different result was reported in the 2026 HOPE trial. HOPE did **not** test blanket intensive lowering: it assigned a higher target after incomplete reperfusion and a lower target after near-complete/complete reperfusion, using both antihypertensives and vasopressors as necessary. This tailored strategy improved functional independence and reduced hemorrhagic transformation. It therefore supports **individualized hemodynamic management**, but should not be interpreted as evidence that every post-thrombectomy patient benefits from SBP <140 mm Hg. ([JAMA Network][3])

## Individual randomized studies

### 1. ENCHANTED — intravenous thrombolysis

**Population/intervention:** 2,196 alteplase-eligible patients with SBP ≥150 mm Hg; intensive target **130–140 mm Hg within 1 hour and for 72 hours** versus guideline target **<180 mm Hg**.

**Benefit:** Any intracranial hemorrhage occurred in 14.8% versus 18.7%; OR 0.75, 95% CI 0.60–0.94—an absolute reduction of 3.9 percentage points.

**Functional outcome:** No difference in the 90-day mRS distribution; OR 1.01, 95% CI 0.87–1.17.

**Risks:** Serious adverse events were not significantly different, 19.4% versus 22.0%.

**Interpretation:** Radiographic bleeding was reduced without translating into better disability outcomes. This intervention began during or immediately around thrombolysis and was not restricted to patients with documented arterial reperfusion. ([PubMed][1])

### 2. BP-TARGET — successful endovascular therapy

**Population/intervention:** 324 patients successfully reperfused with EVT; SBP target **100–129 mm Hg** versus **130–185 mm Hg** for 24 hours.

**Benefit:** None demonstrated. Radiographic intraparenchymal hemorrhage occurred in 42% versus 43%; adjusted OR 0.96, 95% CI 0.60–1.51.

**Risks:** Hypotensive events occurred in 8% versus 3%, although the difference was not statistically significant. Early mortality was 7% versus 4%.

**Interpretation:** Lowering SBP to 100–129 mm Hg did not reduce the trial’s imaging hemorrhage end point. The achieved mean SBPs were 128 and 138 mm Hg, so treatment separation was modest. ([PubMed][4])

### 3. ENCHANTED2/MT — successful thrombectomy

**Population/intervention:** 816 evaluable patients with persistent SBP ≥140 mm Hg following successful EVT; target **<120 mm Hg** versus **140–180 mm Hg** for 72 hours.

**Benefit:** None.

**Risks:** The trial was stopped early. Intensive treatment produced:

* worse 90-day mRS distribution: common OR for poorer outcome 1.37, 95% CI 1.07–1.76;
* more early neurological deterioration: common OR 1.53, 95% CI 1.18–1.97;
* more major disability: OR 2.07, 95% CI 1.47–2.93.

Symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage, serious adverse events, and mortality were not significantly different.

**Interpretation:** A uniform SBP target below 120 mm Hg after successful EVT was harmful despite no detectable increase in symptomatic hemorrhage or mortality. ([ScienceDirect][2])

### 4. OPTIMAL-BP — successful thrombectomy

**Population/intervention:** 305 eligible patients with successful reperfusion and persistent SBP ≥140 mm Hg; target **<140 mm Hg** versus **140–180 mm Hg** for 24 hours.

**Benefit:** None.

**Risks and functional outcomes:**

* functional independence at 90 days: 39.4% versus 54.4%;
* adjusted OR 0.56, 95% CI 0.33–0.96;
* absolute difference −15.1 percentage points;
* SBP <100 mm Hg episodes: 29.7% versus 17.3%;
* malignant cerebral edema: 7.7% versus 1.3%.

Symptomatic ICH was similar, 9.0% versus 8.1%, as was index-stroke mortality, 7.7% versus 5.4%.

**Interpretation:** Even a moderately intensive target below 140 mm Hg reduced functional independence without reducing symptomatic ICH. The trial was stopped early for safety concerns. ([JAMA Network][5])

The 2026 one-year follow-up found functional independence remained numerically lower with intensive control, 40.5% versus 52.7%; adjusted OR 0.59, 95% CI 0.34–1.00, with statistically significant harm in the per-protocol analysis. One-year mortality did not differ. ([PubMed][6])

### 5. BEST-II — successful endovascular therapy

**Population/intervention:** 120 patients assigned to one of three 24-hour SBP targets: **<140**, **<160**, or **≤180 mm Hg**.

**Design:** Phase 2 futility trial, with follow-up infarct volume and utility-weighted 90-day mRS as coprimary outcomes.

**Results:**

* Mean utility-weighted mRS was 0.51 with target <140, 0.47 with target <160, and 0.58 with target ≤180; higher values are better.
* Any ICH occurred in 36%, 32%, and 30%.
* Symptomatic ICH occurred in 5%, 3%, and 5%.
* The predicted probabilities that a future superiority trial would succeed were only 25% for <140 versus ≤180 and 14% for <160 versus ≤180.

**Interpretation:** The formal futility boundaries were not crossed, but results provided little support for benefit and numerically favored the ≤180-mm Hg group for functional outcome. ([JAMA Network][7])

### 6. DETECT — feasibility trial after successful EVT

**Population/intervention:** 30 patients randomized within 1 hour after EVT to **SBP <140 mm Hg** versus **SBP <180 mm Hg** for 48 hours.

**Results:** No differences were found in the prespecified exploratory neurological, functional, hemorrhagic, or transcranial Doppler outcomes.

**Feasibility concern:** Of 221 screened patients, only 30 were randomized, largely because BP frequently fell spontaneously after reperfusion. Mean 48-hour SBPs were 131 versus 139 mm Hg.

**Interpretation:** Too small for efficacy or safety conclusions; importantly, it showed how spontaneous BP normalization may reduce treatment separation and trial enrollment. The article also contained a meta-analysis, but the results above are solely from its randomized primary-study component. ([PubMed][8])

### 7. IDENTIFY — early successful thrombectomy

**Population/intervention:** 383 patients treated with successful anterior-circulation EVT within 6 hours; target **<130 mm Hg** versus **<180 mm Hg** for 24 hours.

**Primary outcome:** Unfavorable 90-day outcome, mRS 3–6, occurred in 71.0% versus 67.5%; RR 1.05, 95% CI 0.92–1.20.

**Risks:**

* Protocol-defined hypotension: 57.5% versus 32.7%; RR 1.76, 95% CI 1.39–2.23.
* Symptomatic ICH, malignant edema, and 90-day death were not significantly different.
* In adjusted secondary analyses, mRS 4–6 was more common with intensive control: adjusted RR 1.25, 95% CI 1.07–1.46. This was not consistently significant in the per-protocol analysis.

**Interpretation:** Targeting <130 mm Hg did not improve the primary functional outcome and substantially increased hypotension, with a secondary signal toward severe disability or death. ([ScienceDirect][9])

### 8. HOPE — reperfusion-guided individualized management

**Population/intervention:** 440 patients with anterior-circulation EVT and mTICI ≥2b. Targets were individualized:

* mTICI 2b: **140–160 mm Hg**;
* mTICI 2c/3: **100–140 mm Hg**;

versus guideline management of **SBP <180 mm Hg** for 72 hours. Antihypertensive agents or vasopressors could be used to keep patients in range.

**Benefits:**

* mRS 0–2 at 90 days: 60.0% versus 47.1%;
* absolute difference 13.3 percentage points, 95% CI 4.1–22.6;
* adjusted OR 1.71, 95% CI 1.11–2.63;
* hemorrhagic transformation: 22.3% versus 31.6%; OR 0.62, 95% CI 0.41–0.95.

**Risks:** Symptomatic ICH was 3.5% versus 3.9%, mortality 15.4% versus 15.6%, and serious adverse events 15.8% versus 12.0%; none differed significantly.

**Interpretation:** A reperfusion-guided hemodynamic strategy improved outcomes. Because the intervention combined different targets according to reperfusion status and permitted BP augmentation, it evaluates a tailored care strategy—not simply “intensive BP lowering.” ([JAMA Network][3])

## Evidence-based conclusion

For patients receiving **intravenous alteplase**, targeting 130–140 mm Hg reduces any intracranial hemorrhage but has not improved functional recovery.

For patients with **successful EVT**, the primary randomized studies do not support routine, uniform lowering to <120–140 mm Hg. Such strategies have generally failed to reduce symptomatic hemorrhage and can increase hypotension, neurological deterioration, cerebral edema, disability, or loss of functional independence. The new HOPE result suggests that the more promising approach may be **reperfusion-specific, individualized BP control with avoidance of both excessive pressure and inadequate cerebral perfusion**, rather than one low target applied to everyone.
