## Intensive vs. Standard Blood Pressure Control After Reperfusion Therapy for Ischemic Stroke: Primary RCT Evidence

**Overall picture:** With one recent exception (HOPE), the RCT evidence in patients treated with endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) has consistently shown that intensive systolic BP (SBP) lowering does **not** improve — and in several trials **worsens** — functional outcome, without a corresponding reduction in symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH).

**BP-TARGET (2021)** — French multicenter open-label RCT (n=324). Compared intensive systolic BP control (mean achieved in 24 h, 128 mm Hg) versus standard systolic BP control (mean achieved in 24 h, 138 mm Hg) and found the intensive approach was safe but showed no improvement in clinical outcomes in patients after successful EVT for anterior-circulation large-vessel occlusion.

**OPTIMAL-BP (2023)** — Korean multicenter open-label, blinded-endpoint RCT across 19 centers (n=306). Randomized patients to intensive BP management (systolic BP target <140 mm Hg) or conventional management (systolic BP target 140–180 mm Hg) during the first 24 hours after successful reperfusion. The trial was stopped early; intensive vs. conventional BP management after EVT reduced functional independence at 3 months.

**ENCHANTED2/MT (2022)** — Chinese multicenter, open-label, blinded-endpoint phase 3 RCT across 44 hospitals (n=821, stopped early). Patients were randomly assigned to more intensive treatment (systolic BP target <120 mm Hg) or less intensive treatment (140–180 mm Hg), with the pragmatic design finding that more intensive BP lowering led to worse functional outcomes. Intensive treatment also worsened the composite outcome of death or early neurological deterioration and major disability at 90 days, with no differences in symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage, serious adverse events, or death alone.

**BEST-II (2023)** — US multicenter, prospective, randomized, open-label, blinded-endpoint (PROBE) pilot trial (1:1:1 design). Patients were randomly assigned to one of three systolic BP targets: a high target of ≤180 mm Hg (control), an intermediate target of <160 mm Hg, or a lower target of <140 mm Hg, maintained for 24 hours after successful EVT. The trial found that SBP targets less than either 140 mm Hg or 160 mm Hg did not differ from the ≤180 mm Hg target in terms of utility score, and suggested a low probability of benefit from lower SBP targets.

**Single-center feasibility pilot trial** (Stroke: Vascular and Interventional Neurology). Participants with persistently elevated BP after successful EVT were randomized within 1 hour from the end of EVT to intensive (systolic BP target <140 mm Hg) or standard BP target (systolic BP <180 mm Hg) for 48 hours, with feasibility as the main endpoint and neurologic deterioration, functional improvement, intracranial hemorrhage, and transcranial Doppler flow dynamics as exploratory endpoints.

**HOPE trial (2026)** — Spanish multicenter PROBE RCT across 11 stroke centers (n=440), the first trial to show benefit. Patients with anterior-circulation stroke who achieved successful recanalization (mTICI ≥2b) were randomized to a reperfusion-tailored BP protocol versus guideline-based standard management; those with good reperfusion (mTICI 2c/3) were targeted to systolic BP 100–140 mm Hg, while those with incomplete reperfusion (mTICI 2b) were targeted to 140–160 mm Hg, maintained for 72 hours. Favorable functional outcome (mRS 0–2 at 90 days) was achieved in 60.0% of the intervention group vs. 46.7% of controls (OR 1.71, 95% CI 1.17–2.50, P=.005), with a lower hemorrhagic transformation rate (22.3% vs. 31.6%, P=.030). This trial used an individualized, reperfusion-guided target rather than a uniform intensive-vs-standard comparison, and was stopped early for lack of funding, so it's best regarded as hypothesis-generating pending confirmation.
