As a clinician managing acute ischemic stroke, you face the delicate balance of preventing hemorrhagic transformation while maintaining cerebral perfusion to at-risk penumbral tissue after successful recanalization. Recent primary randomized controlled trials have strongly challenged the hypothesis that intensive blood pressure (BP) lowering after reperfusion therapy is beneficial, demonstrating significant risks associated with aggressive targets.

## Risks of Intensive Blood Pressure Lowering

The predominant finding across recent primary studies is that intensive blood pressure reduction—specifically targeting a systolic blood pressure (SBP) below 120 to 140 mm Hg after successful endovascular thrombectomy (EVT)—is associated with worse clinical outcomes.

* **Worsened Functional Recovery:** Strict BP control targets significantly decrease the likelihood of functional independence at 90 days. For example, the ENCHANTED2/MT trial demonstrated that intensive BP lowering (target SBP < 120 mm Hg) increased the odds of major disability and poor functional recovery compared to a standard target of 140 to 180 mm Hg (Yang et al., 2022). Similar signals of harm were observed in the OPTIMAL-BP and BEST-II clinical trials, where intensive targets yielded higher rates of poor functional outcomes compared to standard, more permissive management.
* **Neurological Deterioration:** Aggressive BP lowering limits autoregulatory compensation in oligemic areas of the brain. When systemic pressure drops too quickly, microcirculatory blood flow is reduced, exacerbating ischemic injury in previously compromised tissue. Clinically, this manifests as increased rates of early neurological deterioration and early death in intensive treatment arms (Yang et al., 2022).

## Benefits of Intensive Blood Pressure Lowering

The theoretical benefit of intensive BP reduction is the prevention of reperfusion injury and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH). However, primary studies show this benefit is largely unrealized after mechanical thrombectomy.

* **No Significant Reduction in Hemorrhage:** The BP-TARGET trial evaluated whether an intensive SBP target (100–129 mm Hg) would reduce intraparenchymal hemorrhage compared to a standard target (130–185 mm Hg) after successful EVT. The trial found no significant difference in the rate of radiographic hemorrhage at 24 to 36 hours (Mazighi et al., 2021).
* **No Improvement in Outcomes:** Ultimately, intensive targets did not yield higher rates of 90-day functional independence in the major post-thrombectomy randomized trials (Mazighi et al., 2021).

## Summary

While standard guidelines typically recommend maintaining SBP < 180/105 mm Hg after successful reperfusion, aggressively lowering SBP below 130 or 140 mm Hg to prevent hemorrhage appears to cause more harm than good due to the resulting microcirculatory hypoperfusion.
