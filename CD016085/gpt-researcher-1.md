## Scope

I found **eight primary randomized studies** directly comparing assigned blood-pressure strategies after reperfusion treatment for acute ischemic stroke:

* **One** after intravenous thrombolysis.
* **Six** testing a uniform lower target after successful endovascular thrombectomy.
* **One** testing an individualized, reperfusion-guided strategy after thrombectomy.

I excluded studies of blood-pressure treatment before reperfusion, general acute-stroke BP trials without a reperfusion-treated population, observational analyses based only on achieved BP, protocols, reviews, guidelines, editorials, and pooled analyses. For DETECT, I used only the primary pilot-trial component—not the meta-analysis contained in the same publication.

## Main findings

### After intravenous thrombolysis

**ENCHANTED** is the principal direct trial. It randomized patients receiving alteplase with SBP at least 150 mm Hg to a target of **130–140 mm Hg within one hour** versus **less than 180 mm Hg**, maintained for 72 hours. Among 2,196 analyzed participants, achieved 24-hour mean SBP differed by only about 5.5 mm Hg: 144.3 versus 149.8 mm Hg.

There was **no improvement in 90-day modified Rankin Scale distribution** with intensive treatment: common OR 1.01, 95% CI 0.87–1.17. Intensive treatment did reduce **any intracranial hemorrhage**, 14.8% versus 18.7%; OR 0.75, 95% CI 0.60–0.94, but without a corresponding functional benefit. Serious adverse events did not differ significantly. Thus, after alteplase, modestly more intensive lowering appears to reduce mostly radiographic hemorrhage but has not demonstrated better disability outcomes. ([ScienceDirect][1])

### Durability of the OPTIMAL-BP harm signal

At one year, functional independence remained numerically lower following the original 24-hour intensive strategy: **40.5% versus 52.7%**, adjusted OR 0.59, 95% CI 0.34–1.00; P=0.051 in the intention-to-treat analysis. The per-protocol analysis was statistically significant, while one-year mortality did not differ. This suggests that an adverse effect occurring during the first post-thrombectomy day may have durable consequences for recovery. ([PubMed][8])

## The important exception: individualized post-thrombectomy management

**HOPE**, published in June 2026, should not be combined conceptually with trials of uniform intensive lowering. It assigned:

* Patients with **mTICI 2b**, indicating incomplete reperfusion, to SBP **140–160 mm Hg**.
* Patients with **mTICI 2c/3**, indicating near-complete or complete reperfusion, to SBP **100–140 mm Hg**.
* Controls to the broad guideline strategy of SBP **less than 180 mm Hg**.

Antihypertensives or vasopressors could be used to maintain the assigned range. Among 440 analyzed patients, favorable 90-day outcome occurred in **60.0% versus 47.1%**, absolute difference 13.3%, 95% CI 4.1%–22.6%; OR 1.71, 95% CI 1.17–2.50. Hemorrhagic transformation was lower, 22.3% versus 31.6%; OR 0.62, while symptomatic ICH and mortality were similar. Serious adverse events occurred in 15.8% versus 12.0%. ([JAMA Network][9])

The reasonable interpretation is that **avoiding unnecessarily low BP when reperfusion remains incomplete, while permitting lower BP after near-complete reperfusion**, may differ fundamentally from lowering every successfully treated patient to the same target. That mechanistic interpretation remains an inference; HOPE does not show that universal SBP lowering to 100–140 mm Hg is beneficial.

## Overall interpretation

For **uniform intensive lowering after successful EVT**, the evidence does not show a hemorrhage benefit and identifies clinically important functional harm at very low targets:

* **SBP <120 mm Hg:** clearly harmful in ENCHANTED2/MT.
* **SBP <140 mm Hg:** harmful in OPTIMAL-BP, with the difference persisting at one year.
* **SBP 100–129 or <130 mm Hg:** no demonstrated benefit in BP-TARGET or IDENTIFY.
* **Moderate lower targets:** BEST-II was inconclusive but provided little indication that a larger superiority trial would succeed.

The recurring harm is **worse neurological or functional recovery**, not a consistent increase in symptomatic ICH or mortality. This pattern is compatible with impaired cerebral perfusion or failure of microvascular reperfusion despite angiographic reopening, although the target trials were not designed to establish that mechanism.

After **IV alteplase**, lowering toward 130–140 mm Hg produced a modest reduction in any intracranial hemorrhage but no functional benefit. This evidence is less directly transferable to the post-thrombectomy setting because the treatments, reperfusion certainty, stroke severity, and achieved BP separation differ.
