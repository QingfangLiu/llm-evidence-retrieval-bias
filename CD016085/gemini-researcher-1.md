Based on recent primary randomized controlled trials (RCTs) evaluating blood pressure (BP) control after successful reperfusion therapy (endovascular thrombectomy) for acute ischemic stroke, intensive BP lowering offers virtually no clinical benefits and carries a significant risk of worsening patient outcomes.

Here is a synthesis of the evidence drawn exclusively from individual primary studies.

### The Risks of Intensive BP Lowering

The most prominent finding across recent primary literature is that artificially driving systolic blood pressure down to intensive targets (typically <120 mm Hg or <140 mm Hg) following successful reperfusion actively harms patients' functional recovery.

* **Increased Disability and Poor Functional Outcomes:** The ENCHANTED2/MT trial demonstrated that patients assigned to an intensive systolic target (<120 mm Hg) had a significantly higher likelihood of poor functional outcomes and major disability at 90 days compared to those managed with a less intensive, conventional target of 140–180 mm Hg (Yang et al., 2022). Similarly, the OPTIMAL-BP trial found that targeting <140 mm Hg resulted in a substantially lower proportion of patients achieving functional independence (39.4%) compared to conventional management (54.4%) (Nam et al., 2023).
* **Early Neurological Deterioration:** Patients subjected to more intensive blood pressure lowering experienced higher rates of early neurological deterioration, likely due to compromised perfusion to the penumbra—the at-risk brain tissue surrounding the stroke core (Yang et al., 2022).
* **Trial Terminations for Safety:** The signals for harm have been strong enough that independent data and safety monitoring boards terminated both the ENCHANTED2/MT and OPTIMAL-BP trials early due to safety and efficacy concerns (Nam et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2022). Furthermore, the BEST-II trial concluded that lowering targets to <140 mm Hg or <160 mm Hg had a remarkably low probability of showing any clinical benefit if pursued in larger future trials (Mistry et al., 2023).

### The (Lack of) Benefits of Intensive BP Lowering

Historically, the rationale for intensive BP lowering after restoring blood flow was to prevent reperfusion injury and mitigate the risk of hemorrhagic transformation (bleeding into the brain tissue). However, primary clinical trials have failed to validate these theoretical benefits.

* **No Reduction in Intracranial Hemorrhage:** The BP-TARGET trial specifically investigated whether an intensive target (100–129 mm Hg) would reduce the rate of radiographic intraparenchymal hemorrhage compared to standard care (130–185 mm Hg). The trial found no significant reduction in hemorrhage rates between the two groups (Mazighi et al., 2021). The OPTIMAL-BP and ENCHANTED2/MT trials also noted no significant difference in symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage between their intensive and conventional arms (Nam et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2022).
* **No Reduction in Infarct Volume:** The BEST-II trial demonstrated that more aggressive blood pressure targets did not lead to a meaningful reduction in follow-up infarct volumes (Mistry et al., 2023).
* **No Mortality Benefit:** None of the major primary trials have shown a survival benefit or a reduction in stroke-related deaths when deploying intensive blood pressure control (Mazighi et al., 2021; Nam et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2022).
