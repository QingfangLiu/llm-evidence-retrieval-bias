## Summary of the evidence

The five completed RCTs that directly compare intensive vs. standard/conventional blood pressure targets after successful reperfusion therapy (all endovascular thrombectomy, ± prior thrombolysis) for acute ischemic stroke have been remarkably consistent:

- **BP-TARGET** (2021) — intensive SBP target 100–129 mmHg vs. standard 130–185 mmHg for 24 h. No difference in intraparenchymal hemorrhage rate, the primary safety outcome; not powered for functional outcome. This trial assessed whether an intensive systolic blood pressure target resulted in reduced rates of intraparenchymal haemorrhage compared with a standard target.

- **OPTIMAL-BP** (2023) — intensive SBP target <140 mmHg vs. conventional 140–180 mmHg during the first 24 h. Stopped early; this trial found that intensive BP management reduced functional independence at 3 months compared with conventional management, and it enrolled 306 patients with large-vessel occlusion stroke across 19 South Korean centers.

- **BEST-II** (2023) — a phase 2 dose-finding trial comparing three SBP targets (≤180, <160, <140 mmHg). This pragmatic, multisite, randomized, open-label trial with blinded endpoint assessment tested whether targeting SBP below the guideline-recommended ≤180 mmHg threshold is safe and effective. It suggested a low probability of benefit from lower SBP targets, providing futility evidence against a larger phase 3 trial.

- **ENCHANTED2/MT** (2022) — the largest and best-powered trial, comparing intensive SBP <120 mmHg vs. less intensive 140–180 mmHg. With 821 participants, it was terminated early because a more intensive BP-lowering target of <120 mmHg resulted in significantly greater neurological deterioration within 7 days and worse functional outcomes than less intensive SBP control (140–180 mmHg), with no differences in mortality, serious adverse events, or symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage between groups.

- **Single-center feasibility/pilot RCT** (Anadani et al., *Stroke: Vascular and Interventional Neurology*) — a pilot randomized-controlled trial enrolling patients with persistently elevated BP after successful thrombectomy, randomized within 1 hour of the procedure to intensive (SBP <140 mmHg) vs. standard (SBP <180 mmHg) targets for 48 hours, with feasibility as the primary endpoint and neurologic deterioration, functional improvement, intracranial hemorrhage, and transcranial Doppler flow dynamics as exploratory endpoints.

A sixth RCT, **INTENSE**, is an ongoing (not yet completed) multicenter trial registered out of China (Soochow University), running 2024–2026, so primary results are not yet available.
