Managing hemodynamics after endovascular therapy is a pivotal challenge in stroke care. The recent consensus from primary randomized controlled trials indicates that **intensive blood pressure lowering post-reperfusion does not improve functional outcomes and frequently causes harm** compared to standard, guideline-based targets.

Here is a breakdown of the specific risks and lack of anticipated benefits demonstrated in recent primary trials.

## The Risks of Intensive Lowering

Recent large-scale trials have consistently demonstrated that pushing systolic blood pressure too low following successful recanalization compromises neurological recovery. The predominant physiological theory is that cerebral autoregulation is impaired in the ischemic penumbra; aggressively lowering systemic pressure deprives this vulnerable, newly reperfused microvasculature of adequate blood flow.

* **Worse Functional Recovery:** The ENCHANTED2/MT trial randomized patients to an intensive systolic target (<120 mm Hg) versus a standard target (140–180 mm Hg). The trial was halted early due to explicit safety concerns: the intensive group experienced higher rates of early neurological deterioration, major disability, and poor functional recovery at 90 days (Yang et al., 2022).
* **Reduced Functional Independence:** The OPTIMAL-BP trial echoed these findings. Patients subjected to intensive lowering (systolic <140 mm Hg) for the first 24 hours after successful endovascular thrombectomy had significantly lower rates of functional independence at 3 months compared to the conventional control group (140–180 mm Hg) (Nam et al., 2023).
* **Futility and Downward Trends:** The BEST-II trial assessed three target thresholds (≤180, <160, and <140 mm Hg). It found that pushing targets below 160 or 140 mm Hg did not yield higher utility-weighted modified Rankin Scale scores and signaled a high probability of futility and potential harm (Mistry et al., 2023).

## The Lack of Anticipated Benefits

Historically, the primary rationale for strict blood pressure control was to prevent reperfusion injury, symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage (sICH), and malignant edema. However, the data does not support this in practice.

* **No Reduction in Hemorrhage:** The BP-TARGET trial directly tested whether aggressive control prevents bleeding. Targeting a systolic BP of 100–129 mm Hg failed to significantly reduce the rate of radiographic intraparenchymal hemorrhage compared to a standard 130–185 mm Hg target (Mazighi et al., 2021).
* Across the board, the ENCHANTED2/MT, BEST-II, and OPTIMAL-BP trials also found that intensive targets did not yield any significant reductions in symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage rates compared to standard groups (Mistry et al., 2023; Nam et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2022).

Ultimately, these high-quality primary studies support maintaining conventional, permissive systolic parameters (typically targeting <180 mm Hg per current guidelines) rather than pursuing intensive reduction strategies immediately following endovascular thrombectomy.
