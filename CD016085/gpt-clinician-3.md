## Scope

I searched for **primary comparative studies assigning patients to more-intensive versus standard blood-pressure control after reperfusion treatment for acute ischemic stroke**, current through **July 25, 2026**. I excluded protocols, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, narrative reviews, guidelines, opinions, and editorials. The direct evidence consists almost entirely of randomized, open-label, blinded-endpoint trials.

## Bottom line

For patients with **successful endovascular thrombectomy**, a **uniformly intensive SBP target below 120–140 mm Hg** has generally **not improved outcomes**. ENCHANTED2/MT and OPTIMAL-BP found significantly worse functional recovery; BP-TARGET, BEST-II, DETECT, and IDENTIFY found no clinical benefit. Intensive treatment also did not consistently reduce symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage. ([PubMed][1])

The important exception is the **2026 HOPE trial**, which used a reperfusion-guided strategy rather than one low target for everyone: SBP 100–140 mm Hg after near-complete/complete reperfusion but 140–160 mm Hg after incomplete reperfusion, with active prevention and treatment of hypotension. It improved functional independence and reduced hemorrhagic transformation. However, only about 10% of participants had incomplete reperfusion, patients with important residual vascular stenosis were excluded, and the trial stopped below its planned sample size. It therefore supports further evaluation of a **carefully monitored individualized hemodynamic strategy**, not routine blanket lowering below 140 mm Hg. ([JAMA Network][2])

After **intravenous alteplase**, ENCHANTED found that targeting SBP 130–140 mm Hg reduced **any intracranial hemorrhage**, but did not improve 90-day disability. This population was thrombolysis-eligible and largely alteplase-treated, but successful recanalization was not required, making it less directly applicable to the post-thrombectomy setting. ([PubMed][3])

### Longer-term OPTIMAL-BP follow-up

At one year, functional independence remained numerically lower after intensive treatment: **40.5% versus 52.7%**, adjusted OR 0.59, P=0.051 in the intention-to-treat analysis. The per-protocol result was statistically significant, while one-year mortality was similar. This suggests that the early functional harm was not clearly recovered over time. ([PubMed][9])

## Interpretation of benefits and risks

**Potential benefit:** Lowering BP may reduce radiographic reperfusion bleeding. That signal appeared after alteplase in ENCHANTED and with the tailored HOPE protocol. However, most thrombectomy trials did **not** show a reduction in symptomatic ICH, and BP-TARGET did not reduce its primary radiographic hemorrhage outcome. ([PubMed][3])

**Most consistent risk:** The principal harm signal is **worse neurologic and functional recovery**, rather than an obvious increase in symptomatic hemorrhage or early mortality. ENCHANTED2/MT showed more early deterioration and disability, while OPTIMAL-BP showed less independence, more transient SBP below 100 mm Hg, and more malignant edema. A plausible explanation is reduced collateral or microvascular perfusion when systemic pressure is actively lowered despite angiographic reopening. ([PubMed][4])

**Target intensity matters:** SBP **<120 mm Hg** has the clearest evidence of harm. Uniform targets below 130 or 140 have ranged from neutral to harmful and have not demonstrated reproducible benefit. HOPE should not be interpreted simply as supporting SBP below 140; it tested a broader package involving reperfusion-based targets, a defined lower safety boundary, intensive monitoring, vasopressor rescue, and exclusion of patients with important residual vasculopathy. ([PubMed][4])
