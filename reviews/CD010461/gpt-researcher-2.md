## Scope

I conducted a targeted search for **individual comparative primary studies** evaluating sperm-selection add-ons against conventional swim-up, density-gradient centrifugation, or routine morphology/motility-based ICSI selection. I prioritized live birth, ongoing pregnancy, clinical pregnancy, miscarriage, and implantation outcomes. I excluded reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, opinions, protocols, case reports, and studies reporting only post-processing semen characteristics.

This is a broad evidence map rather than a fully deduplicated systematic-review search.

## Overall interpretation from the primary studies

**PICSI has the most secure evidence.** The large HABSelect trial found no statistically significant improvement in term live birth. A reduction in miscarriage is plausible, but smaller trials and the 2025 donor-oocyte study do not demonstrate a consistent live-birth benefit. ([PMC][1])

**IMSI results are inconsistent.** Positive early and small subgroup trials contrast with the multicentre first-cycle trial and the 2026 severe-teratozoospermia RCT, both of which found no meaningful clinical benefit. ([PubMed][5])

**Microfluidic selection reliably improves some sperm-DNA or embryo laboratory measures, but clinical effectiveness is unsettled.** One 122-couple RCT was negative, one 128-couple trial reported a substantial live-birth benefit, and the 2026 IVF RCT was negative despite better DNA-fragmentation reduction. ([PubMed][9])

**MACS, zeta, birefringence, zona and cumulus selection have encouraging individual studies but insufficient independent replication.** Many positive effects come from small, single-centre or subgroup analyses; several studies do not report live birth, and at least one relevant investigator group has a trial under an expression of concern. ([PubMed][13])

Taken together, the primary literature does **not show a consistent improvement in live birth from routinely adding advanced sperm selection to IVF/ICSI**. Possible benefits remain most plausible in selected populations—high sperm DNA fragmentation, severe astheno/teratozoospermia or prior poor outcomes—but these subgroup claims need adequately powered, preregistered and independently replicated trials.
