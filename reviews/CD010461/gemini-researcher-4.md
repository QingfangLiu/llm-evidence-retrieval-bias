It is great to connect with a fellow evidence-synthesis researcher. Stripping away the noise of reviews and guidelines to look strictly at primary data is the best way to understand the true clinical landscape here.

When isolating individual primary studies (randomized controlled trials and primary observational cohorts), a clear pattern emerges: advanced sperm selection techniques generally do not show statistically significant improvements in live birth rates (LBR) or clinical pregnancy rates for **unselected** patient populations. However, the data points to a threshold effect, where these techniques demonstrate value for specific subpopulations (e.g., high DNA fragmentation, advanced maternal age, or recurrent pregnancy loss).

Here is a breakdown of the primary evidence by technique.

## Physiological ICSI (PICSI) / Hyaluronic Acid Binding

PICSI selects mature sperm based on their ability to bind to hyaluronic acid (HA), a molecule naturally found in the cumulus complex surrounding the oocyte.

* **The HABSelect Trial Data:** The largest dataset in this space comes from the HABSelect randomized controlled trial. While the primary trial found no significant difference in overall LBR between PICSI and conventional ICSI, secondary analyses of the primary data showed that HA selection significantly reduced miscarriage rates. Furthermore, the selection method improved live birth outcomes specifically among older couples and was tightly correlated with better sperm DNA quality (West et al., 2022).
* **Unexplained Infertility:** An earlier RCT evaluated PICSI versus standard ICSI in couples with unexplained infertility and normal baseline semen parameters. The study found no significant differences in fertilization rates, top-quality embryo development, or clinical pregnancy rates. While there was a trend toward higher pregnancy loss in the standard ICSI group, it did not reach statistical significance, suggesting normal-parameter cohorts may not benefit from the intervention (Majumdar & Majumdar, 2013).
* **Correlative Success:** An observational cohort study categorized patients by their HA-binding assay scores. They found that in cohorts where sperm had lower HA-binding capacity, using PICSI yielded significantly higher fertilization, clinical pregnancy, and live birth rates compared to those who underwent conventional ICSI (Mokánszki et al., 2014).

## Microfluidic Sperm Sorting (MFSS)

MFSS isolates highly motile sperm through microchannels, aiming to reduce the oxidative stress and physical damage typically caused by centrifugation.

* **Unselected Populations:** A pragmatic RCT directly compared microfluidic preparation against standard density-gradient centrifugation (DGC) for ICSI in an unselected patient population. Intention-to-treat analysis revealed no significant differences in high-quality cleavage-stage embryo fraction, blastocyst development, clinical pregnancy, or ongoing pregnancy rates (Quinn et al., 2022). This supports the consensus that microfluidics performs similarly to standard processing when applied universally rather than selectively.

## Magnetic-Activated Cell Sorting (MACS)

MACS uses annexin V-conjugated magnetic beads to separate and remove apoptotic (dying) sperm that have externalized phosphatidylserine, enriching the sample with intact, non-apoptotic sperm.

* **High DNA Fragmentation:** In a targeted study of males with a high DNA fragmentation index (DFI $\ge$ 30%), researchers compared MACS combined with standard processing (DGC and swim-up) against standard processing alone. While basic fertilization and cleavage rates were similar, the MACS group required significantly fewer transfer cycles and showed a strong trend toward improved cumulative live birth rates (Mei et al., 2021).
* **Broad Application:** Highlighting the importance of patient selection, a massive retrospective analysis of over 48,000 autologous oocyte cycles investigated the addition of MACS to standard processing in **unselected** males. The study found no significant differences in overall pregnancy or live birth rates, concluding that MACS has a very limited clinical impact when applied routinely without specific indications like high DFI (Gil Juliá et al., 2021).
