Here's a set of primary studies (RCTs, prospective/randomized cohort studies, and sibling-oocyte trials) comparing advanced sperm selection techniques against standard selection (density gradient centrifugation/swim-up or standard ICSI) in IVF/ICSI. All systematic reviews, meta-analyses, narrative reviews, and guidelines have been excluded — though I've kept a few review titles visible above only as they surfaced alongside primary studies; none are included in the list below.

## Hyaluronan-based selection (PICSI / HA-ICSI)

- **Miller et al. — HABSelect trial** (Lancet, 2019): Large multicentre RCT (2,772 couples) comparing PICSI vs standard ICSI. Live birth rate did not differ significantly between PICSI (27.4%) and ICSI (25.2%) groups.
- **Alegre, Carrión-Sisternas, Bori et al. (Biomedicines, 2025)**: Triple-blinded RCT in an oocyte-donation population. Clinical outcomes were comparable between groups, though pregnancy rate was somewhat higher in the PICSI arm (74.0% vs 70.9%), and cumulative pregnancy rate was significantly higher with PICSI (88% vs a lower control rate).
- **Novoselsky Persky et al. (Andrology, 2021)**: Sibling-oocyte RCT-style comparison of conventional ICSI vs PICSI.
- **Korosi, Barta, Rokob, Torok (Eur Rev Med Pharmacol Sci, 2017)**: RCT of PICSI with oral myo-inositol pretreatment in oligoasthenoteratozoospermic men.

## Ultra-high magnification selection (IMSI)

- **Antinori et al. (Reprod Biomed Online, 2008)**: First prospective RCT of IMSI vs ICSI in 446 couples with severe oligoasthenoteratozoospermia. IMSI produced a higher clinical pregnancy rate (39.2% vs 26.5%, P=0.004) than standard ICSI in severe male infertility cases.
- **Leandri, Gachet et al. (Andrology, 2013)**: Multicentric RCT of IMSI vs ICSI as a first ART attempt (255 couples). IMSI did not provide significant improvement in clinical outcomes compared with ICSI for implantation or clinical pregnancy in this first-attempt population (contrasting with Antinori's findings in patients with prior failures).

## Microfluidic sperm sorting

- **Aydın Ş, Bulgan Kılıçdağ E, Çağlar Aytaç P et al. (Andrologia, 2022)**: Prospective RCT of microfluidic chip sperm selection vs standard method in male infertility patients.
- **Study on Fertile Chip® vs swim-up in unexplained infertility (2019, sibling-oocyte design)**: 122 couples with unexplained infertility; 61 treated with conventional swim-up and 61 with the microfluidic technique for sperm selection in IVF. Found the microfluidic technique did not change fertilization, clinical pregnancy rate, or live birth rate.
- **Sperm chip vs density gradient/swim-up in normal semen analysis patients**: Randomized prospective cohort study of 213 patients; 102 underwent ICSI with sperm isolated by microfluidic chip and 111 by swim-up.
- **Godiwala, Kwieraga, Almanza et al. — sibling oocyte double-blinded RCT (Fertility and Sterility, 2024)**: Compared microfluidics sperm processing vs density gradient centrifugation on blastocyst euploidy rates.
- **Sibling-oocyte study of Fertile Plus® device (2019)**: Sibling oocytes of 81 patients assigned to swim-up vs Fertile Plus® microfluidic sorting; no statistically significant differences found in fertilization, embryo quality, blastocyst development, clinical pregnancy, implantation, or live birth rates.
- **ZyMōt™ vs conventional methods in patients with high sperm DNA double-strand breaks (2022, retrospective paired-cycle cohort)**: 28 infertile couples with elevated DSB values underwent a first ICSI cycle with conventional preparation and a second cycle using ZyMōt™ microfluidic sorting; the microfluidic method showed statistically significant improvement in clinical outcomes compared to conventional techniques in this high-DSB subgroup.
- **ZyMōt vs DGC/CA0 comparative device study (Japan, jRCT-registered, 2024/25)**: ZyMōt achieved higher rates of fertilization, blastocyst formation, and good-quality blastocysts, with a lower rate of poor-quality cleavage-stage embryos compared with DGC and CA0.

## Magnetic-activated cell sorting (MACS)

- **González-Ravina, Santamaría-López et al. (Cells, 2022)**: Prospective randomized multicenter trial of MACS in donor IUI (181 treatments) — note: IUI rather than IVF/ICSI context, but relevant as a primary RCT on advanced selection technology. MACS was performed after density gradient centrifugation in 90 thawed donor samples versus DGC alone in 91 samples.
- **Clinical outcomes of MACS in infertile men candidates for ICSI (Royan Institute, prospective randomized trial)**: 62 semen samples allocated to MACS-DGC (n=29) vs DGC alone (n=33); no significant difference in fertilization rates, but significant differences favoring MACS in high-quality embryo rate, pregnancy, and implantation rates.
- **MACS in patients with high sperm DNA fragmentation (2020)**: Large retrospective cohort (n=724) — flagged here as it's frequently cited alongside RCTs, though note it is retrospective rather than randomized, so you may want to verify inclusion against your criteria.

## Related design (IVF vs ICSI fertilization method, sibling-oocyte/RCT)

- **Non-male-factor ICSI vs conventional IVF sibling-oocyte RCT (NCT06670586)**: 128 patients, oocytes split between conventional IVF and ICSI arms.
- **INVICSI trial (Nature Medicine/Lancet, 2024–2025)**: Large multicenter RCT (824 women) comparing ICSI vs conventional IVF in non-severe male factor infertility — useful as a comparator/context study rather than an "advanced selection" technique per se.

- Several of these are **sibling-oocyte** designs rather than couple-level randomization, which matters for your risk-of-bias assessment.
