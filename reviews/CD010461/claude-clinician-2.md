## PICSI (hyaluronan-binding selection)

- **Miller D, et al. (2019).** *Physiological, hyaluronan-selected intracytoplasmic sperm injection for infertility treatment (HABSelect): a parallel, two-group, randomised trial.* Lancet 393(10170):416-422. Large multicenter RCT (2772 couples). Found no significant difference in clinical pregnancy or premature birth between groups, but miscarriage was significantly lower with PICSI. No live-birth benefit.
- **Hozyen M, et al. (2022).** *Reproductive Outcomes of Different Sperm Selection Techniques for ICSI Patients with Abnormal Sperm DNA Fragmentation: a Randomized Controlled Trial.* Reprod Sci 29(1):220-228. 302 patients with abnormal SDF randomized to DGC, testicular sperm, PICSI, or MACS; PICSI had the highest pregnancy rate (69.6%) vs DGC lowest (51.4%), p=0.025.
- **Hasanen E, et al. (2020).** *PICSI vs. MACS for abnormal sperm DNA fragmentation ICSI cases: a prospective randomized trial.* J Assist Reprod Genet 37(10):2605-2613. 413 ICSI cases with abnormal SDF randomized to PICSI vs MACS; no significant differences in embryological or pregnancy outcomes.
- Alegre L, et al. (2016). *Embryo quality is improved by PICSI sperm selection: a randomized controlled trial.* Fertil Steril 106:e307 (sibling-oocyte RCT).
- Novoselsky Persky M, et al. (2021). *Conventional ICSI vs. physiological selection of spermatozoa for ICSI (PICSI) in sibling oocytes.* Andrology 9(3):873-877.
- Majumdar G, Majumdar A (2013). *A prospective randomized study to evaluate the effect of hyaluronic acid sperm selection on the ICSI outcome of patients with unexplained infertility having normal semen parameters.* J Assist Reprod Genet 30(11):1471-1475.
- Worrilow KC, et al. (2013). *Use of hyaluronan in the selection of sperm for ICSI: significant improvement in clinical outcomes: multicenter, double-blinded and randomized controlled trial.* Hum Reprod 28:306-314.

## MACS (magnetic-activated cell sorting)

- **Romany L, et al. (2014, controlled/randomized trial).** *Removal of annexin V-positive sperm cells for ICSI in ovum donation cycles does not improve reproductive outcome.* Fertil Steril. Prospective, randomized, triple-blinded, controlled study of 237 couples undergoing ICSI in an ovum-donation program; MACS did not improve fertilization, implantation, ongoing pregnancy, or live-birth rates.
- **González-Ravina C, et al. (2022).** *Effect of Sperm Selection by Magnetic-Activated Cell Sorting in D-IUI: A Randomized Control Trial.* Cells 11(11):1794. 181 donor-IUI treatments randomized to MACS+DGC vs DGC alone; no significant differences in gestation, live birth, or miscarriage rates.
- Dirican EK, et al. (2008). *Clinical outcomes of magnetic activated sperm sorting in infertile men candidates for ICSI.* J Assist Reprod Genet. Prospective randomized trial of 62 samples (MACS-DGC vs DGC alone); MACS-DGC group had significantly higher high-quality embryo, pregnancy, and implantation rates.

## IMSI (high-magnification morphology selection)

- **Leandri RD, et al. (2013).** *Is IMSI beneficial in the first ART cycle? A multicentric randomized controlled trial.* Andrology 1(5):661-668. 255 couples randomized; IMSI showed no significant improvement over ICSI in implantation, clinical pregnancy, or live birth rates.
- Gatimel N, et al. (2016). *IMSI does not improve outcome in patients with two successive IVF-ICSI failures.* J Assist Reprod Genet. No significant improvement in implantation, clinical pregnancy, or live birth.
- Balaban B, et al. (2011). *Pregnancy outcomes in women with repeated implantation failures after IMSI.* Reprod Biomed Online 22(5):472-476. No statistically significant difference in fertilization, implantation, or pregnancy rates, though trends favored IMSI.
- Antinori M, et al. (2008). *Intracytoplasmic morphologically selected sperm injection: a prospective randomized trial.* Reprod Biomed Online 16(6):835-841.
- A retracted RCT also exists (Reprod Biol Endocrinol 2015) — flagging so you can exclude it: it was stopped early over efficacy concerns and later retracted.

## Microfluidic sperm sorting

- **Quinn MM, et al. (2022).** *Microfluidic preparation of spermatozoa for ICSI produces similar embryo quality to density-gradient centrifugation: a pragmatic, randomized controlled trial.* Hum Reprod 37(7):1406-1414. 386 patients randomized; no differences in high-quality cleavage- or blastocyst-stage embryo fraction, or in clinical/ongoing pregnancy rates, between microfluidic separation and DGC in an unselected population.
- Comparative Study of Sperm Selection Techniques for Pregnancy Rates in an Unselected IVF–ICSI Population, PMC10145657 — 239 couples randomized 1:1 to DGC vs microfluidic (ZyMot) preparation (note: described as retrospective in the abstract despite randomization language — worth checking full text before citing as an RCT).
- Basile N, et al. — *Microfluidic sperm sorting improves ICSI outcomes in patients with increased DSB* — this is a retrospective cohort (paired cycles), not randomized; flagging for exclusion if you want RCTs only.

## Other/adjunct technique

- Cumulus-cell column selection RCT (2024/2025, PMC12631016): 88 ICSI cycles randomized to standard DGC vs DGC plus cumulus-cell-column selection; significantly reduced DNA fragmentation and markedly improved implantation, clinical pregnancy, and live birth rates — small single-center trial, worth scrutinizing for bias given the very large effect sizes.

A few notes for your appraisal: the IMSI and microfluidic literature is fairly consistent in showing **no benefit** on hard clinical endpoints in RCTs, despite promising observational data. PICSI has mixed results — HABSelect (the largest, most rigorous RCT) found no live-birth benefit but did find a miscarriage reduction. MACS evidence is heterogeneous and mostly smaller trials.
