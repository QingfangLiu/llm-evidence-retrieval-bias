## Hyaluronan-based selection (PICSI / HA-binding)

- **Miller et al. (HABSelect), Lancet 2019** — largest RCT of PICSI to date (n=2772). Term live birth rate: 27.4% (PICSI) vs 25.2% (ICSI), NS (OR 1.12, 95% CI 0.95–1.34); miscarriage significantly reduced with PICSI. [thelancet.com]
- **West et al., Hum Reprod 2022** (HABSelect mechanistic sub-study, n=1247) — sperm DNA quality/HA-binding score linked to outcomes; PICSI mitigated the age-related decline in live birth, especially with older female age. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35459947]
- **Hozyen et al., Reprod Sci 2022** — 4-arm RCT (n=302) comparing DGC, testicular sperm, PICSI, MACS in men with abnormal sperm DNA fragmentation; highest pregnancy rate with PICSI (69.6%) vs lowest with DGC (51.4%), p=0.025. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34076869]
- **Korosi et al., Eur Rev Med Pharmacol Sci 2017** — RCT of PICSI with oral myo-inositol pretreatment in oligoasthenoteratozoospermic men.
- **Alegre et al., Biomedicines 2025** — triple-blinded RCT comparing PICSI vs ICSI on embryo quality, cumulative pregnancy rate, live birth rate.

## Magnetic-activated cell sorting (MACS)

- **González-Ravina et al., Cells 2022** — RCT of MACS in donor IUI (D-IUI), n=181; no significant differences in gestation, live birth, or miscarriage. [PMC9180176]
- **Romany et al., J Assist Reprod Genet 2017** — RCT-derived obstetric/perinatal outcomes of babies born after MACS-selected sperm.
- Additional MACS RCTs (Stimpfel 2018, Ješeta 2018, Sánchez-Martín 2017, Degheidy 2015, Troya 2015) — sibling-oocyte and prospective randomized trials on non-apoptotic/DNA-fragmentation outcomes (details cited within the Miltenyi reference list).

## Microfluidic sperm sorting (MSS)

- **Yetkinel et al., J Assist Reprod Genet 2019** — prospective RCT of microfluidic chip sperm selection for ICSI in unexplained infertility.
- **NCT03085433 (Weill Cornell), "Sperm Selection by Microfluidic Separation Improves Embryo Quality"** — RCT, n=386 randomized, comparing microfluidic sorting vs standard prep for IVF/ICSI embryo quality outcomes.
- **Ozcan et al., J Obstet Gynaecol Res 2021** — microfluidic sperm sorting vs standard selection in male factor infertility IVF outcomes.
- **Yildiz & Yuksel, J Assist Reprod Genet 2019** — microfluidic chips in patients with recurrent IVF failure.
- **Comparative Evaluation of Two Microfluidic Devices (ZyMōt vs DGC vs swim-up), 2024–25, jRCT1040230045** — registered trial comparing sperm quality/ICSI outcomes.

## Intracytoplasmic morphologically selected sperm injection (IMSI)

- **Leandri et al., Andrology 2013** — multicentric RCT (n=255), first ART cycle: no significant difference in implantation, clinical pregnancy, or live birth between IMSI and ICSI. [onlinelibrary.wiley.com]
- **Antinori et al., Reprod Biomed Online 2008** — prospective RCT (n=446) in severe oligoasthenoteratozoospermia comparing IMSI vs ICSI on pregnancy, miscarriage, implantation.
- **Gatimel et al., J Assist Reprod Genet 2016** — RCT-type comparison in couples with two prior ICSI failures; no significant improvement with IMSI.
- **Balaban et al., Reprod Biomed Online** — prospective randomized study of IMSI under high magnification vs ICSI.
- **Knez et al.** — comparative randomized study of IMSI vs ICSI in couples with prior growth-arrested embryos.

## PIEZO-ICSI (injection technique, not selection per se, but often grouped as "advanced")

- **PLOS One 2025 (Korea)** — RCT (n=100 patients, sibling oocytes), P-ICSI vs C-ICSI: higher fertilization (78.0% vs 72.6%) and lower degeneration with P-ICSI; blastocyst formation NS overall.
- **Human Reprod 2025 abstract (P-167)** — RCT across 1,000 cycles; p-ICSI reduced degeneration and 1PN rates.
- **Sibling oocyte split multicenter trial, Fertil Steril 2024** — n=108, PIEZO-ICSI increased fertilization (71.6% vs 65.6%) and reduced degeneration; no difference in clinical pregnancy/live birth per transfer.

A note on interpretation: across these RCTs, the general pattern is that advanced selection techniques (PICSI, MACS, IMSI, microfluidic sorting) often improve intermediate/lab outcomes (DNA fragmentation, degeneration, embryo quality, miscarriage in some cases) more consistently than they improve the primary clinical endpoint of live birth rate, where results are frequently non-significant in the largest, best-powered trials (HABSelect, Leandri IMSI trial).
