## Clinical takeaway

Across individual comparative studies, **no advanced sperm-selection method has shown a consistent, independently replicated improvement in live birth over standard density-gradient, swim-up, or conventional microscopic selection**.

The clearest findings are:

* **PICSI/hyaluronan selection:** the largest trial found no statistically significant improvement in term live birth, although miscarriage was lower.
* **IMSI:** early positive trials have not been confirmed by multicenter or more recent randomized trials.
* **Microfluidic sorting:** generally improves sperm DNA-fragmentation or embryo-quality measures, but randomized live-birth results are mixed.
* **MACS:** small trials and selected high-DFI cohorts sometimes show benefit, but adequately powered live-birth evidence is lacking.
* **Zeta potential, zona adhesion, and FACS:** each has limited evidence from one or a few trials without robust replication.

Below is a curated set of **individual primary comparative studies**, current through July 17, 2026.

## Randomized or prospectively randomized studies

### PICSI / hyaluronan-binding selection

| Primary study                                         | Population and comparison                                                                                                               | Main patient-level findings                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Miller et al., HABSelect, Lancet 2019**             | 2,772 couples at 16 UK centers; PICSI versus standard ICSI; fresh transfer. Primary endpoint was term live birth.                       | Term live birth: **27.4% versus 25.2%**, OR 1.12, 95% CI 0.95–1.34, *P*=0.18. Clinical pregnancy was virtually identical. Miscarriage per randomized couple was lower with PICSI: **4.3% versus 7.0%**, *P*=0.003. No subgroup benefit according to HA-binding score, maternal age, previous miscarriage, or sperm concentration. This is the most decision-relevant PICSI study. ([PMC][1]) |
| **Worrilow et al., Human Reproduction 2013**          | Multicenter, double-blind RCT; 802 couples stratified by initial HA-binding score; HA-bound sperm versus conventional visual selection. | No statistically significant overall improvement in implantation or clinical pregnancy. Among patients with HA-binding scores ≤65%, pregnancy loss was lower with HA selection, but the trial stopped early because of funding and was not powered for live birth. ([PubMed][2])                                                                                                             |
| **Majumdar and Majumdar, J Assist Reprod Genet 2013** | 156 first-cycle ICSI patients with unexplained infertility and normal semen parameters; PICSI versus conventional ICSI.                 | Similar fertilization, top-quality embryo, and clinical pregnancy rates. Pregnancy loss was numerically lower with PICSI, **12% versus 25%**, but not statistically significant. ([PubMed][3])                                                                                                                                                                                               |

**Interpretation:** PICSI may affect miscarriage, but the large HABSelect trial did not demonstrate a statistically significant increase in term live birth.

### IMSI / high-magnification morphology selection

| Primary study                                                                                     | Population and comparison                                                                    | Main findings                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Antinori et al., Reproductive BioMedicine Online 2008**                                         | 446 couples with severe oligoasthenoteratozoospermia; IMSI versus standard ICSI.             | Clinical pregnancy was higher with IMSI, **39.2% versus 26.5%**, particularly among patients with previous failed cycles. Live birth was not the prespecified or clearly reported primary endpoint. ([PubMed][4])                                                                      |
| **Leandri et al., Andrology 2013**                                                                | Multicenter RCT of 255 couples undergoing their first ART cycle for male-factor infertility. | No improvement in implantation, clinical pregnancy, or live birth: live birth approximately **27% with IMSI versus 30% with ICSI**. | **Intracytoplasmic morphologically selected sperm injection versus conventional ICSI, RCT, 2015** | Triple-blind RCT in first-cycle male-factor infertility; analysis included 121 couples.      | Live-birth delivery per initiated cycle: **18.8% with IMSI versus 15.1% with ICSI**, OR 1.30, 95% CI 0.49–3.42. No statistically significant benefit; trial stopped early after an interim analysis. ([PMC][6])                                                                        |
| **Shakeri et al., Scientific Reports 2026**                                                       | Double-blind RCT of 100 couples with severe teratozoospermia; IMSI versus standard ICSI.     | No significant differences in fertilization, biochemical, clinical or ongoing pregnancy, live birth, miscarriage, selected-sperm DFI, or telomere length. Live birth was numerically lower in the IMSI arm in the reported group ordering, but estimates were imprecise. ([Nature][7]) |
| **Setti et al., Eur J Obstet Gynecol Reprod Biol 2013**                                           | Small RCT of 66 couples in which the female partner was ≥37 years.                           | IMSI produced higher implantation and clinical pregnancy rates, but the study was very small, based its sample size on implantation, and did not establish a live-birth benefit. ([PubMed][8])                                                                                         |

**Interpretation:** IMSI has contradictory early clinical-pregnancy findings, but multicenter and recent randomized studies do not show a reliable live-birth advantage.

### Microfluidic sperm sorting

| Primary study                                                                           | Population and comparison                                                                                   | Main findings                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Yetkinel et al., J Assist Reprod Genet 2019**                                         | RCT of 122 couples with unexplained infertility; microfluidic chip versus conventional swim-up before ICSI. | Clinical pregnancy: **48.3% versus 44.8%**, *P*=0.35. Live birth: **38.3% versus 36.2%**, *P*=0.48. More grade-1 embryos were obtained with microfluidics, without improved live birth. ([PubMed][9])                                                    |
| **Prospective randomized controlled study in male-factor infertility, Andrologia 2022** | 128 couples; Fertile Chip versus swim-up before ICSI.                                                       | Similar fertilization and embryo quality. Implantation, clinical pregnancy, and live birth were higher with the chip; live birth was **46.8% versus 25.0%**, *P*=0.009. Live birth was a secondary endpoint in a single-center trial. ([PubMed][10])     |
| **Huong et al., JBRA Assisted Reproduction 2026**                                       | RCT of 119 IVF couples; microfluidics versus density-gradient centrifugation.                               | Microfluidics reduced DFI more effectively, particularly when baseline DFI was ≥15%, but did not improve clinical outcomes. Among patients with transfer data, live birth was **59.3% with microfluidics versus 63.3% with DGC**, *P*=0.676. ([PMC][11]) |
| **Microfluidic chip sibling-oocyte study, Andrologia 2019**                             | Sibling oocytes from 81 patients assigned to Fertile Plus or swim-up.                                       | No significant differences in fertilization, blastocyst development, implantation, clinical pregnancy, or live birth. Patient-level clinical comparisons were limited because not all paired embryos were transferred. ([PubMed][12])                    |

**Interpretation:** Microfluidic devices reliably change sperm-quality metrics, but that biological effect has not consistently translated into better live birth.

### MACS / Annexin-V selection of non-apoptotic sperm

| Primary study                                   | Population and comparison                                                                 | Main findings                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Ziarati et al., Human Fertility 2018**        | Prospective randomized trial; 62 male-factor ICSI cycles; MACS plus DGC versus DGC alone. | Similar fertilization, but higher high-quality embryo, pregnancy, and implantation rates with MACS. The sample was too small for a dependable live-birth estimate. ([Taylor & Francis Online][13])                                                                              |
| **Mei et al., Asian Journal of Andrology 2022** | Randomized study in men with DFI ≥30%; 39 MACS versus 41 standard DGC/swim-up cycles.     | No significant difference in clinical pregnancy or implantation after the first transfer. First-transfer live birth was **63.2% versus 53.9%**, and cumulative live birth **79.5% versus 70.7%**, but neither difference was reported as statistically significant. ([PMC][14]) |
| **Sheikhi et al., Andrology 2013**              | 74 couples with unexplained infertility; MACS plus DGC versus DGC alone before ICSI.      | Higher fertilization and some embryo-quality measures. Pregnancy was **43.2% versus 35.1%**, and live birth **40.5% versus 27.0%**; differences in pregnancy and live birth were not statistically significant. ([DOI][15])                                                     |
| **MACS-DGC versus DGC clinical trial, 2022**    | 206 couples with male-factor infertility and severe abnormal morphology.                  | Better day-3 embryo quality and implantation with MACS-DGC, but clinical pregnancy was not significantly different, **30.8% versus 22.2%**, *P*=0.19. Live birth was not reported. ([PubMed][16])                                                                               |

**Interpretation:** MACS may improve laboratory or implantation measures in selected populations, but there is no well-powered randomized demonstration of improved live birth.

### Other selection approaches

| Technique and primary study                                             | Comparison                                                                                                                              | Findings                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Zeta-potential selection — Nasr-Esfahani et al., 2016**               | Double-blind randomized trial of 203 male-factor ICSI cycles; DGC plus zeta selection versus DGC alone.                                 | Higher top-quality embryo and pregnancy rates were reported with zeta selection. The study also reported a changed sex ratio at birth. It is a single-center trial without independent live-birth replication. ([PubMed][17])                                                                                                                               |
| **FACS/YoPro1 exclusion — de Geyter et al., 2019**                      | Prospective randomized double-blind trial of 104 male-factor couples; flow-cytometric exclusion of DNA-fragmented sperm versus swim-up. | Cumulative ongoing pregnancy was **51.9% versus 46.2%** and live birth **42.3% versus 34.6%**, neither reaching the prespecified clinically important improvement; the trial was stopped after interim analysis showed the target effect was unlikely. ([PubMed][18])                                                                                       |
| **Solubilized zona-pellucida adhesion — Ganeva et al., Andrology 2024** | 500 couples randomized; analysis included 368 who proceeded to transfer of euploid frozen embryos.                                      | Per transfer, zona-selected sperm produced higher implantation, clinical pregnancy, and live birth—**38.0% versus 25.9%**—and lower miscarriage. However, 132 randomized patients without transfer were excluded, so the analysis lost the protection of randomization and does not establish a per-randomized-couple benefit. ([Wiley Online Library][19]) |

## Large comparative observational studies

These are useful for safety signals and hypothesis generation, but treatment selection and patient differences can strongly influence the results.

| Study                                                          | Main finding                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Sánchez-Martín et al., 2020 — MACS in high SDF**             | Retrospective analysis of 724 ART cycles with high SDF. MACS was associated with lower miscarriage and higher live birth in autologous cycles, **40.9% versus 24.6%**, and donor-oocyte cycles, **51.8% versus 29.4%**. Selection bias and residual confounding remain major concerns. ([PMC][20])                                                                     |
| **Gil Juliá et al., 2021 — MACS with autologous oocytes**      | Multicenter cohort of 46,807 standard and 1,779 MACS patients. Live birth per transfer was virtually identical: **29.2% versus 29.3%**. Only small differences appeared in some cumulative outcomes per embryo or oocyte. ([PMC][21])                                                                                                                                  |
| **Gil Juliá et al., 2022 — MACS with donor oocytes**           | More than 40,000 standard cycles versus 1,240 MACS cycles. MACS did not improve cumulative live birth when results were evaluated over increasing numbers of transferred embryos or oocytes used. ([PubMed][22])                                                                                                                                                       |
| **Balakier et al., 2026 — ZyMōt versus DGC**                   | Retrospective cohort of 1,091 ZyMōt and 1,176 DGC ICSI cycles. ZyMōt was associated with better fertilization, blastulation, cumulative clinical pregnancy, and live birth in donor and non-donor groups, but the nonrandomized design makes confounding by calendar time, indication, laboratory practice, and transfer strategy difficult to exclude. ([PubMed][23]) |
| **Microfluidics in elevated sperm double-strand breaks, 2022** | Paired retrospective cohort of 28 couples whose first conventional ICSI cycle was compared with a later ZyMōt cycle. Pregnancy and live birth improved in the second cycle, but cycle order, treatment changes, and regression to the mean are important alternative explanations. ([PubMed][24])                                                                      |

## Study requiring particular caution

The randomized publication **“PICSI vs. MACS for abnormal sperm DNA fragmentation ICSI cases”** and the related four-arm report are indexed with an **expression of concern**. I would not use their reported clinical advantages for counseling or protocol decisions unless the publication concerns are resolved. ([PubMed][25])

## Practical evidence summary

For routine, unselected IVF/ICSI patients, the current primary-study record does **not** demonstrate that an advanced selection add-on reliably raises the chance of taking home a baby.

A possible exception worth continued study is a **carefully defined male-factor subgroup**, such as markedly elevated sperm DNA fragmentation. Even there, improvements in post-selection DFI do not reliably predict improved live birth, and the positive MACS and microfluidic findings remain inconsistent across trials. PICSI has the strongest evidence for a possible reduction in miscarriage, but that reduction did not produce a statistically significant term-live-birth increase in the large HABSelect trial.
