## Hyaluronan-binding selection (PICSI/HA-ICSI)

- **Miller et al. (HABSelect trial)** — Parallel, two-group RCT, 2772 couples randomized to PICSI vs standard ICSI. Live birth rate did not differ significantly between PICSI (27.4%) and ICSI (25.2%) groups. (*Lancet*, 2019)

- **Novoselsky Persky et al.** — Sibling-oocyte design comparing HA-ICSI vs conventional ICSI within the same patients. (*Andrology*, 2021)

- **Alegre et al.** — Triple-blinded RCT of PICSI vs ICSI in an oocyte-donation population (eliminates female-factor bias). A higher pregnancy rate was observed in the PICSI group than in the ICSI group (74.04% vs. 70.87%), and cumulative pregnancy rate was significantly higher in the PICSI group. (*Biomedicines*, 2025)

- **Korosi et al.** — Prospective RCT of PICSI with oral myo-inositol pretreatment in oligoasthenoteratozoospermic men. (*Eur Rev Med Pharmacol Sci*, 2017)

## Intracytoplasmic morphologically selected sperm injection (IMSI)

- **Leandri et al.** — Multicentric RCT, 255 couples, first ART attempt for male infertility. IMSI resulted in significantly higher implantation (17.3% vs. 11.3%) and clinical pregnancy rates (39.2% vs. 26.5%) compared with conventional ICSI, though the study also notes an earlier RCT of 168 couples found no significant benefit for a first-attempt population. (*Andrology*, 2013)

- **[Retracted] RCT of IMSI vs conventional ICSI** — Triple-blind RCT stopped early by the data safety monitoring committee; final analysis of 121 patients. (*Reprod Biol Endocrinol*, 2015 — note retraction status)

- **Open prospective study, IMSI vs ICSI** — 446 ICSI vs 79 IMSI first-trial cycles; ICSI was statistically better than IMSI for chemical pregnancy, clinical pregnancy, live birth, and fertilization rates. (2016–2017 cohort, PMC)

## Magnetic-activated cell sorting (MACS)

- **Prospective randomized trial, MACS-DGC vs DGC alone** — 62 samples; no significant difference in fertilization rates, but percentage of high-quality embryos, pregnancy, and implantation rates was significantly higher in the MACS-DGC group.

- **MACS RCT in D-IUI (donor insemination) cycles** — 181 treatments; results showed no significant differences in gestation, live birth, or miscarriage rates between MACS and control groups. (PMC, 2022)

- **Gil Juliá et al.** — Large retrospective cohort, autologous ICSI cycles (46,807 standard vs 1,779 MACS). Significant differences only in CLBR per embryo transferred and per MII oocyte used; no significant differences in pregnancy or live birth rates. (*Biology*, 2021)

- **Retrospective multicentre study** — Obstetric/perinatal safety outcomes of MACS vs standard preparation before ICSI. (*Reprod Biomed Online*, 2023)

## Microfluidic sperm sorting

- **Parrella/Quinn et al. pragmatic RCT (NCT03085433)** — Microfluidic (ZyMōt) vs density-gradient centrifugation for ICSI. No difference in the primary outcome of fertilization, but those undergoing microfluidic sperm preparation had more high-quality embryos for transfer. (*Human Reproduction*, 2022)

- **Comparative evaluation of ZyMōt vs CA0 microfluidic devices** — Registered trial (jRCT1040230045), 17 semen samples split across DGC/ZyMōt/CA0, with ICSI outcomes analyzed. ZyMōt achieved higher rates of fertilization, blastocyst formation, and good-quality blastocysts, and a lower percentage of poor-quality cleavage-stage embryos than CA0. (*Reprod Med Biol* / PMC, 2026)

- **Retrospective cohort, ZyMōt vs DGC with PGT-A** — Patients served as own controls; primary outcome blastocyst rate, secondary outcomes fertilized-embryo count and euploidy rates. (*Fertility and Sterility*, 2021)

## Laser-assisted ICSI

- **RCT (NCT00114725)** — Largest RCT of laser-assisted vs conventional mechanical ICSI, comparing fertilization/degeneration rates and embryo development. (*Reprod Biol Endocrinol*, 2007)

## Contextual RCTs on ICSI vs conventional insemination (not sperm-selection technique per se, but relevant comparator studies)

- **Berntsen et al., INVICSI trial** — Multicenter open-label RCT, 824 women, ICSI vs conventional IVF insemination in non-severe male factor infertility. Comparing cumulative live birth rates as the primary outcome between ICSI and c-IVF in patients without severe male factor infertility. (*Nature Medicine*, 2025)
