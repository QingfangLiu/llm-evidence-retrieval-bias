Managing multiple daily injections takes constant recalibration, and optimizing your mealtime insulin can make a significant difference in your daily routine. Based on primary clinical studies in adults with type 1 diabetes on multiple daily injections, here is how the different insulin speeds compare.

## Standard Rapid-Acting vs. Regular Human Insulin

Because regular human insulin has a slower onset and a longer duration of action, it was historically thought to be better for managing high-fat and high-protein meals, which digest slowly and cause delayed glucose spikes. However, a randomized crossover trial evaluating adults on multiple daily injections found no benefit to using regular human insulin for these meals. Standard rapid-acting analogues provided equal late post-meal glucose control compared to regular insulin (Jabłońska et al., 2018).

## Ultra-Rapid vs. Standard Rapid-Acting Analogues

Newer ultra-rapid insulins (such as ultra-rapid lispro or faster aspart) are standard rapid-acting insulins formulated with added excipients that dramatically speed up how quickly the insulin enters and leaves your system.

* **Benefit: Faster onset and early coverage.** Ultra-rapid formulations appear in the bloodstream significantly faster. For example, ultra-rapid lispro appears in the blood about five times faster than standard lispro, resulting in up to a seven-fold greater insulin exposure in the first 15 minutes to blunt early post-meal spikes (Linnebjerg et al., 2020).
* **Benefit: Reduced late hypoglycemia.** Because ultra-rapid insulins absorb faster, they also clear from your system sooner. This shorter duration of action reduces the late glucose-lowering effect, potentially lowering your risk of experiencing a low blood sugar hours after a meal (Linnebjerg et al., 2020).
* **Benefit: Improved time-in-range.** Real-world data shows that adults on multiple daily injections who switch to a faster-acting analogue see tangible improvements. One observational study found that switching to faster aspart increased daily time-in-range by an average of 46 minutes and reduced overall time spent in hyperglycemia, all without increasing the time spent in hypoglycemia (Danne et al., 2021).
* **Harms and Considerations:** The primary studies report that both ultra-rapid and standard rapid-acting insulins are well-tolerated with similar safety profiles. However, because ultra-rapid insulins clear from the blood sooner, the main consideration is adjusting your bolus timing to match your meal composition, as you no longer have the extended "tail" of standard insulin to cover very delayed digestion (Linnebjerg et al., 2020).
