When evaluating a transition from regular human insulin (RHI) to rapid-acting analogues (like lispro or aspart), or from standard rapid analogues to ultra-rapid formulations (like ultra-rapid lispro [URLi] or faster aspart) in a multiple daily injection (MDI) regimen, the clinical literature highlights a distinct shift in pharmacodynamics.

Based strictly on primary trials in adults with type 1 diabetes on MDI, here is how the benefits and harms break down.

## Benefits of Faster-Acting Analogues

**Blunted Postprandial Glucose Spikes**
The defining advantage of faster-acting insulins is their ability to tackle post-meal glucose excursions. In a meal-test study, URLi increased insulin exposure within the first 30 minutes by 2.2-fold compared to standard lispro, which reduced postprandial glucose excursions by 40% to 44% over a 5-hour window (Kazda et al., 2021). The 26-week PRONTO-T1D trial corroborated this in a basal-bolus MDI setting, confirming that mealtime URLi was statistically superior to standard lispro in reducing both 1-hour and 2-hour post-meal glucose spikes (Klaff et al., 2020).

**Increased Time in Range (TIR)**
Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) data demonstrates that the pharmacokinetic speed of these analogues translates to better daily stability. A substudy of the PRONTO-T1D trial revealed that patients using mealtime URLi gained an average of 43.6 more minutes per day in their target glucose range (71–180 mg/dL) compared to standard lispro (Malecki et al., 2020). Real-world evidence from the GoBolus study similarly showed that adults on MDI who switched to fast-acting insulin aspart saw their TIR increase by 46.1 minutes per day, with a concurrent drop in time spent in hyperglycemia (Danne et al., 2021).

**Reduced Nocturnal Hypoglycemia**
Because faster-acting analogues have a shorter duration of action, they are less likely to cause delayed drops in blood sugar overnight. A classic crossover trial evaluating standard insulin lispro versus RHI in patients with impaired hypoglycemia awareness found a 47% lower incidence of severe nocturnal hypoglycemia with lispro (Ferguson et al., 2001). This benefit continues to scale with the newer ultra-rapid generations: the PRONTO-T1D CGM substudy found that mealtime URLi decreased nighttime time in hypoglycemia by 11.5 minutes compared to standard lispro (Malecki et al., 2020).

**Dosing Flexibility**
Faster onset allows patients to inject much closer to the meal—or even shortly after they start eating—without severely compromising control. Kazda et al. (2021) demonstrated that URLi provided better postprandial glucose lowering than standard lispro regardless of whether it was dosed 15 minutes before, immediately before, or 15 minutes after a meal began. This flexibility is a major quality-of-life upgrade compared to RHI, which often requires strict administration 30 to 45 minutes prior to eating.

## Harms and Limitations

**Injection Site Reactions**
The newest ultra-rapid insulins achieve their absorption speed through added excipients (e.g., treprostinil to induce local vasodilation and citrate to increase vascular permeability). These additives can cause local tissue irritation. In the PRONTO-T1D trial, injection site reactions were reported by up to 2.9% of patients using URLi, compared to just 0.2% of patients using standard lispro (Klaff et al., 2020).

**Similar Overall Hypoglycemia and HbA1c Profiles**
While daily variance and postprandial spikes improve, standard HbA1c reductions and total hypoglycemic events often remain quite similar between drug generations. The PRONTO-T1D trial confirmed that URLi was non-inferior (but not strictly superior) to standard lispro for overall HbA1c reduction, and the total incidence of severe or documented overall hypoglycemia was virtually identical between the two treatments (Klaff et al., 2020). Likewise, the GoBolus study noted that while time in hyperglycemia decreased, the time spent in hypoglycemia remained unchanged after switching to faster aspart (Danne et al., 2021).

**Bottom Line for Your Patients:** Upgrading to a faster-acting analogue will likely flatten their post-meal spikes, grant them more freedom around when they inject, and lower their risk of delayed overnight lows. However, they should not expect a drastic reduction in their overall HbA1c, and they should be warned about the slightly increased risk of mild injection site irritation with the newest ultra-rapid formulas.
