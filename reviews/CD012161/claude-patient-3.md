## Summary of what the evidence shows

**Faster-acting analogues vs. regular human insulin (RHI):**
A body of RCTs (mostly from the 1990s–2000s, spanning lispro, aspart, and glulisine) consistently shows small-to-modest reductions in postprandial glucose excursions and modestly lower rates of hypoglycemia — particularly nocturnal and severe hypoglycemia — with faster-acting analogues compared with RHI, but HbA1c differences are often small or non-significant in individual trials a UK trial found HbA1c levels of 7.4% on soluble human insulin versus 7.5% on insulin lispro, with no significant difference, though patients on lispro were less likely to experience nighttime hypoglycemia and more likely to experience morning episodes. The Danish HypoAna trial, which specifically enrolled people prone to recurrent severe hypoglycemia, found a more clinically meaningful benefit: 136 severe hypoglycemic episodes occurred on human insulin versus 105 on analogue insulin, an absolute rate reduction of 0.51 episodes per patient-year and a 29% relative risk reduction.

**Ultra-fast analogues vs. conventional rapid analogues:**
Newer "ultra-fast" formulations (faster aspart/Fiasp, ultra-rapid lispro/Lyumjev) were designed to shift absorption earlier. In adults with T1D on MDI, both have generally shown noninferiority to their parent rapid-acting analogue on HbA1c, with modest improvements in postprandial glucose control. For example, the ONSET 1 trial found the primary endpoint was change from baseline in HbA1c after 26 weeks, comparing mealtime faster aspart, conventional insulin aspart, and postmeal faster aspart, each with insulin detemir, and PRONTO-T1D found both mealtime and postmeal ultra-rapid lispro (URLi) demonstrated non-inferiority to lispro for HbA1c, with postmeal URLi actually showing a significantly higher endpoint HbA1c than lispro.

Overall, differences between agents tend to be small on HbA1c, more evident on postprandial glucose and hypoglycemia timing, and trial-to-trial results vary by population (e.g., people prone to severe hypoglycemia may benefit more).
