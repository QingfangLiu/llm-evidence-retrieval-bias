When synthesizing the evidence for adults with Type 1 Diabetes (T1D) using multiple daily injections (MDI), the literature divides the evolution of prandial insulins into two main clinical shifts: the transition from **regular human insulin (RHI)** to **rapid-acting insulin analogues (RAIAs)** (e.g., lispro, aspart), and the more recent shift from RAIAs to **ultra-rapid-acting insulin analogues (URLAs)** (e.g., ultra-rapid lispro, faster aspart).

Here is a breakdown of the benefits and harms established in pivotal primary trials, followed by a list of the studies.

## Rapid-Acting Analogues vs. Regular Human Insulin

The core physiological problem with RHI is its tendency to aggregate into hexamers in the subcutaneous tissue, which significantly delays its absorption into the bloodstream. RAIAs were engineered to dissociate into monomers much faster, allowing them to better mimic a healthy pancreas's endogenous post-meal insulin spike.

**Benefits**

* **Superior Postprandial Control:** In large multicenter randomized controlled trials (RCTs), RAIAs like insulin lispro and insulin aspart consistently suppress post-meal glucose spikes more effectively than RHI. For instance, insulin lispro reduced the postprandial glucose rise at 1 hour by 1.3 mmol/L compared to RHI (Anderson et al., 1997), and insulin aspart demonstrated similar reductions across all main meals (Home et al., 2000).
* **Reduced Major & Nocturnal Hypoglycemia:** Because RAIAs have a shorter duration of action, they clear from the system before they can cause "late" hypoglycemia. Insulin aspart reduced major nighttime hypoglycemic events requiring parenteral treatment to 1.3% of patients, down from 3.4% with RHI (Home et al., 2000). Anderson et al. (1997) noted an overall 12% reduction in hypoglycemia rates with lispro, with the most significant drop occurring at night.
* **Modest HbA1c Improvements:** Long-term glycemic control generally favors RAIAs over RHI, though the absolute reduction in HbA1c is typically modest—often ranging from 0.12% to 0.16% (Home et al., 2000; Home et al., 2006).

**Harms and Considerations**

* **Minor Hypoglycemia:** While major and nocturnal events decrease, the intense early peak of RAIAs can sometimes misalign with slower-digesting meals. One 3-year extension trial found that while major hypoglycemia remained equivalent, the risk of minor, easily manageable hypoglycemic episodes was actually slightly higher (relative risk 1.24) with insulin aspart compared to RHI (Home et al., 2006).
* **Basal Adjustments:** Because RAIAs taper off much faster than RHI, patients transitioning to them occasionally experience pre-meal hyperglycemia (especially before breakfast or dinner) if their basal insulin regimen is not adequately adjusted to cover the gaps (Home et al., 2000).

## Ultra-Rapid-Acting Analogues vs. Rapid-Acting Analogues

URLAs build on standard RAIAs by adding excipients (like treprostinil and citrate in ultra-rapid lispro, or niacinamide in faster aspart) that induce local vasodilation and vascular permeability, drastically accelerating absorption.

**Benefits**

* **Accelerated Onset:** URLAs appear in the bloodstream significantly faster. In cross-over glucose clamp trials, ultra-rapid lispro (URLi) appeared in the serum 6 minutes faster than standard lispro, yielding a 7.2-fold greater insulin exposure over the first 15 minutes (Linnebjerg et al., 2020).
* **Dosing Flexibility:** The accelerated onset allows for administration immediately at the start of a meal—or even up to 20 minutes after starting—without the severe postprandial spikes seen when standard RAIAs are delayed.
* **Reduced Late Action:** URLAs leave the bloodstream sooner than standard RAIAs. For example, insulin action beyond 4 hours post-dose was reduced by 44–54% with URLi compared to standard lispro, actively reducing the risk of late postprandial hypoglycemia (Linnebjerg et al., 2020).

**Harms and Considerations**

* **Equivalent Total Exposure:** It is important to note that the total insulin exposure and overall glucose-lowering effect remain the same between URLAs and RAIAs; the curve is simply shifted to the left (Linnebjerg et al., 2020). Consequently, overall HbA1c reductions between the two are generally non-inferior rather than dramatically superior.
* **Tolerability:** The addition of vasodilating excipients occasionally leads to minor, localized injection site reactions, though primary safety trials report that URLAs are generally as well-tolerated as standard analogues with no significant difference in severe adverse events (Linnebjerg et al., 2020).
