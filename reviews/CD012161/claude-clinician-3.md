## Faster-acting insulin aspart vs. conventional insulin aspart (MDI, adults with T1D)

The ONSET trial program directly addresses this. In the 26-week ONSET 1 trial (basal insulin detemir), faster aspart improved 1-h postprandial glucose control versus insulin aspart when administered as part of a basal-bolus regimen. Mealtime faster aspart statistically significantly reduced HbA1c compared with insulin aspart, while a postmeal-dosing arm remained noninferior for HbA1c. Over the full 52-week study, estimated mean changes from baseline in HbA1c favored faster aspart significantly over insulin aspart. Rates of confirmed or severe hypoglycemia were similar overall, but higher during the first hour after a meal in the faster aspart arm.

ONSET 8 repeated this comparison with insulin degludec as the basal insulin rather than detemir, again in a randomized double-blind design with mealtime and postmeal faster aspart arms.

## Ultra-rapid lispro (URLi/Lyumjev) vs. conventional lispro (MDI, adults with T1D)

The PRONTO-T1D trial randomized adults with T1D to double-blind mealtime URLi, mealtime lispro, or open-label postmeal URLi with the primary endpoint being change in HbA1c at 26 weeks, plus multiplicity-adjusted postprandial glucose objectives. The 52-week extension found 1-h and 2-h postmeal glucose was statistically significantly lower with URLi than lispro, similar rates of severe/documented/postprandial hypoglycemia overall but a 31% lower rate more than 4 hours after meals with URLi, and more injection-site reactions with URLi (3.3% vs 0.9%).

## Insulin lispro vs. regular (soluble) human insulin (MDI, adults with T1D)

The UK Trial Group's randomized, double-blind crossover study compared lispro with human soluble insulin in patients with Type 1 diabetes on multiple daily injection therapy who inject shortly before meals, examining glucose control, hypoglycemia frequency, and patient preference over 12-week treatment periods, finding lower postprandial glucose but higher pre-meal glucose with lispro.

## Insulin aspart vs. regular human insulin (MDI, adults with T1D)

Two key randomized trials from the same era: Home et al.'s European Insulin Aspart Study Group trial, and Raskin et al.'s trial evaluating insulin aspart as the mealtime insulin in the management of patients with type 1 diabetes, both basal-bolus MDI regimens.

## Insulin glulisine vs. insulin lispro / regular human insulin (adults with T1D)

Dreyer et al. conducted a multinational, controlled, open-label, randomized, parallel-group study comparing insulin glulisine to insulin lispro in adults with Type 1 diabetes over 26 weeks, finding similar HbA1c reduction in both groups. Garg et al. compared glulisine against regular human insulin, each combined with basal glargine.
