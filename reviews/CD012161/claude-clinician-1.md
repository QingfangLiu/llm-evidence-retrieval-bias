## Summary

I found individual primary studies (RCTs) directly comparing faster-acting insulin analogues with regular human insulin or with each other in adults with type 1 diabetes on multiple daily injections (MDI). Below are the key trials organized by comparison type, followed by benefit/harm themes and the full study list.

**What the primary trials generally show:**
- **Analogue vs. regular human insulin:** Rapid-acting analogues (lispro, aspart, glulisine) modestly improve postprandial glucose control and produce small (~0.1–0.5%) HbA1c reductions versus regular human insulin, generally without increasing (and sometimes reducing) minor/nocturnal/severe hypoglycemia — though some trials found a slightly higher rate of minor hypoglycemia with analogues.
- **Ultra-rapid analogues (faster aspart, ultra-rapid lispro) vs. their conventional analogue counterparts:** Noninferior HbA1c with better 1–2 hour postprandial glucose control; faster aspart trials showed a higher rate of hypoglycemia in the first hour after dosing but no overall increase; ultra-rapid lispro trials showed more injection-site reactions.
