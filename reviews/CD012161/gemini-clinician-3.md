When evaluating insulin options for adults with type 1 diabetes on multiple daily injections (MDI), it helps to look directly at the clinical trial data. Because standard rapid-acting analogues (like lispro and aspart) largely replaced regular human insulin as the standard of care years ago, contemporary primary studies focus almost exclusively on comparing these standard rapid-acting formulations against the newer **ultra-rapid-acting** formulations (such as fast-acting insulin aspart and ultra-rapid lispro).

Here is a breakdown of the benefits and harms based on recent primary trials in this specific patient population.

## Benefits of Faster-Acting Analogues

* **Superior Postprandial Glucose (PPG) Control:** The defining clinical advantage of ultra-rapid analogues is their ability to blunt the immediate post-meal glucose spike. In the PRONTO-T1D trial, ultra-rapid lispro significantly lowered both 1-hour and 2-hour post-meal glucose excursions compared to standard lispro (Klaff et al., 2020). Similarly, the *onset 1* trial demonstrated that fast-acting insulin aspart effectively reduced 1-hour PPG increments to a greater degree than standard insulin aspart (Russell-Jones et al., 2017), a finding also confirmed in regional subgroups (Komatsu et al., 2021).
* **Non-Inferior Overall Glycemic Control:** While the rapid onset heavily targets post-meal spikes, overall HbA1c reductions are generally non-inferior when compared to standard analogues over 26 weeks (Klaff et al., 2020; Russell-Jones et al., 2017). Long-term data out to 52 weeks confirms that this efficacy is preserved without deterioration (Bue-Valleskey et al., 2021).
* **Improved Time-in-Range (TIR) in the Real World:** Beyond controlled trial environments, real-world observational data indicates that switching to fast-acting aspart from standard therapies can improve TIR and decrease time spent in hyperglycemia without driving up hypoglycemic events (Lind et al., 2023).
* **Dosing Flexibility:** Because of the accelerated onset, these insulins offer more flexibility. Studies have evaluated post-meal dosing (up to 20 minutes after starting a meal) and found that it still maintains non-inferior HbA1c control compared to pre-meal standard analogues, offering a safety net for patients with unpredictable eating habits (Komatsu et al., 2021; Russell-Jones et al., 2017).

## Safety Profile and Potential Harms

* **Comparable Severe Hypoglycemia Rates:** A common concern with faster insulins is an increased risk of severe "crashing." However, trial data shows that the overall rates of severe or blood-glucose-confirmed hypoglycemia are statistically similar between the ultra-rapid and standard rapid-acting formulations (Klaff et al., 2020; Russell-Jones et al., 2017).
* **Reduced Late Postprandial Hypoglycemia:** Because ultra-rapid analogues have a slightly shorter "tail" of action, they may actually reduce delayed hypoglycemia. For example, 52-week data on ultra-rapid lispro showed a 31% lower rate of hypoglycemia occurring more than 4 hours after a meal compared to standard lispro (Bue-Valleskey et al., 2021).
* **Injection Site Reactions:** The excipients added to accelerate absorption (like citrate and treprostinil in ultra-rapid lispro, or niacinamide in fast-acting aspart) can occasionally cause local tissue irritation. In the PRONTO-T1D extension, mild injection site reactions were slightly more frequent with ultra-rapid lispro (3.3%) than with standard lispro (0.9%), though long-term systemic safety issues have not been identified (Bue-Valleskey et al., 2021).
