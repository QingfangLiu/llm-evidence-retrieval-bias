When synthesizing the evidence for faster-acting insulin analogues versus regular human insulin (RHI) in adults with type 1 diabetes on multiple daily injections (MDI), the primary differences lie in their pharmacokinetic (PK) and pharmacodynamic (PD) time-action profiles. The core clinical tradeoff is superior postprandial control and lifestyle flexibility weighed against a slightly higher incidence of localized injection-site reactions with the newest formulations.

Here is a breakdown of the evidence on benefits and harms.

## Benefits

* **Superior Postprandial Glucose (PPG) Control:** The most consistently demonstrated benefit of both first-generation rapid-acting analogues (e.g., insulin aspart, lispro) and second-generation ultra-rapid analogues (e.g., faster aspart, ultra-rapid lispro) over RHI is the significant reduction in 1-hour and 2-hour postprandial glucose excursions.
* **Reduced Late and Nocturnal Hypoglycemia:** RHI has a prolonged "tail" of action that can last 6 to 8 hours, often remaining active long after meal absorption is complete. Because rapid and ultra-rapid analogues have a much faster offset, they significantly reduce the risk of late postprandial and nocturnal hypoglycemia.
* **Dosing Flexibility:** RHI requires administration 30 to 45 minutes prior to a meal to match carbohydrate absorption. First-generation analogues can be injected 15 minutes before or immediately prior to eating. The newest ultra-rapid analogues offer even greater flexibility—they can be administered right at the start of the meal or up to 20 minutes after meal initiation without a clinically significant deterioration in overall glycemic control (HbA1c).
* **Comparable Overall HbA1c:** While analogues drastically improve the *shape* of the daily glucose curve (increasing time-in-range and reducing variability), their impact on total HbA1c compared to RHI is generally minimal to non-inferior (often a marginal ~0.1% improvement).

## Harms and Risks

* **Injection Site Reactions:** While the overall safety profile is highly comparable across these insulin classes, ultra-rapid formulations achieve their accelerated absorption through added excipients (e.g., niacinamide and L-arginine in faster aspart; citrate and treprostinil in ultra-rapid lispro). These excipients are associated with a slightly higher incidence of mild, localized injection-site pain or infusion-site reactions compared to their first-generation counterparts or RHI.
* **Similar Rates of Severe Adverse Events:** The overall rates of severe hypoglycemia and diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) do not show clinically meaningful differences across these analogue tiers compared to RHI. They are more physiological, but they do not eliminate the baseline risks inherent to intensive insulin therapy.

## Primary Studies

Below is a curated list of individual primary studies (randomized controlled trials) evaluating these comparisons specifically in adults with type 1 diabetes on MDI, moving chronologically from RHI comparisons to the newest ultra-rapid formulations:

1. **Insulin Lispro vs. Regular Human Insulin**
Anderson, J. H., Brunelle, R. L., Koivisto, V. A., Pfützner, A., Trautmann, M. E., Vignati, L., & DiMarchi, R. (1997). Reduction of postprandial hyperglycemia and frequency of hypoglycemia in IDDM patients on insulin-analog treatment. Multicenter Insulin Lispro Study Group. *Diabetes*, 46(2), 265–270.
2. **Insulin Aspart vs. Regular Human Insulin**
Home, P. D., Lindholm, A., Hylleberg, B., & Round, P. (1998). Improved glycemic control with insulin aspart: a multicenter randomized double-blind crossover trial in type 1 diabetic patients. UK Insulin Aspart Study Group. *Diabetes Care*, 21(11), 1904–1909.
3. **Faster Aspart vs. Insulin Aspart**
Russell-Jones, D., Bode, B. W., De Block, C., Franek, E., Heller, S. R., Mathieu, C., Philis-Tsimikas, A., Rose, L., Woo, V. C., Østerskov, A. B., Graungaard, T., & Bergenstal, R. M. (2017). Fast-Acting Insulin Aspart Improves Glycemic Control in Basal-Bolus Treatment for Type 1 Diabetes: Results of a 26-Week Multicenter, Active-Controlled, Treat-to-Target, Randomized, Parallel-Group Trial (onset 1). *Diabetes Care*, 40(7), 943–950.
4. **Ultra-Rapid Lispro vs. Insulin Lispro**
Klaff, L., Cao, D., Dellva, M. A., Tobian, J., Miura, J., Dahl, D., Lucas, J., & Bue-Valleskey, J. (2020). Ultra rapid lispro improves postprandial glucose control compared with lispro in patients with type 1 diabetes: Results from the 26-week PRONTO-T1D study. *Diabetes, Obesity and Metabolism*, 22(10), 1799–1807.
