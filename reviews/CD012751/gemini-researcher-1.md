To support your evidence synthesis, I have bypassed all secondary literature (reviews, meta-analyses, and guidelines) and pulled directly from the landmark Phase 3 randomized controlled trials (RCTs) evaluating recent advanced therapies for moderate-to-severe Crohn’s disease.

Here is a synthesis of the benefits and harms as reported directly in these primary pivotal trials.

## Benefits: Induction and Maintenance of Remission

Recent primary trials evaluate efficacy through dual endpoints: clinical remission (symptom resolution, often measured via the Crohn's Disease Activity Index [CDAI]) and endoscopic response/remission (mucosal healing).

**IL-23 Inhibitors (Risankizumab & Mirikizumab)**
Newer targeted therapies blocking the p19 subunit of IL-23 have shown robust efficacy, even in patients who previously failed other biologics.

* **Risankizumab vs. Ustekinumab (SEQUENCE Trial):** In a direct head-to-head Phase 3 trial of patients who failed anti-TNF therapy, risankizumab was proven non-inferior to ustekinumab for clinical remission at week 24, but demonstrated superiority for endoscopic remission at week 48 (Peyrin-Biroulet et al., 2024).
* **Mirikizumab (VIVID-1 Trial):** This treat-through Phase 3 trial demonstrated mirikizumab's superiority over placebo for both clinical remission and endoscopic response at 52 weeks. It also achieved non-inferiority to ustekinumab for clinical remission at week 52, highlighting its viability as both a first-line biologic and an option for patients who have failed prior therapies (Ferrante et al., 2024).

**JAK Inhibitors (Upadacitinib)**
Small-molecule targeted synthetic therapies offer rapid onset via oral administration.

* **Upadacitinib (U-EXCEL, U-EXCEED, U-ENDURE):** The Phase 3 induction and maintenance program showed that upadacitinib (45 mg induction, 15 mg or 30 mg maintenance) was significantly superior to placebo. Notably, 49.5% (U-EXCEL) and 38.9% (U-EXCEED) of patients achieved clinical remission by week 12. At week 52 (U-ENDURE), nearly half of the patients on the 30 mg maintenance dose sustained clinical remission (Loftus et al., 2023).

**Anti-TNFs vs. Anti-IL-12/23 (Adalimumab vs. Ustekinumab)**

* **SEAVUE Trial:** In this head-to-head trial of biologic-naïve patients, both adalimumab (anti-TNF) and ustekinumab (anti-IL-12/23) proved highly effective. There was no statistically significant difference between the two drugs in achieving clinical remission at 52 weeks, establishing both as strong first-line therapies (Sands et al., 2022).

**Anti-Integrins (Vedolizumab)**

* **GEMINI 2 Trial:** Vedolizumab, which targets gut-selective α4β7 integrins to prevent lymphocyte trafficking to the intestines, showed significant benefits for maintenance. While week-6 clinical remission rates were modest (14.5% vs. 6.8% placebo), patients who responded to induction were highly likely to maintain clinical remission at week 52 on a maintenance schedule (Sandborn et al., 2013).

## Harms: Safety Profiles and Adverse Events

While biologics and small molecules suppress the immune system to reduce gut inflammation, their safety profiles diverge significantly based on their mechanism of action.

**Gut-Selective and Highly Targeted Biologics**
Drugs like vedolizumab (anti-integrin) and the newer IL-23 inhibitors (mirikizumab, risankizumab) generally demonstrate a safety profile comparable to placebo regarding serious adverse events (SAEs).

* In the **VIVID-1** trial for mirikizumab, the incidence of overall adverse events and treatment discontinuations was actually lower than in the placebo group, with no drug-related deaths (Ferrante et al., 2024).
* In the **GEMINI 2** trial, vedolizumab was associated with a higher rate of nasopharyngitis, general infections, and a slightly higher rate of serious infections (5.5%) compared to placebo (3.0%) (Sandborn et al., 2013). However, its gut-selective nature successfully avoided the risk of progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML) seen with systemic integrin inhibitors.

**Small Molecule JAK Inhibitors**
Because JAK inhibitors (upadacitinib) affect broader systemic intracellular signaling, they carry distinct safety warnings not typically seen with targeted monoclonal antibodies.

* The **U-EXCEL, U-EXCEED, and U-ENDURE** trials recorded higher rates of herpes zoster (shingles) infections in upadacitinib groups compared to placebo. Additionally, the higher maintenance dose (30 mg) was associated with higher rates of hepatic disorders and neutropenia. Rare but serious gastrointestinal perforations were also observed during the 45 mg induction phase (Loftus et al., 2023).
