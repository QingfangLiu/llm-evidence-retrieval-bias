## Anti-TNF agents

**Infliximab** — In ACCENT I, 573 patients with active Crohn's disease received an initial infliximab infusion, and responders were randomized to placebo, infliximab 5 mg/kg, or infliximab 5 mg/kg→10 mg/kg every 8 weeks. Patients on scheduled infliximab maintenance were more likely to sustain clinical remission than those switched to placebo, with a median time to loss of response of 38 weeks and over 54 weeks in the two infliximab groups versus 19 weeks with placebo. Safety was comparable across groups.

**Adalimumab** — CLASSIC-I tested induction dosing in 299 anti-TNF-naive patients; adalimumab was superior to placebo for induction of remission, with the optimal regimen being 160 mg at week 0 followed by 80 mg at week 2, and the drug was well tolerated. CHARM then tested maintenance in 778 responders to open-label induction, randomized to placebo, adalimumab every other week, or weekly; remission at week 26 was significantly higher with adalimumab (40% and 47%) than placebo (17%), an effect sustained to week 56.

**Certolizumab pegol** — PRECiSE 1 (n=662) found a modest benefit in response rates at weeks 6 and 26 compared with placebo, but no significant improvement in remission, with early onset of effect. PRECiSE 2 tested maintenance in 425 induction responders randomized to certolizumab or placebo through week 26, showing efficacy that was greater in patients with shorter disease duration.

## Anti-integrin

**Vedolizumab** — The GEMINI 2 trial (published in NEJM) established vedolizumab's efficacy for both induction and maintenance of remission in Crohn's disease, with subsequent long-term data corroborating durability of effect.

## Anti-IL-12/23

**Ustekinumab** — Two induction trials, UNITI-1 (anti-TNF failures, n=741) and UNITI-2 (conventional-therapy failures, n=628), fed into the 44-week maintenance trial IM-UNITI (397 responders). Patients receiving intravenous ustekinumab had significantly higher response rates than placebo in both induction trials, with similar adverse-event rates across groups. In IM-UNITI, clinical remission at week 44 was achieved by 36%, 49%, and 53% of patients on placebo, ustekinumab every 12 weeks, and every 8 weeks respectively.

## Anti-IL-23

**Risankizumab** — ADVANCE and MOTIVATE (induction, combined ~1,419 patients) and FORTIFY (52-week maintenance withdrawal trial) showed significantly higher clinical remission and endoscopic response rates with risankizumab versus placebo, with a similar overall adverse-event incidence across groups. FORTIFY concluded that subcutaneous risankizumab is a safe and efficacious maintenance treatment offering a new therapeutic option for a broad range of patients.

## Oral JAK inhibitor

**Upadacitinib** — U-EXCEL and U-EXCEED (induction) and U-ENDURE (52-week maintenance) enrolled over 1,500 patients combined. A significantly higher percentage of patients on 45 mg upadacitinib versus placebo achieved clinical remission (49.5% vs 29.1% in U-EXCEL; 38.9% vs 21.1% in U-EXCEED) and endoscopic response. In maintenance, remission at week 52 was higher with 15 mg (37.3%) or 30 mg (47.6%) upadacitinib than placebo (15.1%), though herpes zoster infections occurred more frequently with active drug, and hepatic disorders and neutropenia were more common with the 30 mg dose.
