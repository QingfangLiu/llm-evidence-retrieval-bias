## Anti-TNF agents

**Infliximab**
- ACCENT I: 573 patients received a 5 mg/kg intravenous infusion at week 0, then were randomized to placebo, 5 mg/kg, or 5 mg/kg→10 mg/kg maintenance infusions every 8 weeks; combined active groups had markedly higher remission rates than placebo at week 30 (odds ratio 2.7) and longer median time to loss of response.
- SONIC: Randomized, double-blind trial comparing infliximab monotherapy, azathioprine monotherapy, and combination therapy in 508 adults with moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease naive to immunosuppressive or biologic therapy; combination therapy produced a higher rate of corticosteroid-free clinical remission at week 26 than either monotherapy, and infliximab monotherapy outperformed azathioprine monotherapy.

**Adalimumab**
- CLASSIC I: Dose-ranging induction RCT in 299 TNF-antagonist-naive patients with moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease, randomized to adalimumab 40/20 mg, 80/40 mg, 160/80 mg, or placebo at weeks 0 and 2, with adalimumab superior to placebo for inducing remission.
- CHARM: 854 patients received open-label induction, and 778 responders were randomized to placebo, adalimumab 40 mg every other week, or 40 mg weekly through week 56; remission was significantly greater with adalimumab than placebo at both week 26 (40%, 47%, vs 17%) and week 56 (36%, 41%, vs 12%).
- GAIN (referenced in the review above): trial in patients who had lost response to or were intolerant of infliximab.

**Certolizumab pegol**
- PRECiSE 1: Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of certolizumab pegol vs placebo in 662 adults with moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease, stratified by baseline CRP, evaluating induction response.
- PRECiSE 2: A 26-week multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled maintenance trial in patients who had responded to open-label induction; 63% of certolizumab-treated patients met the primary response endpoint at week 26 versus 36% of placebo patients.
- PRECiSE 3 (long-term extension of PRECiSE 2 responders).

## Anti-integrin agents

**Vedolizumab**
- GEMINI 2: Integrated induction and maintenance trial; in the induction trial, 368 patients were randomly assigned to vedolizumab or placebo at weeks 0 and 2, and responders continuing vedolizumab were more likely to be in remission at week 52 than those switched to placebo.
- VISIBLE 2: Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial of a subcutaneous vedolizumab formulation as maintenance therapy; after open-label IV induction, week-6 responders were randomized 2:1 to subcutaneous vedolizumab or placebo, with 48.0% vs 34.3% in clinical remission at week 52.

## Anti-IL-12/23 agent

**Ustekinumab**
- UNITI-1, UNITI-2, and IM-UNITI: Two 8-week IV induction trials (UNITI-1 in patients with prior anti-TNF failure/intolerance, UNITI-2 in conventional-therapy failures) followed by a 44-week subcutaneous randomized-withdrawal maintenance trial (IM-UNITI) in induction responders, randomized to ustekinumab every 8 or 12 weeks or placebo; ustekinumab was superior to placebo for induction response and for maintenance remission at week 44.

## Anti-IL-23 agent

**Risankizumab**
- ADVANCE and MOTIVATE: Randomized, double-masked, placebo-controlled phase 3 induction studies; in MOTIVATE, clinical remission rates were 42% and 40% with risankizumab 600 mg and 1200 mg versus 20% with placebo.
- FORTIFY: Phase 3, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled withdrawal maintenance trial of subcutaneous risankizumab in patients who had responded to induction in ADVANCE or MOTIVATE.

## JAK inhibitor

**Upadacitinib**
- U-EXCEL and U-EXCEED (induction) and U-ENDURE (maintenance): In two phase 3 induction trials, patients with moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease were randomized 2:1 to upadacitinib 45 mg or placebo for 12 weeks; a significantly higher percentage achieved clinical remission (49.5% vs 29.1% in U-EXCEL; 38.9% vs 21.1% in U-EXCEED). In U-ENDURE, more patients had clinical remission at week 52 with upadacitinib 15 mg (37.3%) or 30 mg (47.6%) than placebo (15.1%); herpes zoster infections were more frequent with active drug, and hepatic disorders/neutropenia were more frequent with the 30-mg dose.

## Head-to-head comparative trials

- SEAVUE: Multicenter, randomized, blinded, parallel-group, active-controlled trial comparing ustekinumab to adalimumab through 1 year in biologic-naive patients with moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease; no significant difference was found in the primary outcome between the two drugs, with roughly 65% one-year clinical remission in each arm.
