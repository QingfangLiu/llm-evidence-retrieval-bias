## Scope

I prioritized **primary randomized controlled trials** of advanced therapies in adults with moderate-to-severe luminal Crohn’s disease. I included randomized-withdrawal maintenance studies and major head-to-head trials, plus natalizumab because it provides an important benefit–harm example. I did not use systematic reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, committee statements, or editorials as evidence.

Most older studies defined clinical remission as **CDAI <150**. Newer studies increasingly require patient-reported response plus endoscopic response or remission. Maintenance trials generally re-randomized **induction responders**, producing enriched populations whose remission percentages should not be compared directly across drugs.

## Direct comparative evidence

**Infliximab plus azathioprine versus monotherapy:** SONIC provides strong randomized evidence that combination therapy improves corticosteroid-free remission over infliximab alone in treatment-naive patients. However, its sample and duration cannot reliably quantify rare malignancies or opportunistic infections associated with prolonged dual immunosuppression. ([New England Journal of Medicine][2])

**Ustekinumab versus adalimumab:** In biologic-naive patients in SEAVUE, week-52 clinical remission was 65% with ustekinumab and 61% with adalimumab; the difference was not statistically significant. Serious infections occurred in 2% and 3%, respectively. ([PubMed][15])

**Risankizumab versus ustekinumab:** In anti-TNF-experienced patients in SEQUENCE, risankizumab was noninferior for week-24 clinical remission—58.6% versus 39.5%—and superior for week-48 endoscopic remission—31.8% versus 16.2%. Overall adverse-event incidence was similar. The open-label treatment assignment, despite blinded endpoint assessment, is an important limitation. ([PubMed][16])

**Guselkumab versus ustekinumab:** GALAXI-2/3 included a blinded ustekinumab arm and reported superiority of guselkumab across several week-48 clinical and endoscopic outcomes, with numerically fewer serious adverse events. These findings are recent and presently have less independent replication than the anti-TNF or ustekinumab evidence bases. ([PubMed][13])

## Overall interpretation

The clearest induction and maintenance benefits are demonstrated for anti-TNF agents, ustekinumab, selective IL-23 inhibitors, vedolizumab, and upadacitinib. The magnitude observed in individual trials depends heavily on prior biologic exposure, whether objective inflammation was required, endpoint timing, and whether the maintenance population was restricted to induction responders.

For comparative efficacy, the most informative primary studies are SONIC, SEAVUE, SEQUENCE, and GALAXI-2/3. They suggest:

* Combination infliximab–azathioprine can outperform infliximab alone in immunomodulator- and biologic-naive patients.
* Ustekinumab and adalimumab have similar one-year clinical-remission efficacy in biologic-naive disease.
* Risankizumab provides better endoscopic outcomes than ustekinumab after anti-TNF failure.
* Newer selective IL-23 inhibitors show substantial clinical and endoscopic benefit, but long-term and independent safety data are less mature.

For harms, efficacy RCTs mainly show common and short-term adverse events. They are poorly powered for tuberculosis, opportunistic infection, malignancy, cardiovascular events, thrombosis, or other rare outcomes. Natalizumab-associated PML is the clearest serious treatment-specific signal identified within this trial program. Upadacitinib shows a reproducible herpes-zoster signal and dose-related laboratory abnormalities; anti-TNF trials identify serious infections, tuberculosis, infusion/injection reactions, and immunogenicity as relevant concerns.
