## Anti-TNF agents

**Infliximab**
- The original phase 2 trial was a 12-week multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of infliximab in 108 patients with moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease resistant to standard treatment, establishing initial efficacy.
- ACCENT I, a larger maintenance trial, enrolled 573 patients with a CDAI score of at least 220 and found that scheduled infliximab infusions every 8 weeks produced significantly higher remission rates at week 30 than episodic/placebo dosing, with a similar safety profile across groups.
- A companion trial tested infliximab specifically for draining abdominal or perianal fistulas in 94 adults with Crohn's disease, in a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design.
- SONIC compared infliximab monotherapy, azathioprine monotherapy, and combination therapy in 508 treatment-naive adults with moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease, showing combination therapy outperformed either monotherapy.

**Adalimumab**
- CLASSIC I was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, dose-ranging trial in 299 anti-TNF-naive patients, finding adalimumab superior to placebo for induction of remission, with 160 mg/80 mg dosing identified as optimal and adalimumab well tolerated.
- CLASSIC II extended this as a maintenance follow-up.
- CHARM, a phase 3 maintenance trial, randomized 778 week-4 responders to placebo, adalimumab 40 mg every other week, or adalimumab weekly through week 56, finding remission rates significantly higher with active drug at both weeks 26 and 56.
- GAIN tested adalimumab in patients who had lost response to or could not tolerate infliximab.
- EXTEND assessed adalimumab's effect on endoscopic mucosal healing as a primary endpoint.

**Certolizumab pegol**
- PRECiSE 1 was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of 662 adults with moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease, comparing certolizumab pegol with placebo for induction.
- PRECiSE 2 randomized 428 induction responders to continued certolizumab pegol or placebo for maintenance, with PRECiSE 3 providing longer-term open-label follow-up.

**Natalizumab** (α4-integrin antagonist)
- ENACT-1 and ENACT-2 were randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled studies conducted at 142 centers, evaluating natalizumab as induction and maintenance therapy, with an earlier Pan-European placebo-controlled trial also establishing initial efficacy signals.

## Gut-selective and cytokine-targeted biologics

**Vedolizumab** (α4β7-integrin antagonist)
- GEMINI 2 was a phase 3, randomized, parallel-group, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial with an induction cohort of 368 patients and a maintenance trial of 461 responders randomized to placebo or vedolizumab every 4 or 8 weeks, and found vedolizumab-treated patients more likely to be in remission at week 6 than placebo, and responders who continued vedolizumab more likely to remain in remission at week 52; adverse events were more common with vedolizumab.
- VISIBLE 2 tested a subcutaneous vedolizumab formulation for maintenance, randomizing week-6 responders 2:1 to subcutaneous vedolizumab or placebo, with significantly higher clinical remission at week 52 in the vedolizumab group; injection site reaction was the only new safety signal.

**Ustekinumab** (IL-12/23 p40 inhibitor)
- UNITI-1 and UNITI-2 were 8-week induction trials, and IM-UNITI the maintenance trial. UNITI-1 included 741 patients with prior TNF-antagonist nonresponse or intolerance; UNITI-2 included 628 patients in whom conventional therapy had failed. Responders entered IM-UNITI, where 397 patients were randomized to ustekinumab every 8 or 12 weeks or placebo.

**Risankizumab** (IL-23 p19 inhibitor)
- ADVANCE and MOTIVATE were the induction trials; FORTIFY the maintenance trial. ADVANCE and MOTIVATE were randomized, double-masked, placebo-controlled induction trials, and FORTIFY was a phase 3, multicentre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, maintenance withdrawal study across 273 centers in 44 countries.

## Small-molecule (oral) advanced therapy

**Upadacitinib** (JAK1 inhibitor)
- U-EXCEL and U-EXCEED were induction trials; U-ENDURE the maintenance trial. In these two phase 3 induction trials, patients with moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease were randomized 2:1 to upadacitinib 45 mg or placebo for 12 weeks; responders then entered U-ENDURE, randomized to upadacitinib 15 mg, 30 mg, or placebo for 52 weeks. In U-EXCEL, significantly more patients achieved clinical remission (49.5% vs 29.1%) and endoscopic response (45.5% vs 13.1%) with upadacitinib versus placebo.

Across these trials, the general pattern of harms includes higher rates of infections (including opportunistic and serious infections), infusion or injection-site reactions, and — for JAK inhibitors specifically — additional monitoring concerns around herpes zoster, venous thromboembolism, and malignancy that have been highlighted in regulatory and safety literature (not itself covered here since that would draw on reviews/label data rather than primary RCTs).
