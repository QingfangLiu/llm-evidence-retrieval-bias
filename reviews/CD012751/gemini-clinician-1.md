As a clinician, relying on the foundational phase 3 clinical trials is the best way to cut through the noise and evaluate the actual efficacy and safety of these therapies.

When evaluating biologics and advanced small molecules for moderate-to-severe Crohn’s disease, the primary studies share similar endpoints—clinical remission and endoscopic response—but their distinct mechanisms of action result in very different safety profiles.

Here is a breakdown of the benefits and harms of the major therapeutic classes based strictly on their landmark primary trials, followed by the reference list you requested.

## Anti-TNF Therapy (e.g., Adalimumab)

* **Benefits:** In the CHARM trial, adalimumab demonstrated strong maintenance of remission. Among patients who responded to induction therapy, up to 41% maintained clinical remission at 56 weeks, compared to just 12% of patients on placebo (Colombel et al., 2007). Anti-TNFs remain highly effective, particularly for patients with penetrating or fistulizing phenotypes.
* **Harms:** The primary risks are systemic immunosuppression leading to opportunistic infections, injection-site reactions, and immunogenicity. The formation of anti-drug antibodies is a significant driver of secondary loss of response over time.

## Anti-Integrins (e.g., Vedolizumab)

* **Benefits:** The GEMINI 2 trial established vedolizumab as a highly effective agent for patients who failed either conventional therapies or anti-TNFs. Patients who continued on vedolizumab maintenance therapy were significantly more likely to achieve durable clinical remission at 52 weeks than those switched to placebo (Sandborn et al., 2013).
* **Harms:** Because vedolizumab is gut-selective (blocking the α4β7 integrin from entering the intestinal mucosa), it does not carry the same risk of systemic opportunistic infections or malignancies seen with systemic immunosuppressants. The most common adverse events were nasopharyngitis, headache, and arthralgia.

## IL-12/23 and Selective IL-23 Inhibitors (e.g., Ustekinumab, Risankizumab)

* **Benefits:** These classes offer deep tissue healing with a highly favorable safety profile. The UNITI trials showed that intravenous ustekinumab significantly outperformed placebo during induction, with subcutaneous maintenance successfully sustaining remission through week 44 (Feagan et al., 2016). For the selective IL-23 inhibitor risankizumab, the ADVANCE, MOTIVATE, and FORTIFY trials demonstrated high rates of early symptom improvement and sustained normalization of inflammatory biomarkers, even in patients who had previously failed other biologics (Colombel et al., 2023).
* **Harms:** Both ustekinumab and risankizumab boast excellent safety profiles with very low rates of immunogenicity. In their respective trials, the rates of severe adverse events and serious infections were generally comparable to placebo.

## Small Molecule JAK Inhibitors (e.g., Upadacitinib)

* **Benefits:** As an advanced oral therapy, upadacitinib achieves rapid clinical and endoscopic remission. In the U-ENDURE maintenance trial, 47.6% of patients on the 30-mg daily dose maintained clinical remission at week 52, compared to 15.1% on placebo (Loftus et al., 2023).
* **Harms:** Because they act systemically on Janus kinase pathways, JAK inhibitors carry boxed warnings. The trials noted numerically higher rates of herpes zoster (shingles), hepatic disorders, and neutropenia compared to placebo. Notably, gastrointestinal perforations occurred in a small number of treated patients (Loftus et al., 2023).

## Direct Head-to-Head Evidence

Active comparator trials are rare but highly valuable for treatment sequencing. The **SEAVUE trial** evaluated ustekinumab directly against adalimumab in biologic-naive patients. The study found no statistically significant difference in the primary endpoint: at week 52, 65% of patients on ustekinumab and 61% on adalimumab were in clinical remission (Sands et al., 2022). Both agents were highly effective, suggesting that first-line choices can be driven by patient preference (e.g., oral/subcutaneous vs. intravenous) and the respective safety profiles.
