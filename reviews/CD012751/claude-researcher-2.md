## Overview

The evidence base for biologic/advanced therapies in Crohn's disease induction and maintenance of remission spans several drug classes, each supported by dedicated phase 3 (and some phase 2) randomized, double-blind, placebo- or active-controlled trials:

- **Anti-TNF agents**: infliximab (ACCENT I), adalimumab (CLASSIC I/II, CHARM, GAIN), certolizumab pegol (PRECiSE 1/2)
- **Anti-integrin agents**: natalizumab (ENACT-1/2), vedolizumab (GEMINI 2, VISIBLE 2)
- **Anti-IL-12/23**: ustekinumab (UNITI-1, UNITI-2, IM-UNITI)
- **Anti-IL-23 (p19)**: risankizumab (ADVANCE, MOTIVATE, FORTIFY), mirikizumab (VIVID-1), guselkumab (GALAXI-2/3, phase 2 GALAXI-1)
- **JAK inhibitor**: upadacitinib (U-EXCEL, U-EXCEED, U-ENDURE)
- **Head-to-head active-comparator trial**: SEAVUE (ustekinumab vs. adalimumab)

Across classes, active drugs generally outperform placebo for induction and maintenance of clinical remission, with response magnitude varying by prior biologic exposure (treatment-naive patients tend to respond better than those with prior biologic failure). Safety profiles differ meaningfully: anti-TNFs carry infection/immunogenicity risks, natalizumab carries a PML risk (largely why it's rarely used now), vedolizumab has a favorable gut-selective safety profile, and JAK inhibitors (upadacitinib) carry class warnings (infection, malignancy, thrombosis, cardiovascular risk) per boxed labeling. The one direct head-to-head trial (SEAVUE) found ustekinumab and adalimumab comparably effective and safe in biologic-naive patients.
