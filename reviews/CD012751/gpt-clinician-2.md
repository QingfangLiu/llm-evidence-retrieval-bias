## Scope

This is a focused evidence map of **individual randomized primary studies** in adults with moderately to severely active luminal Crohn’s disease, current through **July 18, 2026**. I excluded systematic reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, opinion pieces, pediatric studies, postoperative prophylaxis studies, and trials primarily addressing fistula closure.

The percentages below should not be used for naïve cross-trial ranking. Older studies generally used **CDAI remission**, newer studies increasingly require patient-reported and endoscopic outcomes, and most maintenance trials rerandomized only patients who had already responded to induction.

## Direct comparative evidence

**Infliximab plus azathioprine:** In SONIC, biologic- and immunomodulator-naïve patients achieved corticosteroid-free remission at week 26 in **56.8% with infliximab plus azathioprine, 44.4% with infliximab alone, and 30.0% with azathioprine alone**. Mucosal healing was **43.9%, 30.1%, and 16.5%**. Serious infections occurred in **3.9%, 4.9%, and 5.6%**, respectively. This establishes an efficacy advantage for combination therapy in this specific, treatment-naïve population, but does not resolve longer-term malignancy tradeoffs. ([PubMed][19])

**Ustekinumab versus adalimumab:** In biologic-naïve patients in SEAVUE, week-52 clinical remission was **65% with ustekinumab and 61% with adalimumab**, with no significant difference. Serious infections occurred in **2% and 3%**, respectively. ([PubMed][20])

**Risankizumab versus ustekinumab:** In anti-TNF-experienced patients in SEQUENCE, risankizumab was noninferior for week-24 clinical remission—**58.6% versus 39.5%**—and superior for week-48 endoscopic remission—**31.8% versus 16.2%**. Overall adverse-event incidence was similar. This is one of the strongest randomized head-to-head results favoring one biologic for objective endoscopic disease control, although the trial was open-label with blinded endpoint assessment. ([New England Journal of Medicine][21])

**Guselkumab versus ustekinumab:** GALAXI 2 and 3 included an active ustekinumab comparator and reported superiority of guselkumab on several week-48 clinical and endoscopic endpoints. Because the trials used hierarchical testing and composite endpoints rather than a single conventional maintenance-remission comparison, the individual endpoint definitions should be reviewed before applying the results as a simple class ranking. ([PubMed][16])

## Practical interpretation

The largest placebo-adjusted induction effects in the pivotal studies were seen with the oral JAK inhibitor upadacitinib and newer IL-23p19 agents, particularly when objective endoscopic endpoints were examined. However, cross-trial differences in prior biologic exposure, placebo response, endpoint definitions, timing, steroid tapering, and handling of rescue therapy prevent a defensible numerical ranking from placebo-controlled studies alone. ([PubMed][8])

Ustekinumab, vedolizumab, and selective IL-23 agents generally had placebo-like short-term overall safety profiles in their pivotal trials. Anti-TNF trials identified infections, immunogenicity, and administration reactions but were not adequately powered for uncommon complications. Upadacitinib produced a more recognizable trial-level signal for herpes zoster and laboratory abnormalities, with occasional gastrointestinal perforation. Natalizumab’s PML risk is qualitatively different and substantially limits its role. ([New England Journal of Medicine][7])

“No significant difference” in serious infection, malignancy, MACE, or VTE should not be interpreted as proof of no risk. Randomized Crohn’s trials usually enrolled hundreds rather than tens of thousands of patients and lasted approximately 12–52 weeks, making them poorly powered for rare or delayed adverse outcomes.
