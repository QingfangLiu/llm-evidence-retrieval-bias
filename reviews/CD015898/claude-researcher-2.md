## Summary of findings

The evidence base on **amount of RBCs given per transfusion** (single- vs multi-unit protocols, or mL/kg dosing) is much smaller than the threshold literature, and clusters into a few clinical populations:

**Adults — single-unit vs. multiple-unit (double-unit) protocols**
- Obstetric/postpartum anemia: a pragmatic RCT found single-unit dosing led to fewer total units transfused with no difference in clinical outcomes, though post-transfusion hemoglobin was lower.
- Hematology/oncology (chemotherapy, stem-cell transplant): a French multicenter non-inferiority RCT (the "1versus2" trial) tested single- vs double-unit transfusion policies against complication rates and total units used.
- A related Canadian pragmatic RCT (OTTOP) tested one- vs two-unit transfusion in oncology outpatients, primarily on transfusion-episode frequency.

**Neonates/infants — transfusion volume (mL/kg)**
- A RCT in very-low-birth-weight preterm infants compared 10 vs 20 mL/kg per transfusion, assessing hemoglobin rise and pulmonary function.

**Children with severe anemia (low-resource settings) — transfusion volume (mL/kg)**
- TRACT trial (Uganda/Malawi): a large factorial RCT comparing 20 vs 30 mL/kg whole-blood-equivalent, with 28-day mortality as the primary endpoint.
- An earlier phase II RCT in Ugandan children compared 20 vs 30 mL/kg whole blood for 24-hour anemia correction and safety.

**Note on cord-blood and platelet "unit" trials:** I found single- vs double-unit RCTs for cord-blood stem-cell transplantation and for platelet transfusion, but excluded these — they are different blood products/interventions, not RBC transfusion dosing.

Given how scattered this literature is across obstetrics, hematology-oncology, neonatology, and pediatric global health, a broader multi-database search (MEDLINE, Embase, CENTRAL, trial registries) would likely surface additional smaller or older single- vs. multi-unit trials I haven't found via web search — useful if you're aiming for systematic-review-level completeness.
