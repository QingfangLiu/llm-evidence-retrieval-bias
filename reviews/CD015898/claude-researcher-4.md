Based on these searches, here is a working list of individual primary studies addressing the amount (single- vs. multiple-unit / dose per episode) of red blood cell transfusion in hospitalized patients. These are trials, cohort studies, and observational studies — not reviews, guidelines, or opinion pieces.

**Randomized controlled trials**

1. Hamm RF, Perelman S, Wang EY, Levine LD, Srinivas SK. *Single-unit vs multiple-unit transfusion in hemodynamically stable postpartum anemia: a pragmatic randomized controlled trial.* Am J Obstet Gynecol. 2021;224(1):84.e1-84.e7. (PMID 32652065) — RCT, n=66, postpartum anemia.

2. SMaRT Blood trial (NCT03419780) — RCT of single- vs multiple-unit pRBC transfusion in non-acute postpartum anemia, University of Pennsylvania.

3. "1versus2" trial — RCT of single- vs double-unit transfusion policy in hematology intensive care (acute leukemia, autologous/allogeneic HSCT patients), reported as: *Effect of single-unit transfusion in patients treated for haematological disease including acute leukemia: A multicenter randomized controlled clinical trial* (ScienceDirect, 2023) and protocol paper in *Trials*/PMC4655487 ("Transfusion strategy in hematological intensive care unit: study protocol for a randomized controlled trial"); also registered as NCT02461264.

4. OTTOP Trial (NCT04544540) — "One Versus Two Unit Transfusions in Oncology Patients": randomized, open-label, pragmatic trial of 1-unit vs 2-unit outpatient RBC transfusion in oncology patients.

**Cohort / observational studies**

5. Webert KE et al. (or corresponding authors) — *Significant reduction of red blood cell transfusion requirements by changing from a double-unit to a single-unit transfusion policy in patients receiving intensive chemotherapy or stem cell transplantation.* (PMID 21933858) — retrospective cohort study.

6. Robinson SD et al. — *Successful Implementation of a Packed Red Blood Cell and Fresh Frozen Plasma Transfusion Protocol in the Surgical Intensive Care Unit* (PMC4444010) — retrospective pre/post cohort study comparing restrictive (largely single-unit) protocol vs prior practice.

7. *Transfusion of 1 and 2 Units of Red Blood Cells Is Associated With Increased Morbidity and Mortality.* Ann Thorac Surg. 2014 — large multicenter propensity-matched cohort study of isolated CABG patients (Michigan), comparing 1–2 unit transfusion vs no transfusion.

8. *Effect of dilutional anemia that can be treated with only one unit of red blood cell transfusion on tissue oxygenation in cardiac surgery patients* (PMC7018202) — prospective observational study comparing minimally transfused (1 unit) vs non-transfused cardiac surgery patients.

9. Huynh N, Dayal S, Sama S, et al. *Inpatient Blood Transfusion Usage at a Community Veterans Affairs Teaching Hospital* (ScienceDirect, 2024) — retrospective chart-review cohort study of inpatient transfusion practice ahead of single-unit protocol implementation.

10. *Red blood cell transfusion ≥ 2 units is a risk factor of long-term survival of infectious endocarditis patients with surgical intervention* (PMC12599631) — retrospective cohort study comparing <2 vs ≥2 unit RBC transfusion.
