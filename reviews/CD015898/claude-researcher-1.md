Based on my searches, here are the individual primary studies I found that directly compare **larger vs. smaller amounts of red blood cells given per transfusion episode** (RBC dose/volume per transfusion — not hemoglobin trigger/threshold trials) in hospitalized patients. I've grouped them by population and noted design.

## Neonates / preterm infants
1. **Paul DA, Leef KH, Locke RG, Stefano JL (2002).** *Transfusion volume in infants with very low birth weight: a randomized trial of 10 versus 20 mL/kg.* J Pediatr Hematol Oncol 24:43-6. — RCT of 10 vs 20 mL/kg PRBC.
2. **Wong W et al. (2005).** *A comparison of high and standard blood transfusion volumes in premature infants.* Acta Paediatr. — RCT, 20 mL/kg (high) vs 15 mL/kg (standard).
3. **Mallett LH et al. (2016).** *Safety and efficacy of packed red blood cell transfusions at different doses in very low birth weight infants.* Proc (Bayl Univ Med Cent). — Double-blind randomized crossover trial, 20 vs 15 mL/kg.
4. **[Author, Chandigarh group] (2022).** *Comparison of Hematocrit Change in Preterm Neonates with Birth Weight Based Versus Formula Based Packed Red Blood Cell Transfusion: A Randomized Control Trial.* Indian J Hematol Blood Transfus. — RCT, 15 mL/kg fixed vs. formula-calculated (larger) volume.

## Children (severe anemia, low/middle-income setting)
5. **Olupot-Olupot P et al. (2014).** *Phase II trial of standard versus increased transfusion volume in Ugandan children with acute severe anemia.* BMC Med 12:67. — RCT, 20 mL/kg vs 30 mL/kg whole blood (or 10 vs 15 mL/kg packed cells).
6. **Maitland K et al., TRACT Group (2019).** *Transfusion Volume for Children with Severe Anemia in Africa.* N Engl J Med 381:420-431. — Large multicenter factorial RCT, 20 vs 30 mL/kg whole-blood equivalent; primary endpoint 28-day mortality.
7. **[TRACT secondary analysis] (2022).** *Whole blood versus red cell concentrates for children with severe anaemia: a secondary analysis of the TRACT trial.* — Secondary analysis examining pack type and volume effects from the TRACT RCT data.

## Children with transfusion-dependent thalassemia
8. **Kaur J et al. (2022).** *Efficacy of packed red blood cell transfusions based on weight versus formula in thalassemic children: An open-label randomized control trial.* Transfusion. — RCT comparing weight-based vs. formula-based (differing) PRBC volumes.

## Adults (mixed hospitalized populations)
9. **[Turkish group] (2017).** *A Randomized Comparison of Hemoglobin Content-Based Versus Standard (Unit-Based) Red Blood Cell Transfusion Policy.* — RCT/crossover-episode design comparing Hb-content-based (variable, often smaller) dosing vs. standard unit-based dosing.

## Adults — single-unit vs. double/multi-unit per transfusion episode (hematology/oncology)
10. **DeZern AE, Williams K, Zahurak M, et al. (2016).** *Red blood cell transfusion triggers in acute leukemia: a randomized pilot study.* Transfusion 56:1750-1757. — Randomized pilot comparing standard (2-unit) vs. augmented/single-unit-based strategies.
11. **Chantepie S, Mear JB, Briant AR, et al. (2021).** *Single-Unit Transfusion Is Non-Inferior to Double-Unit Transfusion... The "1versus2" Prospective Multicentric Randomized Clinical Trial.* Blood 138(Suppl 1):2141 (conference abstract of phase 3 RCT). — RCT, 1-unit vs 2-unit RBC transfusion in leukemia/HSCT patients.
12. **[SMaRT Blood investigators].** *SMaRT Blood: Single-unit Versus Multiple-unit Packed Red Blood Cell Transfusion in Non-acute Postpartum Anemia* (NCT03419780). — RCT, 1 vs 2 units per transfusion episode, postpartum anemia.
13. **OTTOP Trial investigators.** *One Versus Two Unit Transfusions in Oncology Patients – The OTTOP Trial: A Randomized Open-label Pragmatic Controlled Trial* (NCT04544540). — RCT, 1-unit vs 2-unit outpatient transfusion in oncology patients.

## Note on quasi-experimental / non-randomized primary studies (included for completeness, as they are primary data, not reviews)
14. **[Haematologica group].** *Significant reduction of red blood cell transfusion requirements by changing from a double-unit to a single-unit transfusion policy in patients receiving intensive chemotherapy or stem cell transplantation.* Haematologica. — Before-after (quasi-experimental) cohort study, not randomized — flagging in case your synthesis includes NRSI, since the ongoing Cochrane review on this exact topic explicitly includes non-randomized studies of interventions.

A few caveats worth flagging for your synthesis:
- I excluded the **Cochrane review (Roumeliotis et al., 2024, "Larger versus smaller red blood cell volume per transfusion in hospitalized adults, children, and preterm neonates")** since it's a systematic review — but it's directly on your topic and its reference list/included-studies table would be a highly efficient way to cross-check completeness once published.
- **Restrictive vs. liberal transfusion trials** (Hébert TRICC 1999, Villanueva 2013, Carson FOCUS 2006, etc.) test *hemoglobin thresholds* for whether to transfuse, not the *volume given per transfusion event* — I excluded these as off-target for your specific question, though they're easy to conflate with it.
- Several items above are trial registrations/protocols or conference abstracts rather than completed full-text publications (e.g., OTTOP, SMaRT Blood, Chantepie abstract) — worth checking for a completed peer-reviewed report before including in a synthesis.

Given the number of scattered primary studies across very different populations (neonates, African children, thalassemia, oncology/hematology adults, postpartum), a broader systematic search (MEDLINE/Embase/CENTRAL with a validated RCT filter) would likely surface additional smaller trials I couldn't fully capture via web search alone — happy to run further targeted searches on any specific subpopulation if useful.
