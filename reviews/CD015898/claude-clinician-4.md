Here's a search of the primary literature on RBC "dose per transfusion episode" — i.e., transfusing one unit at a time with reassessment vs. transfusing two (or more) units up front. This is a distinct question from restrictive vs. liberal *hemoglobin thresholds*, and the trials/cohorts below isolate volume-per-episode specifically. I excluded reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, and editorials/commentaries as requested.

## What the primary studies show

**Blood utilization / exposure reduction**
Every study that compared single-unit to multi-unit ("double-unit") protocols found the single-unit approach substantially reduced total RBC units transfused per patient/admission — typically 20–50% fewer units — without needing a second unit in the majority of cases. This held in hematologic malignancy/chemotherapy patients, postpartum anemia, general medical inpatients, and orthopedic/mixed medical-surgical cohorts.

**Efficacy / need for retransfusion**
In the postpartum RCT, only ~18–20% of single-unit patients needed a second unit. In hematologic malignancy RCTs (1versus2 trial) and retrospective cohorts, single-unit strategies were non-inferior on complication rates, though patients had somewhat lower post-transfusion/discharge hemoglobin and more frequent (but smaller) transfusion episodes — meaning slightly more nursing/lab workload but not more clinical harm.

**Safety/harms**
Across the RCTs and cohort studies, no increase was seen in mortality, ICU transfer, cardiac events, length of stay, readmission, or infection rates with single-unit vs. multi-unit strategies. The main "cost" identified was logistical: more transfusion episodes, slightly more blood draws, and modestly lower discharge hemoglobin.

**Gaps**
Most of the highest-quality randomized evidence comes from hematology/oncology and postpartum populations; general medical/surgical ward RCT data are sparser (mostly retrospective cohorts or before-after implementation studies), and evidence in children or critically ill patients specifically randomizing *dose per transfusion* (rather than threshold) is very limited.

## Primary studies

1. **Hamm RF, Perelman S, Wang EY, Levine LD, Srinivas SK.** Single-unit vs multiple-unit transfusion in hemodynamically stable postpartum anemia: a pragmatic randomized controlled trial. *Am J Obstet Gynecol.* 2021;224(1):84.e1–84.e7.

2. **Chantepie S, Mear JB, Briant AR, et al.** Single-Unit Transfusion Is Non-Inferior to Double Unit Transfusion in Patients with Hematological Disorders Receiving Allogeneic or Autologous Bone Marrow Transplant or Induction Chemotherapy for Acute Leukemia: The 1versus2 Prospective Multicentric Randomized Clinical Trial. *Blood.* 2021;138(Suppl 1):2141. (Phase 3 RCT)

3. **Berger MD, Gerber B, Arn K, Senn O, Schanz U, Stussi G.** Significant reduction of red blood cell transfusion requirements by changing from a double-unit to a single-unit transfusion policy in patients receiving intensive chemotherapy or stem cell transplantation. *Haematologica.* 2012;97(1):116–122. (Retrospective cohort)

4. **Bowman Z, Fei N, Ahn J, Wen S, Cumpston A, Shah N, et al.** Single versus double-unit transfusion: Safety and efficacy for patients with hematologic malignancies. *Eur J Haematol.* 2019;102(5):383–388. (Retrospective cohort)

5. **Ma M, Eckert K, Ralley F, Chin-Yee I.** A retrospective study evaluating single-unit red blood cell transfusions in reducing allogeneic blood exposure. *Transfus Med.* 2005;15(4):307–312.

6. **Yang WW, Thakkar RN, Gehrie EA, Chen W, Frank SM.** Single-unit transfusions and hemoglobin trigger: relative impact on red cell utilization. *Transfusion.* 2017;57(5):1163–1170. (Before-after PBM implementation study)

7. **Heyes J, Kelly PA, Monaghan K, Lawn M, Dhesi A, Mijovic A.** A single unit transfusion policy reduces red cell transfusions in general medical in-patients. *QJM.* 2017;110(11):735–739.

8. **Warner MA, Schaefer KK, Madde N, Burt JM, Higgins AA, Kor DJ.** Improvements in red blood cell transfusion utilization following implementation of a single-unit default for electronic ordering. *Transfusion.* 2019;59(7):2218–2222.

9. **Or (Ben David) I, et al.** Single vs. two-unit transfusion in postpartum anemia: identifying predictors in a retrospective cohort. *J Matern Fetal Neonatal Med.* 2025.
