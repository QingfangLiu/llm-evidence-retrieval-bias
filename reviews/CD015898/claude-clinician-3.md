## Summary of findings by population

**Preterm/neonatal patients**
- Paul et al. (2002) randomized VLBW infants to 10 vs 20 mL/kg per transfusion: the larger volume produced a significantly greater rise in hemoglobin/hematocrit with no measured difference in pulmonary function.
- A more recent RCT (Indian J Hematol Blood Transfus, 2021) compared a fixed 15 mL/kg dose to a formula-based (hematocrit-targeted) dose in preterm neonates, looking at post-transfusion hematocrit rise and short-term neonatal morbidities.

**Children with severe anemia (low-resource/malaria-endemic settings)**
- The TRACT trial (Maitland et al., NEJM 2019) — a large factorial RCT in Uganda/Malawi — randomized children with severe anemia to 20 vs 30 mL/kg whole-blood-equivalent volumes, examining 28-day mortality and other outcomes.
- A preceding Phase II trial (BMC Medicine, 2014) in Ugandan children tested standard (20 mL/kg) vs increased (30 mL/kg) whole blood volume, finding better hemoglobin recovery and fewer repeat transfusions with the larger volume.
- A secondary analysis of TRACT (Lancet Global Health, 2022) examined pack type (whole blood vs red cell concentrate) effects within the volume-randomized cohort.

**Adults — single-unit vs multi-unit per transfusion episode**
- A pragmatic RCT in postpartum anemia (Am J Obstet Gynecol, 2020) randomized women to a single-unit vs two-unit initial transfusion protocol; the single-unit strategy reduced total units transfused without differences in complications or length of stay.
- The "1versus2" trial (hematology/oncology patients undergoing chemotherapy or HSCT) randomized single- vs double-unit transfusion per episode, with grade ≥3 complications as the primary non-inferiority endpoint (protocol published 2015; results presented at ASH 2021).
- A related non-randomized (pre/post) single-center study (Haematologica, 2012) compared outcomes before and after a hospital shifted from a double-unit to single-unit default policy in hematology patients — useful supporting evidence but not itself randomized.

Note: I excluded hemoglobin-threshold trials (restrictive vs liberal trigger for *starting* a transfusion, e.g., TRICC, FOCUS, TRISS, Villanueva GI bleed trial) because those address *when* to transfuse, not *how much* to give per transfusion — a distinction your Cochrane protocol search also flagged explicitly.
