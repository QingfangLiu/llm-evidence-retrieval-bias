Here's what I found searching for primary studies (RCTs and comparative cohort studies) specifically on the **volume/dose transfused per transfusion event** (e.g., single- vs double-unit, or mL/kg dosing), as opposed to studies of transfusion *threshold* (restrictive vs liberal, i.e., when to transfuse). I've grouped them by population since the evidence base differs substantially.

## Preterm/neonatal patients (mL/kg per transfusion)

- A crossover RCT in very low birth weight infants found that transfusion with 20 mL/kg produced a significantly greater increase in hemoglobin and hematocrit than 10 mL/kg, without detrimental effects on pulmonary function.
- A double-blinded crossover RCT comparing 15 vs 20 mL/kg aliquots in VLBW infants evaluated safety and efficacy of the two doses directly in the same infants.
- A small RCT comparing high-volume (20 mL/kg) vs standard-volume (15 mL/kg) transfusions found no adverse events attributed to the higher-volume transfusions, and the high-volume group trended toward fewer total transfusions.
- A partially blinded RCT in infants <32 weeks gestation comparing 15 vs 20 mL/kg found no difference in cerebral or intestinal tissue oxygenation between volumes, though hematocrit rise and number of subsequent transfusions differed.
- An RCT comparing weight-based (15 mL/kg) vs hematocrit-formula-based dosing in preterm neonates found the formula-based group received a larger mean volume (29.6 vs 18.8 mL) and had a significantly greater rise in hematocrit, with comparable secondary outcomes.

## Adults with hematologic malignancy (single- vs double-unit)

- **Chantepie et al. (Leukemia Research, 2023)** — the "1versus2" multicenter RCT: 230 adults undergoing chemotherapy for acute leukemia or HSCT were randomized to single-unit vs double-unit transfusion whenever hemoglobin fell below 8 g/dL, testing non-inferiority of the single-unit strategy for severe complications, with number of units transfused as a key secondary outcome.
- **Berger et al. (Haematologica, 2012)** — a retrospective cohort study of patients on intensive chemotherapy/HSCT before-and-after a policy change from double- to single-unit transfusion: single-unit transfusion reduced RBC usage by 25% per therapy cycle and 24% per aplasia day, with no evidence of more severe bleeding, more platelet transfusions, or worse survival.
- **Bowman et al. (European Journal of Haematology, 2019)** — a comparative cohort study in patients with hematologic malignancies: the single-unit group received fewer total RBC units per admission but had more separate transfusion episodes per admission than the double-unit group.

## Obstetric patients (postpartum anemia)

- **Hamm et al. (American Journal of Obstetrics & Gynecology, 2021)** — a pragmatic RCT of 66 women with hemodynamically stable postpartum anemia randomized to single- vs double-unit initial transfusion: the single-unit protocol resulted in fewer total units transfused (1.2 vs 2.1 U), with only 18.2% needing a second unit, and no differences in length of stay, 30-day complications, or vital signs/symptoms, though post-transfusion hemoglobin was lower in the single-unit arm.

## Trauma (volume per transfusion, restrictive vs liberal targets — closest primary evidence)

- **RESTRIC trial (Hayakawa et al.)** — a cluster-randomized crossover non-inferiority trial in 22 Japanese trauma centers comparing target hemoglobin ranges (7–9 vs 10–12 g/dL), which drove differences in per-episode transfusion volume: the restrictive strategy resulted in lower transfusion volume, with similar 28-day survival and complication rates between groups, though statistical non-inferiority was not formally demonstrated.

A note on scope: most of the classic "restrictive vs liberal" trials (TRICC, MINT, etc.) set a *hemoglobin trigger* rather than testing a specific *volume per transfusion event*, so I excluded those unless they directly manipulated per-transfusion dose. I also excluded ongoing/unpublished registrations (e.g., the OTTOP oncology single- vs double-unit trial, NCT04544540) since they have no results yet — let me know if you'd like those flagged separately for tracking.
