Here are several key primary studies (specifically, randomized controlled trials) examining the efficacy of nonsurgical treatments for the two most common lower-limb apophysitis conditions in pediatric patients: calcaneal apophysitis (Sever's disease) and tibial tubercle apophysitis (Osgood-Schlatter disease).

## Calcaneal Apophysitis (Sever's Disease)

Sever's disease is the most frequent cause of heel pain in growing children, occurring at the growth plate of the calcaneus. The primary trials investigating conservative treatments reveal a consistent theme: most non-surgical interventions are effective, but highly specialized options do not consistently outperform simpler ones.

* **Treatment Modality Comparisons:** A pragmatic randomized trial by Wiegerinck et al. (2016) compared a "wait-and-see" approach, heel raise inlays, and physiotherapist-supervised eccentric exercises. They found that all three modalities significantly reduced heel pain over a 10-week period, but no single treatment demonstrated clinical superiority over the others.
* **Orthoses vs. Heel Lifts:** When looking strictly at footwear modifications, results are nuanced. Alfaro-Santafé et al. (2021) found that while pain perception improved with both interventions, children wearing custom-made polypropylene foot orthoses showed a statistically greater clinical improvement than those using off-the-shelf heel-lifts. Conversely, James et al. (2016) conducted a factorial randomized trial comparing prefabricated orthoses to simple heel raises. They found a relative functional advantage for the heel raises at the 2-month mark, though by 12 months, no treatment option maintained superiority.
* **Barefoot Athlete Bracing:** For children participating in sports that preclude traditional footwear (e.g., gymnastics, martial arts), managing heel pain is particularly challenging. Sweeney et al. (2023) compared two types of wearable braces designed for barefoot athletes. Both brace types facilitated functional improvements in the foot and ankle over a 3-month period, with neither brace proving statistically superior to the other.

## Tibial Tubercle Apophysitis (Osgood-Schlatter Disease)

Osgood-Schlatter disease involves traction apophysitis at the insertion of the patellar tendon on the tibial tuberosity. While conservative treatments like rest, ice, and NSAIDs are the standard of care, interventional approaches have been trialed for recalcitrant cases.

* **Injectable Therapies:** Nakase et al. (2019) conducted a prospective, randomized double-blind study testing the efficacy of 20% dextrose injections (prolotherapy) against placebo saline injections for patients whose symptoms had not responded to conservative therapy. While both groups experienced a significant increase in functional scores at the 1-month follow-up, the dextrose injections failed to show any therapeutic superiority over the placebo saline injections.
