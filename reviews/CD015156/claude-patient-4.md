Here's a survey of individual primary studies (RCTs, cohort studies, and case series/reports) on nonsurgical treatment of lower-limb apophysitis in children and adolescents, organized by site. I excluded reviews, meta-analyses, and guidelines as you asked — those only show up below when I'm citing them for background facts, not as "evidence" studies themselves.

**Knee — Osgood-Schlatter disease (tibial tuberosity apophysitis)**

- A large Danish trial randomized 130 adolescents with long-standing Osgood-Schlatter pain to either usual care or a structured self-management program combining exercise, education, and activity modification over three months, using knee-related sport function as the primary outcome, comparing self-management including exercise, education, and activity modification against usual care over three months, with knee-related sport function as the primary outcome.
- A prospective cohort study by Rathleff and colleagues tested a 12-week program combining an activity-modification "ladder," knee-strengthening exercises, and gradual return to sport in 51 adolescents with Osgood-Schlatter, reporting it as level-4 case-series evidence.
- A retrospective cohort study of young professional soccer players compared two conservative treatment protocols and tracked pain and ultrasound findings over a 1-month treatment period with 3-month follow-up, finding both approaches reduced pain severity within a month, with significant between-group differences appearing after treatment.
- A double-blind RCT tested hyperosmolar dextrose injections against a comparator for Osgood-Schlatter pain (Wu et al., 2021).
- Two large case-series studies (152 and 150 patients) by Guszczyn et al. evaluated leukocyte-rich platelet-rich plasma (LR-PRP) injections added to standard conservative care, reporting pain and function outcomes on VAS, Lysholm, Tegner, and KOOS scales, with 75% of subjects satisfied with treatment and 72% returning to full physical activity, and a follow-up study comparing outcomes by disease duration (acute vs. chronic).
- A retrospective study directly compared a single PRP injection against a three-session 10% dextrose prolotherapy series in adolescents with refractory Osgood-Schlatter over 12 weeks, since comparative evidence between these two regenerative approaches in this population had been limited.
- A case report described autologous-conditioned plasma treatment in two patients with Osgood-Schlatter disease.
- A Russian study evaluated osteopathic manual treatment in the acute stage of Osgood-Schlatter disease in male athletes aged 10–15.

**Heel — Sever's disease (calcaneal apophysitis)**

- Perhamre and colleagues published a series of controlled studies: an initial trial showing insoles provide effective pain relief; a randomized crossover trial comparing a heel cup against a heel wedge, in which most boys preferred the heel cupwith more than 75% of the boys preferring the heel cup; and a follow-up mechanistic study in 50 children showing the heel cup improved heel-pad thickness and reduced peak pressurewith these effects correlating to a significant reduction in pain when the heel cup was used in a sports shoe.
- A 12-month factorial RCT (James, Williams & Haines, 2016) in 124 Australian children compared footwear advice and foot orthoses, finding heel inserts and prefabricated orthotics improved pain scores initially, but that patients improved equally with or without therapy by three months.
- Wiegerinck and colleagues (2016) ran a pragmatic RCT comparing "wait and see," an orthotic device, and physical therapy for calcaneal apophysitis.
- Alfaro-Santafé and colleagues conducted a CONSORT-compliant RCT of 208 children comparing custom-made foot orthoses against off-the-shelf heel-lifts over 12 weeks, finding both groups improved but that children using custom orthoses showed significantly greater gains in pain and pressure-pain threshold.
- Sweeney and colleagues (2023) ran an RCT in 32 barefoot athletes (mostly gymnasts) comparing two braces — a heel cup and an "X Brace" — over three months, finding both groups improved over time with no significant between-group differences, though pain scores dropped significantly by 2–3 months.
- Kuyucu and colleagues (2017) studied kinesiotaping's effect on ankle dorsiflexion and pain in male athletes with calcaneal apophysitis.
- A case series (Bourke et al.) compared custom orthotics against heel lifts at 12-week follow-up.
- A small case series examined extracorporeal shockwave therapy (ESWT) in seven athletes with calcaneal apophysitis.

**Hip/pelvis — apophysitis (iliac crest, etc.)**

- A published case report ("Treatment of iliac crest apophysitis in the young athlete with bone stimulation: report of 2 cases," *Clinical Journal of Sport Medicine*) is the only nonsurgical-treatment-focused primary study I could find specific to hip apophysitis pain rather than avulsion fracture; most hip literature concerns avulsion injuries (a related but distinct condition) rather than apophysitis pain itself, and is managed similarly with rest, activity modification, and graded return to sport.

**Sinding-Larsen-Johansson syndrome (inferior patellar pole apophysitis)**
I could not find a completed primary treatment study (RCT, cohort, or case series with outcomes data) specific to nonsurgical treatment of this condition — the available literature here is case reports on diagnosis/imaging or narrative/scoping reviews, which you asked me to exclude.
