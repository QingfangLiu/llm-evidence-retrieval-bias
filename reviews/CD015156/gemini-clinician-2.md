Managing apophysitis in active children and adolescents can be challenging, especially when balancing the need for tissue healing with their desire to stay active in sports. Focusing strictly on primary studies (rather than reviews or guidelines), the clinical literature evaluates a range of conservative treatments—from specialized orthotics to structured load management—to address the repetitive traction that drives both Sever's and Osgood-Schlatter diseases.

Here is a breakdown of what the primary literature says about the efficacy of nonsurgical treatments.

## Calcaneal Apophysitis (Sever's Disease)

For heel pain related to the calcaneal apophysis, the evidence suggests that several standard modalities are effective, though custom interventions may provide superior pain relief.

* **Standard Conservative Modalities:** A pragmatic randomized clinical trial evaluated three common approaches: a "wait-and-see" protocol, a prefabricated heel-raise orthotic, and physical therapy focused on eccentric exercises (Wiegerinck et al., 2016). The researchers found that all three treatments successfully yielded clinically relevant and statistically significant reductions in heel pain over time (Wiegerinck et al., 2016). Ultimately, no single intervention proved fundamentally superior to the others, suggesting that clinicians can factor patient preference and cost into their treatment choices.
* **Custom vs. Off-the-Shelf Orthotics:** When specifically looking at orthotic interventions, custom devices may outperform generic ones. A randomized trial compared the effectiveness of custom-made polypropylene foot orthoses against standard off-the-shelf heel-lifts (Alfaro-Santafé et al., 2021). The results demonstrated that children using the custom-made orthoses experienced significantly greater pain relief compared to those using the standard heel-lifts (Alfaro-Santafé et al., 2021).

## Osgood-Schlatter Disease (OSD)

Management of OSD focuses on addressing the traction forces on the tibial tuberosity, primarily through load management and, in more stubborn cases, regenerative medicine.

* **Activity Modification and Strengthening:** Because repetitive overuse and high volumes of sport participation are key drivers of OSD, conservative management has shifted toward structured loading rather than complete rest. A prospective cohort study investigated an intervention targeting these specific factors, utilizing a combination of patient education on activity modification and progressive knee-strengthening exercises (Rathleff et al., 2020).
* **Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP):** For patients seeking active interventions beyond exercise, regenerative injections have been studied. A comparative study evaluated the use of leukocyte-rich platelet-rich plasma (LR-PRP) injections for OSD (Guszczyn et al., 2024). The study reported high treatment efficacy, noting that patients treated in the acute phase of the disease achieved significantly better pain reduction and functional outcomes than those treated in the chronic phase (Guszczyn et al., 2024).
