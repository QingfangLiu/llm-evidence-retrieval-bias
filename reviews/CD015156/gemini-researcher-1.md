It can be challenging to filter out the noise of narrative reviews and committee guidelines when you need raw primary data. Here is a synthesis of individual primary studies—specifically randomized controlled trials (RCTs), prospective cohorts, and case reports—evaluating nonsurgical interventions for the most common lower-limb growth-plate injuries (Sever's disease and Osgood-Schlatter disease) in children and adolescents.

---

## Calcaneal Apophysitis (Sever's Disease)

**Bracing in Barefoot Athletes**
A randomized clinical trial by Sweeney et al. (2023) evaluated two types of specialized heel braces (a heel cup versus an "X" brace) designed for barefoot athletes (e.g., gymnasts and martial artists) aged 7 to 14. Over a 3-month period, both treatment groups showed significant reductions in pain during sports and activities of daily living. However, there were no statistically significant differences in physical function or pain reduction between the two brace designs, suggesting that both forms of structural heel support are viable nonsurgical interventions for barefoot youth.

**Conservative Management for Complex Presentations**
A recent case study examined a 14-year-old athlete presenting with Sever's disease complicated by concurrent posterior tibial tendinopathy and Achilles calcification (Yachaoui et al., 2024). The study highlighted that a multifaceted conservative approach—combining specific stretching regimens, customized orthotic devices, and activity modification—effectively managed this compounded presentation. The patient demonstrated gradual but sustained improvement in pain levels and foot mechanics without needing invasive procedures or surgery.

## Tibial Tubercle Apophysitis (Osgood-Schlatter Disease)

**Hyperosmolar Dextrose Injections (Prolotherapy)**
While rest and physical therapy remain the standard of care, Wu et al. (2021) conducted a double-blind, randomized controlled trial to evaluate ultrasound-guided hyperosmolar dextrose (12.5%) injections versus saline injections for refractory cases of Osgood-Schlatter disease. The study found that patients receiving dextrose injections showed rapid and clinically important improvements in their Victorian Institute of Sport Assessment-Patella (VISA-P) scores at 3, 6, and 12 months compared to the saline group. This data supports prolotherapy as a highly effective nonsurgical alternative for patients unresponsive to baseline conservative care.

**Self-Management and Long-Term Recovery Trends**
A prospective cohort study evaluated the trajectory of self-managed rehabilitation in adolescents aged 10 to 14 with Osgood-Schlatter disease (Rathleff et al., 2024). Interestingly, the study found that short-term symptomatic improvements (at 4 weeks) were not strongly predictive of long-term success. Almost all adolescents in the cohort showed significant symptomatic improvement by the 52-week mark, regardless of how slowly they responded to the initial 4 weeks of the self-management program. This reinforces the necessity of patience and long-term adherence in conservative apophysitis protocols.
