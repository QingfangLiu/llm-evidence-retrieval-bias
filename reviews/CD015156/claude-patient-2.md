## Osgood-Schlatter disease (tibial tuberosity apophysitis)

**Injection therapies (dextrose/prolotherapy):**
- Two double-blind RCTs found a hyperosmolar dextrose injection outperformed saline injection on VISA-Patella scores at 3 months in a 70-patient trial, while an earlier trial by the same lead investigator compared dextrose+lidocaine to saline+lidocaine in 44 knees and found no significant difference between groups, though both improved. A separate 2020 Nakase study titled "No superiority of dextrose injections over placebo injections for Osgood-Schlatter disease" reached a similarly null conclusion. Newer retrospective work compared PRP against dextrose prolotherapy directly.

**Self-management/exercise:**
- A 2020 prospective cohort study of 51 adolescents found a 12-week program of activity modification, education, and progressive knee strengthening was associated with improved self-reported outcomes, hip/knee strength, and jumping performance.
- Building on that pilot work, a 2025 randomized, triple-blinded trial of 130 adolescents (the SOGOOD trial) compared 3 months of a self-management approach (exercise, education, activity modification) to usual care, using the KOOS-child Sport/play subscale as the primary outcome.

**PRP:**
- Guszczyn et al. evaluated pain and knee function in 152 children with chronic OSD before and after leukocyte-rich PRP added to standard conservative treatment, using VAS, Tegner, Lysholm, and KOOS scales, finding 75% patient satisfaction and 72% return to full activity, with significant improvement across all scales. A follow-up 2024 Guszczyn study examined how LR-PRP effectiveness varied by disease duration.

**Other:**
- A 2025 retrospective cohort compared standard local treatment alone versus local treatment plus quadriceps release, finding the combined group had significantly shorter recovery time (1.55 vs 3.58 weeks) and fewer treatment sessions, with no difference in relapse rates.

## Sever's disease (calcaneal apophysitis)

- Wiegerinck et al. (2016) randomized 101 children 8–15 years old to a wait-and-see protocol, a heel-raise inlay, or supervised eccentric calf-strengthening physiotherapy for 10 weeks; all three produced clinically meaningful, statistically significant pain reduction with no significant differences between groups.
- James, Williams & Haines (2016) ran a 12-month 2×2 factorial trial in children aged 8–14 comparing heel-raise vs. prefabricated orthoses, crossed with footwear replacement vs. no replacement, measuring functional disability, pain, and ankle dorsiflexion — building on their earlier protocol/pilot trial (James et al. 2010, *J Foot Ankle Res*).
- Alfaro-Santafé et al. (2021) randomized 208 children aged 9–12 to custom-made polypropylene foot orthoses versus off-the-shelf heel-lifts over 12 weeks, finding both groups improved but the custom-orthoses group showed significantly greater gains in algometry threshold and VAS.
- Sweeney et al. (2023) randomized 43 barefoot athletes (mostly gymnasts) aged 7–14 to a Tuli's Cheetah heel cup or Tuli's X Brace for 3 months, finding both groups improved in ankle/foot function with no significant between-group difference.
