## Bottom line

No single nonsurgical treatment is demonstrably “best” across lower-limb apophysitis.

**Calcaneal apophysitis (Sever disease)** has the most comparative evidence. Mechanical heel off-loading—particularly a **heel cup or heel raise**—has the most consistent short-term support, is inexpensive, and can often be used while activity continues. However, differences between heel devices, exercises, and observation generally disappear by 3–12 months. Custom orthoses produced a very large benefit in one trial but were not consistently superior in another, so routine custom fabrication is not yet strongly established. ([PubMed][1])

For **Osgood–Schlatter disease**, structured load management, education, and strengthening are reasonable active approaches, but the completed SOGOOD randomized trial found that a standardized self-management program was **not superior to usual care** at five months. Dextrose injection evidence is directly conflicting, and dexamethasone iontophoresis remains supported only by a small terminated registry trial. ([BMJ Open Seminars][2])

For **Iselin disease, Sinding-Larsen–Johansson syndrome, and pelvic apophysitis**, evidence is confined to retrospective series and case reports; these cannot identify a superior treatment. ([PubMed][3])

I searched for pediatric primary treatment studies through July 25, 2026. Protocol-only publications, adult populations, reviews, guidelines, and opinion pieces were excluded from the efficacy synthesis. The SOGOOD conference abstract and the dexamethasone trial registry are flagged because complete peer-reviewed reports were not located.

### Taping

Kuyucu et al. randomized 22 male junior footballers aged 8–16 to Kinesio tape or sham tape. At the end of treatment, AOFAS scores were statistically higher with Kinesio tape—99.7 versus 97.4—but pain was not significantly different, 0.1 versus 0.4. Baseline functional scores were also lower in the Kinesio group. This suggests, at most, a small functional benefit without a demonstrated analgesic advantage. ([PubMed][10])

### Practical ranking for calcaneal apophysitis

Based on direct comparative pediatric studies:

1. **Heel cup or heel raise:** best-supported initial device because benefits are plausible, inexpensive, and supported by several studies.
2. **Activity-guided exercise/PT:** produces improvement similar to heel raises or observation by approximately three months.
3. **Custom orthosis:** may provide greater short-term relief in selected children, especially where foot mechanics or persistent symptoms warrant it, but the very large benefit comes primarily from one trial and long-term superiority is unproven.
4. **Kinesio tape or specialized barefoot brace:** possible adjuncts, but no convincing evidence that they outperform simpler mechanical options for pain.

This is not evidence that observation alone is ineffective: in the pragmatic trial, wait-and-see, heel raise, and PT all produced clinically relevant improvement. ([PubMed][5])

## 2. Osgood–Schlatter disease

### Exercise, education, and activity/load modification

The prospective Rathleff cohort treated 51 adolescents aged 10–14 with an activity ladder, pain monitoring, progressive strengthening, and gradual return to sport. At 12 weeks, 80% reported improvement, increasing to 90% at 12 months; however, only 16% had returned to sport at 12 weeks and 69% at one year. Because there was no comparator, natural history, regression to the mean, and contextual effects cannot be separated from treatment effects. ([PubMed][11])

The subsequent **SOGOOD randomized trial** included 130 adolescents aged 10–16 and compared a standardized self-management package with usual care; both contained differing combinations of education, exercise, and activity modification. At five months, the standardized package was not superior:

* Between-group difference in KOOS-child Sport/play change: **−4.8 points**, one-sided 95% CI extending to −12.6; (P=.844).
* Both groups improved by more than 20 points.
* Patient-acceptable symptom state was achieved by 56% versus 64%.
* Positive global change was reported by 98% versus 78%, but this did not translate into superiority on the prespecified primary outcome.

Only the conference-abstract report was located, so detailed risk-of-bias assessment and secondary-outcome interpretation remain limited. ([BMJ Open Seminars][2])

**Interpretation:** active self-management is clinically reasonable, but no particular exercise/load-management package has proven superior to individualized usual care.

### Dextrose injection

Topol et al. randomized 54 adolescent athletes, representing 65 treated knees, to usual care, lidocaine injection, or lidocaine plus 12.5% dextrose. At three months:

* Unaltered sport: 21/21 dextrose, 20/22 lidocaine, and 13/22 usual care.
* Asymptomatic sport: 14/21 dextrose, 5/22 lidocaine, and 3/22 usual care.

The apparent advantage was substantial. However, analyses were conducted by knee despite some participants contributing two knees, and after three months all participants could receive dextrose, weakening the one-year randomized comparison. ([American Academy of Pediatrics][12])

Nakase et al. subsequently randomized 49 knees from 38 adolescents to lidocaine plus 20% dextrose or lidocaine plus saline. Among 43 analyzed knees, both groups improved, but there were **no significant between-group differences at any time point** and no reported adverse effects. ([PubMed][13])

**Interpretation:** dextrose injection cannot presently be considered an established superior treatment. The positive study compared it partly with usual care, whereas the later double-blind placebo-controlled study found no specific dextrose effect.

### Dexamethasone iontophoresis

NCT03606980 randomized children with knee apophysitis to:

* PT plus dexamethasone iontophoresis,
* PT plus saline iontophoresis, or
* PT alone.

The trial enrolled 45 rather than the intended 147 participants and was terminated for slow enrollment. Results were posted to ClinicalTrials.gov in March 2024, but I found no corresponding peer-reviewed primary publication. It should therefore be regarded as registry-only, underpowered evidence rather than a basis for treatment ranking. ([ClinicalTrials.gov][14])

## 3. Other lower-limb apophysitis sites

### Iselin disease—fifth-metatarsal apophysitis

A retrospective series of 27 adolescents reported treatment using varying combinations of rest, footwear modification, controlled-ankle-motion boots, or postoperative shoes. Mean treatment duration was 24 days and mean return to play was 38 days; all cases healed, with three reinjuries and no nonunions. Because treatment assignment was neither standardized nor comparative, the study cannot establish whether a boot is better than footwear modification or rest. ([PubMed][3])

### Sinding-Larsen–Johansson syndrome

A case report described a 13-year-old footballer treated by refraining from sport, with complete clinical and ultrasound recovery after five months. This supports feasibility, not comparative effectiveness. ([PMC][15])

### Iliac-crest apophysitis

A 2025 report described a 16-year-old triathlete with an iliac-crest avulsion on one side and subsequent contralateral apophysitis. Conservative treatment with a staged running and return-to-sport progression over four months was followed by pain-free participation at one year. Because this was a single mixed avulsion/apophysitis case, generalizability is very limited. ([PubMed][16])
