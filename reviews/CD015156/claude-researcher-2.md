## What the primary literature covers, by condition

**Osgood-Schlatter disease (tibial tuberosity apophysitis)**
The strongest evidence base is around injection therapies. Two double-blind RCTs tested hyperosmolar dextrose (prolotherapy) injections against saline/lidocaine controls, with conflicting results — one showed improved VISA-P scores at 3 months, the other found no significant between-group difference. A separate quasi-experimental trial compared extracorporeal shockwave therapy (ESWT) plus conventional therapy against interferential therapy plus conventional therapy, using VAS and WOMAC-type outcomes. A retrospective case series looked at ESWT (including OSD and Sever's patients) for return-to-play timing and safety, and a small uncontrolled pilot study examined radial ESWT specifically in recalcitrant OSD. On the exercise/education side, a Danish group ran a triple-blinded RCT (the SOGOOD trial) comparing a structured self-management program (exercise, education, activity modification) against usual care in 130 adolescents, with the protocol published first and results reported subsequently.

**Calcaneal apophysitis (Sever's disease)** — this condition has the most robust RCT evidence of the three:
- A pragmatic three-arm single-blind RCT (Wiegerinck et al.) compared wait-and-see, heel-raise inlays, and supervised eccentric calf-strengthening exercise, finding all three equally effective.
- A 2×2 factorial RCT (James et al.) compared heel raises vs. prefabricated orthoses, crossed with footwear replacement vs. no replacement, over 12 months.
- A CONSORT-compliant RCT (Alfaro-Santafé et al.) compared custom-made foot orthoses against off-the-shelf heel lifts in 208 children.
- Two related crossover RCTs by Perhamre et al. compared different insole/heel-cup types for pain relief and heel-pad biomechanics.
- A randomized clinical trial (Sweeney et al.) compared two brace types (heel cup vs. an ankle brace) in barefoot athletes (gymnasts).

**Sinding-Larsen-Johansson syndrome (distal patellar apophysitis)**
I could not find a dedicated controlled treatment trial for this condition specifically — the literature here is limited to case reports and a scoping review; management recommendations are extrapolated from OSD/Sever's evidence and expert opinion rather than tested in this population directly. Flagging this as a gap you may want to note in your synthesis.
