# Primary evidence: rFSH versus other gonadotropins in IVF

## Scope

I treated **rFSH** as recombinant follitropin alfa or beta and focused on controlled ovarian stimulation for IVF/ICSI. The principal comparators were:

* Urinary FSH or highly purified urinary FSH
* Human menopausal gonadotropin/menotropins, including highly purified hMG
* Recombinant LH or hMG added to rFSH, considered separately because this tests supplementation rather than replacement

I excluded ovulation-induction/IUI-only studies, comparisons solely between different recombinant FSH products or biosimilars, and nonprimary publications. The search below is a broad primary-study map rather than a formally registered, database-complete systematic review.

## Overall interpretation of the individual trials

### rFSH versus urinary FSH

A recurring finding in the earlier trials was greater **ovarian-response efficiency** with rFSH: more retrieved oocytes, a lower total gonadotropin dose, a shorter stimulation period, or some combination of these outcomes. The largest early trial, with 981 treated participants, found more oocytes and lower dose and duration with rFSH, but no statistically significant difference in ongoing pregnancy per initiated attempt. A double-blind trial similarly found more oocytes and embryos with rFSH but no significant difference in liveborn children. ([PubMed][1])

This ovarian-response advantage was not universal. Several trials found essentially equivalent oocyte yield, pregnancy, or live-birth outcomes. In Baker et al., for example, live birth was 38.2% in both groups. ([ScienceDirect][2])

Some later or subgroup trials produced findings in the opposite direction. In women older than 39, urinary FSH required less total FSH and less FSH per oocyte, although the other outcomes were not significantly different. Abate et al. reported more oocytes, lower gonadotropin use, and shorter stimulation with human-derived FSH but similar pregnancy outcomes. A Chinese trial in women older than 37 found some embryo-development measures favoring urinary FSH, without a clear difference in overall clinical effectiveness. ([PubMed][3])

**Interpretation:** Across the individual studies, rFSH sometimes improves oocyte yield or stimulation efficiency, but there is no consistent corresponding advantage in pregnancy or live birth.

### rFSH versus hMG/HP-hMG

Several larger studies found that rFSH produced more oocytes than HP-hMG, while ongoing pregnancy or cumulative live birth remained broadly similar. In MERIT, mean oocyte yield was 11.8 with rFSH versus 10.0 with HP-hMG, whereas ongoing pregnancy was 22% versus 27%, respectively; the pregnancy difference was not statistically significant, and the predefined noninferiority criterion for HP-hMG was met. ([OUP Academic][4])

A GnRH-antagonist trial reported 14.4 versus 11.3 oocytes in favor of rFSH, but ongoing pregnancy was 32.1% with rFSH and 35.0% with HP-hMG. MEGASET subsequently found cumulative live birth of 38% with rFSH and 40% with HP-hMG. 

The most clinically notable safety result came from MEGASET-HR in predicted high responders. Cumulative live birth was nearly identical—51.5% with rFSH and 50.6% with HP-hMG—but OHSS occurred in 21.4% and 9.7%, respectively. This result applies specifically to women with high ovarian reserve undergoing ICSI and should not automatically be generalized to all IVF patients. ([PubMed][5])

**Interpretation:** rFSH often produces more oocytes, but the large direct trials do not demonstrate a consistent live-birth advantage over HP-hMG. HP-hMG may offer an OHSS advantage in high responders.

### Important design issues

The trials differ substantially in starting dose, dose equivalence, GnRH agonist versus antagonist suppression, IVF versus ICSI, embryo-transfer policy, ovarian-reserve population, and outcome definition. One trial, for example, compared 150 IU/day of rFSH with 225 IU/day of urinary FSH, making product source and nominal dose difficult to separate. 

Many early trials were designed around oocyte yield, gonadotropin consumption, clinical pregnancy, or ongoing pregnancy rather than cumulative live birth. Their embryo-transfer practices also predate current single-embryo-transfer and freeze-all strategies. Therefore, differences in oocyte number should not be interpreted as proven differences in the chance of taking home a baby.

Companion and mechanistic papers should be linked to their parent trial rather than counted as independent studies. For example, the 1997 Out et al. pregnancy follow-up belongs to the large 1995 Puregon-versus-Metrodin trial, and the endocrine/embryology reports derived from MERIT belong to the Andersen et al. randomized cohort.

# Primary studies

## A. rFSH versus urinary or highly purified urinary FSH

1. **Recombinant Human FSH Study Group.** “Clinical assessment of recombinant human follicle-stimulating hormone in stimulating ovarian follicular development before in vitro fertilization.” *Fertil Steril.* 1995;63(1):77–86. Randomized, open, multicenter trial; 123 treated participants. Oocyte yield, pregnancy, live delivery, and safety were similar. DOI: 10.1016/S0015-0282(16)57300-2. ([ScienceDirect][2])

2. **Out HJ, Mannaerts BMJL, Driessen SGAJ, Coelingh Bennink HJT.** “A prospective, randomized, assessor-blind, multicentre study comparing recombinant and urinary follicle stimulating hormone (Puregon versus Metrodin) in in-vitro fertilization.” *Hum Reprod.* 1995;10(10):2534–2540. Randomized trial; 981 treated participants. rFSH produced more oocytes with less drug and shorter stimulation; ongoing pregnancy per attempt was not significantly different. PMID: 8567765. ([PubMed][1])

3. **Hedon B, Out HJ, Hugues JN, et al.** “Efficacy and safety of recombinant follicle stimulating hormone (Puregon) in infertile women pituitary-suppressed with triptorelin undergoing in-vitro fertilization.” *Hum Reprod.* 1995;10(12):3102–3106. Randomized assessor-blind multicenter trial; 99 participants. Mean retrieved oocytes were similar. PMID: 8822422. ([PubMed][6])

4. **Bergh C, Howles CM, Borg K, et al.** “Recombinant human follicle stimulating hormone (Gonal-F) versus highly purified urinary FSH (Metrodin HP): results of a randomized comparative study in women undergoing assisted reproductive techniques.” *Hum Reprod.* 1997;12(10):2133–2139. Approximately 233 evaluable participants. rFSH produced more oocytes and required fewer treatment days/ampoules; clinical pregnancy was not significantly different. DOI: 10.1093/humrep/12.10.2133. 

5. **Hoomans EHM, Andersen AN, Loft A, et al.** “A prospective, randomized clinical trial comparing 150 IU recombinant FSH and 225 IU highly purified urinary FSH in a fixed-dose regimen.” *Hum Reprod.* 1999;14(10):2442–2447. Randomized trial; 165 participants. Similar oocyte and vital-pregnancy outcomes, with substantially less total FSH in the rFSH arm. PMID: 10527965. 

6. **Frydman R, Howles CM, Truong F; French Multicentre Trialists.** “A double-blind, randomized study to compare recombinant human FSH with highly purified urinary FSH in women undergoing assisted reproductive techniques including ICSI.” *Hum Reprod.* 2000;15(3):520–525. Randomized double-blind trial; 278 participants. More oocytes and embryos and shorter stimulation with rFSH; liveborn-child counts were not significantly different. DOI: 10.1093/humrep/15.3.520. 

7. **Lenton E, Soltan A, Hewitt J, et al.** “Induction of ovulation in women undergoing assisted reproductive techniques: recombinant human FSH versus highly purified urinary FSH.” *Hum Reprod.* 2000;15(5):1021–1027. Randomized trial; 168 recruited and 155 treated. No significant differences in oocytes, pregnancy, live birth, or OHSS. PMID: 10783345. ([PubMed][7])

8. **Schats R, De Sutter P, Bassil S, et al.; Feronia and Apis Study Group.** “Ovarian stimulation during assisted reproduction treatment: a comparison of recombinant and highly purified urinary human FSH.” *Hum Reprod.* 2000;15(8):1691–1697. Randomized, single-blind multicenter trial; 496 participants. rFSH produced more oocytes with lower treatment requirements; pregnancy was not significantly different. PMID: 10920087. ([PubMed][8])

9. **Franco JG Jr, Baruffi RLR, Mauri AL, et al.** “A prospective and randomized study of ovarian stimulation for ICSI with recombinant FSH versus highly purified urinary FSH.” *Gynecol Endocrinol.* 2000;14(1):5–10. Randomized trial; 120 participants. Oocyte yield and mature-oocyte numbers were similar. DOI: 10.3109/09513590009167653. ([Taylor & Francis Online][9])

10. **Hugues JN, Barlow DH, Rosenwaks Z, et al.** “Comparison of recombinant and urinary FSH preparations in a short-term GnRH agonist protocol for IVF-ET.” *J Assist Reprod Genet.* 2001;18(4):191–196. Randomized three-arm trial; 88 participants. Recombinant preparations required less total FSH, with no significant difference in ovarian response or IVF outcomes; follitropin alfa and beta were also comparable. PMID: 11432109. ([PubMed][10])

11. **Dickey RP, Thornton M, Nichols J, et al.; Bravelle IVF Study Group.** “Comparison of the efficacy and safety of highly purified human FSH and recombinant follitropin-beta for in vitro fertilization.” *Fertil Steril.* 2002;77(6):1202–1208. Randomized three-arm trial; 177 participants. No significant differences in oocyte, continuing-pregnancy, live-birth, or adverse-event outcomes. PMID: 12057729. ([PubMed][11])

12. **Mohamed MA, Sbracia M, Pacchiarotti A, et al.** “Urinary follicle-stimulating hormone is more effective than recombinant FSH in older women in a controlled randomized study.” *Fertil Steril.* 2006;85(5):1398–1403. Randomized trial in women older than 39; 257 enrolled, with 121 assigned rFSH and 120 urinary FSH. Urinary FSH required less total FSH and less FSH per oocyte; other measured outcomes were not significantly different. PMID: 16600226. ([PubMed][3])

13. **Baker VL, Fujimoto VY, Kettel LM, et al.** “Clinical efficacy of highly purified urinary FSH versus recombinant FSH in volunteers undergoing controlled ovarian stimulation for IVF.” *Fertil Steril.* 2009;91(4):1005–1011. Randomized investigator-blind multicenter trial; 152 participants. Oocyte and clinical-pregnancy outcomes were similar; live birth was 38.2% in both groups. PMID: 18367182. ([PubMed][12])

14. **Abate A, Nazzaro A, Salerno A, et al.** “Efficacy of recombinant versus human-derived FSH on oocyte and embryo quality in IVF-ICSI cycles.” *Gynecol Endocrinol.* 2009;25(8):479–484. Randomized multicenter trial; 401 participants. Oocyte/embryo quality and pregnancy outcomes were similar; human-derived FSH produced more oocytes with less medication and shorter stimulation. PMID: 19572228. ([PubMed][13])

15. **Sohrabvand F, Sheikhhassani S, Bagheri M, et al.** “Comparison of highly purified urinary versus recombinant FSH: effect on ART outcomes in polycystic ovary syndrome.” *Iran J Reprod Med.* 2012;10(3):229–236. Randomized trial; 160 participants with PCOS. Clinical pregnancy, oocyte yield, embryo measures, and OHSS were similar. PMID: 25242998. ([PubMed][14])

16. **Liu X, Hao C, Wang J, et al.** “Efficacy of highly purified urinary FSH versus recombinant FSH in Chinese women over 37 undergoing assisted reproductive technology.” *Int J Fertil Steril.* 2015;8(4):385–392. Randomized trial; 508 participants. Some fertilization and embryo-quality measures favored urinary FSH, but overall clinical efficacy was considered equivalent. PMID: 25780520. ([PubMed][15])

### Additional nonparallel comparative study

17. **Balasch J, Peñarrubia J, Fábregues F, et al.** “Follicular development and hormonal levels following highly purified or recombinant FSH administration after pituitary suppression for IVF.” *J Assist Reprod Genet.* 2000;17(1):20–27. Sequential within-patient comparison with a repeated-uFSH control cohort rather than a conventional parallel RCT. Overall stimulation response was similar, with differences in follicular dynamics, oocyte maturity, and implantation-related measures. PMID: 10754779. ([PubMed][16])

## B. rFSH versus hMG or highly purified hMG

1. **Ng EHY, Lau EYL, Yeung WSB, Ho PC.** “HMG is as good as recombinant human FSH in terms of oocyte and embryo quality: a prospective randomized trial.” *Hum Reprod.* 2001;16(2):319–325. Randomized ICSI trial; 40 participants. No significant differences in ovarian response, mature-oocyte proportion, embryo quality, implantation, or pregnancy. DOI: 10.1093/humrep/16.2.319. ([OUP Academic][17])

2. **Strehler E, Abt M, El-Danasouri I, De Santo M, Sterzik K.** “Impact of recombinant FSH and human menopausal gonadotropins on IVF outcome.” *Fertil Steril.* 2001;75(2):332–336. Randomized trial; 578 participants. rFSH produced more oocytes, but pregnancy after embryo transfer and other principal clinical outcomes were similar. PMID: 11172835. ([PubMed][18])

3. **Westergaard LG, Erb K, Laursen SB, et al.** “Human menopausal gonadotropin versus recombinant FSH in normogonadotropic women down-regulated with a GnRH agonist undergoing IVF/ICSI.” *Fertil Steril.* 2001;76(3):543–549. Prospective randomized factorial study; 379 participants. Oocyte and transferable-embryo outcomes were broadly similar, with interactions involving the down-regulation regimen. DOI: 10.1016/S0015-0282(01)01973-2. ([ScienceDirect][19])

4. **European and Israeli Study Group on Highly Purified Menotropin versus Recombinant FSH.** “Efficacy and safety of highly purified menotropin versus recombinant follicle-stimulating hormone in IVF/ICSI cycles.” *Fertil Steril.* 2002;78(3):520–528. Randomized multinational phase III trial; 781 enrolled and 727 treated. Ongoing pregnancy and safety outcomes were similar, supporting comparable efficacy. PMID: 12215327. ([PubMed][20])

5. **Kilani Z, Dakkak A, Ghunaim S, et al.** “A prospective, randomized controlled trial comparing highly purified hMG with recombinant FSH in women undergoing ICSI.” *Hum Reprod.* 2003;18(6):1194–1199. Randomized trial; 100 participants. HP-hMG required less medication/shorter stimulation, while pregnancy and miscarriage outcomes were similar. PMID: 12773445. ([PubMed][21])

6. **Andersen AN, Devroey P, Arce JC; MERIT Group.** “Clinical outcome following stimulation with highly purified hMG or recombinant FSH in patients undergoing IVF.” *Hum Reprod.* 2006;21(12):3217–3227. Randomized assessor-blind trial; 731 participants. rFSH produced more oocytes; ongoing pregnancy was not significantly different, and HP-hMG met the predefined noninferiority criterion. DOI: 10.1093/humrep/del284. ([OUP Academic][4])

7. **Bosch E, Vidal C, Labarta E, et al.** “Highly purified hMG versus recombinant FSH in ovarian hyperstimulation with GnRH antagonists—a randomized study.” *Hum Reprod.* 2008;23(10):2346–2351. Randomized trial; 280 participants. rFSH produced more oocytes; ongoing pregnancy, implantation, clinical pregnancy, and pregnancy loss were not significantly different. DOI: 10.1093/humrep/den220. 

8. **Melo M, Bellver J, Garrido N, et al.** “A prospective, randomized, controlled trial comparing three gonadotropin regimens in oocyte donors.” *Fertil Steril.* 2010;94(3):958–964. Randomized donor trial; 1,028 donors and 1,079 recipients. rFSH, HP-hMG, and their combination produced similar stimulation, embryo-development, implantation, pregnancy, and miscarriage outcomes. PMID: 19931075. ([PubMed][22])

9. **Devroey P, Pellicer A, Nyboe Andersen A, Arce JC; MEGASET Trial Group.** “A randomized assessor-blind trial comparing highly purified hMG and recombinant FSH in a GnRH-antagonist cycle with compulsory single-blastocyst transfer.” *Fertil Steril.* 2012;97(3):561–571. Randomized trial; 749 participants. HP-hMG was noninferior for ongoing pregnancy; cumulative live birth within one year was 40% with HP-hMG and 38% with rFSH. PMID: 22244781. ([PubMed][23])

10. **Witz CA, Daftary GS, Doody KJ, et al.; MEGASET-HR Study Group.** “Randomized assessor-blinded trial comparing highly purified hMG and recombinant FSH in high responders undergoing ICSI.” *Fertil Steril.* 2020;114(2):321–330. Randomized trial; 620 predicted high responders. Cumulative live birth was similar, while OHSS was lower with HP-hMG. PMID: 32416978. ([PubMed][5])

## C. Trials examining the addition or source of LH activity

1. **Marrs R, Meldrum D, Muasher S, et al.** “Randomized trial to compare recombinant human FSH with or without recombinant human LH in women undergoing assisted reproduction treatment.” *Reprod Biomed Online.* 2004;8(2):175–182. Randomized trial; 431 participants. Adding rLH did not increase the number of metaphase-II oocytes. PMID: 14989794. ([PubMed][24])

2. **Pacchiarotti A, Aragona C, Gaglione R, et al.** “Urinary hMG versus recombinant FSH plus recombinant LH in IVF: a multicenter, prospective, randomized controlled trial.” *Fertil Steril.* 2010;94(6):2467–2469. Randomized trial; 122 participants. Pregnancy, implantation, oocyte, and embryo-quality outcomes were comparable; OHSS risk was greater in the rFSH-plus-rLH group. PMID: 20537626. ([PubMed][25])

3. **Tehraninejad ES, Farshbaf Taghinejad M, Hossein Rashidi B, Haghollahi F.** “Controlled ovarian stimulation with rFSH plus rLH versus hMG plus rFSH in patients undergoing IVF/ICSI: an RCT.” *Int J Reprod Biomed.* 2017;15(7):435–440. Randomized trial; 140 participants. Directly compared recombinant-LH supplementation with hMG supplementation beginning during stimulation. PMID: 29177245. ([PubMed][26])

4. **Shu L, Xu Q, Meng Q, et al.** “Clinical outcomes following long GnRHa ovarian stimulation with highly purified hMG plus rFSH or rFSH in patients undergoing IVF-ET.” *Ann Transl Med.* 2019;7. Multicenter randomized trial; 610 participants. Combination treatment lowered progesterone at trigger and increased fertilization and fresh-transfer availability, but implantation, clinical pregnancy, and OHSS did not significantly differ. PMID: 31157267. ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov][27])
