It is completely normal to feel overwhelmed by the alphabet soup of medications involved in an IVF cycle. Finding the right protocol is a massive part of the process, and wanting to look directly at the clinical data is a great way to advocate for yourself.

As requested, I have bypassed all meta-analyses and narrative reviews to look strictly at individual primary studies—including randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and prospective/retrospective studies—that compare **recombinant FSH (rFSH)** to urinary-derived alternatives like **human menopausal gonadotropin (hMG)** or **urinary FSH (uFSH)**.

Here is what the primary literature says about how they compare.

---

---

## Oocyte (Egg) Yield and Embryo Quantity

The most consistent finding across primary trials is that rFSH tends to recruit more follicles and yield a higher absolute number of eggs than urinary-derived gonadotropins.

* **Higher Egg Counts:** A large retrospective study comparing rFSH to uFSH found that patients using rFSH had a significantly higher number of retrieved oocytes (an average of 13 versus 10) and more transferrable embryos (Yang et al., 2022). Similarly, an older but foundational RCT found that rFSH yielded significantly more oocytes than highly purified hMG (Andersen et al., 2006).
* **Embryo Quality:** Quality comparisons are slightly more nuanced, but generally favorable for rFSH. One RCT found that while clinical pregnancy rates were identical, the total number of embryos and the "best embryo score" were significantly higher in the rFSH group compared to the hMG group (Chapon et al., 2020).

## Live Birth Rates: Fresh vs. Cumulative

Even though rFSH generally yields more eggs, the chances of a successful pregnancy from a *single* embryo transfer are often highly comparable between the different medications. The divergence happens when you look at cumulative success.

* **Fresh Transfers:** Some studies suggest hMG holds its own or slightly edges out rFSH in fresh transfers, possibly due to the natural luteinizing hormone (LH) activity present in hMG. For instance, the trial by Andersen et al. (2006) noted a slightly higher live birth rate per started cycle for hMG (26%) compared to rFSH (22%) in fresh transfers, even with fewer eggs retrieved.
* **Cumulative Live Birth Rates (CLBR):** Because rFSH typically produces a higher number of transferrable embryos, it provides patients with more opportunities for frozen embryo transfers from a single retrieval. The study by Yang et al. (2022) demonstrated that while live birth rates for the first fresh transfer were similar, the *cumulative* live birth rate across all fresh and frozen transfers was significantly higher for rFSH (53.5%) compared to uFSH (43.1%).

## Combined Protocols

In recent years, rather than treating these medications as strictly "either/or," primary studies have increasingly explored combining them to leverage the high egg yield of rFSH alongside the LH-driven follicle maturation support of hMG.

* **For Lower Ovarian Reserve:** A prospective controlled study looked at women with lower anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH < 2.1 ng/mL). They found that supplementing an rFSH baseline with menotropin (hMG) resulted in higher estrogen levels and more top-quality blastocysts compared to using rFSH alone, though the live birth rate on the initial transfer did not significantly differ (Duarte-Filho et al., 2023).
