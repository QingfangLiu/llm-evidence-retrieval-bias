Choosing between recombinant FSH (rFSH) and urinary-derived preparations like highly purified human menopausal gonadotropin (HP-hMG) is one of the most nuanced decisions in reproductive endocrinology.

When isolating the evidence strictly to **primary randomized controlled trials (RCTs)** and excluding pooled analyses or reviews, several consistent clinical themes emerge regarding how these hormones behave during controlled ovarian hyperstimulation.

## Oocyte Yield vs. Embryo Quality

Across different stimulation protocols, rFSH consistently yields a higher absolute number of retrieved oocytes compared to HP-hMG (Andersen et al., 2006; Bosch et al., 2008).

However, this quantitative advantage does not automatically translate into a higher number of top-quality embryos. The LH/hCG activity intrinsic to HP-hMG is thought to induce a more selective follicular recruitment process. As a result, while fewer total follicles develop with HP-hMG, the proportion of mature (MII) oocytes and top-quality embryos often remains comparable to rFSH (Andersen et al., 2006; Chapon et al., 2020).

## Endocrine Profile Dynamics

The distinct pharmacodynamics of these preparations drastically alter the late-follicular endocrine environment:

* **Progesterone:** rFSH administration frequently leads to significantly higher serum progesterone levels on the day of trigger. Even minor premature progesterone elevations can negatively impact endometrial receptivity during fresh embryo transfers (Bosch et al., 2008).
* **Estradiol:** Conversely, patients stimulated with HP-hMG typically exhibit higher peak estradiol levels. This is driven by the continuous LH activity in HP-hMG, which promotes the aromatization of androgens in the theca cells (Andersen et al., 2006; Bosch et al., 2008).

## Pregnancy and Live Birth Efficacy

Despite the distinct differences in oocyte quantity and hormonal profiles, landmark primary RCTs largely establish non-inferiority between rFSH and HP-hMG regarding the ultimate goal: **ongoing pregnancy and live birth rates**.

Whether utilizing a long GnRH agonist protocol (Andersen et al., 2006) or a GnRH antagonist protocol (Bosch et al., 2008; Chapon et al., 2020), overall clinical efficacy remains statistically comparable for standard responders. This allows you to tailor your choice based on patient-specific factors like age, baseline ovarian reserve, risk of OHSS, and cost.
