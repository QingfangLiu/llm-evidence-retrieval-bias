To support your evidence synthesis on ovarian stimulation for IVF, here is a breakdown of how recombinant follicle-stimulating hormone (rFSH) compares to other gonadotropins, drawn strictly from landmark, multicenter randomized controlled trials (RCTs).

## rFSH vs. Urinary FSH (uFSH)

Early pivotal trials established the clinical viability of recombinant formulations over older urinary-derived products. In a prospective, assessor-blinded RCT, Out et al. (1995) compared rFSH (follitropin beta) to uFSH. The study found that patients receiving rFSH had a significantly higher mean number of oocytes retrieved (10.84 vs. 8.95) compared to the uFSH group. While the ongoing pregnancy rate was slightly higher in the rFSH cohort (22.2% vs. 18.2%), this difference did not reach statistical significance.

## rFSH vs. Highly Purified Human Menopausal Gonadotropin (HP-hMG)

The clinical choice between rFSH and HP-hMG—which contains both FSH and LH activity—has been the subject of several large-scale RCTs assessing different down-regulation protocols.

* **In Long GnRH Agonist Protocols:** The MERIT trial (Andersen et al., 2006) was a major assessor-blind RCT comparing HP-hMG to rFSH. It demonstrated that while rFSH yielded a higher total number of oocytes (11.8 vs. 10.0), HP-hMG produced a higher proportion of top-quality embryos. Ultimately, the ongoing pregnancy rates per cycle (27% for HP-hMG vs. 22% for rFSH) established non-inferiority, though superiority of HP-hMG could not be definitively concluded.
* **In GnRH Antagonist Protocols:** The MEGASET trial (Devroey et al., 2012) evaluated the same comparison in antagonist cycles with compulsory single-blastocyst transfer. Ongoing pregnancy rates for fresh cycles (30% HP-hMG vs. 27% rFSH) and cumulative live birth rates (40% vs. 38%) similarly confirmed the non-inferiority of HP-hMG to rFSH, despite the different pharmacodynamic endpoints observed between the two gonadotropins.

## Daily rFSH vs. Long-Acting rFSH (Corifollitropin alfa)

To reduce the injection burden of daily rFSH, fusion proteins lacking LH activity but possessing extended half-lives have been developed. The ENGAGE trial (Devroey et al., 2014) was a double-blind, non-inferiority RCT that compared a single injection of corifollitropin alfa (designed to last seven days) against seven daily injections of standard rFSH in a GnRH antagonist protocol. Ongoing pregnancy rates were practically identical (38.9% vs. 38.1%), establishing non-inferiority. Corifollitropin alfa did yield slightly more cumulus-oocyte complexes, though the incidence of moderate-to-severe ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS) remained equivalent.

## Conventional rFSH vs. Individualized rFSH

More recent trials have focused on individualized dosing of rFSH to optimize safety. The ESTHER-1 phase 3 trial (Nyboe Andersen et al., 2017) compared conventional follitropin alfa dosing with individualized dosing of a newer recombinant formulation (follitropin delta), based on the patient's serum anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH) and body weight. Ongoing pregnancy and live birth rates were similar between the two groups. However, the individualized rFSH approach improved safety by significantly reducing both poor and excessive ovarian responses, thereby reducing the need for OHSS prevention measures.
