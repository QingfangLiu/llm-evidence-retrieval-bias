When evaluating recombinant follicle-stimulating hormone (rFSH) against other gonadotropin preparations for ovarian stimulation, primary literature reveals a nuanced landscape. While daily rFSH (such as follitropin alfa and beta) has long been the standard, it is frequently compared against urinary-derived menotropins (hp-hMG), long-acting recombinant molecules (corifollitropin alfa), and newer human-cell-derived recombinant formulas (follitropin delta).

Here is a breakdown of how rFSH compares to these alternatives based on data from key individual primary studies.

## Daily rFSH vs. Highly Purified hMG (hp-hMG)

Highly purified human menopausal gonadotropin (hp-hMG) contains both FSH and LH activity, which influences follicular recruitment differently than the FSH-only recombinant preparations.

* **Oocyte Yield:** Randomized trials have frequently shown that daily rFSH produces a higher absolute number of retrieved oocytes compared to hp-hMG in GnRH antagonist protocols (Bosch et al., 2008).
* **Safety and Efficiency in High Responders:** While rFSH yields more oocytes, this does not universally translate to improved clinical outcomes. A recent analysis of the MEGASET-HR Phase 4 trial focusing on predicted high-responders found that while rFSH yielded more oocytes, hp-hMG achieved comparable cumulative live birth rates while requiring significantly fewer embryo transfers. Furthermore, hp-hMG was associated with a clinically meaningful reduction in ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS) and early pregnancy loss compared to rFSH (Khair et al., 2023).

## Daily rFSH vs. Long-Acting Corifollitropin Alfa

Corifollitropin alfa is a long-acting hybrid recombinant molecule designed to replace the first seven days of daily rFSH injections, significantly reducing the injection burden on patients.

* **Efficacy and Pregnancy Rates:** Primary data from the large ENGAGE trial demonstrated that a single injection of corifollitropin alfa yields ongoing pregnancy and live-birth rates equivalent to seven days of standard daily rFSH injections (Leader et al., 2013).
* **Comparability Across Regimens:** Subsequent cohort analyses have reinforced these findings, showing no statistically significant differences in fertilization rates, embryo implantation rates, or biochemical pregnancy rates when comparing corifollitropin alfa against either standard daily rFSH or hp-hMG in antagonist protocols (Souza et al., 2017).

## Conventional rFSH vs. Individualized Follitropin Delta

Follitropin delta is a newer generation of rFSH produced in a human cell line (PER.C6) rather than the traditional Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cells used for follitropin alfa/beta. It is uniquely approved for strict individualized dosing based on a patient's serum anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH) level and body weight.

* **Safety and Target Responses:** The landmark ESTHER-1 phase 3 trial established that while overall ongoing pregnancy and live birth rates are similar between individualized follitropin delta and conventional follitropin alfa, the individualized delta protocol provides superior safety. It resulted in fewer extreme ovarian responses (both poor and excessive yields) and significantly reduced the need for preventative OHSS interventions (Nyboe Andersen et al., 2017).
* **Real-World and Expanded Applications:** Real-world retrospective analyses confirm that the strict follitropin delta algorithm achieves a target yield of 8–14 oocytes just as effectively as standard rFSH dosed continuously by experienced clinicians (Gazzo et al., 2024). Ongoing large-scale RCTs, such as the BEYOND trial, continue to validate the efficacy of individualized follitropin delta across both GnRH agonist and antagonist protocols (Lobo et al., 2024).
