## Bottom line

Across individual randomized IVF/ICSI studies, **rFSH has not shown a consistent live-birth advantage over either highly purified urinary FSH or highly purified human menopausal gonadotropin (HP-hMG/menotropins).**

The main differences are usually in **ovarian-response measures**:

* Compared with older urinary FSH preparations, rFSH often required fewer treatment days or fewer international units and sometimes produced approximately one to two more oocytes. Pregnancy and live-birth differences were generally not statistically significant. ([PubMed][1])
* Compared with HP-hMG, which provides both FSH and LH-like activity, rFSH often produced more oocytes, while HP-hMG sometimes produced a higher proportion of favorable embryos. Large trials generally found similar ongoing pregnancy or cumulative live-birth rates. ([OUP Academic][2])
* In one important trial restricted to predicted high responders with AMH ≥5 ng/mL, HP-hMG produced a similar cumulative live-birth rate but substantially less OHSS than rFSH. This result should not automatically be generalized to women with normal or diminished ovarian reserve. ([sciencedirect.com][3])

### What the preparations contain

**rFSH**, such as follitropin alfa or follitropin beta, supplies FSH without clinically meaningful LH activity.

**Highly purified urinary FSH** also primarily supplies FSH, but it is extracted from postmenopausal urine.

**HP-hMG or menotropins** supply FSH together with LH-like activity; in modern HP-hMG, much of that LH activity comes from small amounts of hCG. Thus, rFSH-versus-hMG studies are partly testing whether adding LH/hCG activity changes the ovarian response, not merely whether recombinant or urinary manufacturing is preferable. ([ScienceDirect][4])

## Findings from individual primary studies

### 1. rFSH versus urinary or highly purified urinary FSH

**Out et al., 1995 — 981 women**

This large multicenter randomized trial compared follitropin beta with urinary FSH. rFSH produced more oocytes, 10.84 versus 8.95, with a lower total FSH dose and slightly shorter stimulation. Ongoing pregnancy per fresh attempt was 22.2% versus 18.2%, a nonsignificant difference. When frozen transfers were included, ongoing pregnancy was 25.7% versus 20.4%, reported at the threshold of statistical significance. Safety was similar. ([PubMed][1])

**Interpretation:** rFSH appeared more potent for generating oocytes, but the fresh-cycle pregnancy advantage was uncertain.

**Frydman et al., 2000 — 278 women**

In this double-blind randomized trial, follitropin alfa produced more oocytes than highly purified urinary FSH, 11.0 versus 8.8, and required fewer treatment days and ampules. There were 36 liveborn children in the rFSH group and 33 in the urinary-FSH group, which was not significantly different. OHSS occurred in 5.0% versus 2.2%, also not statistically significant. ([OUP Academic][5])

**Interpretation:** A clearer difference in ovarian response than in live birth.

**Schats et al., 2000 — 496 randomized women**

At the same nominal daily dose, follitropin alfa required fewer treatment days and produced more follicles and oocytes: 13.1 versus 11.4. Pregnancy per cycle was 25.1% versus 20.1%, but this was not statistically significant. Moderate-to-severe OHSS was 2.8% versus 1.2%, also not significantly different. ([PubMed][6])

**Lenton et al., 2000 — 168 recruited; 155 treated**

The mean number of oocytes was similar, 10.2 with rFSH versus 10.8 with highly purified urinary FSH. Live-birth rates were 33.8% versus 26.7%, but none of the efficacy differences reached statistical significance. OHSS rates were also similar. ([PubMed][7])

**Dickey et al., 2002 — 177 women**

This three-arm randomized study compared subcutaneous or intramuscular highly purified urinary FSH with subcutaneous follitropin beta. Oocyte yield, continuing pregnancy, live birth and adverse events did not differ significantly. Injection-site pain was actually lower with the urinary preparation in this particular study. ([PubMed][8])

**Baker et al., 2009 — 152 women**

Women received highly purified urinary FSH or follitropin alfa. Oocyte yield was nearly identical, 16.3 versus 17.1. Clinical pregnancy was 48.7% versus 44.7%, and the live-birth rate was exactly 38.2% in both groups. The confidence intervals were wide enough that the study could not rule out clinically meaningful smaller differences. ([PubMed][9])

**Overall for urinary FSH:** Some older studies suggest rFSH is more efficient per unit and may retrieve modestly more oocytes, but later trials using highly purified urinary FSH found little or no difference. None establishes a consistent live-birth advantage.

### 2. rFSH versus HP-hMG/menotropins

**European and Israeli Study Group, 2002 — 727 treated women**

HP-hMG was compared with rFSH in a long GnRH-agonist IVF/ICSI protocol. Ongoing pregnancy was 25% with HP-hMG versus 22% with rFSH. Clinical pregnancy was 28% versus 24%. Neither difference demonstrated superiority, but HP-hMG met the study’s criterion for comparable efficacy. OHSS, miscarriage and general tolerability were similar. ([PubMed][10])

**MERIT trial, Andersen et al., 2006 — 731 women**

rFSH produced more oocytes, 11.8 versus 10.0. HP-hMG produced a higher proportion of embryos meeting the trial’s top-quality morphology definition, 11.3% versus 9.0%. Ongoing pregnancy was 27% with HP-hMG and 22% with rFSH; HP-hMG was shown to be noninferior, but superiority was not established because the confidence interval included no difference. ([OUP Academic][2])

**Important limitation:** Participants were predominantly young, normal-prognosis patients, and day-3 transfer of one or two embryos was used.

**MEGASET trial, Devroey et al., 2012 — 749 women**

This trial used a GnRH-antagonist protocol and compulsory single-blastocyst transfer, making it more applicable to contemporary practice. Fresh-cycle ongoing pregnancy was 29% with HP-hMG versus 27% with rFSH in the intention-to-treat population. After fresh and frozen transfers within one year, cumulative live birth was 40% versus 38%. HP-hMG was noninferior. ([PubMed][11])

**MEGASET–High Responder trial, Witz et al., 2020 — 620 women**

All participants had AMH ≥5 ng/mL and were therefore predicted high responders. Fresh-cycle ongoing pregnancy was 35.5% with HP-hMG versus 30.7% with rFSH; cumulative live birth was essentially identical, 50.6% versus 51.5%.

The notable safety difference was OHSS: **9.7% with HP-hMG versus 21.4% with rFSH**. Early pregnancy loss was also lower with HP-hMG. rFSH required a lower total gonadotropin dose but generated a more pronounced ovarian response. ([sciencedirect.com][3])

**Interpretation:** This is the strongest primary-study evidence that gonadotropin choice might influence OHSS risk in women with very high ovarian reserve. Trigger selection, freeze-all decisions and dose management remain major contributors to OHSS risk.

### 3. Adding LH activity to rFSH

**Marrs et al., 2004 — 431 women**

Women undergoing ICSI were randomized to rFSH alone or rFSH plus 150 IU/day recombinant LH starting on stimulation day 6. Mature-oocyte numbers were nearly identical, 10.4 versus 10.3, and overall implantation did not improve with LH. An age subgroup suggested a possible benefit among women aged 35 or older, but this was not statistically definitive and was a subgroup finding. ([PubMed][12])

**Shu et al., 2019 — 610 women**

This randomized trial compared rFSH alone with combined HP-hMG plus rFSH. The combination produced a higher fertilization rate, 73.9% versus 69.2%, and more cycles proceeded to fresh transfer. However, implantation, clinical pregnancy and OHSS rates were not significantly different. The primary endpoint was progesterone at trigger rather than live birth. ([PubMed][13])

**Tehraninejad et al., 2017 — 140 women**

rFSH plus hMG was compared with rFSH plus recombinant LH. The hMG combination produced higher reported clinical pregnancy and live-birth outcomes, despite similar oocyte numbers. Because this was a small, single-country trial with only 70 women per arm, it should be considered hypothesis-generating rather than proof that urinary LH activity is superior to recombinant LH. ([PubMed][14])

## Practical interpretation

For most normal responders, the individual trials indicate that **rFSH, highly purified urinary FSH and HP-hMG can all produce effective IVF stimulation**, without a reliably demonstrated difference in live birth. rFSH may produce somewhat more oocytes or require fewer units than some urinary preparations, but retrieving more oocytes is not automatically equivalent to a higher live-birth probability.

Choice may become more consequential when there is:

* Very high AMH or antral follicle count and concern about OHSS.
* A history of excessive or poor response.
* A decision to add LH activity because of age, profound pituitary suppression or a previous suboptimal response.
* A specific need to balance oocyte number against safety and the likelihood of using a freeze-all strategy.

These studies also have limitations. Many older trials used long GnRH-agonist protocols and transferred two or three embryos, whereas contemporary care frequently uses antagonist protocols, agonist triggers when appropriate, blastocyst culture and single-embryo transfer. Several of the large trials were industry-sponsored, although many used blinded embryologists or outcome assessors. ([OUP Academic][2])
