Going through IVF involves navigating a complex world of medications, and it is completely normal to want to understand exactly what you are taking to stimulate your ovaries.

At a high level, **recombinant FSH (rFSH)** is created in a laboratory using recombinant DNA technology, making it highly pure and consistent. In contrast, other gonadotropins like **highly purified urinary FSH (HP-uFSH)** and **human menopausal gonadotropin (hMG / HP-hMG)** are derived and purified from the urine of postmenopausal women. While rFSH contains only FSH, hMG contains a mix of both FSH and Luteinizing Hormone (LH) activity.

To answer your question using strictly primary research (randomized controlled trials), studies generally show that rFSH is highly effective, though it behaves slightly differently in the body compared to urinary-derived alternatives.

## rFSH vs. HP-hMG (Human Menopausal Gonadotropin)

Two of the largest and most well-known primary trials comparing these hormones are the MERIT trial and the MEGASET trial.

* **Oocyte Yield vs. Embryo Quality:** In the MERIT trial, patients who were stimulated with rFSH retrieved a significantly higher number of oocytes (eggs) on average compared to those using HP-hMG. However, the HP-hMG group produced a slightly higher proportion of top-quality embryos (Andersen et al., 2006).
* **Pregnancy and Live Birth Rates:** In GnRH antagonist cycles (a very common IVF protocol today), the MEGASET trial demonstrated that HP-hMG was non-inferior to rFSH. The cumulative live birth rates for a single stimulation cycle were highly comparable between the two groups—roughly 40% for HP-hMG and 38% for rFSH (Devroey et al., 2012). The MERIT trial also found no significant difference in ongoing pregnancy rates between the two treatments in long agonist protocols (Andersen et al., 2006).
* **Hormonal Profile:** rFSH tends to produce a different hormonal environment by the end of the stimulation phase, typically resulting in higher progesterone levels and lower estradiol levels compared to HP-hMG (Andersen et al., 2006).

## rFSH vs. Highly Purified Urinary FSH (HP-uFSH)

When looking specifically at highly purified urinary FSH (which, unlike hMG, does not contain LH activity) compared to rFSH, the outcomes are remarkably similar.

* **Clinical Outcomes:** A randomized, investigator-blind trial compared patients taking a starting dose of 300 IU of either rFSH or HP-uFSH. The researchers found no statistically significant differences in the number of oocytes retrieved, clinical pregnancy rates, or live birth rates between the two groups (Baker et al., 2009). The total amount of medication required to achieve stimulation was also the same.

Ultimately, individual primary trials consistently show that while rFSH may recruit slightly more follicles and retrieve more eggs in certain protocols, both recombinant and urinary-derived gonadotropins result in highly comparable clinical pregnancy and live birth rates (Andersen et al., 2006; Baker et al., 2009; Devroey et al., 2012).
