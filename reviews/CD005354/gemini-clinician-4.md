Selecting the optimal gonadotropin for controlled ovarian stimulation (COS) involves balancing oocyte yield, embryo quality, and patient burden. Based exclusively on primary randomized controlled trials (RCTs), recombinant follicle-stimulating hormone (rFSH) generally provides a more robust follicular response and higher oocyte yield compared to urinary-derived options, but this does not always translate to superior live birth rates or embryo quality.

Here is how rFSH compares to two major alternatives: highly purified human menopausal gonadotropin (HP-hMG) and long-acting recombinant FSH (corifollitropin alfa).

## rFSH vs. Highly Purified hMG (HP-hMG)

HP-hMG (which contains both FSH and LH activity derived from human urine) has been directly compared to rFSH in two of the most foundational, large-scale RCTs in reproductive endocrinology:

* **Oocyte Yield vs. Embryo Quality:** The MERIT trial—a multinational, assessor-blind RCT utilizing a long GnRH agonist protocol—demonstrated that rFSH drives a more vigorous initial ovarian response. Patients treated with rFSH yielded significantly more oocytes (11.8 vs. 10.0) than those treated with HP-hMG (Andersen et al., 2006). However, the HP-hMG cohort produced a significantly higher proportion of top-quality embryos (11.3% vs. 9.0%).
* **Endocrine Environment:** The MERIT trial also highlighted pharmacodynamic differences: rFSH treatment was associated with higher progesterone levels and lower estradiol levels at the end of stimulation, which resulted in an echogenic shift in the endometrium (Andersen et al., 2006).
* **Pregnancy and Live Birth Rates:** Despite the higher oocyte yield with rFSH, both the MERIT and MEGASET trials failed to show a clinical pregnancy advantage for rFSH. In the MEGASET trial—which evaluated patients in a GnRH antagonist cycle with compulsory single-blastocyst transfer—ongoing pregnancy rates after a fresh cycle were 29% for HP-hMG and 27% for rFSH, establishing the non-inferiority of HP-hMG (Devroey et al., 2012).

## Daily rFSH vs. Long-Acting rFSH (Corifollitropin alfa)

For patients where daily injections pose a significant burden, corifollitropin alfa (a recombinant fusion protein acting as a sustained-release FSH) is designed to replace the first seven days of daily rFSH injections.

* **Normal Responders:** The ENGAGE trial, a large double-blind RCT involving over 1,500 patients in a GnRH antagonist protocol, found that a single injection of corifollitropin alfa achieved an ongoing pregnancy rate statistically equivalent to daily rFSH (38.9% vs. 38.1%) (Devroey et al., 2014). Notably, corifollitropin alfa stimulated a slightly higher follicular response, yielding more cumulus-oocyte complexes.
* **Advanced Maternal Age (35–42 years):** In older women with a body weight of ≥50 kg, a randomized trial confirmed that corifollitropin alfa and daily rFSH are equivalent regarding the number of oocytes retrieved, ongoing pregnancy rates, and live birth rates (Vuong et al., 2017).
* **Poor Ovarian Responders (POR):** A recent open-label RCT investigated these two regimens specifically in poor responders. The study found that a single injection of corifollitropin alfa yielded a statistically higher number of mature follicles compared to daily rFSH (5.0 vs 4.2). However, this did not translate to a significant difference in chemical or clinical pregnancy rates between the two groups (Saharkhiz et al., 2024).
