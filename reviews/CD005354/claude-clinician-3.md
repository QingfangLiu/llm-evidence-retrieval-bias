## Summary

Across randomized controlled trials, the comparative picture is nuanced and depends on which gonadotropin and which population is examined:

**rFSH vs. Highly Purified hMG (HP-hMG)** — Multiple RCTs give mixed results. Some (Andersen/Devroey/Arce 2006; the pooled Platteau analysis) found HP-hMG yielded a higher live birth rate despite fewer oocytes retrieved with rFSH producing more oocytes but not necessarily more top-quality embryos. Bosch et al. (2008), in a GnRH-antagonist protocol, found no significant difference in pregnancy rates, though rFSH yielded more oocytes.

**rFSH vs. Urinary hMG/FSH (older preparations)** — Small individual trials (e.g., the pain-perception RCT of rFSH vs. urinary FSH/LH) generally found comparable pregnancy outcomes, with rFSH more expensive but associated with more injection-site pain.

**Special populations** — In PCOS patients, one RCT found rFSH gave more oocytes but similar pregnancy rates, with hMG associated with less OHSS. In poor responders, corifollitropin alfa followed by HP-hMG did not outperform daily rFSH for ongoing pregnancy, though it yielded more cryopreserved embryos.

**Newer rFSH products vs. older rFSH** — Follitropin delta (individualized dosing) was non-inferior to follitropin alfa for pregnancy outcomes (ESTHER-1) with a more favorable response distribution and less OHSS risk. Corifollitropin alfa (single-injection "sustained" FSH) was equivalent/non-inferior to daily rFSH across the Engage, Ensure, and Pursue trials, generally yielding more oocytes.
