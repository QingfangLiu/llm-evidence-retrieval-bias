When evaluating whether blue-light-assisted transurethral resection of bladder tumor (BL-TURBT) is superior to standard white-light (WL-TURBT), primary randomized controlled trials (RCTs) present a nuanced picture. While blue-light cystoscopy consistently improves the visual *detection* of lesions (especially flat, hard-to-see tumors like carcinoma in situ), its impact on long-term clinical outcomes varies depending on the specific patient population.

Here is what the primary literature says regarding your three specific endpoints:

### 1. Preventing Recurrence

The impact on tumor recurrence largely depends on whether the patient is receiving a first-time diagnosis or is a high-risk/recurrent patient.

* **Established Benefit in High-Risk/Recurrent Disease:** Landmark primary RCTs have shown strong recurrence benefits for BL-TURBT. A large multicenter trial utilizing hexaminolevulinate (HAL) found that BL-TURBT significantly reduced recurrence rates at 9 months compared to standard WL-TURBT (47% vs. 56%) (Stenzl et al., 2010). Extended follow-up of this cohort confirmed that this benefit was durable, extending the median time to recurrence to 16.4 months in the blue-light group compared to just 9.4 months in the white-light group (Grossman et al., 2012). Furthermore, in a prospective trial focused specifically on high-risk patients undergoing a repeat resection (re-TURBT), blue-light guidance drastically reduced short-term recurrence rates (11.1% vs. 31.2% for WL-TURBT) (Geavlete et al., 2010).
* **The "First-Diagnosis" Caveat:** For patients presenting with a *first-time* diagnosis of suspected non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC), the advantage is less clear. The recent UK PHOTO RCT, a pragmatic multi-center trial, found no significant difference in 3-year recurrence-free survival between BL-TURBT and WL-TURBT (57.8% vs. approximately 61%), suggesting that standard white light may be sufficient for initial resections in an unselected population (Heer et al., 2022).

### 2. Preventing Progression

While BL-TURBT is highly effective at identifying the high-grade lesions that typically drive disease progression, individual RCTs have struggled to demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in actual progression to muscle-invasive disease or an improvement in overall survival.

* Neither the long-term extension of the HAL landmark trial (Grossman et al., 2012) nor the recent PHOTO trial (Heer et al., 2022) found a definitive survival benefit or a significant reduction in stage progression directly attributable to the use of blue light over white light.

### 3. Avoiding Complications

Using blue-light cystoscopy and its required intravesical photosensitizers (like HAL or 5-ALA) does not significantly increase surgical complications. The procedure is highly tolerable, with adverse events almost entirely attributable to the TURBT procedure itself rather than the optical technology.

* In the PHOTO trial, the frequency of adverse events was very low and virtually identical between the two groups: 5.7% in the BL-TURBT group versus 5.5% in the WL-TURBT group (Heer et al., 2022).
