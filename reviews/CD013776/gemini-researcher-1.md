As an evidence-synthesis researcher, you need the raw primary data—so I have strictly filtered out all systematic reviews, meta-analyses, clinical guidelines, and narrative commentary. The evidence below relies entirely on primary randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and their direct, long-term follow-up cohorts.

The clinical data regarding blue-light-assisted TURBT (BL-TURBT, utilizing hexaminolevulinate) versus standard white-light TURBT (WL-TURBT) for non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC) is nuanced. Early landmark trials demonstrated significant advantages for BL-TURBT in delaying recurrence, but a more recent massive trial complicates the picture for first-diagnosis patients.

## 1. Preventing Cancer Recurrence

The initial landmark phase III RCT established BL-TURBT as superior for detecting lesions and delaying short-term recurrence. At 9 months post-surgery, tumor recurrence was significantly lower in the BL-TURBT group (47%) compared to the standard WL-TURBT group (56%) (Stenzl et al., 2010).

A long-term follow-up of this same cohort confirmed that this clinical advantage persisted over time. The median time to recurrence was significantly extended to 16.4 months in the blue-light group, compared to just 9.4 months in the white-light group (Grossman et al., 2012).

However, the recent UK-based PHOTO trial—a large RCT specifically examining patients with a *first-time* diagnosis of NMIBC—found no significant difference in 3-year recurrence rates between the two methods (Heer et al., 2022). This divergence suggests that while BL-TURBT may be highly beneficial for patients with a history of recurrent disease or multifocal tumors, its recurrence-prevention advantage might be less pronounced in first-diagnosis populations when modern high-definition white-light equipment is used.

## 2. Preventing Disease Progression

Progression (the transition from non-muscle-invasive to muscle-invasive cancer) is a critical survival endpoint. The primary data leans favorably toward BL-TURBT, though the findings often border on the edge of statistical significance due to the long timelines required for muscle-invasive progression.

In their long-term extension study, researchers noted a trend toward lower cystectomy rates (4.8% for blue light vs. 7.9% for white light), though it did not achieve strict statistical significance (Grossman et al., 2012). However, when this long-term data was re-analyzed using the updated International Bladder Cancer Group (IBCG) definitions for disease progression, BL-TURBT patients demonstrated a clinically meaningful trend toward lower progression rates (12.2% vs. 17.6%) and a significantly prolonged time to progression compared to the white-light cohort (Kamat et al., 2016).

## 3. Complications and Adverse Events

Across primary studies, BL-TURBT is consistently shown to be just as safe as standard WL-TURBT. The intravesical instillation of the photosensitizer (hexaminolevulinate) adds negligible systemic toxicity, and the surgical risks are dictated by the resection itself. In the PHOTO trial, the frequency of adverse events was very low and virtually identical between the two arms: 5.7% in the photodynamic diagnosis group versus 5.5% in the standard white-light group (Heer et al., 2022).
