## Bottom line

**Blue-light-assisted TURBT detects more lesions and often leaves less residual tumor, but its effect on clinically meaningful recurrence is inconsistent.** Several older, mostly single-center randomized trials found substantial recurrence reductions. In contrast, the largest recent pragmatic randomized trial and an earlier double-blind placebo-controlled trial found **no recurrence or progression benefit**.

**Progression prevention remains unproven.** A few trials reported fewer progressions, but progression was generally a secondary outcome with few events; the largest contemporary trial showed no significant advantage.

**Intravesical hexaminolevulinate appears to have perioperative complication rates similar to white-light TURBT.** This should not be conflated with **oral 5-aminolevulinic acid**, which can cause frequent intraoperative hypotension and occasional liver-enzyme abnormalities.

## Recent nonrandomized comparative studies

These are useful for contemporary practice but are more susceptible to selection, historical-control and residual-confounding bias.

* **BRIGHT study, Japan:** prospective oral 5-ALA PDD cohort compared with retrospective WL controls. After propensity matching, residual tumor at second TURBT was **25.7% versus 47.3%**, OR 0.39. In a later analysis, matched patients had lower one-year recurrence after second TURBT, HR **0.44**. ([PMC][12])
* **Danish nationwide BCG-treated cohort, 2026:** 4,318 first-diagnosis NMIBC patients. PDD was associated with a modest one-year recurrence reduction, adjusted RR **0.85**, but no significant five-year recurrence benefit, RR **0.94**, and no long-term progression or BCG-response advantage. ([ScienceDirect][13])
* **Japanese oral 5-ALA propensity-matched study, 2023:** recurrence within 500 days favored PDD, HR **0.48**, but systolic BP below 80 mm Hg occurred in **35.6%** of oral-ALA cases; age ≥75 and general anesthesia predicted hypotension. ([PubMed][14])

## Interpretation for counseling

**Recurrence:** It is reasonable to say that blue light probably improves the completeness of the initial resection, particularly when CIS, multifocal disease or subtle lesions are present. However, a guaranteed long-term recurrence reduction cannot be promised. The largest modern RCT found virtually identical outcomes, while several older trials found large benefits.

**Progression:** There is not enough consistent individual-trial evidence to conclude that blue-light TURBT prevents muscle-invasive progression. Positive progression findings come primarily from smaller trials; PHOTO and the placebo-controlled trial were negative.

**Complications:** With **intravesical HAL**, serious perioperative harms appear uncommon and similar to WL-TURBT. With **oral 5-ALA**, hypotension is a material drug-specific risk and liver-enzyme elevation has also been reported; these oral-ALA findings should not be directly attributed to intravesical HAL. A contemporary comparative study also found more moderate/severe hypotension with oral-ALA PDD than with WL-TURBT. ([Wiley Online Library][15])

Overall, the primary-study record supports **better visualization and less residual tumor**, but only **uncertain and context-dependent improvement in recurrence**, **no established progression benefit**, and **no clear surgical-complication advantage** over a high-quality modern white-light TURBT.
