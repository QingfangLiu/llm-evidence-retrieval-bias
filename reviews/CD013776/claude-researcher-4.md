Here's a curated set of **individual primary studies** (randomized controlled trials and prospective comparative studies) on blue-light (photodynamic diagnosis, typically hexaminolevulinate/Hexvix®-based) vs. white-light TURBT for suspected NMIBC. I've excluded reviews, meta-analyses, and guidelines as requested.

**Large multicenter RCTs**

- **Jocham et al., 2005**, *J Urol* — "Improved detection and treatment of bladder cancer using hexaminolevulinate imaging": prospective phase III multicenter RCT establishing early efficacy signal for HAL-guided detection/treatment.

- **Grossman et al., 2007**, *J Urol* — Phase III, multicenter RCT comparing HAL fluorescence cystoscopy with white-light cystoscopy for detecting superficial papillary lesions.

- **Stenzl et al., 2010**, *J Urol* (NCT00233402) — Prospective randomized study enrolling 814 patients at increased risk of recurrence across 28 centers; patients underwent white-light cystoscopy and mapping, then HAL fluorescence cystoscopy patients received additional blue-light inspection before/after TURBT, with follow-up cystoscopy at 3, 6, and 9 months. Found HAL fluorescence cystoscopy significantly improved detection of Ta and T1 lesions and significantly reduced 9-month recurrence.

- **Grossman et al., 2012**, *J Urol* — Long-term follow-up extension of the Stenzl 2010 cohort. Assessed 551 of the original participants (261/280 white-light, 255/271 fluorescence, median follow-up ~53–55 months) and found HAL fluorescence cystoscopy significantly improved long-term recurrence-free survival, with a trend toward improved bladder preservation.

**Other RCTs on recurrence/detection**

- **Geavlete et al., 2009**, *J Endourol* — Compared HAL blue-light cystoscopy with white-light cystoscopy in 64 matched cases each; blue-light diagnosed 95.7% of tumors vs. 66.4% by white light, and the blue-light study group had a recurrence rate of 5.3% vs 23.2% in the white-light control group.

- **Geavlete et al., 2012**, *BJU Int* — Randomized 362 patients to HAL vs. white-light TURBT (all receiving single postoperative mitomycin C), examining whether fluorescence-guided treatment changes affect long-term recurrence.

- **Hermann et al., 2011**, *BJU Int* — Randomized two-center study: fluorescence-guided TURBT reduced bladder tumor recurrence via less residual tumor tissue in Ta/T1 patients.

- **Schumacher et al., 2010**, *Urology* — Prospective randomized study of 446 patients with positive cytology/ultrasound suspicion; high-risk NMIBC patients (CIS, pTaG3, pT1) underwent standard re-TURBT 6 weeks later. Short-term re-TURBT recurrence was 11.1% in the blue-light group vs. 31.2% in the white-light group, with blue-light cystoscopy significantly reducing short-term recurrence across all high-risk categories.

- **O'Brien et al., 2013**, *BJU Int* — Prospective RCT of HAL photodynamic-assisted TURBT plus single-shot mitomycin C vs. conventional white-light TURBT plus mitomycin C in 249 newly presenting suspected NMIBC patients at Guy's Hospital; did not show lower recurrence with HAL-PDD despite more accurate diagnostic assessment — a notable negative/null trial.

- **Unnamed 2-year follow-up RCT**, *International Urology and Nephrology* — "Hexaminolevulinate-guided TURBT does not reduce the recurrence rates after a 2-year follow-up," another prospective randomized trial with a null result.

**Non-inferiority / adjuvant-therapy RCTs**

- **HELENA study (2021)**, *World J Urol* — Multicenter phase III non-inferiority RCT (2010–2016) in 129 patients with EORTC intermediate-risk NMIBC, randomized to white-light TURB plus immediate + maintenance intravesical mitomycin C (n=62) vs. Hexvix® TURB plus immediate-only mitomycin C (n=67), primary endpoint recurrence-free survival at 12 months.

**Recent large pragmatic RCT**

- **PHOTO trial, Grossman/Catto et al. (NEJM Evidence, 2023)** — Pragmatic, open-label, parallel-group RCT across 22 UK NHS hospitals in patients with suspected first-diagnosis intermediate/high-risk NMIBC, randomized 1:1 to PDD-guided vs. standard white-light TURBT, with time to recurrence at 3 years as the primary outcome.

**Recent phase III bridging RCT**

- **China multicenter bridging study (2025)**, *Frontiers in Urology* (NCT05600322) — Prospective, within-patient controlled, multicenter phase III trial at 7 tertiary hospitals (Nov 2022–Jul 2023) comparing HAL blue-light cystoscopy to white-light cystoscopy for bladder cancer detection.

**Single-center comparative study**

- **UK single-centre study**, *BJU Int* (PMC10447208) — Compared blue-light and white-light TURBT with HAL in primary and secondary (re-resection) arms; blue-light re-resection detected significantly more CIS (20.7% vs 51.7% detection difference reported), and overall recurrence rates were 33.3% (primary arm) and 37.5% (secondary arm) at 24 months.

**Large real-world cohort (non-RCT, but primary data)**

- **BRAVO study (2025–2026, Photocure-sponsored)** — Real-world cohort of 626 veterans showing a 38% reduction in recurrence risk with blue-light cystoscopy vs. white-light cystoscopy in high-risk bladder cancer, in routine clinical practice. Note: this is observational/real-world data, not a randomized trial — flagging in case your inclusion criteria are RCT-only.
