Here are individual primary studies (RCTs and prospective comparative studies) comparing blue-light-assisted TURBT (photodynamic diagnosis, PDD) with standard white-light TURBT for non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC). I've excluded reviews, meta-analyses, and guidelines as requested.

## Large multicenter phase III RCTs (recurrence/progression endpoints)

**Stenzl et al., J Urol 2010** — Combined analysis of two prospective randomized multicenter trials using hexaminolevulinate (HAL) blue-light cystoscopy vs. white light in newly diagnosed and recurrent NMIBC. This trial found that hexaminolevulinate-guided fluorescence cystoscopy reduces recurrence in patients with nonmuscle invasive bladder cancer.

**Grossman et al., J Urol 2012** — Long-term follow-up (median 4.5 years) of the Stenzl cohort (255 HAL vs. 261 white-light patients), reporting a durable decrease in recurrence with HAL-enabled fluorescence cystoscopy.

**Kamat et al., Bladder Cancer 2016** — Re-analysis of the same phase III cohort using the newer IBCG progression definition. The study found that in the original analysis, after a median of 4.5 years, 8 HAL patients and 16 white-light patients were deemed to have progressed from NMIBC to muscle-invasive disease, addressing the progression question specifically.

## Other randomized trials (mostly HAL-based)

**Geavlete et al., Urology 2010** — Prospective RCT of 446 patients randomized to blue-light vs. white-light cystoscopy/resection, focused on short-term recurrence at Re-TURBT in high-risk NMIBC (CIS, pTaG3, pT1). The overall short-term recurrence rate at Re-TURBT was 11.1% for the blue-light group versus 31.2% for the white-light group, with blue-light favored across all high-risk subgroups.

**O'Brien et al., BJU Int 2013** — Prospective RCT comparing hexylaminolevulinate PDD-assisted TURBT plus single-shot mitomycin C vs. conventional white-light TURBT plus mitomycin C in newly presenting NMIBC, testing whether blue-light resection lowers recurrence rates.

**Gkritsios et al., Int Urol Nephrol 2014** — Prospective randomized trial (placebo-controlled design referenced via UMIN000008176) reporting that hexaminolevulinate-guided TURBT did **not** significantly reduce recurrence rates over 2-year follow-up — a negative trial worth weighing against the positive ones above.

**Chinese blue-diode-laser RCT (multicenter), PMC 2023** — A different technology (450 nm blue diode laser vs. plasmakinetic electrocautery, not photodynamic dye-based) for TURBT in NMIBC. The novel blue laser was found to be as effective and safe as plasmakinetic electrocautery perioperatively, with the blue laser reducing blood loss and speeding wound healing, though longer follow-up was needed to confirm recurrence-free survival benefit. Note: this is a distinct "blue-light" technology (surgical laser) rather than fluorescence-guided PDD, so make sure it's the comparison you intend.

## Large pragmatic RCT with a negative/null result (important counterweight)

**PHOTO Trial (Heer et al.), NEJM Evidence 2022 / also published as HTA monograph and in European Urology Open Science (cost-effectiveness paper)** — A UK multicenter (22 NHS hospitals), pragmatic, open-label RCT in patients with newly diagnosed intermediate- or high-risk NMIBC, comparing PDD-guided TURBT to white-light TURBT, powered for 3-year recurrence. Over a median follow-up of about 21–22 months, there were 86 recurrences in the PDD group and 84 in the white-light group (3-year recurrence-free survival 57.8% vs. 61.6%; hazard ratio 0.94, 95% CI 0.69–1.28; p = 0.70) — showing no benefit for PDD, and the accompanying economic analysis found it was not cost-effective at 3 years.

## Retrospective/non-randomized comparative primary studies (useful supplementary data, not RCTs)

- **Miyake et al., BJUI Compass Open 2023** — Propensity-score-matched comparative study of 1,578 consecutive primary NMIBC patients (WL-TURBT vs. PDD-TURBT, 2006–2020) using IBCG-defined progression endpoints across nine oncological outcomes.
- **Chan et al. (UK single-center), BJUI Compass 2023** — Non-randomized single-center comparison of HAL blue-light TURBT/biopsy vs. white-light, focused on CIS detection and recurrence rates at 24 months.

## A note on the evidence landscape

You'll notice these primary trials don't all point the same direction — the Stenzl/Grossman/Kamat/Geavlete data show consistent recurrence (and some progression) benefit for blue light, while Gkritsios and especially the large, more recent PHOTO trial found no benefit. The PHOTO investigators and others (e.g., Stenzl's editorial response) have debated whether older positive trials used less-experienced white-light resection technique as the comparator, inflating the apparent blue-light benefit, while PHOTO used "good quality" white-light TURBT as a stronger control — worth considering when weighing generalizability to your practice setting.
