## Clinical bottom line

**Recurrence:** The individual trials are inconsistent. Several older, generally smaller studies found fewer residual tumors or early recurrences with fluorescence-guided TURBT. A large HAL phase III trial also showed a modest reduction in 9-month recurrence and a longer median time to recurrence on extended follow-up. Conversely, several randomized trials found no statistically significant recurrence benefit, including the recent 538-patient pragmatic PHOTO trial. ([PMC][1])

**Progression:** A progression-prevention benefit has **not been convincingly established**. Progression events were uncommon, most trials were not powered for this endpoint, and the larger trials showed no significant difference. A post hoc reanalysis of one HAL trial suggested longer time to a broadly defined progression, but the difference in the proportion progressing was not statistically significant. ([ACS Journals][2])

**Complications:** Blue-light-assisted TURBT generally produced complication and adverse-event rates similar to white-light TURBT. Reported events were predominantly expected TURBT-related problems—hematuria, dysuria, bladder spasms, pain and retention—rather than photosensitizer toxicity. ([PMC][1])

## How I would interpret these studies clinically

The most reproducible advantage is **better visualization and a more complete initial resection**, particularly for CIS, multifocal disease and lesions left after apparently complete white-light resection. Whether this consistently translates into fewer later recurrences appears dependent on patient selection, surgeon/WL-TURBT quality, use of re-TURBT, immediate intravesical chemotherapy and subsequent BCG or chemotherapy.

For counseling a patient, the primary-study evidence supports saying:

* Blue light may decrease residual disease and may reduce recurrence in some settings, but a recurrence benefit is **not guaranteed**, and the largest modern pragmatic trial found none.
* There is currently **no secure evidence that it prevents muscle-invasive progression or bladder-cancer death**.
* It does **not appear to materially increase perioperative complications** compared with standard TURBT.
* The older positive 5-ALA studies should not automatically be treated as equivalent to contemporary HAL/Cysview-assisted TURBT, and several publications are follow-ups of the same randomized cohort rather than independent trials.
