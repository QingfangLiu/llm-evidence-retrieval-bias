## Bottom line

Blue-light-assisted TURBT **finds more tumors—especially flat carcinoma in situ—and may make the initial resection more complete**, but the individual trials disagree on whether this reliably prevents later recurrence. Several older, smaller trials reported meaningful reductions in early or long-term recurrence; other randomized trials, including the large contemporary PHOTO trial, found no recurrence advantage.

For **progression to muscle-invasive or metastatic disease**, no individual study provides convincing proof of benefit. Most trials had too few progression events to answer that question reliably.

For **complications**, comparative trials generally found similar rates with blue and white light. The photosensitizing drugs did not clearly increase bleeding, retention, infection, bladder injury, serious adverse events, or mortality. Blue light may result in additional biopsies of fluorescent but benign inflammatory areas, but this has not translated into a demonstrated increase in major surgical complications.

## Recent nonrandomized primary studies

These are useful for longer-term or contemporary practice questions, but treatment selection and institutional differences can produce confounding.

**Miyake et al., 2023:** Among 1,578 consecutive patients with primary NMIBC, propensity-matched comparisons associated PDD-TURBT with fewer recurrences and fewer broadly defined “IBCG progression” events, such as grade-up or stage-up. There was **no significant reduction in conventional progression to muscle-invasive or metastatic cancer**. ([PubMed][11])

**Hjort et al., 2026:** Danish nationwide cohort of 4,318 first-diagnosis NMIBC patients subsequently treated with BCG; 2,388 had PDD at the primary TURBT. PDD was associated with a modest reduction in recurrence at one year, adjusted RR **0.85** (95% CI 0.76–0.96), but not at five years, RR **0.94** (95% CI 0.87–1.03). There was no long-term progression or BCG-response advantage. Because this was registry based and included only patients who received BCG, it does not establish causality or apply directly to every suspected NMIBC tumor. ([PubMed][12])

## How I would interpret these studies as a patient

The most defensible interpretation of the individual primary studies is:

* **Detection and completeness:** Blue light is likely to uncover additional lesions, particularly CIS, and several trials found much less residual tumor at early repeat TURBT.
* **Recurrence:** A reduction is plausible, particularly when standard white-light resection might otherwise miss multifocal, recurrent or flat disease. However, the benefit is not consistent when blue light is compared with high-quality white-light TURBT plus contemporary intravesical treatment.
* **Progression:** There is currently no dependable individual-trial evidence that blue light prevents muscle-invasive disease, metastasis, cystectomy or bladder-cancer death.
* **Complications:** Blue light itself appears **about as safe as white light**. The major risks remain the usual TURBT risks—bleeding, infection, bladder perforation, urinary retention and anesthesia-related complications—rather than a clearly demonstrated added risk from HAL or 5-ALA.

Because the suspected tumor’s number, appearance, cytology, likelihood of CIS, prior recurrences, and the surgeon’s experience strongly affect the potential value, a practical discussion would be whether blue light is expected to change the completeness of **your first resection**, rather than assuming it has been proven to prevent progression.
