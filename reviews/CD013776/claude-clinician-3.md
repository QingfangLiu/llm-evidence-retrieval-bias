Here are the primary studies (RCTs and other original research, not reviews/meta-analyses) comparing blue-light TURBT to standard white-light TURBT. I've grouped them by whether they used hexaminolevulinate (HAL/Hexvix) or 5-ALA as the photosensitizer, since these are the two agents studied.

## Hexaminolevulinate (HAL/Hexvix)-based trials

**Stenzl et al. 2010, *J Urol*** — Multicenter RCT; found hexaminolevulinate-guided fluorescence cystoscopy reduced recurrence in NMIBC patients compared with white light alone.

**Grossman et al. 2007, *J Urol* (PC B302/01 Study Group)** — Phase III multicenter trial comparing HAL fluorescence cystoscopy with white light cystoscopy for detecting superficial papillary lesions.

**Grossman et al. 2012, *J Urol*** — Follow-up analysis reporting a long-term decrease in bladder cancer recurrence with HAL-enabled fluorescence cystoscopy versus white light.

**O'Brien et al. 2013, *BJU Int*** — Prospective RCT of HAL photodynamic-assisted TURBT plus single-shot mitomycin C versus conventional white-light TURBT plus mitomycin C in newly diagnosed NMIBC.

**Geavlete et al. 2010, *Urology*** — Prospective randomized study of HAL blue-light cystoscopy in high-risk NMIBC, focused on recurrence rates at re-resection (re-TURBT).

**Gkritsios et al. 2014** — Prospective randomized trial reporting that HAL-guided TURBT did **not** reduce recurrence rates at 2-year follow-up (a negative/null result, useful for balance).

**HELENA study (2010–2016 enrollment, published ~2021, *World J Urol*)** — Multicenter phase III non-inferiority RCT comparing Hexvix-guided TURB with immediate instillation chemotherapy vs. white-light TURB with immediate plus maintenance intravesical chemotherapy, primary endpoint recurrence-free survival at 12 months.

**Daneshmand et al. 2018, *J Urol*** — Phase III multicenter comparative study of blue-light *flexible* cystoscopy (office surveillance setting, not resection itself) with HAL vs. white light; relevant mainly for detection/adverse-event data rather than TURBT outcomes directly.

**Chinese bridging study, 2023–2024 (NCT05600322)** — Prospective, within-patient-controlled, multicenter phase III trial in China comparing HAL blue-light cystoscopy to white-light cystoscopy for detection.

## 5-ALA-based trials

**Schumacher et al. 2010, *Eur Urol*** — Prospective randomized multicenter study of TURBT with or without 5-aminolevulinic acid under visible/fluorescent light.

**Stenzl/Penkoff et al. 2011, *Cancer*** — Multicenter randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of 5-ALA-induced fluorescence cystoscopy for detection and clinical outcome.

**Hermann et al.** — Randomized two-center study finding fluorescence-guided TURBT reduced recurrence through less residual tumor tissue in Ta/T1 patients (specific journal/year not confirmed in these results — verify citation before using).

## Pragmatic, more recent RCT (contradicts older trials)

**PHOTO Trial (Heer et al.)** — UK NIHR-funded pragmatic, open-label, multicenter RCT (22 hospitals; 209 vs. 217 patients) in first-diagnosis intermediate/high-risk NMIBC. This is the most methodologically rigorous recent primary trial and is important because it diverges from earlier positive studies:
- Recurrences occurred in 86 patients in the PDD group and 84 patients in the WL group, with a hazard ratio of 0.94 (P = .7), and 3-year recurrence rates were similar at 57.8% versus 61.6%.
- Primary clinical report: NEJM Evidence, 2022 (Heer et al., "A Randomized Trial of PHOTOdynamic Surgery in Non–Muscle-Invasive Bladder Cancer").
- Companion cost-effectiveness paper: found no evidence of a difference in either costs or quality-adjusted life years over 3-year follow-up between PDD-TURBT and WL-TURBT, concluding PDD-TURBT is not supported for managing primary intermediate- or high-risk NMIBC (Yu et al., *European Urology Open Science*, 2023).
- Complications/safety were tracked using the Clavien–Dindo classification for surgical complications and NCI-CTCAE v4.0 for adverse events up to 3 months post-TURBT — useful if you specifically want their complication data.
- Full trial protocol/methods are also published as an NIHR Health Technology Assessment monograph (Heer et al., 2022).

## A note on evidence divergence

You'll notice the older single/multi-center HAL and 5-ALA trials (mostly 2010–2013, often industry- or single-country-sponsored) tend to show recurrence/progression benefit with blue light, while the more recent, larger, pragmatic PHOTO trial (UK NHS setting, 2022) found no benefit. Since you asked me to exclude synthesis pieces, I'd flag that this pattern of divergence is worth noting yourself, or reading the individual methods sections (e.g., differences in resection technique standardization, use of adjuvant intravesical therapy, risk-group inclusion, and blinding) to judge why results differ, since I can't provide that adjudication as a review.
