I've excluded reviews, meta-analyses, and guidelines as requested.

**Randomized controlled trials**

- **Stenzl A, Burger M, Fradet Y, et al. "Hexaminolevulinate guided fluorescence cystoscopy reduces recurrence in patients with nonmuscle invasive bladder cancer." *J Urol* 2010;184(5):1907-1913.** A multicenter trial testing whether hexaminolevulinate-guided fluorescence cystoscopy reduces recurrence in NMIBC patients compared with white-light resection.

- **Grossman HB, Stenzl A, Fradet Y, et al. "Long-term decrease in bladder cancer recurrence with hexaminolevulinate enabled fluorescence cystoscopy." *J Urol* 2012;188(1):58-62.** Long-term follow-up of the Stenzl 2010 cohort assessing durability of the recurrence benefit.

- **O'Brien T, Ray E, Chatterton K, et al. "Prospective randomized trial of hexylaminolevulinate photodynamic-assisted TURBT plus single-shot intravesical mitomycin C vs conventional white-light TURBT plus mitomycin C in newly presenting non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer." *BJU Int* 2013;112(8):1096-1104.** This single-center trial (Guy's Hospital, 249 patients enrolled 2005-2010) tested whether HAL-PDD-assisted resection plus single-shot mitomycin C lowered recurrence versus white-light TURBT plus mitomycin C. It concluded that despite better diagnostic accuracy with HAL-PDD, this trial did not demonstrate lower recurrence rates compared with standard-of-care white-light TURBT plus MMC — one of the few negative trials in this literature.

- **Stenzl A, Penkoff H, Dajc-Sommerer E, et al. "Detection and clinical outcome of urinary bladder cancer with 5-aminolevulinic acid-induced fluorescence cystoscopy: a multicenter randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial." *Cancer* 2011;117:938-947.** A placebo-controlled trial using 5-ALA (rather than hexaminolevulinate) fluorescence.

- **Hermann GG, et al. "Fluorescence-guided transurethral resection of bladder tumours reduces bladder tumour recurrence due to less residual tumour tissue in Ta/T1 patients: a randomized two-centre study."** A two-center randomized trial focused on residual tumor tissue as a mechanism for recurrence reduction.

- **HELENA study (Comperat, Doerfler, et al.). "Hexvix®-TURB vs. white-light TURB followed by intravesical adjuvant chemotherapy — a prospective randomized controlled open-label multicenter non-inferiority study."** PMID 34002265. A multicenter phase III non-inferiority trial in 129 patients with EORTC intermediate-risk NMIBC, comparing Hexvix-guided TURB with single instillation of chemotherapy against white-light TURB with immediate instillation followed by 6 months of maintenance intravesical chemotherapy, with 12-month recurrence-free survival as the primary endpoint.

- **FinnBladder 9 trial (Boström P, et al.), presented EAU 2026, NCT01675219.** A randomized trial in 255 patients with high-recurrence-risk, low-grade pTa NMIBC, comparing white-light TURBT alone against blue-light TURBT combined with optimized adjuvant mitomycin C, among other arms; the blue-light-plus-optimized-MMC arm showed a 58% lower recurrence risk (HR 0.42) versus white-light TURBT alone.

- **PHOTO trial. "A Randomized Trial of PHOTOdynamic Surgery in Non–Muscle-Invasive Bladder Cancer." *NEJM Evidence* 2023.** A pragmatic, open-label, parallel-group randomized trial across 22 UK NHS hospitals in patients with suspected first-diagnosis intermediate- or high-risk NMIBC, randomizing 1:1 to PDD-guided TURBT versus standard white-light TURBT, with time to recurrence at 3 years as the primary outcome.

**Prospective controlled (non-randomized) study**

- **Mariappan P, Rai B, El-Mokadem I, et al. "'Real-life experience': recurrence rate at 3 years with Hexvix® photodynamic diagnosis-assisted TURBT compared with good quality white light TURBT in new NMIBC — a prospective controlled study." *World J Urol* 2017.** Found that when PDD was adopted for all new bladder tumor resections in routine practice, it was associated with significantly reduced recurrence rates at 3 years, particularly in high-risk patients.

A few notes for your discussion with your urologist:
- These trials mostly used hexaminolevulinate (Hexvix/Cysview) or 5-ALA as the photosensitizing agent for blue-light cystoscopy — this is the standard "blue-light TURBT" technology in current use.
- Results are mixed at the individual-trial level (O'Brien's trial was negative on recurrence), which is part of why the pooled evidence is described as "low certainty" in aggregate analyses — worth keeping in mind since you asked for primary studies specifically rather than a synthesized answer.
