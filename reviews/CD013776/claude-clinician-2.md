I have enough to compile a good set of individual primary studies. Here's a curated list of primary studies (RCTs and prospective controlled studies) comparing blue-light/photodynamic-guided TURBT with standard white-light TURBT — no reviews, meta-analyses, or guidelines included.

## Randomized controlled trials

**1. Heer et al., "A Randomized Trial of PHOTOdynamic Surgery in Non–Muscle-Invasive Bladder Cancer," *NEJM Evidence* 2022** (the PHOTO trial)
Pragmatic, open-label, parallel-group RCT across 22 UK NHS hospitals in patients with a first suspected diagnosis of intermediate- or high-risk NMIBC. Participants were assigned 1:1 to photodynamic diagnosis (PDD)-guided TURBT or standard white-light-guided TURBT, with the primary outcome being time to recurrence at 3 years. This is the largest and most methodologically rigorous modern trial; it found no difference in recurrence between PDD and white-light TURBT over 3 years in real-world practice, a result that diverges from earlier, smaller HAL trials. A linked cost-effectiveness analysis (Yu et al., *Eur Urol Open Sci* 2023) found PDD was not more cost-effective.

**2. Stenzl et al., "Hexaminolevulinate guided fluorescence cystoscopy reduces recurrence in patients with nonmuscle invasive bladder cancer," *J Urol* 2010**
Prospective randomized study of 814 patients at increased risk for recurrence, comparing white-light cystoscopy/TURBT alone versus white-light plus hexaminolevulinate (HAL) blue-light inspection before and after resection. Concluded that HAL fluorescence cystoscopy significantly improved detection of Ta/T1 lesions and significantly reduced tumor recurrence at 9 months. (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT00233402)

**3. Grossman et al., "Long-term decrease in bladder cancer recurrence with hexaminolevulinate enabled fluorescence cystoscopy," *J Urol* 2012**
Long-term extension of the Stenzl 2010 cohort. Long-term follow-up was assessed in 551 of the original 551 participants (280 white-light, 271 fluorescence) enrolled in the prospective randomized study. Reports durability of recurrence reduction beyond the initial 9-month endpoint.

**4. O'Brien et al., "Prospective randomized trial of hexylaminolevulinate photodynamic-assisted transurethral resection of bladder tumour (TURBT) plus single-shot intravesical mitomycin C vs conventional white-light TURBT plus mitomycin C in newly presenting non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer," *BJU Int* 2013**
RCT testing HAL-PDD TURBT plus single-shot MMC against white-light TURBT plus MMC in newly presenting NMIBC.

**5. Schumacher/HELENA study — "Hexvix®-TURB vs. white-light TURB followed by intravesical adjuvant chemotherapy," multicentre phase III non-inferiority RCT**
Between 2010 and 2016, 129 patients with EORTC intermediate-risk NMIBC were randomized to white-light TURB with immediate instillation chemotherapy (ICT) followed by maintenance ICT (n=62) or Hexvix-guided TURB with immediate ICT only (n=67), with recurrence-free survival at 12 months as the primary endpoint. This trial tested whether blue-light guidance could allow omission of maintenance chemotherapy while remaining non-inferior.

**6. Daniltchenko et al., "Long-term benefit of 5-aminolevulinic acid fluorescence assisted transurethral resection of superficial bladder cancer: 5-year results of a prospective randomized study," *J Urol* 2005**
Earlier-generation photosensitizer (5-ALA rather than HAL) RCT with 5-year recurrence follow-up.

**7. Geavlete et al., "Hexaminolevulinate fluorescence cystoscopy and transurethral resection of the bladder in noninvasive bladder tumors," *J Endourol* 2009**, and the related **"HAL blue-light cystoscopy in high-risk nonmuscle-invasive bladder cancer—re-TURBT recurrence rates in a prospective, randomized study"**
Prospective randomized studies focused on re-resection settings in high-risk NMIBC.

**8. FinnBladder 9 trial** (Boström et al., presented EAU 2026; formal peer-reviewed publication pending)
Prospective randomized trial testing photodynamic (blue-light) bladder tumor resection with or without adjuvant optimized intravesical mitomycin C; the arm receiving blue-light TURBT plus adjuvant optimized MMC had a 58% lower recurrence risk (HR 0.42, 95% CI 0.22–0.81, p=0.010) versus standard white-light TURBT alone. Note this trial isolates the combined effect of blue light plus an optimized chemotherapy protocol, not blue light alone.

**9. Frontiers in Urology (2025) China bridging study** — prospective, comparative, within-patient controlled multicenter phase III study evaluating HAL blue-light cystoscopy versus white-light cystoscopy for bladder cancer detection. Primarily a detection-accuracy trial rather than a recurrence/progression outcome trial, but individual-patient RCT-design data.

## Non-randomized prospective comparative studies (useful supplementary primary data, not RCTs)

**10. Mariappan, Rai, El-Mokadem, "Real-life experience: early recurrence with Hexvix photodynamic diagnosis-assisted TURBT vs good-quality white-light TURBT," *World J Urol* 2017**
Prospective controlled study of 808 patients recruited over 4 years, comparing "good quality" white-light TURBT (2007–08 cohort) with PDD-TURBT (2009–10 cohort), matched 1:1 on demographic and pathological criteria; recurrence at 3 years was significantly lower with PDD overall (39.0% vs 53.3%, OR 0.56) and in the matched analysis (24.6% vs 50.0%, OR 0.33), with the largest benefit in high-risk patients (52.1% vs 80%, OR 0.27). This is an important real-world (non-randomized) comparison, contrasting with the negative PHOTO RCT result above.

**11. Chan et al., "Blue light transurethral resection and biopsy of bladder cancer with hexaminolevulinate: Histopathological characteristics and recurrence rates in a single UK centre study," *BJUI Compass* 2023**
Single-center prospective study (not randomized) of 101 patients, primary and re-resection arms, evaluating CIS detection and 24-month recurrence.

## A note on interpretation

These primary studies point in different directions depending on era, methodology, and outcome definitions. Earlier industry-sponsored HAL trials (Stenzl 2010, Grossman 2012) and real-world cohort data (Mariappan 2017) consistently favored blue light for reducing recurrence, and this is reflected in pooled meta-analytic estimates. However, the largest, most recent, pragmatic, publicly funded UK trial (PHOTO, 2022) — designed specifically to test blue light under everyday practice conditions rather than in a specialized trial setting — found no significant difference in 3-year recurrence, and its authors speculated this might reflect improvements in "ordinary" white-light technique and surgeon learning curves for PDD.
