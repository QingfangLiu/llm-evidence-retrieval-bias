Based on my search, here are individual primary studies (randomized controlled trials and prospective comparative studies) — not reviews or guidelines — comparing blue-light/photodynamic-diagnosis (PDD) TURBT with standard white-light TURBT for suspected NMIBC:

**Recent large trials**

- **PHOTO trial (Heer et al., NEJM Evidence)** — A pragmatic, open-label, parallel-group RCT across 22 UK NHS hospitals. Patients with a suspected first diagnosis of intermediate/high-risk NMIBC were randomized 1:1 to hexaminolevulinate PDD-guided TURBT or standard white-light TURBT, with time to recurrence at 3 years as the primary outcome, and outcome assessors kept masked to allocation. This trial also included a cost-effectiveness analysis, finding PDD-guided TURBT was more costly than white-light TURBT with no clear quality-of-life difference.

- **FinnBladder 9 (Boström et al.)** — A four-arm RCT (NCT01675219) in patients with pTa low-grade NMIBC at high recurrence risk, comparing white-light TURBT alone, blue-light TURBT alone, white-light TURBT plus adjuvant optimized mitomycin C, and blue-light TURBT plus adjuvant optimized mitomycin C. The combination of blue-light TURBT with adjuvant optimized mitomycin C significantly lowered recurrence risk compared with standard white-light TURBT alone, while recurrence risk did not differ among the other arms.

**Earlier foundational RCTs**

- **Stenzl et al., J Urol 2010** — A prospective randomized study enrolling 814 patients suspected of having bladder cancer at increased recurrence risk, comparing hexaminolevulinate fluorescence cystoscopy with white light, with a within-patient detection comparison design.

- **Grossman et al., J Urol 2012 (long-term follow-up of the Stenzl cohort)** — Assessed long-term recurrence outcomes in the same trial population.

- **O'Brien et al. (BJU Int, 2013)** — RCT comparing hexylaminolevulinate photodynamic-assisted TURBT plus single-shot intravesical mitomycin C against conventional white-light TURBT plus mitomycin C in newly presenting NMIBC; notably this trial did not find that HAL-PDD led to lower recurrence rates compared with best current standard of care.

- **HELENA study (multicenter phase III)** — A prospective randomized open-label non-inferiority trial (2010–2016) in 129 patients with EORTC intermediate-risk NMIBC, comparing white-light TURB with immediate and maintenance intravesical chemotherapy versus Hexvix (hexaminolevulinate) blue-light TURB with immediate chemotherapy only, powered on 12-month recurrence-free survival.

- **Schumacher et al., Eur Urol 2010** — Prospective, randomized, multicentre study of TURBT with or without 5-aminolevulinic acid–induced fluorescence guidance.

- **Denzinger et al., Urology 2007** — 8-year results of a prospective randomized study on 5-ALA fluorescence-guided resection versus standard resection.

- **Geavlete et al., Urology 2010 / J Endourology 2009** — Prospective randomized studies of HAL blue-light cystoscopy in high-risk NMIBC, examining re-TURBT recurrence rates.

- **Fradet et al., J Urol 2007** and **Grossman et al., J Urol 2007** — Phase III multicenter trials comparing hexaminolevulinate fluorescence cystoscopy with white-light cystoscopy for detecting carcinoma in situ and papillary lesions, respectively (diagnostic-accuracy focused, feeding into later recurrence trials).

A note on scope: most controlled trials in this space test **hexaminolevulinate (Hexvix/Cysview)-based blue-light PDD** rather than blue-light alone as a distinct optical modality, since blue-light illumination requires a fluorescent photosensitizer to have diagnostic effect. If your synthesis needs to distinguish PDD-agent trials from device/technology-only comparisons, that's a distinction worth applying during your own screening.
