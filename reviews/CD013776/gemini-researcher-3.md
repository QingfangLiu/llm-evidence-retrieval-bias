When evaluating blue-light assisted transurethral resection of bladder tumor (BL-TURBT) versus standard white-light (WL-TURBT) for non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC), primary study evidence is somewhat divided. Earlier, highly controlled trials demonstrate a clear advantage for blue light in detecting hidden tumors and reducing recurrence, while a more recent, large-scale pragmatic trial suggests the real-world difference may be negligible.

Here is a synthesis of the key primary studies—randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and prospective cohorts—addressing recurrence, progression, and safety.

## 1. Preventing Recurrence and Residual Tumors

The premise of BL-TURBT—often utilizing hexaminolevulinate (HAL) or 5-aminolevulinic acid (5-ALA)—is that it illuminates malignant tissue that white light misses, particularly flat, high-grade lesions like carcinoma in situ (CIS).

**The Evidence Supporting Blue Light:**
Several foundational RCTs and prospective studies have demonstrated that enhanced detection directly translates to lower recurrence rates:

* **Improved Detection and Management:** A large prospective US registry (N = 533) demonstrated that adding BL-TURBT to standard WL-TURBT increased the detection of CIS by 43% and papillary lesions by 12%. Because these hidden lesions were found and resected, clinical management was altered in 14% of cases (Daneshmand et al., 2018).
* **Reduced Long-Term Recurrence:** In a landmark multicenter RCT (N = 814), researchers found a significant improvement in the time to recurrence for patients receiving HAL BL-TURBT compared to WL-TURBT at a 9-month endpoint (Stenzl et al., 2010). Long-term follow-up of this same trial confirmed that these early recurrence reductions were durable, solidifying the role of BL-TURBT in optimizing initial clearance (Grossman et al., 2012).
* **Lower Residual Tumor Rates:** The BRIGHT study, a prospective cohort comparing initial BL-TURBT to retrospective WL-TURBT in high-risk NMIBC patients, found that patients treated with blue light had significantly lower residual tumor rates at their second (restaging) TURBT (25.7% vs. 47.3%), cutting the odds of residual tumors by more than half (Kobayashi et al., 2022).

**The Counter-Evidence (The PHOTO Trial):**
Despite the positive findings in earlier trials, the recent UK-based PHOTO RCT (N = 538) challenged the routine use of BL-TURBT. Designed as a pragmatic, open-label trial reflecting standard clinical practice, it evaluated patients with suspected first-diagnosis, intermediate- or high-risk NMIBC. Over a median follow-up of 21–22 months, the 3-year recurrence-free survival rate was virtually identical: 57.8% for the blue-light group and 54.6% for the white-light group (Heer et al., 2022). The authors concluded that when standard WL-TURBT is performed at a high quality, adding blue light does not significantly reduce recurrence.

## 2. Preventing Disease Progression

While blue light undoubtedly detects more tumors, proving that this detection halts progression to muscle-invasive disease has been statistically difficult for primary studies.

* Most primary RCTs are powered to measure recurrence rather than progression, as progression events are relatively rare and require long-term follow-up.
* In the PHOTO trial, the researchers noted no significant difference between the blue-light and white-light arms regarding disease progression or overall survival (Heer et al., 2022).

## 3. Avoiding Complications and Safety

The evidence strongly agrees that BL-TURBT does not increase the risk of surgical complications or severe adverse events compared to WL-TURBT.

* **Systemic Safety:** In the prospective US registry of 533 patients, repeat use of the HAL photosensitizer was found to be exceptionally safe, with only one mild dermatologic hypersensitivity reaction (0.2%) reported (Daneshmand et al., 2018).
* **Surgical Complications:** In the PHOTO RCT, the frequency of adverse events was both low and statistically similar between the two cohorts (5.7% in the blue-light group vs. 5.5% in the white-light group) (Heer et al., 2022).
