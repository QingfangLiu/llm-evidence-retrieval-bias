## Bottom line

The primary comparative evidence does **not** show a uniform oncologic advantage for blue-light/PDD-assisted TURBT.

* **Residual tumor and very early recurrence:** Blue light generally performs better, consistent with more complete lesion detection and resection.
* **Later recurrence:** Results are heterogeneous. Many older, smaller trials reported substantial reductions, whereas several multicenter or pragmatically conducted trials—including PHOTO—found no meaningful benefit.
* **Progression:** Evidence is sparse and inconsistent. One randomized trial reported a significant reduction, but the largest contemporary trial did not.
* **Complications:** Intravesical hexaminolevulinate or 5-ALA appears approximately as safe as white-light TURBT, but harms were often inadequately reported. **Oral 5-ALA is different** and is associated with clinically important perioperative hypotension and occasional liver-enzyme elevation.

I searched for head-to-head primary studies published through **July 18, 2026** and excluded reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, opinions, and editorials.

## More recent nonrandomized comparative studies

These are useful for applicability but are more vulnerable to surgeon-, center-, calendar-time and treatment-selection confounding.

* **Hoogeveen et al., 2023:** retrospective comparison of 91 PDD and 124 white-light patients; no significant recurrence difference through 60 months, HR **1.12**. ([PubMed][19])
* **Kobayashi et al., 2023:** propensity-matched oral 5-ALA study, 119 patients per group; short-term recurrence favored PDD, HR **0.48**, but systolic blood pressure below 80 mmHg occurred in **35.6%** of oral 5-ALA cases. ([PubMed][20])
* **Japanese experienced-institute cohort, 2023:** 1,578 primary NMIBC cases, with 495 matched per group. PDD was associated with less recurrence and less broadly defined stage/grade progression, but **not less conventional progression to muscle-invasive or metastatic disease**. ([PubMed][21])
* **BRIGHT study, 2024:** prospective PDD cohort compared with retrospectively registered white-light controls; 146 matched per group. One-year recurrence favored oral 5-ALA PDD, HR **0.44**. The nonconcurrent control design limits causal interpretation. ([PubMed][22])
* **Matsushita et al., 2025:** retrospective study of 326 patients older than 70 years. PDD was associated with better recurrence-free survival after matching, but oral 5-ALA produced more intraoperative hypotension and AST/ALT elevation. ([PubMed][23])
* **Danish registry study, 2025:** 8,502 primary NMIBC cases classified according to each department’s level of PDD use. Higher-use departments had lower recurrence and progression risks, particularly for high-risk disease, but exposure was assigned at department rather than individual-patient level. ([PubMed][24])

## Interpretation for an evidence synthesis

### Recurrence

The discrepancy is unlikely to be explained by one factor. Older positive trials frequently had small analyzed samples, high postrandomization exclusions, single-center designs, short follow-up or outcomes dominated by residual disease. Results also varied with surgical expertise, immediate chemotherapy, mandatory repeat TURBT, primary versus recurrent disease, tumor multiplicity and risk group. PHOTO studied contemporary specialist practice and found no benefit, suggesting that blue light’s incremental value may shrink when baseline white-light TURBT quality is high. ([PubMed][14])

### Progression

A progression-prevention effect is **not established**. Rolevich reported a significant benefit, while Stenzl 2011 and Schumacher found no difference, Daniltchenko reported only a numerical imbalance, and PHOTO found no significant benefit with numerically more progression events in the PDD arm. The trials were generally underpowered for progression. ([PubMed][12])

### Complications

For **intravesical HAL or 5-ALA**, comparative trial data suggest similar perioperative and urinary adverse-event rates, not fewer complications. Kriegmair found no side-effect difference, Stenzl reported almost identical overall event rates, O’Brien reported no HAL-related events, and PHOTO found uncommon, similar events. ([ScienceDirect][15])

For **oral 5-ALA**, safety should be analyzed separately. Comparative and perioperative cohorts consistently identify hypotension as a route-specific systemic risk, particularly in older patients and with general anesthesia; liver-enzyme elevation has also been reported. ([PubMed][20])

## Practical synthesis conclusion

**Blue-light-assisted TURBT is better at identifying otherwise occult lesions and often reduces residual tumor. It may reduce recurrence in some settings, but the recurrence benefit is inconsistent and was absent in the largest contemporary pragmatic RCT. A reduction in clinically consequential progression has not been reliably demonstrated. Intravesical photosensitizers appear similarly safe to white-light TURBT rather than safer; oral 5-ALA carries a distinct hypotension risk.**

For extraction and meta-analysis, I would stratify the studies by:

1. Intravesical HAL, intravesical 5-ALA or oral 5-ALA.
2. Primary versus recurrent suspected tumor.
3. Residual disease at planned repeat TURBT versus subsequent recurrence.
4. Use and balance of immediate intravesical chemotherapy and later BCG.
5. Contemporary specialist-quality TURBT versus older routine practice.
6. Conventional progression to muscle-invasive/metastatic disease versus broader stage- or grade-up definitions.
