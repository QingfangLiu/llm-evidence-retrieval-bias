Here are individual primary studies comparing blue-light (photodynamic diagnosis, PDD/HAL, or 5-ALA) TURBT with standard white-light TURBT for non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC). I've excluded systematic reviews, meta-analyses, narrative reviews, guidelines, and editorials/comments.

**Randomized controlled trials**

- **Stenzl et al. (2010), *J Urol*** — Prospective RCT of 814 patients at increased recurrence risk. All patients underwent white light cystoscopy and mapping of lesions, followed by transurethral resection, and patients in the fluorescence group also received intravesical hexaminolevulinate to induce fluorescence of cancerous lesions. Hexaminolevulinate fluorescence cystoscopy significantly improved detection of Ta and T1 lesions and significantly reduced the rate of tumor recurrence at 9 months (NCT00233402).

- **Grossman et al. (2012), *J Urol*** — Long-term follow-up of the above cohort. Long-term follow-up was assessed in 551 participants enrolled in the original prospective, randomized study, with 280 patients in the white-light group and 271 in the fluorescence group followed with cystoscopy at 3, 6, and 9 months after initial resection or until recurrence, then extended for long-term outcomes.

- **Grossman et al. (2007), *J Urol* 178:62–67** — Phase III multicenter trial comparing hexaminolevulinate fluorescence cystoscopy against white-light cystoscopy for detecting superficial papillary lesions in bladder cancer patients.

- **Fradet et al. (2007), *J Urol* 178:68–73** — Companion phase III multicenter trial comparing hexaminolevulinate fluorescence cystoscopy vs. white light for detection of carcinoma in situ (CIS).

- **Jocham et al. (2005), *J Urol* 174:862–866** — Prospective, phase III multicenter study of hexaminolevulinate imaging vs. white light for bladder tumor detection and treatment.

- **O'Brien et al. (2013), *BJU International*** — Prospective RCT at Guy's Hospital. Investigators conducted a prospective randomized trial of hexylaminolevulinate photodynamic-diagnosis-assisted TURBT plus single-shot intravesical mitomycin C vs standard white-light-assisted TURBT plus single-shot mitomycin C; 249 patients with newly presenting suspected NMIBC were enrolled at Guy's Hospital between March 2005 and April 2010, with 90% of eligible patients agreeing to randomization.

- **Schumacher et al. (2010), *Eur Urol* 57:293–299** — Prospective, randomized multicenter study of TURBT with or without 5-aminolevulinic acid under visible and fluorescent light.

- **Denzinger et al. (2007), *Urology* 69:675–679** — 8-year results of a prospective randomized study assessing recurrence risk reduction with 5-ALA-induced fluorescence diagnosis.

- **Babjuk et al. (2005), *BJU International* 96:798–802** — RCT testing whether 5-ALA-induced fluorescence cystoscopy during TURBT reduces recurrence risk in stage Ta/T1 bladder cancer.

- **Filbeck et al. (2002), *J Urol* 168:67–71** — RCT reporting clinically relevant improvement in recurrence-free survival with 5-ALA-induced fluorescence diagnosis in superficial bladder tumors.

- **PHOTO Trial — Heer et al. (2022–2023), *NEJM Evidence* / *European Urology* / NIHR Health Technology Assessment** — Large pragmatic UK multicenter RCT (22 NHS hospitals). Participants with a suspected first diagnosis of NMIBC at intermediate or high risk for recurrence were assigned 1:1 to PDD-guided TURBT or standard white-light-guided TURBT, with the primary outcome being time to recurrence at 3 years. The findings revealed no significant difference in the primary outcome measure, and cost-effectiveness analysis showed PDD-TURBT was not more cost-effective than white-light TURBT at 3 years. This trial also separately reported on adverse events and complications up to 3 months from TURBT, using the Clavien–Dindo classification for surgical complications and NCI CTCAE v4.0 for adverse events.

- **HELENA Study — (2010–2016), published in *World Journal of Urology* (PMC8521513)** — A prospective randomized controlled open-label multicenter non-inferiority study; 129 patients with EORTC intermediate-risk NMIBC treated with TURB were randomized to either white-light TURB with immediate and maintenance intravesical chemotherapy, or Hexvix-guided TURB with immediate instillation only, with recurrence-free survival at 12 months as the primary endpoint.

- **FinnBladder 9 Trial — Boström et al. (2026), presented at EAU 2026** — A prospective randomized trial comparing photodynamic (blue-light) bladder tumor resection to traditional TURBT with or without adjuvant optimized intravesical mitomycin C; the arm receiving blue-light TURBT plus adjuvant optimized mitomycin C had a 58% lower recurrence risk (HR 0.42, 95% CI 0.22–0.81, p=0.010) versus standard white-light TURBT alone.

**Prospective (non-randomized) controlled studies**

- **Filadelfo et al. — 'Real-life' UK study, *World Journal of Urology* (PMC5693980)** — A prospective controlled study recruiting all new, consecutive NMIBC patients receiving "good quality" first TURBT over a 4-year period, comparing white-light TURBT (2007–08) with PDD-TURBT (2009–10), matched 1:1 on demographic and pathological criteria; recurrence at 3 years was significantly lower with PDD (24.6% vs 50.0%, OR 0.33, p<0.001).

- **UK single-centre study, *PMC10447208*** — Retrospective/prospective comparison of blue-light TURBT and biopsy with hexaminolevulinate vs. white-light TURBT, reporting histopathological detection rates and recurrence outcomes, including higher secondary CIS detection with HAL-PDD.

- **Person-time cumulative incidence study, *PMC7911613*** — Compared oral 5-ALA PDD-assisted TURBT to conventional white-light TURBT in 100 patients with primary NMIBC (40 white-light, 60 PDD), finding PDD assistance was an independent predictor of better recurrence outcome (HR 0.39, 95% CI 0.16–0.95).

**A note on interpretation:** these individual trials vary in size, risk-group inclusion criteria, adjuvant chemotherapy use, and follow-up duration, and their results are not uniform — most earlier single- and multi-center HAL/5-ALA trials found reduced recurrence with blue-light TURBT, while the more recent, larger, pragmatic PHOTO trial found no significant difference in recurrence at 3 years.
