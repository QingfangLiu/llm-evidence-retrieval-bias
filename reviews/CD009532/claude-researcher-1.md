I've compiled a set of individual primary studies (RCTs and one prospective cohort trial) on iron supplementation in blood donors, excluding all reviews/guidelines/comments. Here they are, organized chronologically with key design details:

## Primary studies: iron supplementation in blood donors

**1. Kiss JE, Brambilla D, Glynn SA, et al.** "Oral Iron Supplementation After Blood Donation: A Randomized Clinical Trial" (the **HEIRS** trial). *JAMA*. 2015;313(6):575–583.
A total of 215 donors were studied to understand the effect of blood donation and iron supplementation on iron balance. Iron supplementation for 56 days following blood donation led to full recovery of ferritin to pre-donation levels. This is the pivotal RCT of low-dose oral iron in blood donors.

**2. Cable RG, et al.** "Effect of iron supplementation on iron stores and total body iron after whole blood donation." *Transfusion*. 2016;56(8):1990–1997. — Ancillary/companion analysis of the HEIRS trial data on total body iron.

**3. Mast AE, Bialkowski W, Bryant BJ, et al.** "A randomized, blinded, placebo-controlled trial of education and iron supplementation for mitigation of iron deficiency in regular blood donors" (**STRIDE** study). *Transfusion*. 2016;56(6 Pt 2):1588–1597.
Frequent blood donors were randomly assigned into five arms for 2 years of follow-up: three double-blinded arms provided 60 once-daily pills after each donation (38, 19, or 0 mg of iron), and two single-blinded arms provided iron status (ferritin) or no information letters after each donation. Longitudinal analyses showed improved iron status in iron pill groups and worsening iron status in control groups, with the iron pill groups experiencing a net increase of approximately 0.6 g/dL Hb compared to control groups. (Trial design paper: Bialkowski W, Bryant BJ, Schlumpf KS, et al. "The strategies to reduce iron deficiency in blood donors randomized trial: design, enrolment and early retention." *Vox Sang*. 2015;108(2):178–185.)

**4. Mast AE, Szabo A, Stone M, Cable RG, Spencer BR, Kiss JE; REDS-III.** "The benefits of iron supplementation following blood donation vary with baseline iron status." *Am J Hematol*. 2020;95(7):784–791. — Secondary/subgroup analysis of RCT data.

**5. Waldvogel-Abramowski S, et al. (ISUB trial)** "The effects of intravenous iron supplementation on fatigue and general health in non-anemic blood donors with iron deficiency: a randomized placebo-controlled superiority trial." (journal article, PMC7449957).
203 donors were randomly assigned to a single intravenous dose of 800 mg iron-carboxymaltose and 202 to placebo; at 6–8 weeks, self-rated fatigue scores showed no significant group difference.

**6. "A randomized trial of blood donor iron repletion on red cell quality for transfusion and donor cognition and well-being."** *Blood*. 2022;140(25):2730–. PMID 36069596.
Adult iron-deficient donors (n=79; ferritin <15 μg/L) were randomized to intravenous iron repletion (1 g low-molecular-weight iron dextran) or placebo, with posttransfusion red cell recovery as the primary outcome.

**7. Karregat JHM, Meulenbeld A, Quee FA, et al. (FORTE trial)** "Ferritin-guided iron supplementation as an alternative or complement to prolonged blood donation intervals (FORTE): a double-blind, randomised, controlled trial." *Lancet Haematology*. 2025;12:e694–e704 (also posted on medRxiv, Jan 2025).
Donors aged 18–80 with ferritin ≤30 μg/L were randomized to one of six groups (0, 30, or 60 mg elemental iron, daily or alternate-day) for 56 days; iron supplementation—60 mg daily in particular—effectively mitigated iron deficiency, low ferritin, and low haemoglobin.

**8. High-dose IV vs oral iron trial protocol/results** — "High-dose intravenously administered iron versus orally administered iron in blood donors with iron deficiency: study protocol for a randomised, controlled trial." (Austria; PMC5084417).
Donors with ferritin ≤30 ng/ml were randomized 1:1 to 1 g IV ferric carboxymaltose vs. oral iron fumarate (100 mg twice daily) for 8–12 weeks, with transferrin saturation as the primary endpoint. (Note: this source is the protocol paper — check for the linked results publication if you need outcome data.)
