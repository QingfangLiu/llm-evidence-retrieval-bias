Research in this area relies heavily on randomized controlled trials (RCTs) that track the physiological and operational outcomes of blood donors. The findings from key primary studies demonstrate that iron supplementation significantly accelerates the recovery of iron stores and decreases the incidence of low-hemoglobin deferrals.

## Impact on Hemoglobin and Ferritin Recovery

The kinetics of iron store replenishment were closely tracked in a major 2015 RCT by Kiss et al., which compared recovery times in donors taking daily iron versus those receiving no treatment.

* **Accelerated Hemoglobin Recovery:** Among donors with low baseline ferritin (≤26 ng/mL), supplementation reduced the mean time to recover 80% of post-donation hemoglobin to 32 days, compared to 158 days for unsupplemented donors (Kiss et al., 2015).
* **Accelerated Ferritin Recovery:** The same low-ferritin group saw their median ferritin levels return to baseline in 21 days with iron supplements. In contrast, unsupplemented donors took more than 168 days, with 67% failing to recover their iron stores entirely within the 24-week observation period (Kiss et al., 2015).

## Mitigation of Donor Deferral

The Strategies to Reduce Iron Deficiency (STRIDE) trial provides the strongest primary evidence regarding the operational impact of iron supplementation on donation frequency and deferral rates.

* **Mitigating Deficiency:** In this blinded, placebo-controlled trial, regular donors provided with low-dose iron supplements (19 mg or 38 mg of elemental iron) successfully mitigated donation-induced iron deficiency compared to those receiving placebos or simple educational letters (Mast et al., 2016).
* **Reducing Deferrals:** Follow-up operational data from the STRIDE cohort demonstrated that donors randomized to the iron pill arms experienced significantly fewer low-hemoglobin deferrals compared to control groups or their own pre-study baseline (Cable et al., 2017). Because of the lower deferral rate, these supplemented donors ultimately achieved a higher rate of successful red blood cell donations.
