Yes, primary clinical trials demonstrate that routine oral iron supplementation significantly accelerates the recovery of both hemoglobin and ferritin (iron stores) after blood donation. Without supplementation, the majority of donors do not recover their baseline iron stores within the standard 8-week (56-day) inter-donation interval, which directly leads to subsequent deferrals.

---

Here are the key randomized controlled trials establishing these outcomes:

### 1. The HEIRS Trial (2015)

This landmark randomized controlled trial evaluated 215 blood donors given either 37.5 mg of elemental iron daily or no iron for 24 weeks post-donation.

* **Hemoglobin Recovery:** Supplementation reduced the time to 80% hemoglobin recovery to roughly **31 days**, compared to 78–158 days without iron.
* **Ferritin Recovery:** Among donors with low baseline ferritin, median recovery time was 21 days with iron versus >168 days without.
* **Key Insight:** Without iron, **67% of donors did not recover their baseline iron stores by 168 days** (about 24 weeks).

| Metric (Median Time to Recovery) | With Iron (37.5 mg/day) | Without Iron |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hb Recovery (Low Ferritin Donors)** | 32 days | 158 days |
| **Hb Recovery (Normal Ferritin Donors)** | 31 days | 78 days |
| **Ferritin Recovery (Low Ferritin)** | 21 days | > 168 days |
| **Ferritin Recovery (Normal Ferritin)** | 107 days | > 168 days |

### 2. HEIRS Total Body Iron Analysis (2016)

A follow-up analysis of the HEIRS cohort by Cable et al. evaluated total body iron (TBI) dynamics.

* **Findings:** Donors taking iron fully replaced the donated iron, while those not taking iron experienced a sustained physiological deficit.
* **Key Insight:** **88% of the benefit** of iron supplementation occurred within the first 8 weeks after donation. This suggests that a targeted 8-week course immediately following donation captures almost all the hematologic benefit.

### 3. The FORTE Trial (2025)

A recent double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial involving 830 donors with low ferritin (≤30 µg/L). Participants were randomized to 30 mg or 60 mg of elemental iron, given either daily or on alternate days for 56 days.

* **Findings:** Compared to placebo, all iron supplementation groups exhibited significantly lower odds of iron deficiency and low hemoglobin at 56 days.
* **Key Insight:** The **60 mg daily** regimen yielded the lowest odds ratio for low ferritin (OR 0.52), proving highly effective at preventing deferral windows for frequent donors.

### 4. The STRIDE Trial (2014)

A randomized study of 692 donors comparing educational interventions (letters suggesting iron supplements or delayed donation) against the active provision of 19 mg or 38 mg iron pills.

* **Findings:** The study confirmed that regular donation induces iron-deficient erythropoiesis in the vast majority of donors (74.6% of women, 52.7% of men at enrollment).
* **Key Insight:** Dietary adjustments alone fail to compensate for the ~230 mg of iron lost per donation; active oral supplementation or extended deferral intervals are required.
