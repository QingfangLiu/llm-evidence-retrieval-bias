Here are the key primary clinical trials (excluding all reviews, meta-analyses, and guidelines) that directly investigate the efficacy of oral iron supplementation for improving iron stores and reducing deferral rates in blood donors.

## The REDS-II Study

The Hemoglobin and Iron Recovery Study is arguably the most foundational modern randomized clinical trial on this topic.

* **Key Findings:** The trial demonstrated that low-dose iron supplementation (37.5 mg elemental iron) significantly shortens the time to 80% hemoglobin recovery in both iron-depleted and iron-replete donors.
* **Impact on Stores:** Donors taking iron recovered their baseline iron stores in a median of 76 days. In stark contrast, 67% of the unsupplemented donors still had not recovered their iron stores by day 168 (Kiss et al., 2015).

## The STRIDE Study

The Strategies to Reduce Iron Deficiency (STRIDE) trial specifically evaluated practical interventions for mitigating iron deficiency in frequent donors.

* **Efficacy:** The primary trial compared iron supplementation against educational interventions and a placebo, confirming that active iron replacement recovers predonation iron and hemoglobin significantly faster than placebo (Mast et al., 2016).
* **Dosage Equivalency:** A highly relevant longitudinal analysis of the STRIDE data showed that a very low dose of 19 mg elemental iron is essentially equivalent to 38 mg at mitigating iron deficiency over repeated phlebotomies. This is a critical finding for balancing efficacy with the gastrointestinal side effects that often reduce donor compliance (Bialkowski et al., 2017).

## The FORTE Trial

This recent, large-scale double-blind trial out of the Netherlands aimed to fine-tune exactly *how* iron should be administered to balance biological efficacy with tolerability.

* **Design Focus:** The trial tested varying doses (30 mg vs. 60 mg) and frequencies (daily vs. alternate days) of ferrous bisglycinate—a formulation chosen to reduce GI discomfort.
* **Relevance:** The study explicitly measures the impact of these regimens on ferritin recovery, actual donor return rates, and self-reported side effects to establish an optimal protocol (Karregat et al., 2022).

## Early Deferral-Specific Trials

Research targeting the specific subset of female donors who were already deferred due to low hemoglobin (often via copper sulfate screening) dates back several decades.

* **Key Findings:** An early randomized trial evaluated 75 mg of daily iron against a placebo. While the iron group showed significant biological improvement in serum ferritin and hemoglobin over successive visits, the study uniquely found that this did not translate into a statistically significant increase in the donors' immediate suitability to donate blood at subsequent visits compared to the placebo cohort (Cable et al., 1988).
