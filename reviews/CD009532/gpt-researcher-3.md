## Bottom line

Primary trials consistently show that iron supplementation improves ferritin, total-body iron, or post-donation iron recovery. Evidence that it prevents **actual low-hemoglobin donor deferral** is more limited because many studies measured hemoglobin eligibility thresholds rather than observed donation attempts. The strongest direct trial found deferral rates of **8% with iron versus 36% with placebo**, while an earlier trial in already-deferred women improved iron indices but not subsequent donation suitability.

## Additional primary studies supporting iron-store recovery

* **Waldvogel et al., 2012:** RCT of 154 nonanemic but iron-deficient female donors. Four weeks of ferrous sulfate increased Hb by an estimated 5.2 g/L and ferritin by 14.8 ng/mL relative to placebo. Deferral was not assessed. *BMC Medicine* 2012;10:8. DOI: 10.1186/1741-7015-10-8; PMID 22272750. ([PubMed][9])

* **Radtke et al., 2004, two-unit RBC apheresis:** Double-blind crossover-type RCT of 260 regular donors receiving 100 mg iron/day or placebo across repeated two-unit RBC collections. Ferritin fell after each donation during placebo periods but remained stable or increased during iron periods. *Transfusion* 2004;44:1463–1467. DOI: 10.1111/j.1537-2995.2004.04045.x; PMID 15383019. ([PubMed][10])

* **Birgegård et al., 2010:** 120 regular donors randomized to 200 mg IV iron sucrose or 20 days of oral iron sulfate after each donation for one year. IV iron increased storage iron more than oral treatment; there was no placebo/no-iron arm and deferral was not reported. *Vox Sanguinis* 2010;99:354–361. DOI: 10.1111/j.1423-0410.2010.01368.x; PMID 20598107. ([PubMed][11])

* **Krayenbuehl and colleagues’ 2020 IV-iron trial:** Approximately 405 nonanemic, iron-deficient repeat donors were randomized to 800 mg IV ferric carboxymaltose or placebo. Ferritin was about 114 µg/L higher and Hb 5.7 g/L higher with iron at six to eight weeks, although fatigue did not improve. Deferral was not reported. ([PubMed][12])

### Trial-publication relationships

For study counting, **Kiss 2015 and Cable 2016 are companion reports from the same 215-participant HEIRS trial**. The Cable report provides the detailed total-body-iron analysis: at 24 weeks, total-body iron increased by 281 mg relative to predonation values with supplementation but decreased by 74 mg without supplementation; 88% of the observed supplementation benefit occurred in the first eight weeks. It should not be counted as an independent trial. ([PubMed][13])

Overall, the primary-study pattern is strong for **improving iron stores**, and generally supportive—but based on fewer direct observations—for **preventing low-Hb donation failure**. The outcome distinction matters: ferritin improvement, attaining an Hb threshold, and an actual deferral at a presenting donation are related but not interchangeable.
