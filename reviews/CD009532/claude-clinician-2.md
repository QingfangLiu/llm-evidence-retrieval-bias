Here are individual primary studies (RCTs and prospective cohort studies) on iron supplementation in blood donors, its effect on iron stores, and its effect on donation deferral. I've excluded systematic reviews, meta-analyses, narrative reviews, guidelines, and letters/comments (e.g., the 2014 Cochrane review and the FORTE-related "congratulatory" comment piece are omitted).

**Oral iron supplementation trials**

- **Kiss JE, Brambilla D, Glynn SA, et al. "Oral Iron Supplementation After Blood Donation: A Randomized Clinical Trial." JAMA. 2015;313(6):575-583.** The pivotal HEIRS (Hemoglobin and Iron Recovery Study) trial — a randomized controlled trial of low dose iron supplementation in blood donors, showing 56 days of oral iron led to fuller recovery of ferritin toward pre-donation levels compared with placebo.

- **Cable RG, Wickenden C, et al. "Effect of iron supplementation on iron stores and total body iron after whole blood donation." Transfusion. 2016.** Ancillary analysis of 215 donors examining how blood donation and iron supplementation affect iron balance, using total body iron calculations.

- **Mast AE, Bialkowski W, Bryant BJ, Wright DJ, Birch R, Kiss JE, D'Andrea P, Cable RG, Spencer BR. "A randomized, blinded, placebo-controlled trial of education and iron supplementation for mitigation of iron deficiency in regular blood donors." Transfusion. 2016.** RCT testing donor education plus low-dose iron supplementation as strategies to mitigate iron deficiency in regular donors.

- **Bialkowski W, Kiss JE, Wright DJ, et al. "Estimates of total body iron indicate 19 mg and 38 mg oral iron are equivalent for the mitigation of iron deficiency in individuals experiencing repeated phlebotomy." Am J Hematol. 2017;92:851-857.** Secondary dose-comparison analysis from HEIRS trial data.

- **Mast AE, Szabo A, Stone M, Cable RG, Spencer BR, Kiss JE (REDS-III). "The benefits of iron supplementation following blood donation vary with baseline iron status." Am J Hematol. 2020;95(7):784-791.** Analysis identifying which donor subgroups (by baseline ferritin) benefit most from supplementation.

- **Waldvogel-Abramowski S, Pedrazzini B, Vaucher P, Bize R, Cornuz J, Tissot JD, et al. "Clinical evaluation of iron treatment efficiency among non-anemic but iron-deficient female blood donors: a randomized controlled trial." BMC Medicine. 2012;10:8.** RCT in female blood donors with decreased ferritin but no anemia, evaluating oral iron treatment efficiency (protocol paper: Pedrazzini B, et al. Trials. 2009;10:4).

- **Karregat JHM, Meulenbeld A, Quee FA, et al. "Ferritin-guided iron supplementation as an alternative or complement to prolonged blood donation intervals (FORTE): a double-blind, randomised, controlled trial." Lancet Haematology. 2025;12:e694-e704.** Six-arm double-blind RCT (placebo vs. 30 mg or 60 mg elemental iron, daily vs. alternate-day) in donors with ferritin ≤30 µg/L; found daily supplementation with 60 mg of elemental iron was the most effective strategy for mitigating iron deficiency, low ferritin, and low haemoglobin, without gastrointestinal discomfort or affecting donors' willingness to return.

**Intravenous iron trials**

- **Radtke H, et al. "The effects of intravenous iron supplementation on fatigue and general health in non-anemic blood donors with iron deficiency: a randomized placebo-controlled superiority trial." Scientific Reports. 2020.** RCT of IV iron in non-anemic iron-deficient donors.

- **Spencer BR, et al. (DIDS — Donor Iron Deficiency Study). "A randomized trial of blood donor iron repletion on red cell quality for transfusion and donor cognition and well-being." Blood. 2022;140(25):2730.** RCT of IV ferric carboxymaltose 800 mg vs. placebo; notably, in post hoc analyses, at the donation ~5 months after randomization, female subjects in the placebo group would more likely be deferred from allogeneic donation by being unable to meet minimum hemoglobin and hematocrit criteria, as compared with those receiving iron repletion — directly relevant to your deferral question.

**Related but not iron-supplementation trials (context only, not what you asked for)**

- Baart AM et al. FIND'EM trial (Lancet 2024) and its protocol paper (BMC/PMC 2020) test *ferritin-guided donation intervals*, not supplementation — flagging in case useful for comparison, but I've excluded it from the core list since your question was about supplements specifically.

A note on quality: several of these (HEIRS, FORTE, DIDS, Waldvogel-Abramowski) are well-powered, blinded RCTs with deferral or hemoglobin/ferritin recovery as endpoints, so they're the strongest evidence for your clinical question.
