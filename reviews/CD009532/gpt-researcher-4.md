## Bottom line

Yes. Individual donor studies—especially randomized trials—consistently show that oral iron improves ferritin or total-body iron and accelerates hemoglobin recovery after donation. Fewer studies measured **actual low-hemoglobin deferral**, but those that did generally found better eligibility or fewer deferrals with supplementation.

I excluded systematic reviews, guidelines, opinions, editorials, protocols, and autologous-donation studies from the evidence table below.

## Most informative core set

For an evidence synthesis focused on **both iron stores and avoidance of low-Hb inability to donate**, the highest-yield independent studies are:

1. **Gordeuk 1990** — direct repeat-deferral outcome.
2. **Radtke 2004 whole-blood trial** — repeated donations and dose comparison.
3. **Marks 2014** — ferritin, iron deficiency, Hb, and donation eligibility.
4. **HEIRS/Kiss 2015** — detailed Hb and ferritin recovery kinetics.
5. **STRIDE/Mast 2016** — long-term repeated-donor strategy.
6. **FORTE/Karregat 2025** — largest modern ferritin-targeted dose/frequency RCT.

The evidence is strongest for **biochemical iron restoration and Hb recovery**. Evidence for **actual operational deferral** is positive but thinner because many trials measured Hb or laboratory eligibility rather than whether the donor physically returned and was deferred.
