Here are individual primary studies (RCTs and prospective/quasi-experimental trials) on iron supplementation and iron stores/deferral risk in blood donors — no reviews, meta-analyses, or guidelines included.

**Large multicenter RCTs (REDS-III network)**

- **Kiss JE, et al. "Oral Iron Supplementation After Blood Donation: A Randomized Clinical Trial." JAMA. 2015;313(6):575–583.** The landmark REDS-III trial randomizing donors to oral iron vs. placebo after donation; showed iron supplementation speeds hemoglobin/ferritin recovery and reduces subsequent low-hemoglobin deferral.

- **Cable RG, et al. "Effect of iron supplementation on iron stores and total body iron after whole blood donation." Transfusion. 2016.** The HEIRS trial — a total of 215 donors were randomized to receive ferrous gluconate daily (37.5 mg iron) or no iron for 24 weeks after blood donation, examining effects on iron stores and total body iron.

- **Mast AE, et al. "A randomized, blinded, placebo-controlled trial of education and iron supplementation for mitigation of iron deficiency in regular blood donors." Transfusion. 2016.** The STRIDE trial, testing iron supplementation (with/without education) against placebo in regular donors with low ferritin.

- **Hod EA, Brittenham GM, Bitan ZC, et al. "A randomized trial of blood donor iron repletion on red cell quality for transfusion and donor cognition and well-being." Blood. 2022;140(25):2730–2739.** At the second blood donation visit 4 to 6 months after randomization, hemoglobin increased by a mean 1.4 g/dL, ferritin increased by 66 μg/L, and zinc protoporphyrin decreased, as compared with the placebo group; in post hoc analyses, female donors in the placebo group were more likely to be deferred from donation by failing to meet minimum hemoglobin/hematocrit criteria compared with those receiving iron repletion.

**Recent Dutch trials (Sanquin)**

- **Meulenbeld A, et al. "Ferritin-guided iron supplementation as an alternative or complement to prolonged blood donation intervals (FORTE): a double-blind, randomised, controlled trial." Lancet Haematology. 2025;12:e694–e704** (also posted as medRxiv preprint 2025). Donors with ferritin ≤30 μg/L were randomized to placebo, 30 mg, or 60 mg of elemental iron (ferrous bisglycinate), taken daily or on alternate days, for 56 days, evaluating effects on ferritin/hemoglobin recovery, side effects, and donor return. The trial found that all examined dosages and frequencies of iron supplementation effectively reduced iron deficiency, low ferritin, and low hemoglobin, with daily 60 mg emerging as optimal.

**Swiss studies**

- **Pittori C, Buser A, Gasser UE, et al. "A pilot iron substitution programme in female blood donors with iron deficiency without anaemia." Vox Sanguinis. 2011;100(3):303–311.** Women ≤50 years old with adequate hemoglobin but serum ferritin ≤10 ng/mL were offered 16 weeks of iron substitution with an extended donation interval, compared prospectively with retrospective untreated controls. Not randomized, but a controlled prospective intervention study.

- **Waldvogel S, Pedrazzini B, Vaucher P, et al. "Clinical evaluation of iron treatment efficiency among non-anemic but iron-deficient female blood donors: a randomized controlled trial." BMC Medicine. 2012;10:8.** 154 female donors with iron deficiency without anemia were randomized one week post-donation to four weeks of oral ferrous sulfate vs. placebo; hemoglobin rose 5.2 g/L and ferritin rose 14.8 ng/mL more in the iron group than placebo, though the authors concluded no clinically meaningful fatigue benefit.

- **Waldvogel-Abramowski S, et al. "Effects of oral supplementation of iron on hepcidin blood concentrations among non-anaemic female blood donors: a randomized controlled trial." Vox Sanguinis. 2016.** A related RCT from the same cohort focused on hepcidin/iron-absorption mechanisms.

- **Fontana S, Jüni P, Niederhauser C, Keller P. "Lack of effectiveness of intravenous iron infusion in healthy blood donors with low ferritin: a double-blind randomized controlled trial." Vox Sang. 2014;107(Suppl S1):98** (ISBT congress abstract). IV iron RCT in low-ferritin donors.

- **Nater A, et al. "The effects of intravenous iron supplementation on fatigue and general health in non-anemic blood donors with iron deficiency: a randomized placebo-controlled superiority trial." Scientific Reports. 2020.** 203 donors received a single 800 mg IV iron-carboxymaltose dose vs. 202 placebo; ferritin and hemoglobin rose significantly more with iron (mean differences 114.2 μg/L ferritin, 5.7 g/L hemoglobin), though fatigue scores did not differ between groups.

**German studies (older but frequently cited primary RCTs)**

- **Radtke H, Tegtmeier J, Rocker L, Salama A, Kiesewetter H. "Daily doses of 20 mg of elemental iron compensate for iron loss in regular blood donors: a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study." Transfusion. 2004;44(10):1427–1432.**

- **Radtke H, Mayer B, Rocker L, Salama A, Kiesewetter H. "Iron supplementation and 2-unit red blood cell apheresis: a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study." Transfusion. 2004;44(10):1463–1467.**

- **Radtke H, Tegtmeier J, Rocker L, Salama A, Kiesewetter H. "Compensating for iron loss in regular blood donors using ferrous gluconate and ascorbic acid." Transfusion. 2005;45(7):1236–1237.**

**Other relevant clinical/observational primary study**

- **Bryant BJ, Yau YY, Arceo SM, Daniel-Johnson J, Hopkins JA, Leitman SF. "Iron replacement therapy in the routine management of blood donors." Transfusion. 2012;52(7):1566–1575.** A prospective interventional study of routine iron replacement in donors with depleted iron stores.
