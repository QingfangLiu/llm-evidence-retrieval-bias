Integrating immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) into the management of resectable (early-stage) non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) represents a major paradigm shift. For older adults, treatment decisions require balancing the proven survival advantages against the potential for immune-mediated toxicities and the impacts on surgical candidacy.

Based on primary Phase 3 randomized controlled trials evaluating neoadjuvant (pre-surgical), adjuvant (post-surgical), and perioperative (both) immunotherapy regimens against placebo or chemotherapy alone, here is the breakdown of benefits and harms.

## Benefits of Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors

* **Improved Event-Free and Disease-Free Survival:** The addition of ICIs significantly prolongs the time patients live without disease recurrence. Perioperative pembrolizumab and durvalumab regimens have demonstrated profound event-free survival (EFS) benefits compared to neoadjuvant chemotherapy alone (Heymach et al., 2023; Wakelee et al., 2023). In the strictly adjuvant setting, atezolizumab and pembrolizumab improve disease-free survival (DFS), with the most pronounced benefits often seen in patients whose tumors express PD-L1 (Felip et al., 2021; O’Brien et al., 2022).
* **Higher Pathologic Complete Response (pCR):** Administering immunotherapy prior to surgery drastically increases the rate of pCR—meaning no viable tumor cells are found in the resected tissue. For instance, the AEGEAN trial showed that adding durvalumab to neoadjuvant chemotherapy yielded a pCR rate of 17.2%, compared to just 4.3% with chemotherapy and placebo (Heymach et al., 2023). Achieving a pCR is a strong surrogate predictor for long-term overall survival.
* **Efficacy in Older Adults:** While the primary phase 3 trials are powered for the general study population, their prespecified age subgroup analyses (typically stratifying patients as <65 versus ≥65 years) generally demonstrate that older adults derive a similar magnitude of EFS and pCR benefit as younger cohorts (Felip et al., 2021; Heymach et al., 2023; O’Brien et al., 2022; Wakelee et al., 2023).

## Harms and Surgical Considerations

* **Immune-Related Adverse Events (irAEs):** Checkpoint inhibitors can trigger systemic inflammatory toxicities. Common irAEs include thyroid dysfunction (hyper- and hypothyroidism), pneumonitis, colitis, and hepatitis. In the KEYNOTE-091 trial, Grade 3 or worse adverse events occurred in 34% of patients receiving adjuvant pembrolizumab compared to 26% on placebo, with treatment-related deaths occurring in 1% of the pembrolizumab group (O’Brien et al., 2022).
* **Feasibility of Surgery:** A primary concern with neoadjuvant therapy—especially in older, potentially frail populations—is whether treatment toxicity might delay or entirely prevent a curative resection. Reassuringly, the primary trials confirmed that adding ICIs to neoadjuvant chemotherapy does not substantially compromise surgical feasibility. The proportion of patients who successfully undergo complete resection and the rates of surgical complications remain similar between the chemo-immunotherapy and chemotherapy-alone groups (Heymach et al., 2023; Wakelee et al., 2023).
* **Tolerability in Frailty:** It is important to note that clinical trial populations typically require excellent performance statuses (ECOG 0 or 1) and adequate baseline organ function. Because older adults frequently present with competing comorbidities and physiological immunosenescence, the incidence of irAEs in real-world older populations can sometimes be more challenging to manage than the trial data suggest.
