## Scope

I found **12 randomized primary trials** comparing a perioperative immune-checkpoint inhibitor strategy with placebo, chemotherapy without immunotherapy, best supportive care, or observation in resectable NSCLC through **July 25, 2026**. I excluded single-arm studies, active immunotherapy-versus-immunotherapy comparisons, reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, editorials, and conference reports lacking a full primary publication.

No qualifying randomized trial evaluated **neoadjuvant checkpoint-inhibitor monotherapy versus no immunotherapy**. The preoperative trials added a checkpoint inhibitor to platinum chemotherapy; the adjuvant-only trials tested checkpoint-inhibitor monotherapy after resection.

## Overall findings

For selected, operable patients—mostly stage II–III and ECOG performance status 0–1—adding a PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor to neoadjuvant chemotherapy consistently increased pathological complete response and prolonged event-free survival. Mature overall-survival benefit has been demonstrated with:

* **CheckMate 816:** 5-year OS 65.4% versus 55.0%, HR 0.72.
* **KEYNOTE-671:** 36-month OS 71% versus 64%, HR 0.72.
* **RATIONALE-315:** 36-month OS 79.3% versus 69.3%, HR 0.65. ([PubMed][1])

By contrast, **adjuvant-only immunotherapy after upfront surgery has produced mixed results**. IMpower010 and KEYNOTE-091 improved disease-free survival, whereas BR.31 and ANVIL did not. A broad, definitive overall-survival advantage for adjuvant-only therapy has not been established. ([ASCO Publications][2])

## Applicability to older adults

The evidence is most applicable to **physiologically fit older adults**, not to the broader geriatric NSCLC population:

* Median ages were generally about **61–67 years**. ANVIL had one of the oldest populations—median 66–67—but was an adjuvant-only negative trial. Very few reports provide sufficiently detailed outcomes for patients aged 75 or 80 years and older. ([PubMed][14])
* Participants generally had ECOG performance status 0–1, adequate organ function, and had already been judged operable. Frailty, cognition, falls, functional reserve, polypharmacy, competing mortality, and geriatric-assessment variables were generally not analyzed.
* TD-FOREKNOW excluded patients older than 70, making it especially difficult to generalize to older surgical candidates. ([JAMA Network][5])
* In KEYNOTE-671, the OS point estimate appeared less favorable in participants aged 65 or older than in younger participants. However, these subgroup analyses were descriptive, underpowered, and had confidence intervals overlapping the overall effect; they should not be interpreted as proof that older adults do not benefit. ([PMC][15])
* Relevant harms in an older patient include pneumonitis, colitis, hepatitis, endocrinopathies, nephritis, myocarditis and other uncommon immune toxicities, layered on top of platinum-doublet cytopenias, infection risk, neuropathy, renal injury, fatigue, and possible loss of preoperative functional reserve. Across the randomized trials, perioperative immunotherapy generally did **not** reduce the overall probability of reaching surgery, but an individual severe toxicity can still eliminate a narrow surgical window.
