Six large phase 3, randomized, placebo/best-supportive-care–controlled trials form the evidence base for perioperative (neoadjuvant, adjuvant, or both) checkpoint inhibitor use in resectable stage I–IIIB(N2) NSCLC. None of these trials specifically restricted enrollment to older adults, but all report age-based subgroup data (commonly using a 65 or 75-year cutoff), which is the best available evidence for your population. Below is a synthesis organized by treatment strategy, followed by the study list.

### Neoadjuvant-only (chemo-immunotherapy before surgery)
**CheckMate 816** randomized 358 patients to neoadjuvant nivolumab plus platinum-doublet chemotherapy vs chemotherapy alone for 3 cycles before surgery. Neoadjuvant nivolumab plus chemotherapy had a significant benefit over chemotherapy alone with respect to event-free survival and pathological complete response and had no adverse effect on surgical feasibility or surgical outcomes. With a minimum follow-up of 21.0 months, the combination reduced the risk of disease recurrence, progression, or death by 37% (HR 0.63) when given before surgery, and this benefit was later confirmed for overall survival at final analysis.

### Perioperative (neoadjuvant + adjuvant)
- **CheckMate 77T**: perioperative nivolumab led to a statistically significant and clinically meaningful event-free survival benefit and improved pathologic complete response rate vs placebo plus chemotherapy in resectable NSCLC, with median EFS not reached with nivolumab vs 18.4 months with placebo (HR 0.58). A nodal-status exploratory analysis further showed among patients with N2 disease, nivolumab improved event-free survival (1-year rate 70% vs 45%; HR 0.46) and pathological complete response (22.0% vs 5.6%).
- **KEYNOTE-671**: participants with resectable stage II, IIIA, or IIIB (N2) NSCLC were randomized to neoadjuvant pembrolizumab or placebo with cisplatin-based chemotherapy for 4 cycles, followed by surgery and adjuvant pembrolizumab or placebo for up to 13 cycles. At ~5 years, pembrolizumab significantly improved EFS (HR 0.58) and OS (HR 0.72; P=0.0052) vs chemotherapy alone. Notably for your older-adult focus, a 4-year update found the OS benefit with pembrolizumab was observed across most patient subgroups, except for those who were at least 65 years old (HR, 1.01; 95% CI, 0.73-1.41) — an important signal of attenuated benefit in older patients.
- **AEGEAN**: perioperative durvalumab plus chemotherapy vs chemotherapy alone; treated with durvalumab plus chemotherapy before and after surgery showed a 32% reduction in EFS versus chemotherapy alone (HR 0.68), and neoadjuvant durvalumab plus chemotherapy resulted in pCR and MPR rates of 17.2% and 33.3% versus 4.3% and 12.3% for chemotherapy alone.

### Adjuvant-only (after surgery, +/- prior chemo)
- **IMpower010**: a randomised, multicentre, open-label, phase 3 study evaluating adjuvant atezolizumab versus best supportive care after adjuvant platinum-based chemotherapy in resected stage IB–IIIA NSCLC. DFS was significantly improved with adjuvant atezolizumab versus BSC following platinum-based chemotherapy, but at longer-term follow-up the median OS in the ITT population was not estimable, with a stratified HR of 0.995 (95% CI 0.78–1.28), indicating no clear overall survival advantage outside PD-L1–high subgroups.
- **PEARLS/KEYNOTE-091**: pembrolizumab significantly improved disease-free survival compared with placebo and was not associated with new safety signals in completely resected, PD-L1-unselected, stage IB–IIIA NSCLC, after complete resection and, when recommended, adjuvant chemotherapy, regardless of PD-L1 expression.

### Harms/safety themes across trials
Across AEGEAN, KEYNOTE-671, and CheckMate 77T, surgical feasibility was preserved — high R0 resection rates (approximately 90% or more) were observed across the active study arms, with numerically higher R0 resection rates reported with durvalumab versus placebo in AEGEAN and pembrolizumab versus placebo in KEYNOTE-671. Safety in the KEYNOTE-671 stage II subgroup was consistent with the overall study population, with no deaths due to treatment-related or immune-mediated adverse events among participants with stage II disease — reassuring, though this doesn't isolate an elderly-specific safety signal.
