As an evidence-synthesis researcher, you will find that there are almost no phase 3 trials in this space restricted *exclusively* to older adults. Instead, the primary data for this population is embedded within the landmark phase 3 randomized controlled trials (RCTs) evaluating immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) in resectable non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC).

These primary studies provide the foundational data and include prespecified subgroup analyses by age (typically comparing patients <65 years to those ≥65 years). To synthesize the data for older adults, you will need to extract the hazard ratios directly from the forest plots in the publications and their supplementary appendices.

Here is a breakdown of the benefits and harms observed in these primary studies across neoadjuvant, adjuvant, and perioperative approaches.

## Benefits

**Neoadjuvant Approaches (Before Surgery)**
Administering an ICI alongside chemotherapy before surgery significantly improves early tumor clearance. In the CheckMate 816 trial, adding nivolumab to neoadjuvant chemotherapy yielded a vastly superior pathological complete response (pCR) rate (24% vs. 2.2% with chemotherapy alone) and significantly longer event-free survival (EFS) (Forde et al., 2022).

**Adjuvant Approaches (After Surgery)**
For patients who undergo upfront surgery followed by adjuvant chemotherapy, adding an ICI extends disease-free survival (DFS). The IMpower010 trial demonstrated that adjuvant atezolizumab improves DFS compared to best supportive care, particularly in patients whose tumors express PD-L1 (Felip et al., 2021). Similarly, the PEARLS/KEYNOTE-091 trial showed a significant DFS benefit when using adjuvant pembrolizumab versus a placebo (O’Brien et al., 2022).

**Perioperative Approaches (Before and After Surgery)**
Recent trials have evaluated a continuous "sandwich" approach: neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy followed by surgery, and then adjuvant immunotherapy. The AEGEAN trial (durvalumab) and the KEYNOTE-671 trial (pembrolizumab) both reported that this perioperative strategy yields significant improvements in both pCR and EFS compared to a placebo plus chemotherapy (Heymach et al., 2023; Spicer et al., 2024).

## Harms and Surgical Feasibility

A major concern when synthesizing data for older adults is whether the added toxicity of ICIs prevents patients from undergoing curative surgery or increases postoperative complications.

* **Surgical Outcomes:** The primary trials consistently report that adding a neoadjuvant ICI does not decrease the rate of definitive surgery, nor does it increase surgical morbidity or mortality. For instance, in CheckMate 816, surgical cancellation rates were actually slightly lower in the chemoimmunotherapy arm than in the chemotherapy-alone arm (Forde et al., 2022). Similar surgical feasibility was confirmed with perioperative durvalumab (Heymach et al., 2023).
* **Adverse Events:** ICIs introduce a unique profile of immune-mediated adverse events (imAEs), such as pneumonitis, thyroid dysfunction, myocarditis, and rash. While the overall incidence of severe (Grade 3 or 4) treatment-related adverse events is generally comparable between chemoimmunotherapy and chemotherapy alone, patients require strict monitoring for these immune toxicities, which can be particularly impactful for older patients with existing comorbidities (Spicer et al., 2024).
