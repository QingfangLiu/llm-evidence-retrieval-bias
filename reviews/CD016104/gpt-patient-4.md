## Bottom line

I identified **12 randomized primary trial programs** comparing an immune-checkpoint-inhibitor strategy with chemotherapy alone, placebo, observation, or best supportive care in resectable non–small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). I excluded reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, editorials, and single-arm studies.

For patients fit enough for platinum chemotherapy and surgery:

* **Immune therapy plus chemotherapy before surgery** consistently increases the chance that no viable cancer is found at surgery and generally prolongs event-free survival.
* **Overall-survival improvement has now been demonstrated** with preoperative nivolumab plus chemotherapy in CheckMate 816 and with perioperative pembrolizumab and tislelizumab in KEYNOTE-671 and RATIONALE-315. The smaller NADIM II trial also reported an overall-survival advantage. ([PubMed][1])
* **Immune therapy given only after surgery has mixed results.** IMpower010 and KEYNOTE-091 improved disease-free survival in their primary populations, but BR.31 and ANVIL did not. A broad overall-survival benefit from postoperative-only immunotherapy has not been established. ([PubMed][2])
* Adding immunotherapy creates risks of immune-related pneumonitis, hepatitis, colitis, thyroid or other endocrine disorders, nephritis, myocarditis, and other inflammatory toxicities. Most are manageable, but rare fatal events occurred in several trials.

“Limited to one area” is not a formal stage. These results apply to **resectable, nonmetastatic disease**, usually stage II–III or trial-defined stage IB tumors at least 4 cm. They may not apply to a small stage IA tumor or to “oligometastatic” stage IV disease.

**Terminology:** pCR means no viable cancer in the removed lung tumor or lymph nodes; EFS/DFS is time without progression, recurrence, or death; OS is overall survival.

## Immune therapy before surgery only

### CheckMate 816 — nivolumab plus chemotherapy

In 358 patients with stage IB tumors at least 4 cm through stage IIIA disease, three preoperative cycles of nivolumab plus platinum chemotherapy were compared with chemotherapy alone.

* Median EFS: **31.6 vs 20.8 months**, HR 0.63.
* pCR: **24.0% vs 2.2%**.
* Surgery was performed in **83.2% vs 75.4%**.
* At approximately five years, OS was **65.4% vs 55.0%**, HR 0.72.
* Grade 3–4 treatment-related adverse events were **33.5% vs 36.9%**; adding nivolumab did not produce a higher overall rate of severe treatment-related toxicity in this trial. ([PubMed][3])

This is particularly important because it shows that **preoperative treatment alone, without mandated postoperative nivolumab, can improve survival**.

### TD-FOREKNOW — camrelizumab plus chemotherapy

This Chinese phase 2 trial randomized 94 patients with stage IIIA or selected IIIB disease to preoperative camrelizumab plus chemotherapy or chemotherapy alone.

* pCR: **32.6% vs 8.9%**.
* Survival data were immature in the primary publication.
* Grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events: **25.6% vs 11.1%**.
* Immune-mediated events were frequent but reported as grade 1–2; no treatment-related deaths occurred.
* Eligibility stopped at age 70, substantially limiting applicability to older people over 70. ([PMC][4])

I did not identify a randomized preoperative trial of **checkpoint inhibitor alone**, without chemotherapy, against placebo or no treatment that met your requested comparison.

## Immune therapy both before and after surgery

In these trials, the experimental group received checkpoint inhibitor with chemotherapy before surgery and then checkpoint inhibitor alone after surgery. Therefore, they **do not determine how much additional benefit comes from continuing immunotherapy after the operation**.

### NADIM II — nivolumab

Eighty-six patients with stage IIIA–IIIB disease received preoperative nivolumab plus chemotherapy or chemotherapy alone; patients in the experimental group with complete resection subsequently received six months of nivolumab.

* pCR: **37% vs 7%**.
* Surgery: **93% vs 69%**.
* Two-year progression-free survival: **67.2% vs 40.9%**, HR 0.47.
* Two-year OS: **85.0% vs 63.6%**, HR 0.43.
* Grade 3–4 adverse events: **19% vs 10%**. ([New England Journal of Medicine][5])

These are promising survival findings, but this was a relatively small phase 2 trial.

### KEYNOTE-671 — pembrolizumab

This phase 3 trial randomized 797 patients with stage II–IIIB disease to pembrolizumab or placebo with four cycles of preoperative cisplatin-based chemotherapy, followed by surgery and up to 13 postoperative doses.

* Median EFS: **47.2 vs 18.3 months**, HR 0.59.
* Three-year OS: **71% vs 64%**, HR 0.72.
* pCR: **18.1% vs 4.0%**.
* Grade 3–5 treatment-related events: **45% vs 38%**.
* Complete treatment discontinuation because of toxicity: **14% vs 5%**.
* Immune-mediated adverse events: **26% vs 9%**; one pembrolizumab-treated patient died from pneumonitis. ([PubMed][6])

The OS estimate in participants aged 65 or older was less decisive than the overall result, but the age subgroup was not powered to determine that older age eliminates benefit.

### AEGEAN — durvalumab

Approximately 800 patients received durvalumab or placebo with preoperative platinum chemotherapy and then postoperative durvalumab or placebo.

* EFS HR: **0.68**.
* One-year EFS: **73.4% vs 64.5%**.
* pCR: **17.2% vs 4.3%**.
* Grade 3–4 adverse events from any cause: **42.4% vs 43.2%**.
* Immune-mediated events: **23.7% vs 9.3%**, mainly lower grade.
* OS was not yet definitive in the primary report. ([PubMed][7])

### CheckMate 77T — nivolumab

Patients with stage IIA–IIIB disease received nivolumab or placebo with preoperative chemotherapy, followed by one year of postoperative nivolumab or placebo.

* Eighteen-month EFS: **70.2% vs 50.0%**, HR 0.58.
* pCR: **25.3% vs 4.7%**.
* Major pathologic response: **35.4% vs 12.1%**.
* Grade 3–4 treatment-related adverse events: **32.5% vs 25.2%**.
* OS was immature in the primary publication. ([PubMed][8])

### Neotorch — toripalimab

The published interim analysis concerned 404 Chinese patients with stage III disease.

* EFS HR: **0.40**; median EFS was not reached with toripalimab versus 15.1 months with placebo.
* pCR: **24.8% vs 1.0%**.
* Grade 3 or higher treatment-emergent adverse events: **63.4% vs 54.0%**.
* Fatal adverse events: **3.0% vs 2.0%**.
* Immune-related adverse events: **42.1% vs 22.8%**.
* The population was predominantly male, with a median age of 62, which limits generalizability. ([PubMed][9])

### RATIONALE-315 — tislelizumab

This Chinese phase 3 trial randomized 453 patients with stage II–IIIA disease.

* Final EFS HR: **0.58**.
* Three-year OS: **79.3% vs 69.3%**, HR 0.65.
* Major pathologic response: approximately **56% vs 15%**.
* Grade 3 or higher adverse events: **72% vs 66%**, largely chemotherapy-associated blood-count toxicities.
* Serious treatment-related events: **15% vs 8%**.
* Median age was 62, and approximately 91% were men. ([ScienceDirect][10])

## Immune therapy only after surgery

### IMpower010 — atezolizumab

After complete resection and platinum chemotherapy, 1,005 patients with trial-defined stage IB tumors at least 4 cm through stage IIIA disease were randomized to atezolizumab or best supportive care.

* In stage II–IIIA tumors with PD-L1 of at least 1%, DFS HR: **0.66**.
* In all stage II–IIIA patients, DFS HR: **0.79**.
* In the full randomized population, the interim OS HR was **0.995**, showing no broad survival advantage at that analysis.
* An exploratory PD-L1 50% or higher subgroup had a favorable OS estimate, HR 0.43.
* Atezolizumab-related grade 3–4 adverse events occurred in approximately **11%**; treatment-related fatal events occurred in approximately **1%**. ([PubMed][2])

The benefit was therefore mainly a **recurrence-delay benefit in selected populations**, especially those with PD-L1-positive stage II–IIIA disease, rather than a demonstrated overall-population survival benefit.

### PEARLS/KEYNOTE-091 — pembrolizumab

A total of 1,177 patients with completely resected stage IB tumors at least 4 cm through stage IIIA disease received pembrolizumab or placebo for up to 18 cycles; postoperative chemotherapy was recommended or considered according to stage.

* Median DFS: **53.6 vs 42.0 months**, HR 0.76.
* The PD-L1 50% or higher subgroup did not meet its DFS endpoint, HR 0.82.
* Grade 3 or higher adverse events: **34% vs 26%**.
* Serious adverse events: **24% vs 15%**.
* Four treatment-related deaths occurred with pembrolizumab and none with placebo, including myocarditis/cardiogenic or septic shock, pneumonia, and sudden death.
* Mature OS benefit has not been established in the primary publication. ([PubMed][11])

### BR.31 — durvalumab

In this 1,415-patient trial, durvalumab was compared with placebo after complete resection, with postoperative chemotherapy optional.

* In the primary EGFR/ALK-negative, PD-L1 25% or higher population, DFS HR: **0.93**, with no significant benefit.
* No DFS benefit appeared in the broader prespecified populations.
* Grade 3–4 adverse events: **26% vs 20%**. ([PubMed][12])

### ANVIL/ALCHEMIST — nivolumab

After planned standard postoperative therapy, 935 patients with tumors at least 4 cm and/or node-positive disease were randomized to nivolumab for up to a year or observation.

* Median DFS: **71.3 vs 68.8 months**, HR 0.97.
* In tumors with PD-L1 of at least 50%, DFS HR: **0.86**, also not significant.
* The trial was stopped for futility.
* Median ages were 66 and 67, making this one of the more age-relevant trials, although participants still had to be sufficiently fit for trial enrollment and prior standard treatment. ([JAMA Network][13])

This trial is important because it directly shows that **one year of postoperative nivolumab did not improve recurrence outcomes compared with observation** after patients completed their planned standard care.

## How well does this evidence apply to an older adult?

The trials generally enrolled people with good performance status and adequate organ and lung function. Median ages were mostly in the early-to-mid 60s. ANVIL had median ages of 66–67, but TD-FOREKNOW excluded anyone over 70; RATIONALE-315 and Neotorch had median ages of approximately 62. Frail adults, people aged 75–80 or older, and those with substantial heart, lung, renal, autoimmune, or functional limitations were underrepresented. ([JAMA Network][13])

Consequently:

* The evidence supports efficacy in **fit older adults**, but is less certain for frail older adults.
* Chemotherapy fitness is central because nearly all positive preoperative trials paired the checkpoint inhibitor with platinum chemotherapy.
* Existing interstitial lung disease, severe COPD, prior pneumonitis, active autoimmune disease, organ transplantation, chronic immunosuppression, renal reserve, hearing, neuropathy, nutrition, mobility, cognition, and social support can materially change the benefit–harm balance.
* EGFR and ALK testing is important because many perioperative trials excluded known sensitizing alterations or analyzed EGFR/ALK-negative populations, and those cancers may require a different postoperative strategy.

The highest-yield details for matching your situation to these trials are the **exact clinical stage, tumor size, lymph-node involvement, histology, EGFR/ALK and other molecular results, PD-L1 percentage, performance status, lung function, and whether platinum chemotherapy is feasible**.
