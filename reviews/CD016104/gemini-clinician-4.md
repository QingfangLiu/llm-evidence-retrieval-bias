Treating older adults with early-stage, resectable non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) requires a careful balance. The addition of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) to surgical management has radically shifted the standard of care, but weighing those survival benefits against the risk of immune-mediated toxicities in a geriatric population is a nuanced clinical decision.

Based on the primary phase 3 clinical trials evaluating ICIs in the neoadjuvant (pre-surgery), adjuvant (post-surgery), and perioperative (both) settings, here is a breakdown of the benefits and harms, followed by the specific primary studies you requested.

## Efficacy and Benefits

The primary advantage of integrating ICIs with surgery—often combined with platinum-based chemotherapy—is a significant delay in disease recurrence and improved long-term survival.

* **Improved Pathological Complete Response (pCR):** Using ICIs alongside chemotherapy before surgery dramatically increases the pCR rate—meaning no viable tumor cells remain in the resected tissue or lymph nodes. For example, in the KEYNOTE-671 trial evaluating perioperative pembrolizumab, the pCR rate was notably higher than with chemotherapy alone (Wakelee et al., 2023). A similar substantial increase was observed with perioperative durvalumab in the AEGEAN trial (Heymach et al., 2023).
* **Prolonged Event-Free Survival (EFS):** Patients receiving neoadjuvant/perioperative immunotherapy have a significantly lower risk of disease recurrence, progression that prevents surgery, or death. The AEGEAN and KEYNOTE-671 trials both demonstrated clinically meaningful improvements in EFS compared to chemotherapy alone (Heymach et al., 2023; Wakelee et al., 2023).
* **Adjuvant Disease-Free Survival (DFS):** For patients who proceed directly to surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy, the addition of subsequent adjuvant ICIs also offers a strong benefit. The IMpower010 trial showed that adjuvant atezolizumab significantly improved DFS, particularly in patients whose tumors expressed PD-L1 at $\ge$ 1% (Felip et al., 2021).

## Safety and Harms

While ICIs do not generally impair a patient's ability to undergo planned surgery, they introduce unique toxicity profiles distinct from standard cytotoxic chemotherapy.

* **Immune-Related Adverse Events (irAEs):** The addition of ICIs carries the risk of systemic inflammation due to immune system activation. Common irAEs include endocrinopathies (such as thyroiditis, hyperthyroidism, and hypothyroidism), which frequently require lifelong hormone replacement (Felip et al., 2021).
* **Severe Toxicities:** More serious but less common immune-mediated harms include pneumonitis, colitis, and hepatitis. In KEYNOTE-671 and AEGEAN, the overall incidence of severe (Grade 3 or 4) adverse events was similar between the ICI and placebo arms, largely because the toxicity was driven by the chemotherapy backbone; however, irAEs led to treatment discontinuation in a notable minority of patients (Heymach et al., 2023; Wakelee et al., 2023).

## Considerations for Older Adults

While these landmark trials enrolled general adult populations, their pre-planned subgroup analyses typically stratify patients by age (usually $< 65$ vs. $\ge 65$ years).

In these trials, older adults ($\ge 65$ years) derived similar relative benefits in pCR, EFS, and DFS as the broader trial populations (Felip et al., 2021; Heymach et al., 2023; Wakelee et al., 2023). The primary hurdle for older adults is less about age diminishing the efficacy of the immunotherapy, and more about baseline fitness: patients must have the physiological reserve to tolerate the platinum-doublet chemotherapy backbone required in the neoadjuvant setting, as well as the reserve to withstand potential severe immune-mediated adverse events (like pneumonitis) if they occur.
