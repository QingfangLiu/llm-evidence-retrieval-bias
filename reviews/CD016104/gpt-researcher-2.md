## Scope

I identified **12 unique randomized controlled trials** with published primary results through **July 25, 2026**. CheckMate 816 contributes two eligible randomized regimen comparisons—nivolumab plus chemotherapy and nivolumab plus ipilimumab—so there are **13 treatment comparisons**.

I included studies of resectable or completely resected stage IB tumors ≥4 cm through stage IIIB-N2 NSCLC in which the control group received the same chemotherapy backbone without an immune-checkpoint inhibitor, placebo, best supportive care, or observation. I excluded single-arm studies and trials in which every randomized group received an immune-checkpoint inhibitor.

## Applicability to older adults

None of the trials was designed specifically for a geriatric population or powered to detect treatment-by-age interactions. Participants were generally medically fit, with ECOG performance status 0–1. CheckMate 77T is relatively informative because its median age was 66 years and approximately 56% were ≥65; ANVIL had median ages of 66 and 67 years. Conversely, TD-FOREKNOW excluded people older than 70 years. ([New England Journal of Medicine][1])

Most main reports provided exploratory efficacy estimates for an age cutoff of 65 years, but few supplied usable data for people ≥75, frailty, multimorbidity, functional status, or age-specific toxicity. In KEYNOTE-671, the OS point estimate appeared less favorable among participants ≥65, but its confidence interval overlapped the overall estimate; the investigators emphasized that subgroup analyses were descriptive and underpowered. ([PMC][2])

Accordingly, these trials mainly address **fit older adults**, not the broader population of older surgical candidates.

### Interpretation of the preoperative/perioperative evidence

Adding PD-1 or PD-L1 inhibition to neoadjuvant chemotherapy consistently increased pCR and prolonged EFS. Statistically significant OS benefits are now established in CheckMate 816, KEYNOTE-671 and RATIONALE-315; NADIM II also reported an OS advantage but was a small phase 2 trial. OS remains immature in several other phase 3 studies.

The chemotherapy-free nivolumab–ipilimumab comparison produced more pathological responses and less high-grade toxicity than chemotherapy, but its survival estimates were inconclusive. Thus, the most secure comparative evidence remains for **checkpoint inhibition combined with neoadjuvant chemotherapy**.

### Interpretation of the postoperative-only evidence

The adjuvant-only results are inconsistent:

* IMpower010 supports a DFS benefit primarily in PD-L1-selected disease, especially PD-L1 ≥50%, rather than across all resected patients.
* KEYNOTE-091 improved DFS overall, but not in its PD-L1 ≥50% coprimary population.
* BR.31 and ANVIL were negative despite large sample sizes and long follow-up.

This contrast suggests that the benefit seen with perioperative regimens cannot automatically be attributed to the postoperative component. None of the current placebo-controlled studies isolates adjuvant continuation after identical neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, so the incremental value of the postoperative ICI portion remains unresolved.

## Overall conclusion for older adults

For medically fit adults ≥65 with resectable NSCLC, neoadjuvant or perioperative chemoimmunotherapy provides convincing improvements in pathological response and EFS, and some regimens now improve OS. However:

* treatment effects specifically among people ≥75 remain uncertain;
* age-specific serious and immune-mediated toxicity is inadequately reported;
* frail adults, patients with poor performance status and those with substantial multimorbidity were underrepresented;
* adjuvant-only immunotherapy produces less consistent efficacy while still exposing patients to immune-related toxicity for approximately one year.
