As an evidence-synthesis researcher, you are looking at a clinical space that has completely shifted over the last few years. While dedicated phase 3 trials explicitly restricted to older adults are rare, the landmark randomized controlled trials (RCTs) establishing the standard of care for resectable (stage IB–IIIA) non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) all feature robust, pre-planned age subgroup analyses—typically stratifying outcomes for the $\ge$ 65 years cohort.

To support your synthesis using only primary literature, here is a breakdown of the foundational phase 3 RCTs assessing the benefits and harms of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) in this population.

## Neoadjuvant Therapy (Before Surgery)

The core benefit of giving ICIs before surgery is the potential to induce a pathological complete response (pCR)—eliminating viable tumor cells before the lung is resected—which correlates strongly with improved survival.

* **CheckMate 816:** This trial compared neoadjuvant nivolumab plus platinum-based chemotherapy against chemotherapy alone (Forde et al., 2022). It demonstrated a significant improvement in event-free survival (EFS) and a striking increase in pCR (24.0% vs. 2.2%). Subgroup analyses showed that the $\ge$ 65 years demographic experienced a hazard ratio for EFS consistent with the overall intention-to-treat population, indicating the efficacy holds for older adults.

## Adjuvant Therapy (After Surgery)

Adjuvant immunotherapy aims to eliminate micrometastatic disease that remains after surgery and standard chemotherapy.

* **IMpower010:** This study evaluated adjuvant atezolizumab versus best supportive care following resection and adjuvant chemotherapy (Felip et al., 2021). It found a significant disease-free survival (DFS) benefit, particularly in patients whose tumors expressed PD-L1 on $\ge$ 1% of tumor cells. The DFS benefit was maintained in the older adult subgroup.
* **PEARLS/KEYNOTE-091:** Evaluating adjuvant pembrolizumab versus placebo, this trial found that pembrolizumab significantly prolonged DFS regardless of PD-L1 expression levels (O’Brien et al., 2022).

## Perioperative Therapy (Before and After Surgery)

Recent trials have investigated "sandwiching" surgery between neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy and adjuvant immunotherapy to maximize both initial tumor downstaging and long-term systemic control.

* **KEYNOTE-671:** This trial utilized pembrolizumab alongside neoadjuvant chemotherapy, followed by adjuvant pembrolizumab (Wakelee et al., 2023). The regimen produced a statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvement in both EFS and major pathological response rates compared to the placebo group.
* **AEGEAN:** Testing a similar perioperative approach with durvalumab, this study confirmed significant EFS and pCR benefits over neoadjuvant chemotherapy alone (Heymach et al., 2023).

## Weighing the Harms

When synthesizing data for older adults, the toxicity profile requires careful attention, as decreased physiological reserve can amplify the impact of these harms:

1. **Immune-Related Adverse Events (irAEs):** Across all trials, ICIs introduced unique autoimmune toxicities not seen with standard chemotherapy, such as pneumonitis, hypothyroidism, and hepatitis (Felip et al., 2021; O’Brien et al., 2022).
2. **Surgical Feasibility:** A primary concern with neoadjuvant ICIs in older, frailer patients is that severe treatment-related adverse events might delay or ultimately preclude curative-intent surgery. However, primary data from both CheckMate 816 and KEYNOTE-671 indicate that the addition of ICIs to neoadjuvant chemotherapy did not significantly impede the feasibility or timing of surgery compared to chemotherapy alone (Forde et al., 2022; Wakelee et al., 2023).
