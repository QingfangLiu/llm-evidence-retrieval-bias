# Scope

I treated “limited to one area” as **resectable, nonmetastatic NSCLC**, generally stages IB–IIIB depending on the trial. Eligible comparisons were randomized studies in which the experimental arm received a PD-1/PD-L1 checkpoint inhibitor before surgery, after surgery, or both, and the control arm received the same background chemotherapy with placebo, chemotherapy alone, observation, or best supportive care.

As of **July 25, 2026**, I identified **14 randomized primary studies with outcome data**:

* **12 peer-reviewed full reports**
* **1 results-posted registry study:** MERMAID-1
* **1 conference abstract:** NADIM ADJUVANT

I excluded single-arm and observational studies, randomized comparisons in which every arm contained a checkpoint inhibitor, unresectable chemoradiation populations, and trials without results. For example, IMpower030 is completed but has no posted or published primary results yet. ([ClinicalTrials.gov][1])

## Main conclusions

**Neoadjuvant or perioperative chemoimmunotherapy has the most consistent evidence.** Eight randomized trials found higher pathological response rates, and the larger phase 3 trials generally found better event-free survival. Overall-survival benefit has now been demonstrated in CheckMate 816, KEYNOTE-671, and RATIONALE-315; NADIM II also produced an encouraging survival result but was a small phase 2 study. ([New England Journal of Medicine][2])

**Adjuvant-only checkpoint inhibition has produced mixed results.** IMpower010 and PEARLS/KEYNOTE-091 improved disease-free survival in their prespecified populations, but BR.31 and ANVIL were negative. MERMAID-1 was too small and curtailed to be informative, while the interim NADIM ADJUVANT result did not cross its significance threshold. ([PubMed][3])

**The evidence is indirect for geriatric practice.** No trial was designed specifically for older or frail adults. Participants generally had ECOG performance status 0–1, median ages were mostly in the early-to-mid 60s, adults aged 75 or older were poorly characterized, and age-specific adverse-event results were rarely reported. TD-FOREKNOW excluded patients older than 70. ANVIL was among the more age-representative studies, with median ages of 66 and 67 years, but it was negative. ([JAMA Network][4])

# Interpretation specifically for older adults

### Benefit

The individual phase 3 forest plots generally do not show a convincing loss of **EFS/DFS benefit at age 65 or older**, but those analyses are exploratory, have wide confidence intervals, and use chronological age rather than frailty, cognition, functional reserve, multimorbidity, or life expectancy. CheckMate 816 reported survival consistency across most subgroups. KEYNOTE-671 is a cautionary exception: its OS point estimate appeared less favorable in participants aged 65 or older, although the age confidence interval overlapped the overall trial effect and no statistically reliable treatment-by-age interaction was established. ([New England Journal of Medicine][2])

### Harms

The trials establish that checkpoint inhibitors add immune-mediated risks—including pneumonitis, colitis, hepatitis, endocrinopathies, dermatologic toxicity, and rare myocarditis—but **they do not establish whether those risks are greater in adults aged 75–85, frail adults, or people with substantial cardiopulmonary comorbidity**. Most published safety tables pool all ages. Rare fatal immune toxicities occurred in KEYNOTE-091, ANVIL, and NADIM ADJUVANT. ([PubMed][14])

### Important design limitation

The perioperative trials do **not** isolate the benefit of postoperative continuation. Their experimental arms added checkpoint inhibition both before and after surgery, while control arms omitted it in both phases. Therefore, KEYNOTE-671, AEGEAN, CheckMate 77T, Neotorch, and RATIONALE-315 cannot determine how much of the benefit came from the neoadjuvant component versus the adjuvant component. ([PubMed][8])
