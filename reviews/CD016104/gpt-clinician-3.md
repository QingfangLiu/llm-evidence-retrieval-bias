## Scope

I found **13 randomized primary trials** comparing perioperative immune checkpoint inhibition with the same background treatment **without an immune checkpoint inhibitor**—chemotherapy alone, placebo plus chemotherapy, placebo alone, best supportive care, or observation. I excluded single-arm studies, active-versus-active trials without a non-ICI control, unresectable disease studies, reviews, guidelines, editorials, and pooled analyses.

Most evidence concerns **resectable stage II–III NSCLC**. Some trials included stage IB only when the tumor was at least 4 cm; these results do not establish benefit for stage IA or smaller stage IB tumors.

## Findings most relevant to older adults

**Neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy has the most consistent evidence.** Across several trials, adding a PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor before surgery increased pathologic complete response and prolonged event-free survival. Overall-survival improvement has now been demonstrated with neoadjuvant nivolumab plus chemotherapy and with the perioperative pembrolizumab and tislelizumab regimens. ([PubMed][1])

**Adjuvant-only immunotherapy is less consistent.** Atezolizumab and pembrolizumab improved disease-free survival in their respective trials, but adjuvant durvalumab and nivolumab subsequently failed to improve disease-free survival. There is not a reliable class-wide adjuvant benefit. ([PubMed][2])

**The older-adult evidence is indirect.** None of these trials was designed specifically for geriatric patients. Participants were generally surgery-eligible, had ECOG performance status 0–1, and had median ages around the early to mid-60s. ANVIL had median ages of 66 and 67 years, but few trials provide useful results specifically for patients aged 75 or older, and none incorporated frailty or comprehensive geriatric assessment. TD-FOREKNOW excluded patients older than 70. ([PubMed][3])

**Perioperative trials do not isolate the postoperative contribution.** KEYNOTE-671, AEGEAN, CheckMate 77T, Neotorch, and RATIONALE-315 randomized the entire preoperative-plus-postoperative strategy. They cannot determine whether patients who have already received neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy need the additional year of postoperative immunotherapy. CheckMate 816 establishes that a neoadjuvant-only course can itself improve survival. ([PubMed][4])

## Practical interpretation for an older surgical candidate

For a physiologically fit older adult with **resectable, EGFR/ALK-negative stage II–III disease**, the clearest randomized evidence favors adding a checkpoint inhibitor to **neoadjuvant platinum-doublet chemotherapy**. The strongest direct survival evidence is currently available for CheckMate 816, KEYNOTE-671, and RATIONALE-315. CheckMate 816 is particularly informative when limiting treatment burden is important because it used only three preoperative chemoimmunotherapy cycles and no protocol-mandated postoperative immunotherapy. ([PubMed][1])

Potential harms particularly relevant to older adults include chemotherapy-related cytopenias and functional decline, pneumonitis in patients with limited pulmonary reserve, colitis and dehydration, hepatitis, prolonged corticosteroid exposure, thyroid or adrenal dysfunction that may be permanent, and rare myocarditis or treatment-related death. Grade 3 or worse toxicity generally ranged from approximately one-third to nearly one-half in the international phase 3 chemoimmunotherapy trials, although some studies reported higher rates because they counted all chemotherapy-associated laboratory abnormalities. ([PubMed][5])

For a frail patient, someone with ECOG performance status 2 or worse, severe autoimmune disease, substantial cognitive or functional impairment, or very advanced age, these trials provide limited direct evidence. Individual estimates of life expectancy, competing mortality, pulmonary and cardiac reserve, postoperative recovery probability, and the consequences of high-dose corticosteroids may be as important as chronological age.
