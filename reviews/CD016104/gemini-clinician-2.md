Treating older adults with early-stage, resectable non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) requires carefully balancing the aggressive biology of the disease against the physiological reserves of the patient. In recent years, integrating immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs)—either neoadjuvantly (before surgery), adjuvantly (after surgery), or perioperatively (both)—has fundamentally changed the standard of care, offering significant benefits over surgery and chemotherapy alone.

Here is a breakdown of the benefits and harms of these approaches, grounded entirely in the landmark phase 3 trials.

## Core Benefits

* **Pathologic Complete Response (pCR):** Adding an ICI to neoadjuvant platinum-doublet chemotherapy drastically increases the odds of finding no viable tumor cells at the time of resection. For instance, the CheckMate 816 trial demonstrated that adding nivolumab to neoadjuvant chemotherapy increased the pCR rate from 2.2% to 24.0% (Forde et al., 2022).
* **Event-Free and Disease-Free Survival (EFS/DFS):** Across all treatment timings, ICIs significantly delay recurrence. This benefit holds true whether using a neoadjuvant-only approach (Forde et al., 2022), a purely adjuvant approach post-resection (Felip et al., 2021; O’Brien et al., 2022), or a continuous perioperative approach (Cascone et al., 2024; Spicer et al., 2024).
* **Overall Survival (OS):** We are now seeing mature data confirming that these surrogate endpoints translate to actual survival. The KEYNOTE-671 trial (evaluating perioperative pembrolizumab) recently demonstrated a statistically significant improvement in overall survival over chemotherapy alone, fundamentally proving the long-term benefit of early ICI integration (Spicer et al., 2024).

## Efficacy and Considerations in Older Adults

While older adults are routinely enrolled in these phase 3 trials, they represent a subgroup with distinct clinical considerations:

* **Maintained Efficacy (65–74 years):** Subgroup analyses consistently show that patients in the 65–74 age bracket derive EFS and DFS benefits that closely mirror the overall trial populations.
* **Attenuated Benefit (≥75 years):** Data for patients 75 and older is frequently limited by small enrollment numbers. In some trials, the hazard ratios for this uppermost age group carry wider confidence intervals or show a blunted statistical benefit compared to younger cohorts (Felip et al., 2021; O’Brien et al., 2022). This is often attributed to the competing risks of non-cancer mortality and higher rates of treatment discontinuation due to frailty, rather than a failure of the immunotherapy itself.

## Harms and Surgical Feasibility

* **Surgical Safety:** A major historical concern was that neoadjuvant ICIs might cause tissue inflammation that complicates surgery, or cause toxicities that delay it entirely. However, trials consistently report that adding immunotherapy to neoadjuvant chemotherapy does *not* decrease the proportion of patients who undergo surgery, nor does it increase the rate of surgical cancellations or perioperative complications (Cascone et al., 2024; Forde et al., 2022).
* **Immune-Related Adverse Events (irAEs):** ICIs introduce the risk of irAEs such as pneumonitis (which can complicate baseline COPD or surgical recovery), thyroid dysfunction, and colitis. While severe (grade 3 or 4) irAEs are uncommon, older adults generally have less physiologic reserve to recover from them if they occur.
* **Toxicity vs. Schedule:** Older adults are particularly susceptible to the toxicities of the platinum-chemotherapy backbone. Neoadjuvant strategies, which typically cap chemotherapy at 3 to 4 cycles, are sometimes better tolerated by older adults than prolonged, post-surgical adjuvant chemotherapy regimens.
