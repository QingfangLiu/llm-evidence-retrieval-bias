I now have enough well-documented primary studies to give you a solid, varied list. A quick note on the evidence itself: hip OA has been studied less than knee OA, and several land-based exercise trials enroll mixed hip-and-knee samples rather than hip-only cohorts — I've flagged which is which below.

## Summary

Individual RCTs generally find that land-based exercise produces **small-to-moderate improvements in pain and physical function** in hip OA compared with no exercise/usual care, though effect sizes are modest and some trials find larger benefits for pain than for function (or vice versa). Head-to-head trials comparing exercise types (e.g., adding manual therapy, comparing dosage) often find no added benefit from the extra component. Long-term follow-up of at least one trial (Svege et al.) suggests exercise therapy may delay progression to total hip replacement.

## Primary studies (RCTs)

1. **Fernandes L, Storheim K, Sandvik L, Nordsletten L, Risberg MA (2010).** *Efficacy of patient education and supervised exercise vs patient education alone in patients with hip osteoarthritis: a single blind randomized clinical trial.* Osteoarthritis and Cartilage, 18(10):1237–43. (Hip-only; n=109)

2. **Svege I, Nordsletten L, Fernandes L, Risberg MA (2015).** *Exercise therapy may postpone total hip replacement surgery in patients with hip osteoarthritis: a long-term follow-up of a randomised trial.* Annals of the Rheumatic Diseases, 74(1):164–9. (Long-term follow-up of the Fernandes et al. trial)

3. **Tak E, Staats P, Van Hespen A, et al. (2005).** *The effects of an exercise program for older adults with osteoarthritis of the hip.* Journal of Rheumatology, 32(6):1106–13. (Hip-only)

4. **van Baar ME, Dekker J, Oostendorp RA, Bijl D, Voorn TB, Lemmens JA, Bijlsma JW (1998).** *The effectiveness of exercise therapy in patients with osteoarthritis of the hip or knee: a randomized clinical trial.* Journal of Rheumatology, 25(12):2432–9. (Mixed hip/knee sample)

5. **van Baar ME, Dekker J, Oostendorp RA, Bijl D, Voorn TB, Bijlsma JW (2001).** *Effectiveness of exercise in patients with osteoarthritis of hip or knee: nine months' follow up.* Annals of the Rheumatic Diseases, 60(12):1123–30. (Follow-up of the above trial; mixed hip/knee)

6. **Krauß I, Steinhilber B, Haupt G, Miller R, Martus P, Janßen P (2014).** *Exercise Therapy in Hip Osteoarthritis—A Randomized Controlled Trial.* Deutsches Ärzteblatt International, 111(35–36):592–9. ("Hip School" program; hip-only, n=217)

7. **French HP, Cusack T, Brennan A, Caffrey A, Conroy R, Cuddy V, et al. (2013).** *Exercise and Manual Physiotherapy Arthritis Research Trial (EMPART) for osteoarthritis of the hip: a multicenter randomized controlled trial.* Archives of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation, 94(2):302–14. (Hip-only, n=131; compares exercise alone vs. exercise + manual therapy vs. waitlist control)

8. **Foley A, Halbert J, Hewitt T, Crotty M (2003).** *Does hydrotherapy improve strength and physical function in patients with osteoarthritis – a randomised controlled trial comparing a gym based and a hydrotherapy based strengthening programme.* Annals of the Rheumatic Diseases, 62(12):1162–7. (Mixed hip/knee; includes a land-based/gym exercise arm)

9. **Bieler T, et al. (2017).** *Effects of medical exercise therapy in patients with hip osteoarthritis: A randomized controlled trial with six months follow-up. A pilot study.* Musculoskeletal Science and Practice. (Hip-only pilot RCT)
