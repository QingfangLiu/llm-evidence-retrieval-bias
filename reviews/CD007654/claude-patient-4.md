Here's what the primary-study evidence shows on weight-loss medications and blood pressure/hypertension-related outcomes. I focused on individual RCTs and their published main or blood-pressure-specific findings, not reviews or meta-analyses.

## GLP-1/GIP receptor agonists

**SELECT trial (semaglutide, no diabetes, established CVD)** — In this large placebo-controlled cardiovascular outcomes trial, the observed decrease of 3.3 mm Hg in systolic blood pressure was greater than the 2 mm Hg decrease that a separate meta-analysis had predicted would yield a 7% reduction in vascular mortality. A prespecified subgroup analysis presented at AHA found that among the 82% of participants with a history of hypertension, semaglutide significantly and consistently reduced systolic blood pressure across baseline BP categories compared with placebo, with reductions ranging from about 2.8 to 3.7 mmHg depending on category, and semaglutide showed consistent cardiovascular benefit regardless of hypertension history or baseline BP.

**SUSTAIN-6 (semaglutide, type 2 diabetes, CV outcomes)** — At week 104, mean systolic blood pressure fell from a baseline of 135.6 mmHg to 132.2 mmHg in the 0.5 mg group and 130.3 mmHg in the 1.0 mg semaglutide group, versus smaller reductions in the placebo group, producing a between-group difference of 1.3 mmHg (not significant) at the lower dose and 2.6 mmHg (p<0.001) at the higher dose. The authors noted that reductions in glycated hemoglobin, weight, and systolic blood pressure may all have contributed to the observed cardiovascular risk reduction.

**SURMOUNT-1 (tirzepatide, obesity)** — Weight reduction with tirzepatide was accompanied by greater improvements in all measured cardiovascular and metabolic risk factors, including waist circumference, systolic and diastolic blood pressure, and fasting measures, compared with placebo.

**SURMOUNT-5 (tirzepatide vs. semaglutide, head-to-head)** — In this active-comparator trial in people with obesity and at least one obesity-related complication (including hypertension), tirzepatide demonstrated significantly greater improvements than semaglutide at 72 weeks in systolic and diastolic blood pressure, along with other cardiometabolic markers.

## Phentermine/topiramate

**CONQUER trial** — This placebo-controlled RCT enrolled overweight/obese adults with ≥2 comorbidities, including hypertension. At the two highest phentermine/topiramate doses, treatment produced a 5.2 mmHg reduction in systolic blood pressure and about a 3.0 mmHg reduction in diastolic blood pressure, alongside dose-related weight loss.

**EQUIP trial** — A companion phase 3 RCT in severely obese adults using higher-dose phentermine/topiramate, also placebo-controlled, with blood pressure and metabolic endpoints reported.

**Ambulatory BP substudy (phentermine/topiramate ER)** — A dedicated randomized, double-blind trial specifically measuring 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure, since office readings can understate true effects. The study enrolled 565 adults with overweight/obesity, excluded those with baseline BP above 140/90, and used mean 24-hour systolic BP via ambulatory monitoring as its primary endpoint.

## Bottom line
Across these independent RCTs, weight loss with GLP-1/GIP-based drugs and with phentermine/topiramate is consistently accompanied by modest but statistically significant reductions in blood pressure (roughly 2–6 mmHg systolic), and in SELECT this occurred alongside reduced cardiovascular events. This is observational-within-trial correlation between weight loss and BP change, not necessarily proof that weight loss itself (versus a drug's other mechanisms) is the cause — some trials note BP effects appearing before much weight is lost, suggesting partly independent mechanisms (natriuresis, sympathetic effects, etc.).
