## What the studies show

**GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide)**

The largest cardiovascular-outcomes trial, **SELECT** (Lincoff et al., *NEJM* 2023), randomized 17,604 adults with established cardiovascular disease and overweight/obesity (no diabetes) to semaglutide 2.4mg or placebo. This multinational, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial found that semaglutide reduced cardiovascular risk associated with overweight and obesity in the absence of diabetes. A prespecified subgroup analysis presented at AHA found that among the 82% of SELECT participants with a history of hypertension, those on placebo had a higher rate of major cardiovascular events than those on semaglutide, and semaglutide consistently lowered systolic blood pressure across all baseline BP categories, from normal through stage 2 hypertension.

An individual-patient-data pooled analysis of three semaglutide obesity trials (STEP 1, 3, 4), published in the *European Heart Journal*, specifically examined people with hypertension. Across 3,136 participants, semaglutide produced a systolic BP reduction of about 5 mmHg more than placebo overall, with similar reductions (roughly 3–5 mmHg) in subgroups with diagnosed hypertension, elevated baseline BP, and even apparent treatment-resistant hypertension, with the effect substantially mediated by weight loss.

**Dual GLP-1/GIP agonist (tirzepatide)**

The **SURMOUNT-1** trial (Jastreboff et al., *NEJM* 2022) and its ambulatory-blood-pressure substudy provide primary-study evidence here. The 24-hour ambulatory BP substudy found significant reductions in blood pressure across every tirzepatide dose compared with placebo, with no significant difference in effect based on baseline blood pressure or presence of hypertension. Office-based measurements told a similar story: participants on tirzepatide were far more likely than those on placebo to have normal blood pressure at week 72 (58% vs. 35%), with effects broadly consistent across baseline BP subgroups.

**Phentermine/topiramate**

The **CONQUER** trial (Gadde et al., *Lancet* 2011) randomized overweight/obese adults with ≥2 comorbidities (including hypertension) to placebo or one of two doses of phentermine/topiramate. This placebo-controlled phase 3 trial found significant improvements in blood pressure, along with waist circumference and several metabolic markers, compared with placebo. A dedicated ambulatory BP monitoring trial later confirmed this with objective 24-hour measurements rather than office readings.

**Bariatric surgery (not a medication, but worth flagging)**

Since you're comparing options, the **GATEWAY** trial (Schiavon et al., *Circulation* 2018, *Ann Intern Med* 2020) is the strongest primary-study evidence for treating hypertension by inducing major weight loss — a surgical rather than pharmacological approach, but useful context. This randomized trial assigned patients with obesity and hypertension on ≥2 medications to gastric bypass plus medical therapy or medical therapy alone, with the primary outcome being a ≥30% reduction in antihypertensive medications while maintaining BP under 140/90.

## A caveat worth knowing

Most of this evidence is about **blood pressure as a secondary/surrogate outcome**, not hard outcomes like stroke or heart attack, specifically *in* people who already have hypertension. SELECT is the exception — it used cardiovascular events as its primary outcome, though its subgroup analysis by hypertension status was still a secondary analysis. None of these trials was designed with hypertension remission as the sole primary endpoint outside of GATEWAY (surgical) and the STEP/SURMOUNT pooled analyses (post hoc).
