Here are individual primary studies (randomized controlled trials) evaluating weight-loss medications and their effects on blood-pressure/cardiovascular outcomes in people with obesity or overweight, several specifically enrolling or reporting on hypertensive subgroups. I excluded reviews, meta-analyses, and guidelines as requested.

**GLP-1 receptor agonists / dual agonists**

The SELECT trial randomized patients with overweight or obesity and preexisting cardiovascular disease (without diabetes) at 804 sites in 41 countries to semaglutide 2.4 mg or placebo, finding semaglutide superior to placebo in reducing cardiovascular death, nonfatal MI, or nonfatal stroke over a mean follow-up of 39.8 months. (Lincoff AM et al., N Engl J Med, NEJMoa2307563)

A pooled analysis of five randomized STEP trials assessed changes in antihypertensive medication use with semaglutide 2.4 mg versus placebo, reporting hypertension remission (BP <140/90 without antihypertensive medications) in 5.7% of the semaglutide group versus 3.4% of placebo, and confirming that systolic BP improved significantly with semaglutide versus placebo from randomization to week 68. (Tchang et al., Obesity, 2025)

STEP 1 was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 1,961 adults with obesity/overweight without diabetes, assigned 2:1 to semaglutide 2.4 mg or placebo for 68 weeks plus lifestyle intervention. (Wilding JPH et al., N Engl J Med, NEJMoa2032183)

STEP 5 evaluated semaglutide 2.4 mg versus placebo over 104 weeks in 304 randomized adults with obesity/overweight, assessing weight and cardiometabolic risk factors. (Garvey WT et al., Nature Medicine)

SURMOUNT-3 was a double-blind, placebo-controlled trial randomizing 579 adults with obesity/overweight to tirzepatide (maximum tolerated dose) or placebo for 72 weeks after an initial 12-week intensive lifestyle intervention. (Wadden TA et al., Nature Medicine)

SURMOUNT-1 was a multi-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial comparing tirzepatide 5, 10, and 15 mg to placebo in 2,539 adults with obesity or overweight with hypertension, dyslipidemia, obstructive sleep apnea, or cardiovascular disease as qualifying comorbidities. (Jastreboff AM et al., N Engl J Med)

SURMOUNT-5 was a phase 3b, open-label, randomized, active-controlled trial comparing maximum tolerated doses of tirzepatide versus semaglutide in adults with obesity but without diabetes over 72 weeks. (Aronne LJ et al., N Engl J Med, NEJMoa2416394)

**Phentermine/topiramate**

CONQUER was a 56-week phase 3, randomized, placebo-controlled trial assigning overweight/obese adults with two or more comorbidities (hypertension, dyslipidemia, diabetes/prediabetes, or abdominal obesity) to placebo, low-dose, or high-dose phentermine/topiramate. (Gadde KM et al., Lancet, 2011)

A prespecified analysis of the CONQUER trial evaluated changes in cardiovascular risk factors, including blood pressure in the hypertensive subgroup, stratified by treatment and magnitude of weight loss. (ScienceDirect/Am J Cardiol)

A separate randomized, multicenter, double-blind study enrolled 565 adults with overweight/obesity to evaluate phentermine/topiramate extended-release, phentermine alone, and placebo using ambulatory blood pressure monitoring. (ScienceDirect)

**Orlistat**

XENDOS was a 4-year, double-blind, prospective study randomizing 3,305 obese patients to lifestyle changes plus orlistat 120 mg or placebo three times daily, with blood pressure as a secondary endpoint. (Torgerson JS et al., Diabetes Care, 2004)

- Bakris G, Calhoun D, Egan B, et al. Orlistat improves blood pressure control in obese subjects with treated but inadequately controlled hypertension. *J Hypertens*. 2002;20:2257-2267 — a randomized trial specifically in obese patients with inadequately controlled hypertension.

A few notes for your use: several of these (STEP pooled analysis, SURMOUNT-3/5) report blood pressure as a secondary/exploratory endpoint rather than the primary outcome, so treat those BP findings as hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory. The Bakris and CONQUER BP-focused analyses are the most directly targeted at hypertensive outcomes.
