Based on my searches, here are individual primary studies (RCTs) that directly investigate weight-loss medicines in people with hypertension, or that enrolled/analyzed hypertensive subgroups and reported blood pressure or cardiovascular health outcomes. I've excluded reviews, meta-analyses, and guidelines as requested.

**GLP-1 receptor agonist / dual agonist trials**

Lincoff AM et al. (NEJM, 2023) — the SELECT trial found that in patients with preexisting cardiovascular disease and overweight or obesity but without diabetes, weekly subcutaneous semaglutide 2.4 mg was superior to placebo in reducing cardiovascular death, nonfatal MI, or nonfatal stroke over a mean follow-up of 39.8 months. A prespecified subgroup analysis of this same trial reported that among 17,604 randomized patients, 14,392 (82%) had a history of hypertension, and semaglutide showed consistent cardiovascular benefit regardless of hypertension history or baseline blood pressure category (SELECT hypertension subgroup analysis, Circulation, 2024).

The SURMOUNT-1 trial (NCT04184622) assessed tirzepatide 5, 10, and 15 mg in adults with BMI ≥27, including a prospective ambulatory blood-pressure-monitoring substudy in participants with baseline BP under 140/90 mmHg who were on stable antihypertensive therapy, showing reductions in daytime and nighttime BP (published in *Hypertension*).

**Phentermine/topiramate and topiramate trials**

McMahon FG et al. (Am J Cardiol, 2000) — a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 531 obese subjects with established hypertension assigned to placebo or topiramate 96 or 192 mg/day, finding weight loss of 5.9% and 6.5% with topiramate versus 1.9% with placebo, and dose-related reductions in diastolic BP of 5.5 and 6.3 mmHg versus 2.1 mmHg with placebo.

Tonstad S et al. (Int J Obesity, 2004) — a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of topiramate (96, 192, 256 mg/day) in 1,289 obese subjects, including those with controlled hypertension, showing significant weight loss and improvements in blood pressure and glucose tolerance over one year.

Bays HE et al. (2024) — a randomized, multicenter, double-blind study of phentermine/topiramate extended-release versus phentermine alone versus placebo in 565 adults with overweight/obesity, most of whom had hypertension, using 24-hour ambulatory BP monitoring; PHEN/TPM reduced systolic BP by 3.2 mmHg versus placebo and 4.7 mmHg versus phentermine alone.

Gadde KM et al. (the CONQUER trial) — a 56-week phase 3 trial randomizing overweight/obese adults with two or more comorbidities (including hypertension) to placebo or two doses of phentermine/topiramate CR, with weight loss as the primary endpoint. A dedicated cardiovascular risk sub-analysis of CONQUER, evaluating changes in cardiovascular risk factors specifically in obese participants with dyslipidemia and/or hypertension receiving phentermine/topiramate ER, was also published (ScienceDirect, 2013).

**Bariatric surgery (procedural, not pharmacologic — noted for context)**

Schiavon CA et al. (the GATEWAY trial) — a randomized, single-center trial in 100 patients with obesity and hypertension (using ≥2 antihypertensive medications), comparing Roux-en-Y gastric bypass plus medical therapy to medical therapy alone, with the primary endpoint of ≥30% reduction in antihypertensive medications while maintaining BP <140/90 mmHg at 12 months. Note: this is a surgical, not pharmacologic, intervention — I've included it only because it's frequently discussed alongside weight-loss drug trials, but it doesn't meet your "weight-loss medicines" scope.
