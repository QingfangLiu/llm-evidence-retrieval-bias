The short answer is yes—recent primary clinical trials strongly demonstrate that modern anti-obesity medications, particularly GLP-1 and dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonists, significantly improve overall cardiovascular health and directly lower blood pressure in patients with obesity and hypertension.

I have filtered the literature to exclude all reviews and meta-analyses, focusing strictly on high-quality, primary randomized controlled trials (RCTs) that investigate these clinical outcomes.

## Cardiovascular Risk Reduction

The most definitive evidence regarding overall health improvement comes from the **SELECT trial**, which was designed specifically to measure cardiovascular outcomes rather than just weight loss.

* **Major Adverse Cardiovascular Events (MACE):** In a trial of over 17,000 patients with overweight or obesity and preexisting cardiovascular disease (without diabetes), semaglutide 2.4 mg reduced the risk of a composite primary endpoint—cardiovascular death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, or nonfatal stroke—by 20% compared to placebo (Lincoff et al., 2023).
* **Benefit Across Blood Pressure Profiles:** A secondary analysis of the SELECT trial demonstrated that this 20% reduction in MACE was consistent regardless of the patient's baseline blood pressure category. Furthermore, semaglutide significantly lowered systolic blood pressure across all baseline categories (Verma et al., 2024).

## Direct Blood Pressure Lowering

Both semaglutide and tirzepatide produce clinically meaningful reductions in blood pressure. While these improvements are largely mediated by significant weight loss, early cardiovascular benefits suggest these medications may also improve endothelial function independently of weight reduction.

* **Tirzepatide and BP Normalization:** A stratified analysis of the **SURMOUNT-1 trial** evaluated the blood pressure of adults taking once-weekly tirzepatide. By 72 weeks, treatment resulted in a net reduction of 6.8 mmHg in systolic and 4.2 mmHg in diastolic blood pressure. Crucially, 58.0% of patients on tirzepatide achieved a *normal* blood pressure by the end of the trial, compared to only 35.2% on placebo (Krumholz et al., 2024).
* **Substantial Weight Loss as the Driver:** The primary SURMOUNT-1 and STEP 1 trials confirm the underlying mechanism of profound weight reduction. Tirzepatide (15 mg) drove a mean weight loss of 20.9% (Jastreboff et al., 2022), while semaglutide (2.4 mg) achieved a 14.9% reduction (Wilding et al., 2021). In the SURMOUNT-1 BP sub-analysis, mediation statistics indicated that weight loss explained about 68% of the systolic and 71% of the diastolic blood pressure reductions (Krumholz et al., 2024).
