It takes a lot of strength to focus on your mental health while also thinking about tackling a smoking habit. Being in an inpatient psychiatry ward is a challenging environment, but the good news is that starting a quit-smoking program while you are there is not only possible—it is highly effective.

Primary studies (specifically randomized controlled trials, which are the gold standard for testing medical treatments) show that when smoking cessation interventions are initiated during a psychiatric hospital stay, they significantly improve your chances of successfully quitting and staying smoke-free after you are discharged.

## How Inpatient Quit Programs Help

**1. They Give You a Structured Head Start**
A hospital setting provides a controlled environment to manage the hardest part of quitting: early nicotine withdrawal. A clinical trial evaluating adults with severe mental illness found that a multi-part intervention started during an inpatient stay—and importantly, continued after discharge—led to significantly higher engagement in treatment and better quit rates six months later compared to standard, usual care (Brown et al., 2021).

**2. Quitting Does Not Harm Your Mental Health Recovery**
A common fear is that quitting smoking will worsen psychiatric symptoms, increase stress, or derail treatment. The evidence shows this isn't true. Research on adults in a locked acute psychiatry unit found that initiating tailored tobacco cessation treatment did not negatively impact mental health or psychiatric severity (Prochaska et al., 2014).

**3. They May Keep You Out of the Hospital**
Interestingly, getting help with smoking might benefit your broader recovery. In the same study, the group that received standard care (no specialized smoking intervention) had a significantly higher likelihood of psychiatric rehospitalization than the group that received the smoking cessation treatment (Prochaska et al., 2014). The researchers noted that comprehensive cessation treatment appears to decrease rehospitalization risk, perhaps by providing broader therapeutic coping benefits.

## What Makes These Programs Successful?

The studies show that the most successful programs share a few key features:

* **Combination therapy:** Using medications to manage physical cravings (like nicotine replacement patches or gum) alongside psychological counseling or motivational interviewing (Brown et al., 2021; Prochaska et al., 2014).
* **Sustained care:** The support shouldn't stop when you leave the ward. Programs that transition your care to community-based support, outpatient therapy, or telephone counseling after discharge yield the highest long-term success rates (Brown et al., 2021).

If you are considering quitting, talking to your care team about starting nicotine replacement and getting counseling while you are still admitted is a medically proven way to set yourself up for success.
