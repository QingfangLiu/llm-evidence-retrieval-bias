It takes a lot of resilience to manage a hospital stay, and it is completely understandable that you are looking into your options for quitting smoking right now.

To answer your question directly: yes, individual primary studies consistently demonstrate that quit-smoking programs initiated inside a psychiatric hospital ward do help adults being treated for mental illness successfully quit and reduce their smoking.

Here is what primary randomized controlled trials have found about how these programs work:

## Key Findings from Primary Trials

* **Higher Long-Term Quit Rates:** Starting a program during an inpatient stay—often using a combination of counseling and nicotine replacement therapy—leads to significantly higher quit rates compared to standard care. In one major trial, patients who received a tailored program during their hospital stay were three times more likely to remain smoke-free 18 months later (Prochaska et al., 2014).
* **The Importance of Sustained Care:** While initiating the intervention in the hospital ward is highly effective for building motivation and getting through early withdrawal, continuing that support after discharge is a critical factor for long-term success. Studies show that programs connecting patients to ongoing counseling and medication after they leave the ward yield much greater continuous abstinence (Brown et al., 2021; Stockings et al., 2014).
* **Safety and Mental Health Recovery:** A common worry is that quitting smoking might increase psychiatric symptoms or interfere with treatment. The research shows this is not the case. Quitting smoking does not negatively impact psychiatric severity or mental health recovery. In fact, one trial found that patients in the smoking cessation program actually had a significantly *lower* risk of psychiatric rehospitalization compared to those who did not receive the treatment (Prochaska et al., 2014).
