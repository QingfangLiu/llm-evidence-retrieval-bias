Based on my searches, here are individual primary studies (not reviews, meta-analyses, or guidelines) that examine smoking-cessation programmes initiated on psychiatric inpatient wards for adults with mental illness:

**1. Prochaska JJ, Hall SE, Delucchi K, Hall SM (2014)**
*Efficacy of initiating tobacco dependence treatment in inpatient psychiatry: a randomized controlled trial.* American Journal of Public Health, 104(8):1557–1565.
RCT (n=224) on a locked acute psychiatry unit in San Francisco with a 100% smoking ban. Compared motivational tobacco treatment + NRT vs. usual care. Found higher abstinence in the intervention group at 3, 6, 12, and 18 months, and lower psychiatric rehospitalization.

**2. Stockings EA, Bowman JA, Baker AL, et al. (2014)**
*Impact of a postdischarge smoking cessation intervention for smokers admitted to an inpatient psychiatric facility: a randomized controlled trial.* Nicotine & Tobacco Research, 16(11):1417–1428.
RCT (n=205) at an Australian inpatient psychiatric facility; postdischarge psychosocial + pharmacological support vs. treatment as usual.

**3. Metse AP, Wiggers J, Wye P, et al. (2017)**
*Efficacy of a universal smoking cessation intervention initiated in inpatient psychiatry and continued post-discharge: A randomised controlled trial.* Australian & New Zealand Journal of Psychiatry (or related journal).
RCT (n=754) across four Australian psychiatric inpatient facilities; brief motivational interview + self-help in hospital, followed by 4 months of post-discharge pharmacological/psychosocial support vs. usual care.

**4. Prochaska JJ, Fletcher L, Hall SE, Hall SM (2006) / related San Francisco Bay Area cohort — Prins B, et al.**
*Smokers with serious mental illness and requests for nicotine replacement therapy post-hospitalisation.* Tobacco Control (analysis derived from a randomized trial of counselling + NRT across three smoke-free San Francisco Bay Area hospitals, n=816).
Examines quit attempts, NRT requests, and abstinence at 1 week post-discharge among psychiatric inpatients.

**5. Prins B, Hrywna M, Delnevo CD, et al./ Rogers ES, et al. (2021)**
*Sustained Care Smoking Cessation Intervention for Individuals Hospitalized for Psychiatric Disorders: The Helping HAND 3 Randomized Clinical Trial.* JAMA Psychiatry.
RCT of a multicomponent, sustained-care smoking cessation intervention vs. usual care for adults with serious mental illness, begun during and continued after psychiatric hospitalization.

**6. Barnett PG, Wong W, Jeffers A, Hall SM, Prochaska JJ (2015)**
*Cost-effectiveness of smoking cessation treatment initiated during psychiatric hospitalization: analysis from a randomized, controlled trial.* Journal of Clinical Psychiatry, 76(10):e1285-91.
Economic analysis using data from a randomized trial (stage-tailored intervention vs. standard care) among adult inpatients at a smoke-free psychiatric unit (Langley Porter Psychiatric Institute, UCSF), reporting biochemically verified abstinence over 18 months alongside cost outcomes.

A few notes:
- I excluded protocol-only papers (which describe planned trials but don't yet report cessation outcomes) and a purely descriptive chart-review study (Prochaska, Gill, Hall 2004) that documented smoking rates and treatment gaps but did not test an intervention's efficacy.
- I also excluded SCIMITAR+ (Lancet Psychiatry) since that trial recruited participants largely through primary/community mental health services rather than initiating treatment specifically on an inpatient ward.
- Some of these studies (e.g., #1–#3) come from the same Australian or UCSF research groups but represent genuinely separate trials/samples.
