## What "SC-MIGS" covers
These procedures lower eye pressure by working on the natural outflow pathway — either bypassing the trabecular meshwork into Schlemm's canal (stents like iStent/iStent inject, Hydrus Microstent), incising/ablating the trabecular meshwork and canal wall (Trabectome, Kahook Dual Blade), or dilating/opening the full circumference of the canal (GATT, ab interno canaloplasty/ABiC, OMNI, Streamline).

## Benefits reported in primary studies
- **IOP and medication reduction**: Across RCTs, IOP drops of roughly 20–35% and reductions of 1–2 medications are consistently reported, whether combined with cataract surgery or standalone. The pivotal iStent and HORIZON (Hydrus) trials both showed significantly higher rates of medication-free, controlled IOP versus cataract surgery alone at 1–5 years.
- **Durability**: The HORIZON 5-year data followed patients randomized to receive a Hydrus Microstent combined with cataract surgery versus cataract surgery alone, and separate 5-year analyses reported sustained reductions in secondary surgery need and medication use, plus a lower rate of visual field progression versus cataract surgery alone — a rare finding for a MIGS device.
- **Device comparisons**: The COMPARE RCT found more Hydrus subjects were medication-free at 12 months than those receiving two first-generation iStents, with fewer secondary surgeries in the Hydrus group, suggesting canal-scaffolding devices may outperform focal bypass stents on some measures, though the two showed similar safety profiles.
- **Circumferential approaches (GATT, ABiC, OMNI)** tend to show larger IOP reductions (30–37%) than focal stents, likely because they address a larger segment of the outflow system, and multiple cohorts report success rates around 75–90% at 1–2 years, including in more advanced or refractory glaucoma.
- **Favorable safety relative to trabeculectomy**: A comparative cohort study of GATT versus trabeculectomy found similar success probabilities at 12 months between the two techniques (about 70–77%), with SC-MIGS generally carrying a lower complication burden than filtering surgery.

## Harms/risks reported in primary studies
- **Hyphema and transient IOP spikes**: Ab interno trabeculotomy/Trabectome studies note this is a recognized early complication, since the canal and collector channels are directly opened to blood reflux.
- **Secondary surgery**: Failure/reoperation rates vary widely by device and disease severity — GATT cohorts report 24–34% failure by defined criteria in refractory or angle-closure populations, while stent trials in milder disease report low single-digit percentages.
- **Limited long-term data for newer devices**: Many OMNI/GATT/ABiC studies are retrospective or short-to-medium follow-up (1–3 years); only Hydrus (HORIZON) and iStent have long-term (5-year) RCT data.
- **Learning curve effects**: Some GATT series explicitly report outcomes during a "learning curve" period, indicating surgeon experience affects results.
