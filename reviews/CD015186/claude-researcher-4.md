## Summary

The primary-study literature on SC-MIGS spans several device/technique subtypes, each targeting Schlemm's canal or the trabecular meshwork/inner wall via different mechanisms (stenting, viscodilation, or incision/excision):

- **Trabecular micro-bypass stents (iStent, iStent inject):** Multiple industry-sponsored RCTs (pivotal FDA trials) combined with cataract surgery show significant IOP and medication reduction versus cataract surgery alone, with an excellent short-term safety profile; effectiveness may depend on baseline IOP.
- **Schlemm's canal microstent (Hydrus):** The HORIZON RCT is the largest and longest MIGS RCT (5-year follow-up), showing sustained IOP/medication reduction, lower rates of secondary surgery, and — notably — reduced visual field progression compared with cataract surgery alone.
- **GATT (suture-based circumferential trabeculotomy):** Mostly retrospective/prospective case series (rarely RCTs) showing efficacy across POAG, juvenile OAG, pseudoexfoliative glaucoma, and eyes with prior failed filtering surgery, generally with a favorable safety profile but variable success definitions across studies.
- **OMNI system (canaloplasty + trabeculotomy):** Retrospective/prospective single-arm multicenter studies (ROMEO) with follow-up to 2–3 years.
- **Trabectome (ab interno trabeculectomy):** Case series and a comparative cohort study against trabeculectomy, showing lower success rates but a better safety profile than trabeculectomy.
- **Kahook Dual Blade goniotomy:** Numerous prospective/retrospective case series, including some comparative (vs. microhook trabeculotomy) and population-specific (Black/Afro-Latinx, Japanese, high myopia) cohorts.
- **Ab-interno canaloplasty (iTrack/ABiC):** Prospective registry data and retrospective case series with follow-up to 3–6 years, generally as standalone or combined with cataract surgery.

A clear gap across the literature: **head-to-head RCTs between different SC-MIGS techniques (not just vs. cataract surgery alone) are rare** — most trials compare a single device against cataract-surgery-alone or medical therapy, and most technique comparisons (e.g., GATT vs. KDB, OMNI vs. Hydrus) are retrospective or non-randomized. One RCT protocol (BANG vs. GATT) was identified as an example of trial designs beginning to address this gap.
