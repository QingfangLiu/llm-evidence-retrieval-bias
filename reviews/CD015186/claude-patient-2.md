Here's an overview of the evidence base for SC-MIGS (canal-based procedures: iStent/iStent inject, Hydrus Microstent, Trabectome, GATT, ab interno canaloplasty/ABiC, OMNI), drawn from individual primary studies (RCTs, prospective cohorts, and large retrospective/registry series) rather than reviews or guidelines.

## Benefits

**IOP and medication reduction (device vs. cataract surgery alone).** In the iStent inject pivotal RCT, eyes randomized to stent implantation plus cataract surgery had significantly greater IOP reduction without medication versus cataract surgery alone, with a favorable two-year safety profile.Clinically and statistically greater reductions in IOP without medication were achieved after iStent inject implantation with cataract surgery versus cataract surgery alone, with excellent safety through 2 years. Similarly, the HORIZON trial (Hydrus Microstent) found that at 24 months, a Schlemm canal microstent combined with phacoemulsification produced superior reduction in diurnal IOP and medication use compared with phacoemulsification alone in eyes with mild-to-moderate primary open-angle glaucoma. Longer-term HORIZON follow-up showed durability: at 3 years, 73% of microstent-group eyes were medication-free compared with 48% of cataract-surgery-only eyes, and the microstent group had a lower cumulative probability of needing incisional glaucoma surgery (0.6% vs. 3.9%). At 5 years, 59% of Hydrus Microstent patients remained medication-free.

**Stent vs. medication as first-line treatment.** A 5-year RCT of newly diagnosed, treatment-naive POAG compared two trabecular micro-bypass stents against topical prostaglandin, evaluating IOP control without added medication or surgery as the endpoint.

**Head-to-head device comparisons.** The COMPARE study, a randomized trial of standalone Hydrus vs. two iStent devices, found that the Hydrus group had a greater rate of complete surgical success and reduced medication use, with more Hydrus subjects medication-free at 12 months, while the two devices had similar safety profiles.

**Non-implant canal-based approaches (GATT, ABiC, Trabectome, OMNI).** These circumferential or ablative approaches to Schlemm's canal also show consistent IOP and medication reductions across many prospective and retrospective series, including in more advanced and secondary glaucomas: a large retrospective GATT series reported IOP decreasing from a mean of 20.65 mmHg to 14.1 mmHg and medications decreasing from 3.47 to 1.4, with 1-year success ranging from 76–96% depending on glaucoma type and whether combined with cataract surgery. A 4-year GATT case series similarly tracked long-term IOP, medication, and visual acuity outcomes. A real-world IRIS Registry analysis of standalone OMNI (canaloplasty + trabeculotomy) found clinically and statistically significant IOP reductions sustained through 3 years across mild, moderate, and severe glaucoma, in both phakic and pseudophakic eyes. A prospective multicenter registry of ab interno canaloplasty found significant IOP and medication reduction with a favorable safety profile whether performed alone or with cataract surgery, across diverse glaucoma populations.

## Harms / safety findings

Across these studies, reported adverse events are generally mild and less severe than with traditional filtering surgery (trabeculectomy/tube shunts), but they are not zero:
- The Trabectome literature (individual case series) reports transient postoperative IOP spikes, hyphema, and a minority of treatment failures requiring further surgery (e.g., 14% of Trabectome-alone patients were classified as failures in one series).
- HORIZON reported no procedure- or device-related serious adverse events resulting in vision loss in either group, and Alcon's summary of the same trial notes a 55% relative reduction in incisional secondary surgical intervention for Hydrus patients versus cataract surgery alone, with comparable serious-adverse-event rates.
- COMPARE reported two eyes in the Hydrus group and one in the iStent group with best-corrected visual acuity loss of two or more lines, and secondary glaucoma surgery in two 2-iStent eyes versus none in the Hydrus group.
- GATT and ABiC series report hyphema as the most common early complication, generally self-limited, along with transient IOP spikes; failure/reoperation rates vary by glaucoma severity and etiology (e.g., roughly a quarter of eyes classified as failures in some GATT cohorts).
- Outcomes are worse in some higher-risk subgroups — e.g., standalone ab interno trabeculotomy had lower 36-month success in eyes with high myopia (45%) than in non-myopic eyes (65%).

Overall, the individual-study evidence supports SC-MIGS as producing moderate, durable IOP and medication reduction with a low rate of vision-threatening complications, but device/technique choice affects the magnitude of benefit (e.g., Hydrus outperforming iStent in some head-to-head data), and failure/reoperation still occurs in a meaningful minority of eyes, particularly in more severe or secondary glaucoma.
