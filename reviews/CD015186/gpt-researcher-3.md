## Scope

I conducted a targeted evidence search updated **July 25, 2026**. I included human primary studies of open-angle glaucoma evaluating ab-interno procedures that incise, excise, open, or viscodilate the trabecular meshwork/Schlemm’s canal: GATT or suture trabeculotomy, Kahook Dual Blade or microhook goniotomy, Trabectome/ab-interno trabeculectomy, ab-interno canaloplasty, and excimer laser trabeculotomy.

I treated trabecular microstents such as iStent as comparators rather than SC-MIGS. Reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, opinions, editorials, and comments were excluded. The synthesis below emphasizes randomized comparative studies; selected comparative and prospective observational studies are listed separately.

## Main findings

### SC-MIGS added to cataract surgery versus cataract surgery alone

The evidence is **inconsistent across procedures and studies**.

* In an 18-eye pilot RCT, adding 360° suture trabeculotomy produced substantially higher medication-free success at 24 months than phacoemulsification alone: 77.8% versus 11.1% under the less stringent definition and 46.7% versus 0% under the stricter definition. The study was much too small to estimate uncommon harms reliably. ([PubMed][1])
* Conversely, a randomized KDB study involving 42 eyes from 33 participants found no difference at 12 months in mean IOP—16.0 versus 15.0 mmHg—or medication use between phaco-KDB and phacoemulsification alone. Randomizing two eyes from some participants also creates a potential unit-of-analysis concern. ([PubMed][2])
* The larger Maheshwari trial randomized 114 participants. At 24 months, phaco-microhook trabeculotomy produced lower mean IOP than phaco alone—15.2 versus 18.2 mmHg—and greater percentage IOP reduction—42.6% versus 28.1%. Success under the least stringent criterion was 83.3% versus 57.8%. However, baseline medication use was imbalanced between groups. ([PubMed][3])
* In the 120-participant Swedish trial, phaco-KDB had greater composite success than phaco alone—87.5% versus 52.5%—and a greater reduction in medication use, but the mean IOP change was similar: −3.5 versus −2.8 mmHg. The population included ocular hypertension, POAG, and pseudoexfoliative glaucoma. ([ScienceDirect][4])

Thus, the incremental benefit of SC-MIGS over phacoemulsification appears more consistent for **reducing medication burden or achieving composite success** than for producing a clearly greater mean IOP reduction in every trial.

### SC-MIGS versus trabeculectomy

Two randomized trials were extremely small.

* In a 32-person RCT, mitomycin-C trabeculectomy achieved lower IOP during the first three months, but IOP did not differ significantly from ab-interno goniotrabeculotomy between 6 and 24 months. More postoperative complications occurred after trabeculectomy. ([PubMed][5])
* A phaco-Trabectome versus phaco-trabeculectomy trial stopped early after enrolling only 19 participants. IOP and medication use did not differ significantly at 6 or 12 months, but severe complications occurred only in the trabeculectomy group. One Trabectome participant required another glaucoma procedure. ([PubMed][6])
* A much larger but nonrandomized retrospective comparison found a markedly lower 24-month IOP with trabeculectomy than with Trabectome—10.2 versus 15.9 mmHg—and higher surgical success—76.1% versus 22.4%. Additional glaucoma procedures were required in 43.5% after Trabectome versus 10.8% after trabeculectomy. Except for hyphema, complications were more frequent after trabeculectomy. Confounding by indication remains an important limitation. ([PubMed][7])

Taken together, conventional trabeculectomy is more likely to reach a **very low target IOP** and appears more durable, while SC-MIGS avoids bleb-related surgery and generally has fewer severe postoperative complications. The randomized evidence is insufficient to quantify that trade-off precisely.

### Comparisons among SC-MIGS procedures

The clearest efficacy–harm trade-off was found between GATT and ABiC.

* In a 77-person RCT, 12-month mean IOP was lower after GATT than after ABiC—16.0 versus 19.0 mmHg. Medication-free proportions were 77.8% and 57.2%, although that difference did not meet conventional statistical significance. Hyphema occurred in 87% after GATT versus 47% after ABiC, and imaging-detected supraciliary effusion occurred in 92% versus 71%. ([PubMed][8])
* Randomizing 99 eyes to 360°, superior 180°, or inferior 180° suture trabeculotomy found no material difference in IOP or medication reduction. Level-forming hyphema was more frequent with the 360° incision, indicating no demonstrated efficacy gain from the larger incision but more bleeding. At five years, outcomes remained similar among groups, although only 56 of 99 participants completed follow-up. ([PubMed][9])
* In 45 pseudophakic POAG eyes, GATT produced much higher complete and qualified success than bent-needle goniotomy—60.1% versus 4.5% and 64.7% versus 22.7%, respectively. Mean IOP after medication washout did not differ significantly, and transient hyphema occurred in 56.5% versus 40.9%. ([PubMed][10])
* KDB created a larger persistent trabecular opening than microhook trabeculotomy in a 58-eye RCT, but there was no corresponding advantage in IOP, medications, endothelial-cell density, or complications. The primary endpoint was anatomical rather than clinical. ([Nature][11])
* Three randomized canaloplasty variants—ab externo, mini-ab externo, and ABiC—produced broadly similar IOP and medication reductions at one and three years, with no statistically significant differences between procedures. The trial included only 16 participants per group. ([PubMed][12])

### SC-MIGS versus other less-invasive treatment

* Compared with SLT in a 30-eye RCT, excimer laser trabeculotomy reduced mean IOP by 29.6% versus 21% at 24 months. However, differences in complete and qualified success were not statistically clear. ([PubMed][13])
* In a 164-participant trial, phaco-KDB and phaco-iStent produced similar mean IOP and medication reductions at 12 months. The composite of at least 20% IOP reduction or reduction of at least one medication favored KDB—93.7% versus 83.3%—with broadly similar safety. ([Mayo Clinic][14])

## Harms

The characteristic adverse event is **blood reflux or transient hyphema**.

* In the small phaco-suture-trabeculotomy trial, intraoperative reflux bleeding occurred in all combined-procedure eyes and postoperative hyphema in approximately 22%; it resolved within one week. ([PubMed][1])
* In the incision-extent RCT, level-forming hyphema occurred in approximately 26% of 360° cases versus 9% and 6% of the two 180° groups; one 360° participant required an anterior-chamber washout. ([PubMed][9])
* GATT generated more hyphema and supraciliary effusion than ABiC, although most events were transient. ([PubMed][8])
* In the Maheshwari trial, four participants developed hyphema and one required anterior-chamber washout. No new complications or interventions were reported between 12 and 24 months. ([PubMed][3])
* In the Swedish trial, transient hyphema occurred in 15% after KDB. Early IOP spikes occurred in 15% after KDB, 10% after iStent, and 37.5% after phacoemulsification alone. ([ScienceDirect][4])

Early IOP spikes also occur after SC-MIGS, but their reported frequency varies considerably with the procedure and definition. Serious persistent hypotony, sight-threatening infection, and severe bleb-related complications were not prominent in these studies, but most trials were too small to exclude uncommon serious harms.

## Overall interpretation

The primary studies suggest that SC-MIGS can reduce IOP and medication burden in people with open-angle glaucoma, particularly when baseline IOP is elevated. However:

* Added benefit over cataract surgery alone varies substantially by procedure and trial.
* Broader trabeculotomy, especially GATT, may provide greater IOP lowering but causes more transient bleeding and imaging-detected ciliochoroidal or supraciliary changes.
* Extending a trabeculotomy from 180° to 360° has not shown better pressure control in randomized evidence but increases hyphema.
* Compared with trabeculectomy, SC-MIGS generally has a less severe complication profile but is less reliable when a very low target IOP is required.
* Evidence for visual-field preservation, health-related quality of life, and long-term avoidance of blindness is very limited. Most studies focused on IOP, medication use, and short-term surgical complications.
* Confidence is limited by small samples, single-center designs, heterogeneous glaucoma diagnoses, frequent concomitant cataract surgery, inconsistent medication washout, differing success definitions, and incomplete long-term follow-up.
