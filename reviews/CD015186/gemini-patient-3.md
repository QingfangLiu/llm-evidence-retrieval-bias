Minimally invasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS) acting on Schlemm's canal (SC-MIGS) aims to enhance the eye's natural drainage system by targeting the trabecular meshwork. This allows aqueous humor to flow more easily into Schlemm's canal, lowering intraocular pressure (IOP) with a higher safety profile than traditional, invasive surgeries like a trabeculectomy.

Based on the primary clinical studies you requested, here is a breakdown of the benefits and harms of SC-MIGS procedures.

### Benefits of SC-MIGS

SC-MIGS procedures—which include both implantable devices and stent-free surgeries—are highly effective at lowering IOP and reducing the burden of daily glaucoma medications.

* **Sustained IOP Reduction:** Implantation of devices like the Hydrus Microstent (which dilates and scaffolds Schlemm's canal) during cataract surgery results in a clinically significant reduction in unmedicated IOP (≥20% reduction) when compared to undergoing cataract surgery alone (Samuelson et al., 2019).
* **Reduced Medication Dependency:** A major benefit is decreasing the need for daily hypotensive eye drops, which are often difficult to tolerate. Trabecular micro-bypass stents, such as the iStent inject, have been shown to be at least as effective as using two concurrent topical glaucoma medications to manage IOP (Giamporcaro et al., 2014).
* **Real-World Efficacy Across Devices:** Direct comparisons of devices confirm their efficacy. While some real-world data suggests the Hydrus Microstent may offer a statistically superior IOP reduction and medication reduction compared to the iStent inject W, both successfully achieve target surgical outcomes for patients with mild to moderate primary open-angle glaucoma (Weich et al., 2025).
* **Success of Stent-Free Approaches:** Procedures that do not leave a device behind, such as Gonioscopy-Assisted Transluminal Trabeculotomy (GATT), physically unroof Schlemm's canal entirely to improve drainage. Primary outcomes confirm GATT is safe and effectively reduces both IOP and medication burden in primary open-angle glaucoma (Aktas et al., 2022).

### Harms and Complications

While MIGS boasts a much stronger safety profile and faster recovery time than traditional incisional glaucoma surgery, there are still risks and potential adverse events:

* **Hyphema:** Bleeding into the anterior chamber (hyphema) is the most common early postoperative complication. This is especially prevalent in procedures that directly cut or unroof the trabecular meshwork, such as GATT, though it typically resolves spontaneously in the weeks following surgery without needing further intervention (Aktas et al., 2022).
* **Device-Specific Issues:** For implantable stents, there can be rare cases of partial or complete device obstruction over time, limiting their long-term efficacy. However, no serious device-related complications or significant safety differences compared to standard cataract surgery are generally observed (Samuelson et al., 2019; Weich et al., 2025).
* **Need for Secondary Surgery:** A fraction of patients will not achieve the desired target IOP or may experience disease progression, requiring additional incisional glaucoma surgeries down the line. That said, patients receiving an SC-MIGS implant like Hydrus are much less likely to need subsequent IOP-lowering surgery than those undergoing cataract surgery alone (Samuelson et al., 2019).
