## Scope

I treated **SC-MIGS** as an ab-interno procedure that increases conventional outflow by bypassing, incising, excising, dilating, or scaffolding the trabecular meshwork–Schlemm’s canal pathway. This includes trabecular bypass stents, canal scaffolds, goniotomy/trabeculotomy, ab-interno canaloplasty and excimer-laser trabeculotomy.

I prioritized randomized comparative studies in adults with primary or secondary open-angle glaucoma. Reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, opinions and editorials were excluded. The search was updated through **July 25, 2026**, but this is a targeted evidence map rather than a formally registered exhaustive review.

## Overall interpretation

### Benefits

**1. When combined with cataract surgery, the clearest incremental benefit is fewer glaucoma medications.**

The strongest long-term evidence comes from the 556-eye HORIZON trial. At five years, adding Hydrus to cataract surgery increased medication-free status from 46% to 66%, increased the proportion with IOP ≤18 mmHg without medication from 33.8% to 49.5%, and reduced subsequent incisional glaucoma surgery from 6.2% to 2.4%. The average medicated IOP difference between groups was comparatively small. ([PubMed][1])

The pivotal iStent inject trial similarly found more eyes with at least a 20% unmedicated diurnal IOP reduction at two years—75.8% versus 61.9% after cataract surgery alone—and a mean incremental unmedicated IOP reduction of approximately 1.6 mmHg. Safety was similar between groups. ([PubMed][2])

However, a more pragmatic 104-eye randomized trial found **0.6 fewer medications per eye** with iStent inject at two years, but no sustained between-group IOP difference and no difference in ocular-surface or glaucoma-related quality-of-life measures. ([ScienceDirect][3])

Earlier first-generation iStent trials also favored combined surgery for medication-free IOP control, although the medication difference diminished by two years in the pivotal trial. ([PubMed][4])

**2. Incisional or excisional trabecular procedures have much less consistent comparative results.**

A small 18-eye pilot trial found markedly higher two-year success with 360° suture trabeculotomy plus phacoemulsification than with phacoemulsification alone. ([PubMed][5])

A 114-eye trial of phacoemulsification plus Tanito microhook trabeculotomy reported a large 12-month IOP difference—12.5 versus 20.0 mmHg—and less medication use than phacoemulsification alone. Baseline medication use was imbalanced, however, and the unusually large treatment effect warrants replication. ([ScienceDirect][6])

In contrast, a 42-eye randomized trial found that adding Kahook Dual Blade goniotomy to cataract surgery did **not** significantly improve IOP or medication use at 12 months in well-controlled mild-to-moderate disease. ([PubMed][7])

Thus, differences in baseline IOP, medication washout, glaucoma severity, incision extent and outcome definitions probably explain part of the heterogeneity.

**3. For standalone SC-MIGS, head-to-head evidence suggests that the particular procedure matters.**

In the 152-eye COMPARE trial, standalone Hydrus produced greater complete surgical success, 0.6 fewer medications and 22.6 percentage points more medication-free eyes than two first-generation iStents at 12 months, with similar overall safety. ([PubMed][8])

In the newer INTEGRITY trial, iStent infinite and Hydrus produced similar six-month mean diurnal IOP reductions when complications were ignored. A complication-inclusive endpoint favored iStent infinite, which had fewer placement problems and peripheral anterior synechiae. These are interim six-month results from a planned 24-month trial and should not yet be regarded as durable comparative evidence. ([Springer][9])

GATT produced a lower mean IOP than ab-interno canaloplasty at 12 months—16.0 versus 19.0 mmHg—but medication-free and complete-success differences did not reach statistical significance in the 77-eye trial. ([PubMed][10])

**4. More extensive trabeculotomy does not necessarily produce better long-term control.**

In a 99-eye three-arm trial, 360°, upper-180° and lower-180° suture trabeculotomy produced similar IOP and medication reductions. At five years, the mean IOP across groups was 13.5 mmHg, but only 56.5% completed follow-up, and no meaningful advantage of 360° treatment was identified. ([PubMed][11])

**5. Comparisons with trabeculectomy are too small to establish equivalence.**

A 32-eye trial of ab-interno goniotrabeculotomy versus mitomycin-C trabeculectomy found lower IOP with trabeculectomy during the first three months, but similar IOP from six through 24 months. ([ScienceDirect][12])

A 19-person phaco-Trabectome versus phaco-trabeculectomy trial was stopped early because of poor recruitment. It found similar one-year IOP and medication use, but was far too underpowered to establish equivalent efficacy. ([PubMed][13])

**6. Evidence for excimer-laser trabeculotomy is limited to a very small trial.**

In 30 eyes, ELT and 180° SLT both lowered IOP over two years. ELT produced a numerically larger reduction, but complete- and qualified-success differences were not statistically significant. ([PubMed][14])

## Harms

The characteristic SC-MIGS adverse events are generally **angle-related and early postoperative**, rather than bleb-related:

* **Hyphema or blood reflux:** particularly common after circumferential or extensive trabeculotomy. In the GATT-versus-ABiC trial, hyphema was recorded in 87% versus 47%, respectively. In the microhook-plus-phaco trial, four of 57 treated eyes developed hyphema and one required anterior-chamber washout. ([PubMed][10])
* **Transient IOP elevation:** reported after goniotomy, trabeculotomy and ELT. In the ELT trial, transient spikes occurred in three of 15 ELT eyes and two of 15 SLT eyes. ([Springer][15])
* **Peripheral anterior synechiae, obstruction or malposition:** more relevant to implanted devices. In INTEGRITY, improper placement occurred in 1.1% of iStent infinite and 7.9% of Hydrus eyes; surgical complications overall occurred in 3.3% versus 16.9%. ([Springer][9])
* **Reoperation:** uncommon but not absent. In COMPARE, two iStent eyes and no Hydrus eyes underwent additional glaucoma surgery by 12 months. In the GATT-versus-ABiC trial, additional surgery was required in one and three eyes, respectively. ([PubMed][8])
* **Corneal endothelial effects:** the five-year HORIZON report found no clinically or statistically significant difference in endothelial-cell loss between Hydrus-plus-cataract surgery and cataract surgery alone. ([PubMed][1])
* **Serious hypotony or bleb-related events:** uncommon with SC-MIGS itself. In the small trials against trabeculectomy, serious complications occurred predominantly or exclusively in the trabeculectomy groups. ([ScienceDirect][12])

Rare or delayed harms remain uncertain because even the largest trials are not powered for very uncommon events, and many studies have only one or two years of follow-up.

## Evidence gaps

The evidence applies most directly to **mild-to-moderate OAG undergoing cataract surgery**. There is less dependable randomized evidence for severe glaucoma, very low target pressures, pseudophakic standalone surgery or previously operated eyes.

Most trials emphasize IOP, medication use and composite “success” outcomes. Evidence that SC-MIGS slows visual-field loss, prevents visual impairment, or improves quality of life remains limited. The pragmatic iStent trial found no quality-of-life difference at two years, while the five-year suture-trabeculotomy report included visual-field outcomes but had substantial attrition. ([ScienceDirect][3])

Masking is frequently incomplete, outcome definitions vary substantially, and many implant trials involved manufacturer employees or sponsorship. Results based on medication washout may also differ from routine-care medicated IOP effects.
