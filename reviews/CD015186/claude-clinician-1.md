## Summary of findings

SC-MIGS procedures work by directly accessing Schlemm's canal — via trabecular micro-bypass stents (iStent, iStent inject), a canal-scaffolding device (Hydrus Microstent), or incisional/viscodilation techniques that open or dilate the canal (Trabectome, Kahook Dual Blade goniotomy, GATT, OMNI/ab interno canaloplasty). Below is what the primary literature shows, organized by device class.

**Trabecular micro-bypass stents (iStent, iStent inject).** The original iStent pivotal RCT (240 eyes) and the iStent inject pivotal RCT randomized 505 eyes 3:1 to iStent inject implantation with cataract surgery versus cataract surgery alone in mild-to-moderate POAG, and found clinically and statistically greater unmedicated IOP reduction with the stent through 2 years, with excellent safety. A 5-year follow-up of this pivotal RCT specifically assessed corneal endothelial cell density and loss, addressing a key safety concern for angle-based devices. A separate multicenter RCT tested iStent inject as a standalone (non-cataract) procedure against topical medication.

**Canal-scaffold (Hydrus Microstent).** The 24-month multicenter HORIZON RCT randomized subjects 2:1 to a Hydrus Microstent or no stent after phacoemulsification, and found superior IOP and medication reduction with the microstent, with the later COMPARE trial showing higher surgical success and greater medication reduction versus first-generation iStent. Long-term HORIZON follow-up data extend to 5 years.

**Excisional goniotomy (Kahook Dual Blade).** Mostly single-arm prospective case series (e.g., a 7-center North American series and a 2-year series across OAG/ACG subtypes), but there is a head-to-head RCT: a prospective, randomized, active-controlled trial randomized 164 eyes to KDB-phaco or iStent-phaco, with the primary outcome being ≥20% IOP reduction or ≥1 medication reduction at 12 months, finding KDB may offer a higher probability of patient-specific success.

**Ab interno trabeculotomy (Trabectome).** Primarily cohort studies (including a Mayo Clinic single-surgeon case series and international multicenter comparisons), plus one notable RCT comparing it against traditional trabeculectomy: IOP fell from 28.1 to 15.9 mmHg (43.5% reduction) in the ab interno trabeculectomy group versus 26.3 to 10.2 mmHg (61.3% reduction) with trabeculectomy, with success rates of 22.4% vs 76.1% at 2 years, though the ab interno group had fewer complications — useful for benefit/harm trade-off framing even though it isn't SC-MIGS-vs-SC-MIGS.

**GATT (360° trabeculotomy).** Mostly retrospective/prospective case series, but an RCT now exists comparing two Schlemm's-canal-based needle/trabeculotomy techniques: a randomized trial of 45 eyes comparing BANG versus GATT found GATT eyes achieved higher success rates, lower mean IOPs, and required less medication at 11 months. Four-year outcome data are also available.

**Circumferential viscodilation/trabeculotomy (OMNI, iTrack/ABiC).** Evidence is largely single-arm/retrospective (ROMEO study series), but randomized data exist: a randomized controlled trial compared STREAMLINE canaloplasty to trabecular micro-bypass (iStent inject), and device-vs-device RCTs (iTrack Advance vs OMNI) are ongoing.
