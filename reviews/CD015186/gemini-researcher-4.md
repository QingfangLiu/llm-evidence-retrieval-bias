As an evidence-synthesis researcher, you are looking for high-quality, primary clinical trial data (excluding secondary literature like systematic reviews) to isolate the exact efficacy and safety profiles of Schlemm's canal minimally invasive glaucoma surgery (SC-MIGS).

SC-MIGS encompasses both implantable microstents (e.g., Hydrus Microstent, iStent inject) and tissue-modifying devices (e.g., OMNI Surgical System) designed to bypass or dilate the trabecular meshwork to enhance aqueous outflow directly into Schlemm's canal.

Here is a synthesis of the benefits and harms of SC-MIGS for primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG) based exclusively on landmark primary studies, which are listed at the end of this response.

## Benefits: Efficacy and Medication Burden

**Intraocular Pressure (IOP) Reduction**
Primary randomized controlled trials consistently demonstrate that SC-MIGS provides superior IOP reduction when combined with cataract surgery compared to cataract surgery alone. In the HORIZON trial, 77.3% of eyes that received a Hydrus Microstent achieved a $\ge$ 20% reduction in unmedicated diurnal IOP at 24 months, compared to just 57.8% of the control group (Samuelson et al., 2019a). The iStent inject pivotal trial mirrored this magnitude of benefit, with 75.8% of the micro-bypass group achieving the $\ge$ 20% reduction threshold versus 61.9% of the control eyes, accompanied by a mean reduction of 7.0 mmHg (Samuelson et al., 2019b).

Beyond microstents, primary evidence for tissue-modifying SC-MIGS shows parallel benefits. The ROMEO study, which evaluated ab interno canaloplasty and trabeculotomy using the OMNI Surgical System, confirmed effective and sustained IOP control combined with cataract surgery through 12 months postoperative (Hirsch et al., 2021).

**Medication Burden Reduction**
Reducing patient reliance on topical hypotensive drops is a core benefit of SC-MIGS. The HORIZON trial showed a dramatic reduction in the mean number of medications, dropping from 1.7 at baseline to just 0.3 at 24 months in the Hydrus group (Samuelson et al., 2019a). Similarly, 84% of responding treatment eyes in the iStent inject trial were completely medication-free at 23 months (Samuelson et al., 2019b).

When used as a standalone procedure (without concurrent cataract surgery), differences between SC-MIGS devices emerge. The COMPARE trial randomized patients to receive either a single Hydrus Microstent or two iStent devices. At 12 months, the Hydrus cohort demonstrated a significantly higher rate of complete surgical success (defined as freedom from repeat surgery, IOP $\le$ 18 mmHg, and no medications) and a higher proportion of medication-free patients compared to the iStent group (Ahmed et al., 2020).

## Harms: Safety Profile and Adverse Events

The defining characteristic of SC-MIGS is its high safety profile relative to traditional filtering surgeries (like trabeculectomy).

**Complications and Secondary Interventions**
Both the HORIZON trial and the iStent inject pivotal trial reported that the overall safety profile of the device implantation was highly favorable and statistically similar to the cataract-surgery-only control groups (Samuelson et al., 2019a; Samuelson et al., 2019b). No serious ocular adverse events were directly attributed to the microstents in the HORIZON study.

In the standalone COMPARE trial, both devices exhibited similar, excellent safety profiles. However, secondary glaucoma surgery was required in 3.9% of the 2-iStent group, compared to 0% in the Hydrus group. Minor visual acuity loss (defined as a loss of $\ge$ 2 lines) was exceedingly rare but did occur in two Hydrus eyes and one iStent eye (Ahmed et al., 2020).
