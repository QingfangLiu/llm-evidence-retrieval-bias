## Scope

I treated **SC-MIGS** as an ab interno procedure that restores conventional aqueous outflow by bypassing or removing trabecular meshwork, stenting/dilating Schlemm’s canal, or performing canaloplasty/trabeculotomy. This includes iStent-family devices, Hydrus, Kahook Dual Blade (KDB), GATT, ab interno canaloplasty and OMNI. I excluded suprachoroidal and subconjunctival MIGS.

This is a targeted primary-study evidence map through **July 25, 2026**, emphasizing randomized comparative studies and adding important prospective cohorts where controlled evidence is unavailable. Reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, opinions and editorials were excluded.

## Clinical bottom line

For people with predominantly **mild-to-moderate open-angle glaucoma undergoing cataract surgery**, the best-supported benefit of SC-MIGS is **reducing glaucoma-medication burden**. The additional IOP reduction over phacoemulsification alone is generally modest—often approximately 1–2.5 mmHg in washed-out comparisons—although responder outcomes may differ more substantially. Hydrus has the strongest randomized long-term evidence, including lower medication use and fewer subsequent incisional glaucoma operations through five years. iStent-family devices also reduce medication use, but some independent trials found no persistent between-group IOP difference. ([PubMed][1])

Evidence for **KDB, GATT, canaloplasty and OMNI** shows potentially meaningful pre–post reductions in IOP and medications, particularly when baseline IOP is elevated. However, the incremental benefit beyond cataract surgery is less certain because trials are smaller and many studies are uncontrolled or retrospective. ([PubMed][2])

The usual harms are transient hyphema, short-term IOP elevation and inflammation. Implant-specific problems include malposition, obstruction and peripheral anterior synechiae. Serious vision-threatening complications were uncommon in the trials, but even the pivotal studies were not large enough to reliably quantify very rare events. ([AAO Journal][3])

## Benefits by procedure

### Trabecular micro-bypass: iStent and iStent inject

In the original 240-eye randomized iStent trial, cataract surgery plus one iStent produced unmedicated IOP ≤21 mmHg at one year in 72% versus 50% after cataract surgery alone; 66% versus 48% achieved at least a 20% unmedicated IOP reduction. The two-year report continued to show a greater proportion reaching unmedicated IOP ≤21 mmHg, although the difference was smaller. ([PubMed][4])

In the 505-eye pivotal iStent inject trial, 75.8% versus 61.9% achieved at least a 20% reduction in unmedicated diurnal IOP at two years. Mean reduction was 7.0 versus 5.4 mmHg, an incremental effect of about 1.6 mmHg. Medication-free IOP ≤18 mmHg occurred in 63.2% versus 50.0%. ([PubMed][1])

Smaller, less industry-centered randomized studies give a more conservative picture. In a 104-eye Australian trial, iStent inject reduced medication use by an adjusted 0.6 medication per eye at two years and increased medication freedom from 36% to 57%, but produced **no significant IOP or patient-reported quality-of-life advantage** beyond four weeks. The 2026 Swedish three-arm trial similarly found that iStent inject W and KDB reduced medications more than cataract surgery alone, while mean IOP reduction was similar among all three groups. ([PubMed][5])

As standalone initial treatment, two first-generation iStents were compared with travoprost in 101 treatment-naïve eyes. At five years, mean IOP was almost identical—16.5 versus 16.3 mmHg—but fewer stent-treated eyes required add-on medication, and reported treatment success was 77% versus 53%. This unusual treatment strategy has less direct applicability to current US practice than combined cataract procedures. ([PubMed][6])

### Hydrus Schlemm canal scaffold

The 100-eye HYDRUS II randomized trial found that adding Hydrus to cataract surgery increased the two-year ≥20% washed-out IOP response from 46% to 80%, lowered washed-out IOP from 19.2 to 16.9 mmHg, and increased medication freedom from 38% to 73%. ([PubMed][7])

The larger 556-eye HORIZON trial found an incremental 2.3-mmHg washed-out IOP reduction at two years and approximately 0.4 fewer medications with Hydrus. At five years, 66% versus 46% were medication-free, 49.5% versus 33.8% had medication-free IOP ≤18 mmHg, and cumulative incisional glaucoma surgery occurred in 2.4% versus 6.2%. ([ScienceDirect][8])

A post hoc HORIZON analysis found slower visual-field mean-deviation loss with Hydrus plus cataract surgery than with cataract surgery alone: −0.26 versus −0.49 dB/year. This is potentially important because most MIGS studies measure surrogate outcomes, but it remains a post hoc finding from one implant trial rather than definitive proof of preserved vision or quality of life. ([PubMed][9])

### Head-to-head implant comparisons

In the 152-eye standalone COMPARE trial, Hydrus produced greater complete surgical success, approximately 0.6 fewer medications and 22.6 percentage points more medication-free eyes than two first-generation iStents at 12 months. Secondary glaucoma surgery occurred in no Hydrus eyes and two iStent eyes; visual and overall safety outcomes were similar. ([PubMed][10])

The newer INTEGRITY trial compared three iStent infinite stents with Hydrus as standalone procedures. At six months, the unadjusted ≥20% IOP response was similar—82.7% versus 78.9%. A prespecified composite that counted medication use and surgical complications as failure favored iStent infinite, primarily because surgical complications were reported in 3.3% versus 16.9%. This is an interim six-month result from an industry-associated trial; the planned longer follow-up will be more informative. ([PubMed][11])

### KDB excisional goniotomy

The main cataract-controlled KDB RCT was small—42 eyes with relatively well-controlled baseline IOP. At 12 months, adding KDB did not significantly improve either IOP or medication use compared with cataract surgery alone. ([PubMed][2])

The 2026 Swedish trial found greater medication reduction and composite success with KDB or iStent than with cataract surgery alone, but again found no additional mean IOP reduction. Observational KDB series involving higher baseline IOPs generally report larger pre–post reductions, but those estimates cannot separate treatment effect from cataract surgery, medication changes, regression to the mean or selection bias. ([ScienceDirect][12])

### GATT and ab interno canaloplasty

In a 77-eye randomized comparison, GATT produced lower mean IOP than ab interno canaloplasty at 12 months—16.0 versus 19.0 mmHg. Medication freedom was numerically higher with GATT, 77.8% versus 57.2%, but medication use and complete-success differences were not statistically significant. Additional glaucoma surgery was required in one GATT and three canaloplasty eyes. ([PubMed][13])

This suggests that opening the trabecular meshwork circumferentially may produce greater pressure reduction than canal dilation alone, but at the cost of more postoperative blood and fluid-related findings.

### OMNI canaloplasty plus trabeculotomy

GEMINI was a prospective but uncontrolled combined-cataract cohort. Among 149 operated eyes, unmedicated diurnal IOP fell from 23.8 to 15.6 mmHg at 12 months, medications fell from 1.8 to 0.4 and 80% were medication-free. Among 66 participants entering its extension, similar outcomes persisted through 36 months. Because there was no phacoemulsification-only control, these results do not establish how much benefit was attributable specifically to OMNI. ([PMC][14])

ROMEO, a retrospective multicenter cohort, found larger IOP reductions among eyes with baseline IOP above 18 mmHg and mainly medication reduction among eyes starting at 18 mmHg or below. This baseline-dependent pattern is important when counseling a person whose medicated IOP is already in the low-to-mid teens. ([PubMed][15])

## Harms

**Bleeding and early pressure elevation:** Transient hyphema is particularly common after tissue-incisional procedures. In the ABiC-versus-GATT trial, hyphema occurred in 87% with GATT and 47% with canaloplasty, while supraciliary effusion was reported in 92% and 71%, respectively. In a large retrospective KDB series, day-one hyphema occurred in 17.3% and a >10-mmHg IOP spike in 10.2%. GEMINI reported hyphema in 6% and transient IOP elevation in 2%. ([PubMed][13])

**Implant position, obstruction and synechiae:** At five years in HORIZON, PAS was present in 14.6% of Hydrus eyes versus 3.7% of controls; device obstruction was reported in 8.4%, malposition in 1.4%, and no device removals occurred. PAS did not appear to reduce IOP efficacy in that analysis. ([AAO Journal][3])

**Corneal endothelium:** Five-year iStent inject follow-up found similar mean endothelial-cell loss to cataract surgery alone—14.3% versus 14.8%. HORIZON’s longitudinal analysis also found no significant difference in endothelial-cell-loss rate from months 3–60. However, a separate post hoc threshold analysis found >30% cell loss more often with Hydrus than its control, 20.8% versus 10.6%, although persistent corneal edema was not reported. The different analyses used different endothelial endpoints, so both results should be discussed rather than treating either as conclusive. ([PubMed][16])

**Need for later intervention:** SC-MIGS reduces but does not eliminate later drops, laser or filtering surgery. The five-year Hydrus randomized data showed a lower—but nonzero—incisional surgery rate; uncontrolled real-world series typically report higher subsequent-intervention rates than pivotal trials. ([PubMed][17])

## Applicability to patient selection

Most pivotal randomized evidence concerns cataract patients with **mild-to-moderate POAG**, medicated IOP around the high teens and washed-out IOP approximately 21–36 mmHg, without previous incisional glaucoma surgery. Evidence is thinner for normal-tension glaucoma, eyes already at low-teens IOP, advanced disease requiring a very low target, prior filtration surgery, and SC-MIGS as a standalone substitute for trabeculectomy or tube surgery. ([PubMed][1])

For counseling, the evidence supports framing SC-MIGS primarily as a way to achieve **mid-teen pressure with fewer medications and possibly delay more invasive surgery**, rather than as a reliable route to single-digit or very low-teens IOP.
