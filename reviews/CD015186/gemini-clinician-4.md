The shift toward Schlemm’s canal-based minimally invasive glaucoma surgeries (SC-MIGS)—whether via microstenting (e.g., Hydrus, iStent inject), viscodilation (e.g., OMNI), or excisional goniotomy (e.g., Kahook Dual Blade)—has fundamentally altered the risk-reward calculus of surgical intervention for open-angle glaucoma.

Here is a breakdown of the evidence on the benefits and harms of SC-MIGS based entirely on primary clinical trials (primarily comparing these interventions to phacoemulsification alone).

## Core Benefits

**Sustained Intraocular Pressure (IOP) Reduction**
SC-MIGS devices achieve clinically significant IOP lowering by bypassing or ablating the juxtacanalicular trabecular meshwork, the site of highest fluid resistance. In the 24-month pivotal randomized controlled trial (RCT) for the iStent inject, 75.8% of patients achieved a ≥20% reduction in unmedicated diurnal IOP compared to 61.9% in the cataract surgery alone group (Samuelson et al., 2019). Similarly, the US cohort of the HORIZON RCT for the Hydrus microstent demonstrated a ≥20% IOP reduction in 78.5% of patients versus 54.5% of controls at two years (Jones et al., 2019).

**Medication Independence**
For many patients, freeing them from the daily burden and surface toxicity of topical hypotensives is the most meaningful clinical endpoint. At 5 years in the HORIZON trial, 66% of eyes with a Hydrus Microstent remained entirely medication-free, compared to just 46% of those who had cataract surgery alone (Ahmed et al., 2022).

**Reduced Need for Incisional Surgery**
Long-term data suggest that early SC-MIGS intervention can alter the trajectory of the disease and prevent the need for more aggressive surgeries. At 3 years, the Hydrus cohort in the HORIZON trial had a dramatically lower cumulative probability of needing subsequent incisional glaucoma surgery (e.g., trabeculectomy or tube shunt) at 0.6% compared to 3.9% in the control group (Ahmed et al., 2021). This protective trend persisted through 5 years of follow-up (2.4% vs. 6.2%) (Ahmed et al., 2022).

**Efficacy in Complex Sub-types**
While most pivotal trials focus on primary open-angle glaucoma, excisional tools like the Kahook Dual Blade (KDB) have shown primary success outside of standard presentations. For example, a retrospective series of patients with uveitis-associated open-angle glaucoma demonstrated a significant reduction in medication burden and successful IOP control using KDB goniotomy (Miller et al., 2022).

## Harms and Safety Profile

**Favorable Safety Compared to Traditional Surgery**
Unlike trabeculectomies, SC-MIGS avoid conjunctival blebs, mitigating the risks of hypotony, blebitis, and choroidal effusion. Across major trials, the overall incidence of severe adverse events for SC-MIGS combined with cataract surgery is statistically comparable to cataract surgery alone, with no reports of serious device-related vision loss (Jones et al., 2019; Samuelson et al., 2019).

**Corneal Endothelial Cell Loss (ECL)**
Because these devices reside in the anterior chamber, progressive ECL is a primary concern. Reassuringly, the 5-year follow-up of the HORIZON trial found no statistically or clinically significant difference in the rate of endothelial cell loss between the Hydrus (19%) and control groups (13%), confirming long-term safety for the endothelium (Ahmed et al., 2022).

**Device-Specific Minor Complications**
The most frequent device-related events tend to be anatomical or transient:

* **Synechiae and Obstruction:** In the HORIZON trial, peripheral anterior synechiae (PAS) were more common in the Hydrus group at 5 years (14.6% vs. 3.7%), though the majority of these were non-obstructive and did not negatively impact IOP outcomes (Ahmed et al., 2022). For the iStent inject, early post-operative events like transient stent obstruction (6.2%) were noted (Samuelson et al., 2019).
* **Hyphema:** With excisional techniques like KDB, gravity-dependent hyphema can cause mild, transient IOP spikes in the first postoperative week, though these typically self-resolve without intervention.
