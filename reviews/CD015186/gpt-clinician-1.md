## Scope

I treated **SC-MIGS** as ab-interno procedures that improve conventional outflow through the trabecular meshwork, Schlemm’s canal, or collector channels:

* Trabecular/Schlemm’s-canal implants: iStent, iStent inject/infinite, Hydrus.
* Trabecular excision or ablation: Kahook Dual Blade and Trabectome.
* Circumferential or segmental trabeculotomy: GATT and suture trabeculotomy ab interno.
* Ab-interno canaloplasty/viscodilation, alone or combined with trabeculotomy: ABiC/iTrack and OMNI.

I excluded suprachoroidal and subconjunctival procedures, reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, opinions, and editorials. The search was updated through **July 25, 2026**. Because this is a large literature, the list prioritizes randomized/comparative studies, long-term extensions, and larger prospective cohorts rather than every retrospective case series.

# Main findings

## 1. Hydrus combined with cataract surgery has the strongest long-term comparative evidence

In HORIZON, 556 eyes with cataract and mostly mild-to-moderate POAG were randomized after phacoemulsification to Hydrus or phacoemulsification alone. At 24 months:

* ≥20% unmedicated diurnal IOP reduction: **77.3% vs 57.8%**, an absolute difference of 19.5 percentage points.
* Additional mean unmedicated IOP reduction attributable to Hydrus: approximately **2.3 mmHg**.
* Medication difference: approximately **0.4 fewer medications**. ([PubMed][1])

At five years:

* Medication-free: **66% vs 46%**.
* IOP ≤18 mmHg without medication: **49.5% vs 33.8%**.
* Subsequent incisional glaucoma surgery: **2.4% vs 6.2%**.
* No evidence of continuing excess endothelial loss between months 3 and 60 in the main HORIZON analysis. ([PubMed][2])

A post hoc visual-field analysis found slower mean-deviation progression with Hydrus plus cataract surgery, approximately **−0.26 vs −0.49 dB/year**. This is clinically interesting but should be viewed as a secondary analysis rather than a prespecified primary endpoint. ([PubMed][3])

**Interpretation:** For a patient already undergoing cataract surgery, Hydrus provides a modest additional IOP effect, a clearer medication-sparing effect, and the best available evidence of durability and reduced subsequent incisional surgery.

## 2. iStent provides a smaller, predominantly medication-sparing incremental benefit

The 505-eye iStent inject pivotal trial found at two years:

* ≥20% unmedicated diurnal IOP reduction: **75.8% with iStent inject vs 61.9% with phaco alone**.
* Mean unmedicated IOP reduction: **7.0 vs 5.4 mmHg**, an incremental difference of about **1.6 mmHg**.
* Medication-free IOP ≤18 mmHg: **63.2% vs 50.0%**. ([PubMed][4])

An independent, smaller randomized trial of 104 eyes produced a more conservative result: at two years, iStent inject reduced treatment by about **0.6 additional medications**, but there was **no sustained between-group IOP difference** and no difference in ocular-surface or glaucoma-related quality-of-life measures. ([ScienceDirect][5])

Earlier first-generation iStent trials similarly showed better medication-free control than phaco alone, but the absolute IOP differences were modest and medication differences diminished by two years. ([PubMed][6])

**Interpretation:** In well-controlled mild-to-moderate OAG undergoing cataract surgery, the most defensible expected benefit is reducing drops while maintaining similar IOP—not necessarily achieving a substantially lower medicated IOP.

## 3. Head-to-head implant studies do not establish one universal “best” device

In the standalone COMPARE randomized trial, Hydrus produced greater complete success and medication reduction than two first-generation iStents at 12 months; medication freedom was 22.6 percentage points higher with Hydrus. Overall safety appeared similar. ([PubMed][7])

The newer INTEGRITY trial compared three iStent infinite implants with one Hydrus as standalone surgery. At six months:

* ≥20% mean-diurnal IOP reduction was similar without accounting for complications: **82.7% vs 78.9%**.
* Mean reduction was approximately **7.4 vs 7.2 mmHg**.
* Reported surgical complications were lower with iStent infinite: **3.3% vs 16.9%**, driven partly by Hydrus malposition and peripheral anterior synechiae.

However, this was an interim six-month analysis, many authors were manufacturer employees, and the primary analysis incorporated surgical complications into treatment failure. Longer follow-up is important before treating it as definitive. ([PubMed][8])

## 4. Evidence for KDB or Trabectome added to cataract surgery is less certain

A small randomized KDB trial—42 eyes with relatively well-controlled mild-to-moderate glaucoma or ocular hypertension—found **no significant 12-month IOP or medication advantage** from adding KDB to phacoemulsification. ([PubMed][9])

Prospective and retrospective KDB series generally report baseline-to-follow-up reductions, but without a phaco-only control those changes cannot be attributed solely to goniotomy. Examples include:

* Prospective 52-eye phaco-KDB series: IOP **16.8 to 12.4 mmHg** at 12 months. ([PubMed][10])
* Retrospective 197-eye series: phaco-KDB IOP **16.7 to 13.8 mmHg**; standalone KDB **20.4 to 14.1 mmHg**, although only 16 standalone eyes remained at 12 months. ([PubMed][11])

A randomized comparison of phaco-Trabectome versus phaco-trabeculectomy was stopped early after enrolling only 19 patients. Similar one-year pressures were reported, with serious complications only in the trabeculectomy group, but the trial was far too small to demonstrate equivalence or noninferiority. ([PubMed][12])

**Interpretation:** KDB and Trabectome are supported mainly by uncontrolled or retrospective evidence. The incremental benefit beyond cataract surgery is less securely quantified than for Hydrus or iStent.

## 5. GATT can produce larger IOP reductions, particularly from higher baselines, but causes more early bleeding and pressure instability

In the original adult OAG series, POAG eyes had mean IOP reductions of about **11 mmHg at 12 months**, while transient hyphema was present in approximately **30%** at one week and 9% of the entire cohort required further glaucoma surgery. ([PubMed][13])

A larger 24-month follow-up reported a mean POAG reduction of approximately **9.2 mmHg** and 1.43 medications, although failure rates varied substantially by glaucoma subgroup. ([PubMed][14])

In the randomized comparison with ABiC:

* Twelve-month IOP: **16.0 mmHg with GATT vs 19.0 mmHg with ABiC**.
* Medication-free: approximately **78% vs 57%**, not statistically definitive.
* Hyphema: **87% with GATT vs 47% with ABiC**.
* Supraciliary effusion detected on imaging: **92% vs 71%**.
* Additional glaucoma surgery: one GATT eye and three ABiC eyes. ([PubMed][15])

A randomized trial comparing 360° with superior or inferior 180° suture trabeculotomy found an overall reduction from **18.6 to 13.7 mmHg** and from 3.1 to 1.4 medications at 12 months; the study was primarily about incision extent rather than comparison with medical therapy or another procedure. ([PubMed][16])

**Interpretation:** GATT may be attractive when a larger pressure reduction is needed and the distal outflow system is believed to remain functional. Early hyphema and IOP spikes require more intensive postoperative surveillance, particularly in advanced glaucoma.

## 6. OMNI canaloplasty plus trabeculotomy has encouraging prospective results, but no randomized phaco-only comparison

The prospective GEMINI study enrolled 149 eyes undergoing OMNI plus cataract surgery. At 12 months, among the effectiveness cohort:

* Washed-out diurnal IOP: **23.8 to 15.6 mmHg**.
* Medications: **1.8 to 0.4**.
* Medication-free: **80%**.
* Transient hyphema: **6%**.
* Transient IOP elevation: **2%**.
* No secondary glaucoma surgery during the initial 12 months. ([PMC][17])

A 36-month extension included only 66 consenting patients but suggested durability: mean unmedicated IOP was **16.3 mmHg**, and 74% were medication-free. The substantial attrition and absence of a control group limit certainty. ([PubMed][18])

ROMEO retrospective cohorts found meaningful IOP or medication reductions, but secondary intervention occurred in approximately **5%–10% by 12 months** and **8.3% after 12 months** in the extended cohort. ([PubMed][19])

**Interpretation:** OMNI results are promising, especially as an implant-free option, but the apparent magnitude cannot be directly compared with randomized implant trials because GEMINI did not include a phaco-only group.

# Harms seen in primary studies

### Transient hyphema

This is the characteristic adverse event of trabeculotomy or tissue-excision procedures:

* GATT: about 30% in the original series, and considerably higher when actively sought in the ABiC-versus-GATT trial. ([PubMed][13])
* KDB: approximately 17% on postoperative day 1 in one large retrospective series. ([PubMed][11])
* OMNI plus phaco: approximately 6% in GEMINI. ([PMC][17])

Most cases resolved, but hyphema can delay visual recovery and may contribute to early IOP spikes.

### Early IOP elevation

Reported rates vary with the definition and procedure:

* KDB series: **10.2%** had a spike >10 mmHg above baseline at one week. ([PubMed][11])
* OMNI GEMINI: approximately **2%** transient IOP elevations. ([PMC][17])
* GATT studies show that spikes can be frequent in certain populations, especially juvenile or advanced glaucoma, and prolonged spikes may predict failure. ([PubMed][20])

### Implant malposition, obstruction, and peripheral anterior synechiae

These are specific to implanted devices. Hydrus trials reported focal PAS as the notable device-related event; the recent INTEGRITY study found more malposition and PAS with Hydrus than iStent infinite at six months. ([PubMed][21])

### Corneal endothelial effects

Five-year iStent inject data found no significant difference from phaco alone in mean endothelial-cell density or the proportion with major cell loss. ([PubMed][22])

A cross-trial primary-data analysis found >30% endothelial-cell loss in **20.8% of Hydrus eyes versus 10.6% of controls**, although the excess appeared to occur mainly during the early postoperative period, did not continue to accelerate after three months, and was not associated with persistent corneal edema. ([PubMed][23])

### Failure and subsequent surgery

SC-MIGS preserves future filtering-surgery options, but it does not eliminate the possibility of further intervention. Rates were low in large mild-to-moderate cataract trials but higher in some standalone or uncontrolled cohorts. For example, five-year incisional surgery in HORIZON was 2.4% with Hydrus; ROMEO reported approximately 8% additional laser or surgery beyond 12 months; and the 2026 real-world standalone iStent infinite series reported incisional reintervention in 7.8% by 12 months. ([PubMed][2])

# Clinical interpretation

For counseling a typical patient with mild-to-moderate OAG and cataract:

* **Most established option:** Hydrus plus phaco, particularly when durable medication reduction is important.
* **iStent inject:** reasonable when the primary goal is drop reduction while maintaining similar IOP; the incremental IOP reduction may be small.
* **KDB/Trabectome/OMNI:** potentially useful implant-free approaches, but the isolated benefit beyond phaco is less certain because controlled trials are limited.
* **Standalone GATT or canal surgery:** may produce larger pressure reductions in eyes with higher baseline IOP, at the cost of more hyphema and early IOP variability.
* **Very low target IOP:** Across the studies, average postoperative pressures generally clustered in the mid-teens. These trials do not establish that SC-MIGS reliably achieves low-teens or single-digit targets required by some advanced or rapidly progressing eyes. ([PubMed][1])

The most important evidence limitations are that most pivotal trials enrolled mild-to-moderate disease, cataract surgery itself lowers IOP, endpoints and medication-washout protocols vary, many studies are industry-associated, and patient-centered outcomes and visual-field progression were rarely primary outcomes.
