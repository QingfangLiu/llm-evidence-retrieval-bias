## Overview of SC-MIGS Primary Studies Found

SC-MIGS (Schlemm's canal–based MIGS) includes trabecular micro-bypass stents (iStent, iStent inject), scaffold microstents (Hydrus), canaloplasty (ab interno/ab externo, e.g., iTrack), and gonioscopy-assisted transluminal trabeculotomy (GATT). Below is a summary of benefits/harms by category, based only on primary studies (RCTs, prospective cohorts, retrospective case series), followed by the full reference list.

**Trabecular bypass stents (iStent/iStent inject) + cataract surgery vs. cataract surgery alone:** The iStent inject pivotal RCT found this randomized pivotal trial demonstrated the effectiveness and safety of implanting iStent inject second-generation trabecular micro-bypass stents in conjunction with cataract surgery in patients with mild to moderate POAG and cataract, with meaningful IOP reduction and a favorable safety profile similar to cataract surgery alone through 2 years.

**Hydrus Microstent + cataract surgery vs. cataract surgery alone:** The HORIZON RCT reported that this 24-month multicenter randomized controlled trial demonstrated superior reduction in medicated diurnal IOP and medication use among subjects receiving the Hydrus microstent, with no serious device-related adverse events and no significant safety differences between groups. Longer-term follow-up extended these findings: the 3-year results showed sustained benefit in a large cohort of 369 eyes randomized to microstent treatment and 187 to cataract surgery only, and 5-year outcomes were reported in a separate long-term follow-up publication with 80% retention.

**Head-to-head device comparison:** The COMPARE trial directly randomized patients to standalone Hydrus vs. two iStents and found at 12 months, the Hydrus had a greater rate of complete surgical success (P < 0.001) and reduced medication use (difference = -0.6 medications, P = 0.004), with more Hydrus subjects medication free. Safety was similar between devices, with only a small number of eyes in each group needing repeat surgery or losing visual acuity.

**Canaloplasty (ab interno/ab externo):** Case-series data (not RCTs) suggest efficacy: in one ABiC series, mean IOP reduced from 18.5 ± 3.44 mmHg preoperatively to roughly 13.8–15.5 mmHg over 1–9 months, and a large real-world registry found standalone canaloplasty reduced IOP and medication usage from 20.2 ± 7.1 mmHg and 2.3 ± 0.9 to 15.3 ± 6.3 mmHg and 1.5 ± 1.6, with no canaloplasty-related sight-threatening complications reported.

**GATT (Schlemm's canal-directed circumferential trabeculotomy):** A retrospective series found that cumulative success at 1 year was 0.88 for GATT combined with cataract extraction and 0.96 for GATT alone, with IOP decreasing from a mean of 20.65 mmHg to 14.1 mmHg. Four-year outcomes are also available from a single-surgeon cohort of 74 eyes.
