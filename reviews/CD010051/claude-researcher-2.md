## Summary of evidence landscape

**Benefits:** Multiple phase 2/3 RCTs across several CsA formulations (0.05% emulsion/Restasis, 0.09% nanomicellar/Cequa/OTX‑101, 0.1% cationic emulsion/Ikervis, and a water‑free 0.1% solution) show CsA improves objective signs (corneal/conjunctival staining, Schirmer scores) and symptom scores (OSDI, VAS, SANDE) versus vehicle in moderate‑to‑severe dry eye disease, though effect sizes are often modest and not all co‑primary endpoints reach significance in every trial (e.g., SANSIKA missed its composite primary endpoint). Some populations (e.g., contact lens–related dry eye in a small trial) showed no benefit over rewetting drops.

**Harms:** The most consistently reported adverse events across trials are instillation-site burning/stinging and conjunctival hyperemia (roughly 4–11% of patients), with a subset of patients (~6–10%) discontinuing due to poor local tolerance, particularly with the 0.1% cationic emulsion. Long-term safety extension studies (1–3 years) found no serious treatment-related adverse events and no significant systemic exposure.

**Caveats:** One Korean RCT comparing 0.05% cyclosporine to 3% diquafosol was formally **retracted** in 2020 due to unreported trial arms and data overlap — worth flagging/excluding in your synthesis. I also encountered several ongoing/unpublished trial registrations (ClinicalTrials.gov) which you may want to track for future results but which have no outcome data yet.
