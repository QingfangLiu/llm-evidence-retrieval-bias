## Bottom line

Across individual randomized trials, topical cyclosporine A consistently improves **objective ocular-surface signs**, particularly corneal/conjunctival staining and, in some studies, tear production. The average improvement in **dry-eye symptoms** is less consistent: several large trials found no meaningful difference from vehicle in global dryness or OSDI scores, although some found benefits for particular symptoms or responder subgroups. ([ScienceDirect][1])

The principal harm is **temporary burning, stinging, or pain after instillation**. Its frequency varies considerably by formulation—from rates close to vehicle with some water-free preparations to approximately 24%–29% with nanomicellar 0.09% and cationic-emulsion 0.1% formulations in pivotal trials. Serious ocular or systemic treatment-related events were uncommon, and measured systemic cyclosporine exposure was generally undetectable or negligible. ([AAO Journal][2])

I searched primary human studies through **July 25, 2026** and did not use systematic reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, committee opinions, or editorials for the findings below.

## Benefits

### Ocular-surface damage

This is the most reproducible benefit.

* In the original 877-participant pivotal trials, cyclosporine 0.05% and 0.1% emulsions improved corneal staining and categorized Schirmer results more than vehicle. The 0.05% formulation also reduced artificial-tear use and improved blurred vision and investigators’ global assessments. There was no clear dose-response advantage for 0.1% over 0.05%. ([ScienceDirect][1])
* In SICCANOVE, once-daily cationic cyclosporine 0.1% produced a statistically greater improvement in corneal staining than vehicle, although the mean incremental difference was relatively small: approximately **0.23 grade** on the modified Oxford scale. ([PubMed][3])
* In SANSIKA, involving severe keratitis, corneal staining improved more with cyclosporine, but the prespecified composite endpoint combining staining and symptom improvement was not statistically significant. ([Sage Journals][4])
* Both major nanomicellar cyclosporine 0.09% trials found improvements in corneal and conjunctival staining. ([PMC][5])
* Water-free 0.1% cyclosporine improved total corneal staining in ESSENCE-1 and ESSENCE-2. In ESSENCE-2, the mean incremental difference at day 29 was modest, **−0.4 points on a 15-point scale**, but 71.6% versus 59.7% achieved a decrease of at least three grades—an absolute difference of 12.6%, or roughly one additional staining responder for every eight patients treated. ([PMC][6])
* A separate Chinese phase 3 trial of water-free 0.1% cyclosporine similarly improved total corneal staining at 29 days but did not improve its coprimary dryness score. ([PubMed][7])

Whether a small improvement in staining will translate into an improvement that a particular patient notices cannot be assumed; the sign–symptom relationship was weak in several studies.

### Tear production

Evidence suggests that some patients have a clinically substantial Schirmer response.

In the 744-participant phase 3 trial of nanomicellar 0.09% cyclosporine, **16.6%** of treated eyes versus **9.2%** of vehicle-treated eyes had a Schirmer increase of at least 10 mm after 84 days. The absolute difference was 7.4%, approximately one additional responder for every 14 treated eyes. Earlier 0.05% and dose-ranging trials also reported improvements in Schirmer results, although this outcome has not been positive in every formulation or study. ([PubMed][8])

### Symptoms and quality of life

This is the most uncertain part of the benefit.

* The original 0.05% emulsion trials found improvement in selected outcomes—blurred vision, artificial-tear use, and clinician global assessment—but not consistent superiority across dryness, burning, itching, pain, photophobia, or total OSDI. ([ScienceDirect][1])
* In both nanomicellar trials, global symptom scores improved substantially in all groups, but cyclosporine did **not** separate from vehicle. ([PMC][5])
* In SANSIKA, OSDI improved by almost the same amount with cyclosporine and vehicle: −13.6 versus −14.1 points. ([Sage Journals][4])
* SICCANOVE did find a small responder advantage: at least 25% improvement in discomfort occurred in 50.2% with cyclosporine versus 41.9% with vehicle. ([PubMed][3])
* ESSENCE-1 did not meet its OSDI endpoint, although a separate dryness VAS favored cyclosporine by about five points on a 100-point scale. ESSENCE-2 found no benefit for the primary dryness score. ([PMC][6])
* One Chinese 0.05% emulsion trial did find better OSDI scores at days 56 and 84, as well as improvements in multiple individual symptoms. ([PubMed][9])

Thus, symptom relief is possible, but the trials do not support promising that an average patient will experience a large or rapid symptomatic benefit.

### Onset and persistence

Newer water-free formulations demonstrated improvements in corneal staining within **two weeks**, while nanomicellar staining differences were generally evident by four weeks. Symptomatic effects, when present, were less predictable. A 52-week open-label extension of water-free cyclosporine found sustained improvements from the original baseline, but because everyone received active treatment and there was no continuing control group, it provides stronger information about long-term safety than comparative effectiveness. ([PMC][6])

## Harms

### Burning, stinging, and instillation pain

These are the most clinically relevant adverse effects, and formulation appears important.

* **Nanomicellar 0.09%:** instillation-site pain occurred in 24.2% versus 4.3% with vehicle; it was generally mild and described as brief stinging or burning. About 2.4% discontinued because of ocular adverse events. ([AAO Journal][2])
* **Cationic emulsion 0.1%:** in SANSIKA, instillation-site pain occurred in 29.2% versus 8.9% with vehicle and was mostly mild. ([Sage Journals][4])
* **Water-free 0.1%, ESSENCE-1:** instillation pain occurred in 2.5% versus 1.2%; three active-treatment patients discontinued because of ocular events. ([PMC][6])
* **Water-free 0.1%, ESSENCE-2:** instillation-site reactions occurred in 10.2% versus 8.8%, almost all mild; adverse-event discontinuation was 0.5% versus 0.7%. ([PMC][10])
* **0.05% emulsion in the Chinese phase 3 trial:** drug-related adverse events occurred in 5.0% versus 2.5%, most commonly ocular pain. ([PubMed][9])

Rates should not be compared as though these were head-to-head trials: definitions, populations, vehicles, and adverse-event collection differed.

Other reported ocular effects include transient redness, irritation, tearing, blurred vision, pruritus, foreign-body sensation, and eyelid edema. ([PMC][6])

### Serious and systemic harms

No consistent signal for serious ocular toxicity, increased intraocular pressure, clinically important visual-acuity loss, or treatment-related systemic toxicity emerged in the major trials. Serious events that occurred were generally judged unrelated to treatment. ([AAO Journal][2])

In the SANSIKA extension, blood cyclosporine was undetectable or negligible in nearly all participants; the two patients with clearly elevated levels had previously used systemic cyclosporine. A long-term 0.1% emulsion extension lasting up to three years reported no serious treatment-related events or systemic adverse effects; burning was reported by 10.9%, stinging by 3.9%, and conjunctival hyperemia by 3.4%. ([PMC][11])

### Important limitations

Most controlled trials lasted only 1–6 months. Vehicles often contain lubricating or surface-retentive ingredients and produced substantial improvements themselves, making the drug’s incremental symptom benefit difficult to demonstrate. Outcome definitions varied, and many studies were sponsored by the product manufacturer. Long-term controlled evidence and independent head-to-head comparisons among commercially available formulations remain limited. ([PubMed][12])

For an individual treatment decision, the most realistic expectation is **improved ocular-surface staining and possibly increased tear production over weeks to months**, with uncertain symptom improvement and a meaningful possibility of initial instillation discomfort.
