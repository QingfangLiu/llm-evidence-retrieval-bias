## Benefits reported in trials

**Signs of dry eye (objective measures).** The original phase 3 program that led to FDA approval of CsA 0.05% and 0.1% (Restasis) found that both concentrations were safe and effective for moderate-to-severe DED, improving both objective and subjective measures compared with vehicle, in a multicenter, randomized, double-masked trial of 877 patients tracking corneal staining, Schirmer test, tear break-up time, and OSDI. The earlier phase 2 dose-ranging trial in 162 patients tested four CsA concentrations against vehicle over 12 weeks and established the dose-response relationship that led to the 0.05%/0.1% concentrations tested in phase 3.

**Newer formulations.** A water-free cyclosporine 0.1% solution was tested in the ESSENCE-2 trial, a multicenter, double-blind, vehicle-controlled phase 3 trial in China with a 14-day artificial-tear run-in, comparing water-free cyclosporine to vehicle twice daily for 29 days, with an open-label extension assessing longer-term safety and efficacy. Cequa (cyclosporine 0.09%, a nanomicellar formulation) was studied in a phase 2b/3 dose-ranging trial and a phase 3 confirmatory trial; the FDA review of that program found a statistically significant, higher percentage of eyes with a clinically meaningful increase in tear production (≥10 mm Schirmer wetting) with Cequa versus vehicle — about 17% of Cequa-treated eyes versus about 9% of vehicle-treated eyes in each study.

**Population-specific and combination trials.** A placebo-controlled phase III trial in Chinese patients tested 0.05% CsA emulsion specifically in that population. A more recent RCT tested CsA against artificial tears in patients with dry eye after cataract surgery. Another trial examined CsA combined with punctal plugs versus CsA alone for treating DED and ocular inflammation.

## Harms/adverse effects reported

Across these trials, the most consistently reported adverse effects are **local, not systemic**: burning/stinging and instillation-site pain are the most common reasons for discontinuation. In the original 877-patient program, 2.2% of subjects discontinued treatment due to burning and stinging. For the higher-concentration 0.09% formulation, FDA review data show a higher rate of local irritation — instillation site pain in 22% and conjunctival hyperemia in 6% of patients being the most common adverse reactions. The FDA's overall risk-benefit conclusion for that formulation was that the potential benefits through increased tear production outweighed the identified (local, transient) risks.

## Notes on limitations

- One relevant RCT (comparing 0.05% cyclosporine with 3% diquafosol) was **retracted** due to unreliable/overlapping data — worth knowing since it may still surface in older reference lists.
- Effect sizes for symptom improvement (as opposed to objective signs) are often modest in placebo-controlled trials, and benefits typically take weeks to months to emerge — this is consistent across the phase 2 and phase 3 programs.
- Some newer formulation trials (ESSENCE-2, Cequa) were conducted in specific populations (e.g., Chinese patients, or those switched from Restasis) and may not generalize to all DED patients.
