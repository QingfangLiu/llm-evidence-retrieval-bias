## Benefits reported in primary studies

**Efficacy signals are consistent but modest, and not always statistically significant on every endpoint.** Across pivotal RCTs, CsA eye drops (in various concentrations: 0.05%, 0.09%, 0.1%) generally show:
- Increased tear production (Schirmer test scores) versus vehicle in a randomized, multicenter, vehicle-controlled, double-masked, phase 3 clinical trial, a significantly greater percentage of eyes in the treatment group achieved a clinically meaningful increase in Schirmer test scores compared to vehicle.
- Improved ocular surface staining (corneal/conjunctival) in several trials, though effect sizes are often small.
- Symptom (OSDI/global symptom score) improvement is less consistent — in the OTX-101 phase 3 trial, the global symptom score was reduced from baseline in both treatment groups by approximately 30%, with no significant separation between the drug and vehicle groups, highlighting a recurring issue: vehicle/placebo response is often large, so symptom benefit over placebo is harder to demonstrate than sign (objective test) benefit.
- Early trials (Sall et al. 2000; Stevenson et al. 2000) established the original efficacy/dose-response data supporting FDA approval of 0.05% CsA (Restasis).
- Long-term open-label follow-up (ESSENCE-2 OLE) suggests symptom improvement continues over the course of a year, with scores reaching their lowest point after 12 months of treatment.

## Harms/tolerability reported in primary studies

- The most consistently reported adverse event is **ocular burning/stinging on instillation**, described across pharmacokinetic and safety studies as the most common adverse event associated with ophthalmic ciclosporin emulsion use in 12-week and 1-year clinical safety studies, with no serious drug-related adverse events occurring.
- Discontinuation due to burning/stinging occurred in about 2.2% of subjects (19 of 877) in the two original multicenter Sall et al. trials of 0.05% cyclosporine, though real-world/anecdotal intolerance dropout rates are sometimes quoted much higher.
- Long-term safety data (ESSENCE-2 OLE, 52 weeks) found no deaths during the study, with three patients discontinuing due to an adverse event (two nonocular, one mild ocular burning), and 27.5% of patients reporting an ocular treatment-emergent adverse event over the year, most commonly mild instillation-site pain (6.5%).
- No signal of vision-threatening toxicity or systemic toxicity has emerged from these trials; adverse events are localized and generally mild-to-moderate.

**Caveat:** Individual trials differ in formulation (anionic emulsion vs. nanomicellar vs. water-free solution), concentration, comparator (vehicle vs. active comparator), and population (moderate-severe DED, post-cataract DED, Sjögren's-associated DED, contact lens wearers), so results aren't perfectly comparable. I did not screen every trial for risk of bias — for a treatment decision, it's worth discussing these results and your specific DED subtype/severity with your ophthalmologist, since some trials show real differences between formulations in tolerability and speed of onset.
