Based on primary randomized controlled trials, venepuncture (drawing blood from a vein with a needle) is consistently shown to be superior to a heel lance for full-term newborn babies across several key clinical metrics, provided it is performed by a trained practitioner.

Here is a breakdown of what the primary literature shows regarding the two methods:

### Pain and Infant Distress

Venepuncture is significantly less painful for term neonates than a heel lance. In randomized trials using validated pain metrics like the Neonatal Facial Coding System (NFCS) and the Neonatal Infant Pain Scale (NIPS), infants undergoing venepuncture demonstrated substantially lower pain scores compared to those receiving a heel prick (Larsson et al., 1998; Ogawa, 2005; Shah et al., 1997). Furthermore, both the incidence and total duration of infant crying are notably reduced when venous sampling is used (Larsson et al., 1998; Ogawa, 2005).

### Procedural Efficiency and Success Rate

Heel lancing frequently fails to yield an adequate blood sample on the first attempt, requiring clinicians to repeatedly squeeze the infant's heel or perform multiple skin punctures. By contrast, venepuncture has a much higher first-attempt success rate. One trial demonstrated that a sufficient blood sample was obtained with a single skin puncture in 86% of venepuncture cases, compared to only 19% to 40% of heel lance cases, depending on the lancet size used (Larsson et al., 1998).

Because it requires fewer repeated attempts, venepuncture actually takes less total time to perform and collect the adequate sample volume than a heel lance (Larsson et al., 1998).

### Parental Perception

A common clinical concern is that a needle draw might look more frightening to parents. While mothers whose infants were assigned to the venepuncture group initially reported higher anxiety *before* the blood draw, they ultimately rated their babies' pain levels significantly lower *after* observing the procedure than the mothers who watched a heel lance (Shah et al., 1997).
