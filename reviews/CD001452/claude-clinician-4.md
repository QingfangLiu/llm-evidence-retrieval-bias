Based on my search, here are the individual primary studies (RCTs) that directly compare venepuncture vs. heel lance for blood sampling in full-term neonates. These are the trials that have consistently formed the evidence base for this comparison (several were pooled in the Cochrane systematic review by Shah & Ohlsson, which I've excluded per your request, but I'm listing the primary trials it draws on plus others found independently).

**Consistent finding across studies:** venepuncture is associated with lower pain scores (across various validated pain scales — NFCS, NIPS, PIPP), shorter crying duration, and a lower rate of repeat skin puncture, compared with heel lance, in term neonates.

## Primary studies

1. **Shah VS, Taddio A, Bennett S, Speidel BD.** Neonatal pain response to heel stick vs venepuncture for routine blood sampling. *Arch Dis Child Fetal Neonatal Ed.* 1997;77(2):F143–4. doi:10.1136/fn.77.2.F143. PMID: 9377140.

2. **Larsson BA, Tannfeldt G, Lagercrantz H, Olsson GL.** Venipuncture is more effective and less painful than heel lancing for blood tests in neonates. *Pediatrics.* 1998;101(5):882–6. doi:10.1542/peds.101.5.882. PMID: 9565419.

3. **Eriksson M, Gradin M, Schollin J.** Oral glucose and venepuncture reduce blood sampling pain in newborns. *Early Hum Dev.* 1999;55(3):211–8. doi:10.1016/S0378-3782(99)00018-3. PMID: 10463785. (2×2 factorial RCT: venepuncture vs. heel stick, with/without oral glucose, in 120 healthy full-term newborns.)

4. **Kvist LJ.** Can venepuncture reduce the pain of neonatal PKU-sampling? A randomised study. *Vård i Norden.* 2002;22(1):27–29.

5. **Logan PW.** Venepuncture versus heel prick for the collection of the newborn screening test. *Aust J Adv Nurs.* 1999;17(1):30–6.

6. **Ogawa S, Ogihara T, Fujiwara E, Ito K, Nakano M, Nakayama S, Hachiya T, Fujimoto N, Abe H, Ban S, Ikeda E, Tamai H.** Venepuncture is preferable to heel lance for blood sampling in term neonates. *Arch Dis Child Fetal Neonatal Ed.* 2005;90(5):F432–6. doi:10.1136/adc.2004.069328. PMID: 15871991. (RCT, n=100, also tested sucrose vs. water — the "without sucrose" arms give the direct venepuncture vs. heel lance comparison.)

A note on scope: I was able to verify studies #1, #2, #3, and #6 in detail via PubMed/PMC abstracts. Studies #4 (Kvist) and #5 (Logan) are cited repeatedly in the bibliography of the Cochrane review as included primary trials, but I could not pull their full abstracts directly (older/regional nursing journals not indexed with accessible abstracts) — you may want to verify these two via your institutional library access before relying on them.
