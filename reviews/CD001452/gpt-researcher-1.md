## Bottom line

For **routine metabolic-screening or diagnostic samples requiring more than a single drop**, the individual studies generally favor venepuncture: it produced lower pain scores and, in several studies, fewer repeat punctures or shorter collection times. Six of the seven studies restricted to term newborns reported less pain with venepuncture under at least one relevant comparison. ([PMC][1])

The important exception was a study of **bedside blood-glucose monitoring**, where only one drop was needed. It found no significant pain difference, venepuncture took substantially longer, and several secondary outcomes numerically favored heel prick. ([PubMed][2])

Therefore, “better” appears to depend partly on:

* **Required blood volume:** venepuncture looks more advantageous for screening cards or larger samples; heel lance may remain efficient for one-drop testing.
* **Operator and equipment:** successful venepuncture depended on trained staff and appropriate needle size.
* **Sweet-tasting analgesia:** glucose or sucrose reduced pain and sometimes narrowed the difference between procedures.

## Interpretation and limitations

The most consistent advantage of venepuncture occurred during **newborn-screening or diagnostic collection**, where heel sampling commonly involved squeezing and sometimes repeated lancing. The blood-glucose study illustrates why results should not automatically be generalized to very small-volume point-of-care samples.

The evidence has several limitations:

* Most trials were small, single-center studies.
* Operators could not be blinded, and assessor blinding was inconsistently reported.
* Allocation procedures were not always adequately described.
* Techniques differed by needle size, lancet type, operator experience, required blood volume, and use of glucose or sucrose.
* Adverse-event reporting was limited and provides little evidence about uncommon harms.
* The large Spanish study included infants born at 35–36 weeks and therefore is not strictly direct evidence for an exclusively term population.

I did not identify a direct comparative primary report published after the two 2012 studies.
