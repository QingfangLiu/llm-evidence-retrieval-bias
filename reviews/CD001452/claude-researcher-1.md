## Summary

I identified randomized/controlled clinical trials that directly compared venepuncture with heel lance (heel stick/heel prick) for blood sampling in **full-term (term) neonates**. I excluded systematic reviews, meta-analyses (e.g., the Shah & Ohlsson Cochrane review), narrative reviews, guidelines, and commentaries — these were used only to trace citations to the underlying primary trials, not cited as evidence themselves.

Across these trials, the consistent finding is that venepuncture, performed by a skilled practitioner, produces lower pain scores and shorter crying time than heel lance, though it can take longer to obtain an adequate sample and occasionally requires a repeat puncture. One study (Saththasivam 2009), using heel prick specifically for glucose monitoring, found heel prick was quicker with no difference in pain scores — illustrating that results can vary by outcome/context.

## Primary studies (individual trials)

1. **Larsson BA, Tannfeldt G, Lagercrantz H, Olsson GL.** Venipuncture is more effective and less painful than heel lancing for blood tests in neonates. *Pediatrics.* 1998;101(5):882–886.

4. **Logan PW.** Venepuncture versus heel prick for the collection of the Newborn Screening Test. *Aust J Adv Nurs.* 1999;17(1):30–36.
