## Primary studies: venepuncture vs. heel lance in full-term newborns

I searched for individual primary studies (not reviews) directly comparing venepuncture and heel lance for blood sampling in term/full-term neonates. Below is what I found, organized by relevance to your question.

**Studies that directly randomize/compare venepuncture vs. heel lance in term neonates:**

1. **Shah VS, Taddio A, Bennett S, Speidel BD.** Neonatal pain response to heel stick vs venepuncture for routine blood sampling. *Arch Dis Child Fetal Neonatal Ed.* 1997;77(2):F143–F144. — 27 infants randomly allocated to venepuncture or heel stick; NIPS scores were higher in the heel stick group, and mothers rated their infant's pain as lower in the venepuncture group.

2. **Larsson BA, Tannfeldt G, Lagercrantz H, Olsson GL.** Venipuncture is more effective and less painful than heel lancing for blood tests in neonates. *Pediatrics.* 1998;101(5):882–886. — 120 healthy full-term infants randomly assigned to heel lancing (large or small lancet) or venipuncture of the dorsum of the hand.

3. **Larsson BA, Tannfeldt G, Lagercrantz H, Olsson GL.** Alleviation of the pain of venepuncture in neonates. *Acta Paediatrica.* 1998;87(7):774–779.

4. **Eriksson M, Gradin M, Schollin J.** Oral glucose and venepuncture reduce blood sampling pain in newborns. *Early Human Development.* 1999;55(3):211–218. — 120 full-term newborns randomized to venepuncture or heel stick, with or without oral glucose; pain assessed via crying duration, PIPP score, and heart rate changes.

5. **Ogawa S, Ogihara T, Fujiwara E, et al.** Venepuncture is preferable to heel lance for blood sampling in term neonates. *Arch Dis Child Fetal Neonatal Ed.* 2005;90(5):F432–F436. — 100 healthy full-term newborns randomized to one of four groups (VP or HL, with or without sucrose); pain assessed by neonatal facial coding system (NFCS) and crying.

6. **Kvist LJ, Jonsson K, Tornestrand BM, Edwinson Mansson M.** Can venepuncture reduce the pain of neonatal PKU-sampling? A randomised study. *Vård i Norden.* 2002;22(3):27–30.

7. **Shrestha M, Adhikari R.** Comparison of Pain Response to Venepuncture Versus Heel Lance Blood Sampling in Term Neonates. *J Nepal Paediatr Soc.* 2012;32(2):99–104. — 200 term neonates undergoing blood sampling for bilirubin or glucose estimation, randomly assigned to heel lance or venepuncture groups (100 each); pain assessed by Neonatal/Infant Pain Scale (NIPS). This study's conclusion (venepuncture less painful) aligns with the others, though a citing source notes at least one other study found the opposite direction — worth checking full text for exact effect size.

8. **Logan PW.** Venepuncture versus heel prick for the collection of the Newborn Screening Test. *Aust J Adv Nurs.* 1999;17(1):30–36.
