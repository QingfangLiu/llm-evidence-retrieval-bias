Based on primary clinical trials and observational cohorts evaluating children under 12, the evidence regarding vitamin B12 supplementation is mixed. While supplementation reliably corrects biochemical markers of B12 status, its clinical benefits on cognition and development are most pronounced when B12 is combined with folic acid or given to highly deficient subgroups, rather than being administered alone as a general intervention.

Here is a breakdown of the primary data on neurodevelopment, growth, and metabolic health.

## Randomized Controlled Trials (RCTs)

* **Gross Motor and Problem-Solving Skills:** A double-blind RCT conducted in North India investigated daily supplementation of vitamin B12 and/or folic acid in children aged 6 to 30 months over a six-month period. The study found that vitamin B12 alone yielded significant improvements only in gross motor scores. However, children who received combined supplementation of **both** vitamin B12 and folic acid demonstrated substantial improvements in gross motor functioning and problem-solving skills compared to the placebo group (Kvestad et al., 2015).
* **Long-Term Cardiometabolic Health:** Researchers followed up with the same North Indian cohort six to seven years post-intervention to assess cardiometabolic risk markers. The children who received combined vitamin B12 and folic acid in early childhood maintained significantly lower plasma total homocysteine (tHcy) concentrations. Furthermore, standalone vitamin B12 supplementation was associated with a lower leptin-adiponectin ratio in certain subgroups, pointing toward persistent, beneficial metabolic effects in impoverished populations (Manapurath et al., 2023).
* **Preschool Cognitive Function:** In contrast, a trial in Nepal provided 600 infants (aged 6–11 months) with 2 µg of daily vitamin B12 for an entire year. Despite biochemical tests confirming that the supplementation effectively corrected B12 status, researchers found no difference between the B12 group and the placebo group regarding preschool cognitive function, intelligence quotients, or general neurodevelopment when the children were evaluated at 30 to 47 months of age (Ulak et al., 2022).

## Observational Cohort Data

* **Long-Term Neurodevelopment Associations:** While intervention trials show mixed results, observational data strongly links natural B12 status to development. An observational follow-up of Nepalese children demonstrated that natural markers of vitamin B12 status during infancy (measured via plasma tHcy and methylmalonic acid) were significantly and positively associated with improved total development scores, social perception tasks, and visuospatial abilities five years later (Kvestad et al., 2017).

> **Clinical Takeaway:** Standalone vitamin B12 supplementation effectively improves biochemical status but has not consistently translated to measurable cognitive or developmental gains in general pediatric populations compared to placebo. However, combining vitamin B12 with folic acid shows robust, placebo-controlled evidence for improving early motor skills, problem-solving, and long-term metabolic health.
