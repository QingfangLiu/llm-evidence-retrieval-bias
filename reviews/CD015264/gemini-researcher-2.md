Here is a synthesis of the evidence from individual primary studies (randomized controlled trials and clinical trials) evaluating vitamin B12 supplementation in children under 12.

Overall, while vitamin B12 supplementation reliably corrects biochemical markers of deficiency, its observable clinical impacts on neurodevelopment, growth, and anemia are mixed, often depending on the baseline nutritional status of the children.

### **Neurodevelopment and Cognitive Function**

The effect of B12 on neurodevelopmental outcomes appears to depend heavily on whether the child is concurrently deficient in other nutrients, or already stunted.

* **Null findings in general at-risk populations:** A large, community-based randomized placebo-controlled trial (RCT) in Nepal provided marginally stunted 6- to 11-month-old infants with 2 μg of B12 daily for 12 months. Despite a significant improvement in metabolic B12 markers (e.g., lowered homocysteine and methylmalonic acid), the supplementation had no significant effect on neurodevelopment as measured by Bayley-III scores (Strand et al., 2020). A follow-up study of this same cohort at 30–47 months of age similarly found no long-term benefits on preschool cognitive function or full-scale intelligence quotient (Ulak et al., 2022).
* **Targeted improvements:** Conversely, a 6-month RCT in young North Indian children (6–30 months) using a factorial design (B12, folic acid, both, or placebo) found that B12 alone significantly improved gross motor functioning. When combined with folic acid, children showed significant improvements in both gross motor and problem-solving skills. The strongest effects were observed in children who were already stunted and those with elevated total homocysteine at baseline (Kvestad et al., 2015).

### **Growth and Hematological Outcomes (Anemia)**

Primary studies frequently evaluate hemoglobin levels and physical growth to see if B12 resolves infantile anemia and stunting, but results in unselected populations have been largely disappointing.

* **Physical growth and hemoglobin:** In the aforementioned Nepal cohort, 12 months of B12 supplementation yielded no significant differences in attained physical growth or hemoglobin concentrations compared to the placebo group (Strand et al., 2020). Similarly, an Indian trial evaluating 6 months of daily B12 and/or folic acid supplementation in children aged 6–36 months found no improvement in hemoglobin concentration; almost 90% of the cohort remained anemic at the end of the trial (Kumar et al., 2017).
* **Treatment of established deficiency:** In a clinical context where children already have a diagnosed nutritional B12 deficiency, high-dose oral supplementation has been found to be just as effective as intramuscular (parenteral) therapy at normalizing serum B12 and partially resolving anemia. This suggests that while B12 may not prevent anemia in general populations, oral supplements are a highly viable active treatment for confirmed deficiencies (Sezer et al., 2018).

### **Long-Term Cardiometabolic Outcomes**

There is emerging evidence that early B12 supplementation might offer sustained metabolic benefits later in childhood. A follow-up of the North Indian cohort (originally supplemented between 6 and 30 months of age) assessed the children 6 to 7 years later. Children who had received combined B12 and folic acid supplementation maintained significantly lower total homocysteine concentrations. Furthermore, B12 supplementation was associated with a lower leptin-adiponectin ratio, suggesting persistent protective effects against future cardiometabolic risk (Manapurath et al., 2023).
