As a parent, wanting to make sure your child has the right nutritional foundation is completely natural. To answer your question using only primary research studies (specifically randomized controlled trials, which are the gold standard for testing this), the results are mixed.

Whether vitamin B12 supplements improve health and development compared to a placebo seems to depend heavily on whether the child is already deficient, has an underlying condition, or is at high risk for poor nutritional status.

Here is a breakdown of what individual trials have found when evaluating vitamin B12 supplementation in children under 12:

## When B12 Supplementation Shows Benefits

**1. Improving Motor and Problem-Solving Skills in At-Risk Populations**
A placebo-controlled trial conducted in North India focused on children aged 6 to 30 months who were at high risk for poor nutritional status due to poverty and vegetarian dietary practices. The researchers found that children given a daily supplement of vitamin B12 along with folic acid for six months showed significant improvements in gross motor skills and problem-solving abilities compared to the placebo group. Supplementation with vitamin B12 alone (without folic acid) also significantly improved gross motor scores (Kvestad et al., 2015).

**2. Correcting Known Deficiencies**
While this focuses on treating a diagnosed issue rather than preventative supplementation, a study compared oral vitamin B12 tablets against the traditional injection method in children (aged 1 month to 17 years). The study found that oral supplements are highly effective. Both methods successfully normalized vitamin B12 and hemoglobin levels within a month for children who had a diagnosed deficiency (Sezer et al., 2018).

**3. Supporting Children with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD)**
An 8-week randomized trial looked at children with ASD, who often have specific metabolic differences (such as a reduced ability to methylate DNA). The children were given methyl B12 via injection. The study found that clinician-rated symptoms of autism significantly improved in the B12 group compared to the placebo group, alongside improvements in cellular methylation capacity (Hendren et al., 2016).

## When B12 Supplementation Shows No Effect

**1. No Cognitive Benefits for Mildly Deficient Infants**
A rigorously designed trial followed 600 infants in Nepal (starting at 6–11 months old). They were given daily vitamin B12 supplements alongside standard multivitamins, compared to a placebo group that just received the standard multivitamins without B12, for a full year. Despite blood tests confirming that the supplements successfully improved their B12 levels, follow-up testing when the children reached preschool age (30–47 months) showed no difference in their full-scale IQ or general neurodevelopment compared to the placebo group (Ulak et al., 2022).

This suggests that even if a child has subclinical (mild) B12 deficiency, correcting it with a supplement doesn't automatically translate to measurable cognitive or developmental boosts later in early childhood.

## The Bottom Line

If your child eats a standard diet that includes animal products (meat, dairy, eggs) or fortified foods, they likely get enough B12 naturally, and these primary trials do not suggest a developmental benefit to adding a B12 supplement "just in case." However, if your child is raised on a strict vegan or vegetarian diet, has a diagnosed deficiency, or has specific metabolic conditions, B12 supplementation has been shown to successfully correct metabolic imbalances and support motor development.
