Here are individual primary studies (RCTs) on vitamin B12 supplementation in children <12 years, excluding systematic reviews/meta-analyses/narrative reviews/guidelines/editorials:

**Nepal (Bhaktapur cohort — same underlying trial + follow-ups)**
- Strand TA et al. (2017). *The effect of vitamin B12 supplementation in Nepalese infants on growth and development: study protocol for a randomized controlled trial.* Trials 18:187. — Protocol for the RCT below.
- Strand TA et al. (2020, PLOS Medicine). *Effects of vitamin B12 supplementation on neurodevelopment and growth in Nepalese Infants: A randomized controlled trial.* — 600 marginally stunted infants (6–11 mo), 2 μg B12 vs placebo daily × 12 mo; no effect on Bayley-III, growth, or hemoglobin, but improved metabolic markers (tHcy, MMA).
- Kvestad I et al. *The effect of infant vitamin B12 supplementation on neurodevelopment: a follow-up of a randomised placebo-controlled trial in Nepal.* Br J Nutr. — Longer-term follow-up of the same cohort.
- Secondary-outcomes paper: *The Effect of Daily Vitamin B-12 Supplementation on the Incidence of Common Childhood Infections in Nepal* (ScienceDirect/J Nutr, 2026) — secondary analysis of infection incidence from the same RCT.

**North India (Delhi factorial trial — 1000 children, multiple outputs)**
- Strand TA et al. (2015). *Vitamin B-12, folic Acid, and growth in 6- to 30-month-old children: a randomized controlled trial.* Pediatrics 135(4):e918–26.
- Kvestad I et al. (2015). *Vitamin B12 and Folic Acid Improve Gross Motor and Problem-Solving Skills in Young North Indian Children: A Randomized Placebo-Controlled Trial.* PLOS ONE 10(6):e0129915. — 2×2 factorial, 422–1000 children 6–30 mo, B12/folic acid/both/placebo × 6 mo.
- 6-year follow-up: *Vitamin B12 and/or folic acid supplementation on linear growth: a 6-year follow-up study...* — height outcomes in 791 children at ~7 years.
- 6–7 year follow-up: *Daily Folic Acid and/or Vitamin B12 Supplementation Between 6 and 30 Months of Age and Cardiometabolic Risk Markers After 6–7 Years* — homocysteine, leptin, adiponectin outcomes, n=791.

**Norway (Torsvik/Ueland trials)**
- Torsvik I et al. (2013). *Cobalamin supplementation improves motor development and regurgitations in infants: results from a randomized intervention study.* Am J Clin Nutr 98:1233–1240.
- Torsvik IK et al. (2015). *Motor development related to duration of exclusive breastfeeding, B vitamin status and B12 supplementation in infants with a birth weight between 2000–3000 g, results from a randomized intervention trial.* BMC Pediatr 15:218.

**Nigeria**
- *Vitamin B Supplementation and Homocysteine Reduction in Nigerian Children with Nephrotic Syndrome: A Randomized Controlled Trial* (PMC12129721). — Single-blind RCT, 48 children, folic acid + B6 + B12 vs placebo × 6 mo; homocysteine as primary outcome.

**Uganda**
- Ndeezi G et al. (2011). *Multiple micronutrient supplementation improves vitamin B12 and folate concentrations of HIV infected children in Uganda: a randomized controlled trial.* Nutr J 10:56. — Multi-micronutrient (includes B12) vs standard care in HIV-infected children.

A few notes for your synthesis:
- Several of these share the same parent trial (Nepal Bhaktapur; North India Delhi) — treat as linked reports rather than independent studies to avoid double-counting in a meta-analysis.
- The Nigerian and Ugandan trials give B12 only as part of a combination (with folic acid/B6, or multi-micronutrient), so they may only partly meet a strict "B12 alone vs. same-without-B12" comparator, depending on your inclusion criteria.
- I did not find additional isolated-B12 RCTs in East Asia, Latin America, or high-income countries outside Norway during this search — if useful I can run more targeted searches by region or by outcome (e.g., only cognition, only anemia).
