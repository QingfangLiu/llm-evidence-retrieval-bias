Here are individual primary studies (RCTs) on vitamin B12 supplementation and child nutrition/health outcomes in children under 12. I've excluded the Cochrane review, narrative reviews, and protocol-only papers where a corresponding results paper exists.

## Growth, neurodevelopment, and cognition trials

**1. Strand et al. 2015 — Pediatrics**
"Vitamin B-12, folic acid, and growth in 6- to 30-month-old children: a randomized controlled trial." Factorial RCT (N≈1000), New Delhi, India — vitamin B12, folic acid, both, or placebo daily for 6 months, growth outcomes measured.

**2. Kvestad/Taneja et al. 2015 — PLOS ONE**
Randomized, double-blind trial in children aged six to 30 months, receiving placebo or vitamin B12 and/or folic acid for six months in a 1:1:1:1 factorial design. Children who received both vitamin B12 and folic acid showed higher scores in gross motor and problem-solving domains than placebo; vitamin B12 alone significantly improved gross motor scores. (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT00717730)

**3. Strand et al. 2020 — PLOS Medicine**
Community-based, individually randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in Bhaktapur, Nepal enrolling 600 marginally stunted 6–11-month-old infants, randomized 1:1 to 2 μg vitamin B12 or placebo daily for 12 months. Vitamin B12 supplementation improved metabolic response but did not affect neurodevelopment, growth, or hemoglobin concentration.

**4. Kvestad et al. — British Journal of Nutrition (follow-up)**
Follow-up of the above Nepal RCT examining neurodevelopmental effects 1–2 years after intervention ended.

**5. Kvestad, Taneja, Upadhyay et al. 2020 — Pediatrics**
"Vitamin B12, folate, and cognition in 6- to 9-year-olds: a randomized controlled trial" — longer-term follow-up cognition results from the North Indian cohort.

**6. Strand/Taneja et al. — 6-year follow-up study, North India**
Follow-up study of a factorial, double-blind, RCT in 1000 young children who received folic acid, vitamin B12, both, or placebo daily for 6 months starting at 6–30 months; height measured in 791 children six years later, showing a small, non-significant effect of vitamin B12 on linear growth.

**7. Chandyo et al. 2017 — BMJ Open (protocol, but tied to completed trial with published outcomes)**
"Effects of vitamin B12 supplementation in pregnancy and postpartum on growth and neurodevelopment in early childhood" — Nepal RCT.

**8. Srinivasan, Thomas, Kapanee et al. 2017 — Maternal & Child Nutrition**
Randomized controlled clinical trial examining effects of oral maternal vitamin B12 supplementation during pregnancy and early lactation on cognitive development in children, with children of mothers who received B12 scoring significantly higher on expressive language.

## Hemoglobin/anemia and biochemical status trials

**9. Kumar/Taneja/Strand et al. 2016 — Clinical Nutrition**
Randomized placebo-controlled trial in New Delhi, India; children randomized into four groups (vitamin B12, folic acid, both, or placebo) for 6 months. Supplementation of either or both vitamins did not improve hemoglobin concentration in young children 6–36 months of age.

**10. Kurpad/Yajnik et al. — Nutrient bar and yogurt trials (medRxiv/J. Nutr)**
Two double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomized, directly-observed-therapy trials; the nutrient bar trial was conducted in a village school in Pune, India in children aged 10–13 years, testing B12-fortified nutrient bar versus placebo. B12-fortified bars significantly increased plasma B12 concentrations above baseline compared with placebo, and homocysteine fell significantly with B12 supplementation; B12-fortified foods were effective in improving B12 status in Indian children.

**11. Taneja et al. 2013 — American Journal of Clinical Nutrition**
"Folic acid and vitamin B-12 supplementation and common infections in 6–30-mo-old children in India: a randomized placebo-controlled trial" — same North Indian cohort, infection outcomes.

## Other pediatric conditions

**12. Nigerian Nephrotic Syndrome trial (2024/2025) — journal TBD**
Single-blind RCT at University College Hospital, Ibadan, Nigeria; 48 children with nephrotic syndrome and elevated homocysteine randomized to daily folic acid, vitamin B6, and vitamin B12 versus placebo for six months, showing significant homocysteine reduction in the intervention group.

## Cardiometabolic follow-up

**13. Taneja et al. 2023 — Journal of Nutrition**
Follow-up study of a 2×2 factorial, double-blind RCT of vitamin B12 and/or folic acid supplementation in 6–30-month-old children in India, reassessed after 6–7 years for cardiometabolic risk markers (homocysteine, adiponectin, leptin) in 791 children.
