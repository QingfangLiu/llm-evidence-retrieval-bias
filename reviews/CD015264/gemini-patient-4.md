If you are considering whether a vitamin B12 supplement will genuinely benefit your child's health and development, looking at randomized controlled trials (the gold standard of primary research) provides the clearest picture. By isolating primary studies that directly compare vitamin B12 supplementation against a placebo in young children, the data shows that while supplements successfully correct nutritional deficiencies in the blood, the outward physical and developmental benefits are mixed and heavily dependent on the child's baseline health.

Here is what the primary trial data shows across different health domains:

### Nutritional Status and Metabolism

Primary studies consistently confirm that vitamin B12 supplementation reliably improves a child's internal metabolic profile. For example, a large population-based trial in Nepal involving 600 marginally stunted infants found that one year of daily B12 supplementation significantly improved the children's B12 nutritional status. The supplements successfully lowered plasma markers of deficiency, such as total homocysteine and methylmalonic acid, compared to a placebo (Strand et al., 2020).

### Physical Growth and Anemia

While B12 is often promoted for physical growth and preventing anemia, trials do not consistently support these outward benefits in children who receive supplements. In the same Nepalese trial, despite the clear improvements in their internal metabolic profiles, the children receiving B12 showed no significant differences in physical growth or hemoglobin concentrations (markers for anemia) compared to the placebo group (Strand et al., 2020).

### Neurodevelopment and Cognitive Skills

The impact of B12 on a child's brain development seems to depend on whether it is paired with other nutrients and the child's starting nutritional status:

* **Mixed Results for B12 Alone:** A follow-up study on the Nepalese children assessed their cognitive function when they reached 30 to 47 months of age. The researchers found that the infants who had received a year of B12 supplementation did not show any improvement in preschool cognitive function (such as intelligence quotients) compared to those who took a placebo (Ulak et al., 2022).
* **Benefits When Combined with Folate:** Conversely, a trial in North Indian children aged 6 to 30 months tested B12 alone, folic acid alone, and a combination of both against a placebo. The children who received *both* vitamin B12 and folic acid demonstrated significant improvements in gross motor skills and problem-solving compared to the placebo group (Kvestad et al., 2015). B12 alone provided a small boost to gross motor scores, but it had no effect on other developmental outcomes without the addition of folic acid (Kvestad et al., 2015). These benefits were also most pronounced in children who were already stunted or highly deficient to begin with.

### Specific Health Conditions (Autism Spectrum Disorder)

There is also primary research looking at targeted high doses of B12 for specific neurological conditions. An 8-week trial investigated subcutaneous methyl B12 injections in children with autism spectrum disorder. The study found that clinician-rated symptoms of autism improved significantly more in the B12 group compared to the placebo group, and these improvements correlated directly with cellular methylation changes in their blood (Hendren et al., 2016). However, parent-rated behavioral checklists did not show a difference between the two groups (Hendren et al., 2016).

If your child has a known deficiency or a restricted diet (such as a strict vegan diet), the evidence shows B12 supplements will successfully correct their metabolic numbers. However, if your child is generally well-nourished, the primary clinical evidence does not strongly suggest that extra B12 alone will significantly boost their growth or cognitive development.
