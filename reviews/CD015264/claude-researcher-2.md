Based on my searches, here are individual primary studies (RCTs and other controlled intervention studies) on vitamin B12 supplementation in children under 12 — excluding systematic reviews, meta-analyses, narrative reviews, guidelines, and editorials. I've grouped them by focus area.

## Direct oral B12 supplementation trials (child as recipient)

**Nepal cohort (Strand, Ulak, Chandyo et al., Bhaktapur)**
- Strand and colleagues conducted a community-based, individually randomized, double-blind placebo-controlled trial enrolling 600 marginally stunted, 6- to 11-month-old infants, randomized 1:1 to 2 μg vitamin B12 or placebo daily for 12 months, both groups also receiving 15 other vitamins/minerals, with primary outcomes of neurodevelopment (Bayley-III), growth, and hemoglobin, finding improved metabolic B12 status but no effect on neurodevelopment, growth, or hemoglobin (*PLOS Medicine*, 2020). Study protocol published separately (*Trials*, 2017).
- A follow-up of this same 600-child Nepalese RCT found no effect of 2 µg vitamin B12 daily for a year on neurodevelopment at 1- and 2-year post-intervention follow-ups (*British Journal of Nutrition*, 2022).
- Chandyo, Kvestad, Ulak et al. randomised 800 pregnant Nepalese women (1:1) to 50 μg oral vitamin B12 or placebo daily from early pregnancy through 6 months postpartum, with primary outcomes of infant length-for-age z-scores at 12 months and Bayley-III cognitive scores (*The Lancet*, 2023) — note: intervention is maternal, but outcomes are measured in the infant.
- An ongoing Norwegian RCT is collecting baseline B12 status data in infants aged 6–15 weeks ahead of a screening-vs-control randomization (*American Journal of Clinical Nutrition*, 2025) — baseline/cross-sectional report published; trial itself still in progress.

**North India cohort (Taneja, Kvestad, Strand et al., New Delhi)**
- Kvestad, Taneja, Kumar et al. ran a randomized, double-blind, factorial (1:1:1:1) trial in children aged 6–30 months given placebo, vitamin B12, folic acid, or both for 6 months, measuring development via Ages and Stages Questionnaire-3 in 422 children; combined B12+folic acid produced higher gross motor and problem-solving scores, and B12 alone significantly improved gross motor scores (*PLOS ONE*, 2015).
- In the same completed New Delhi trial, growth was a predefined secondary outcome: vitamin B12 combined with folic acid increased mean weight by 120 g and length by 0.29 cm versus placebo (referenced in a related protocol document; original result reported in Strand et al., *Pediatrics* 2015, "Vitamin B12, folic acid, and growth in 6- to 30-month-old children: a randomized controlled trial").
- Taneja, Chowdhury, Kvestad, Bhandari, Strand followed up the same factorial trial 6 years later, measuring height in 791 of the original 1000 children; there was a small, non-significant effect of vitamin B12 on linear growth and no effect of folic acid, with a significant subgroup effect in children with baseline plasma cobalamin <200 pmol/L (*British Journal of Nutrition*, 2023).

**Vitamin B12 deficiency/megaloblastic anemia treatment trials**
- A study comparing parenteral versus oral vitamin B12 treatment in children with serum cobalamin <300 pg/mL found significant post-treatment increases in vitamin B12 in both arms, with anemia persisting in a similar proportion of children in each group at 1 month (referencing an earlier trial by Chandelia et al., who randomized 150 children with nutritional anemia to iron/folic acid versus iron/folic acid/cobalamin) (*Hematology*, 2018).
- A prospective, randomized comparative study in children aged 1–15 years with confirmed vitamin B12 deficiency megaloblastic anemia compared sublingual versus intramuscular B12 for 12 weeks (2024/2025, PMC).

**Vitamin B12 in specific pediatric conditions**
- Orimadegun, Ademola, Asinobi conducted a single-blind RCT at University College Hospital, Ibadan, Nigeria, randomizing 48 children with nephrotic syndrome and elevated homocysteine to daily folic acid (5 mg) + vitamin B6 (50 mg) + vitamin B12 (1 mg) versus placebo for six months, finding a significant homocysteine reduction in the intervention group (*Journal of Nutrition and Health*, 2025).

## Food-based / fortification trials with B12 as a component (not a pure supplement, but relevant primary intervention studies)

- The Child Nutrition Project (Kenya) compared school snacks of githeri (vegetable stew) alone, githeri+milk, or githeri+meat against a no-snack control in rural Kenyan schoolchildren, finding vitamin B-12, riboflavin, vitamin A and calcium intakes rose more in Milk and Meat groups than Control (Neumann et al., *Journal of Nutrition*, 2003).
- A related cluster-randomised feeding trial from the same project assigned 12 primary schools to githeri+meat, githeri+milk, githeri+oil, or control, examining effects on standardized test scores and nutrient intake (Hulett et al., *British Journal of Nutrition*, 2014).
- A further analysis from this same randomised, controlled feeding trial (two cohorts, 518 and 392 schoolchildren) examined effects of meat/milk/githeri feeding on arm muscle area, noting B12 intake from animal-source foods predicted gains (*British Journal of Nutrition*, 2012).

## Study protocols (trial not yet reporting primary results, or ongoing)
- MATCOBIND: a double-blind, multicentre RCT enrolling 720 vegetarian pregnant women in India and Nepal, randomised to 250 mcg vs 50 mcg methylcobalamin daily through 6 months postpartum, with primary outcome being infant neurodevelopment at 9 months (protocol, *BMJ Open*, 2020) — maternal intervention, infant outcome.
