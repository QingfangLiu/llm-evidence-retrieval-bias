It is completely natural to want to ensure your child is getting optimal nutrition for their development. When looking specifically at primary, randomized placebo-controlled trials testing vitamin B12 supplementation in children, the evidence presents a mixed picture.

Most high-quality primary studies that directly compare B12 supplements to a placebo focus on infants and toddlers in populations at a higher risk of deficiency. Here is a summary of the key individual trials:

## Studies Showing Positive Developmental Effects

**North Indian Toddler Trial (Kvestad et al., 2015)**
In a randomized, double-blind trial, 422 children (aged 6 to 30 months) were given either a placebo, vitamin B12 alone, folic acid alone, or a combination of both for six months.

* **Findings:** The researchers found that children receiving vitamin B12 alone showed a significant improvement in gross motor scores compared to the placebo group. Children who received both vitamin B12 and folic acid demonstrated significant improvements in both gross motor functioning and problem-solving skills (Kvestad et al., 2015).

## Studies Showing No Developmental or Growth Effects

**Nepalese Infant Trial (Strand et al., 2020)**
This large, double-blind randomized controlled trial followed 600 infants (aged 6 to 11 months). The children received either 2 µg of vitamin B12 or a placebo daily for one year. (Both groups also received a baseline of 15 other vitamins and minerals).

* **Findings:** The vitamin B12 supplement successfully improved the children's metabolic markers (proving the supplement was absorbed and improved their internal B12 status). However, the supplementation had **no effect** on their neurodevelopment, physical growth, or hemoglobin concentrations compared to the placebo group (Strand et al., 2020).

**Nepalese Trial Preschool Follow-Up (Ulak et al., 2022)**
Researchers followed up with the same group of children from the Strand et al. (2020) study when they reached 30 to 35 months of age to see if there were delayed benefits to the earlier supplementation.

* **Findings:** They found that the daily vitamin B12 provided during infancy still yielded no beneficial effects on full-scale intelligence quotients (FSIQ) or any other preschool cognitive functions when compared to the children who had taken the placebo (Ulak et al., 2022).
