## What "prophylactic" vs "selective" means in these trials
- **Prophylactic (or "immediate")**: surfactant given at or shortly after birth, before signs of RDS develop, often based on gestational age/birth weight risk alone.
- **Selective (or "rescue")**: surfactant given only once the infant shows clinical/radiographic signs of RDS.

## What the older trials (surfactant vs. no surfactant era) found
The earliest trials tested prophylactic surfactant against placebo/no surfactant, not against a selective strategy, and consistently showed surfactant reduced mortality and air leaks (pneumothorax, interstitial emphysema) compared with no surfactant at all.

## What the head-to-head prophylactic-vs-selective trials found
Once these were tested directly against each other, results shifted with era and delivery method:

- **Kendig et al., NEJM 1991** (infants <30 weeks) — Prophylactic surfactant reduced mortality and pneumothorax compared with rescue therapy given after RDS was established, when both groups were intubated regardless.
- **Dunn et al., Pediatrics 1991** — In neonates <30 weeks, this trial found prophylaxis was not clearly superior to early treatment, and raised the concern that prophylaxis unavoidably treats some infants who never would have developed RDS.
- **Kattwinkel et al., Pediatrics 1993** (29–32 weeks) — Prophylactic surfactant was more effective than early treatment at reducing RDS severity in this narrower gestational-age band.
- **Kendig et al., Pediatrics 1998** — Compared strategies of prophylaxis itself, still generally favoring very early dosing.
- **Once nasal CPAP (nCPAP) became standard initial support, the calculus changed**: trials like **Sandri/CURPAP, Pediatrics 2010**, comparing prophylactic surfactant + nCPAP against nCPAP with early selective surfactant, found **no benefit of prophylactic surfactant** — rates of mechanical ventilation, death, and major morbidities were statistically similar between groups. Similarly, **Rojas et al., Pediatrics 2009** found very early selective surfactant with nCPAP could be as effective without giving surfactant to every infant.

## Net picture
In infants who are electively intubated at birth, prophylactic surfactant shows a mortality/air-leak advantage over delayed rescue treatment in older trials. But once nCPAP is used as first-line respiratory support (current standard practice in many NICUs), giving surfactant only selectively — to those infants who actually develop signs of RDS — appears to produce **similar outcomes with less unnecessary intubation and surfactant exposure**, which is why selective/early-rescue strategies combined with nCPAP became more favored in many units by the 2010s.

Because trial design (era, gestational age, respiratory support strategy) meaningfully changes the answer, it's worth discussing with your baby's neonatologist which body of evidence applies to your specific gestational age and planned respiratory support strategy.

## Primary studies (individual RCTs/clinical trials, not reviews)

1. **Kendig JW, Notter RH, Cox C, et al.** A comparison of surfactant as immediate prophylaxis and as rescue therapy in newborns of less than 30 weeks' gestation. *N Engl J Med.* 1991;324(13):865-871.

2. **Dunn MS, Shennan AT, Zayack D, Possmayer F.** Bovine surfactant replacement therapy in neonates of less than 30 weeks' gestation: a randomized controlled trial of prophylaxis versus treatment. *Pediatrics.* 1991;87(3):377-386.

3. **Kattwinkel J, Bloom BT, Delmore P, et al.** Prophylactic administration of calf lung surfactant extract is more effective than early treatment of respiratory distress syndrome in neonates of 29 through 32 weeks' gestation. *Pediatrics.* 1993;92(1):90-98.

4. **Kendig JW, Ryan RM, Sinkin RA, et al.** Comparison of two strategies for surfactant prophylaxis in very premature infants: a multicenter randomized trial. *Pediatrics.* 1998;101(6):1006-1012.

5. **Bevilacqua G, Parmigiani S, Robertson B, et al.** Prophylaxis of respiratory distress syndrome by treatment with modified porcine surfactant at birth: a multicentre prospective randomized trial. *J Perinat Med.* 1996;24:1-12.

6. **Sandri F, Plavka R, Ancora G, et al; CURPAP Study Group.** Prophylactic or early selective surfactant combined with nCPAP in very preterm infants. *Pediatrics.* 2010;125(6):e1402-e1409.

7. **Rojas MA, Lozano JM, Rojas MX, et al; Colombian Neonatal Research Network.** Very early surfactant without mandatory ventilation in premature infants treated with early continuous positive airway pressure: a randomized, controlled trial. *Pediatrics.* 2009;123(1):137-142.

8. **Dunn MS, Kaempf J, de Klerk A, et al; Vermont Oxford Network DRM Study Group.** Randomized trial comparing 3 approaches to the initial respiratory management of preterm neonates. *Pediatrics.* 2011;128(5):e1069-e1076.

Note that trials 1–5 were conducted in an era when infants were routinely intubated; trials 6–8 reflect the more current context where nasal CPAP is used as initial respiratory support, which is why their conclusions differ.
