## Overview of the evidence base

The trials fall into two broad eras that answer somewhat different questions, and it's worth keeping them separate:

**Older era (1985–1995, pre-routine antenatal steroids, pre-widespread CPAP):** These trials randomized infants at high risk of RDS (by gestational age) to either surfactant given in the delivery room/first minutes of life before RDS was clinically confirmed ("prophylactic") versus surfactant given only after the infant was intubated and RDS was established ("rescue"/selective). Most infants in both arms ended up mechanically ventilated regardless of allocation.

**Modern era (2000s–2010s, routine antenatal steroids, CPAP-based initial stabilization):** These trials compare prophylactic intubation-surfactant-extubation against a strategy of starting all infants on CPAP and giving surfactant selectively only if they fail CPAP. This is the comparison most relevant to current practice.

### Benefits reported for prophylactic surfactant (older trials)
- Reduced neonatal mortality and reduced pulmonary air leak (pneumothorax, pulmonary interstitial emphysema) compared with rescue treatment in several of the classic trials (e.g., Kendig 1991, Merritt 1991, Egberts 1993, Kattwinkel 1993).
- Some trials (e.g., Kendig 1991) also reported lower rates of intraventricular hemorrhage.

### Harms/downsides reported for prophylactic surfactant
- Unnecessary intubation and surfactant exposure in infants who would not have gone on to need it (roughly half of extremely preterm infants supported on CPAP alone avoid intubation entirely in the modern trials).
- In the CPAP-era trials (Rojas 2009, Sandri/CURPAP 2010, Dunn 2011), prophylactic or very-early intubation-surfactant strategies did **not** show a mortality or BPD advantage over starting with CPAP and treating selectively; some analyses (reflected across these trials, and highlighted in the Verder 1994 and later CPAP-comparison literature) suggest selective/early-rescue strategies combined with CPAP reduce the composite outcome of death or bronchopulmonary dysplasia compared with more aggressive early intubation-based approaches.
- Complications tied to intubation itself (procedural instability, need for sedation/paralysis in some protocols) are avoided when surfactant is deferred and CPAP is used first.

### Bottom line the trials point to
The very old prophylactic-vs-strict-rescue trials (pre-CPAP era) favored prophylaxis, largely because "rescue" in that era meant waiting until severe RDS had already caused lung injury before intubating. Once CPAP became standard initial support and antenatal steroids became routine, later trials comparing prophylactic surfactant to a CPAP-first/early-selective-surfactant strategy generally favored the CPAP-first approach or showed no benefit to prophylaxis — which is why current practice has shifted toward CPAP with selective/early-rescue surfactant rather than routine prophylaxis for most infants.

## Primary studies

**Older-era trials (prophylactic vs. rescue, pre-CPAP protocols)**
1. Kendig JW, Notter RH, Cox C, et al. A comparison of surfactant as immediate prophylaxis and as rescue therapy in newborns of less than 30 weeks' gestation. *N Engl J Med*. 1991;324(13):865-871.
2. Merritt TA, Hallman M, Berry C, et al. Randomized, placebo-controlled trial of human surfactant given at birth versus rescue administration in very low birth weight infants with lung immaturity. *J Pediatr*. 1991;118(4 Pt 1):581-594.
3. Dunn MS, Shennan AT, Zayack D, Possmayer F. Bovine surfactant replacement therapy in neonates of less than 30 weeks' gestation: a randomized controlled trial of prophylaxis versus treatment. *Pediatrics*. 1991;87(3):377-386.
4. Egberts J, de Winter JP, Sedin G, et al. Comparison of prophylaxis and rescue treatment with Curosurf in neonates less than 30 weeks' gestation: a randomized trial. *Pediatrics*. 1993;92(6):768-774.
5. Kattwinkel J, Bloom BT, Delmore P, et al. Prophylactic administration of calf lung surfactant extract is more effective than early treatment of respiratory distress syndrome in neonates of 29 through 32 weeks' gestation. *Pediatrics*. 1993;92(1):90-98.
6. Walti H, Paris-Llado J, Egberts J, et al. (French Collaborative Multicentre Study Group). Porcine surfactant replacement therapy in newborns of 25-31 weeks' gestation: a randomized, multicentre trial of prophylaxis versus rescue with multiple low doses. *Acta Paediatr*. 1995;84(8):913-921.
7. OSIRIS Collaborative Group. Early versus delayed neonatal administration of a synthetic surfactant — the judgment of OSIRIS. *Lancet*. 1992;340(8832):1363-1369.
8. Gortner L, Wauer RR, Hammer H, et al. Early versus late surfactant treatment in preterm infants of 27 to 32 weeks' gestational age: a multicenter controlled clinical trial. *Pediatrics*. 1998;102(5):1153-1160.

**Modern-era trials (prophylactic surfactant vs. CPAP with early/selective surfactant)**
9. Verder H, Robertson B, Greisen G, et al. (Danish-Swedish Multicenter Study Group). Surfactant therapy and nasal continuous positive airway pressure for newborns with respiratory distress syndrome. *N Engl J Med*. 1994;331(16):1051-1055.
10. Rojas MA, Lozano JM, Rojas MX, et al. Very early surfactant without mandatory ventilation in premature infants treated with early continuous positive airway pressure: a randomized, controlled trial. *Pediatrics*. 2009;123(1):137-142.
