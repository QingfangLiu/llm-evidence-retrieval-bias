Here's an overview of the primary literature comparing prophylactic vs. selective (rescue) surfactant administration in preterm infants at risk of RDS, followed by a full list of the individual trials identified.

## Summary of the evidence base

The core evidence comes from a set of randomized (and a few quasi-randomized) controlled trials conducted mostly in the late 1980s through 1990s, comparing intubation and surfactant administration shortly after birth (before signs of RDS) against waiting for clinical/radiographic RDS to develop before treating. A second, more recent generation of trials (SUPPORT 2010, Dunn 2011, Sandri 2010) re-examined this question in the context of widespread CPAP use, since CPAP-based stabilization changed how "selective" treatment is applied in practice.

Findings across studies were not fully consistent, which is exactly why this is a good synthesis question:
- Earlier trials suggested that infants intubated and treated with prophylactic surfactant had improved clinical outcomes, with initial improvement in respiratory status and decreased incidence of RDS in infants receiving prophylactic surfactant, along with decreases in pneumothorax, pulmonary interstitial emphysema, mortality, and bronchopulmonary dysplasia or death.
- More recent trials, which reflect current practice combining early CPAP with selective surfactant, suggest that stabilization on CPAP with surfactant treatment reserved for infants who develop breathing problems may be more effective than the more aggressive prophylactic approach, and that prophylactic surfactant may not improve outcomes and could increase risk of lung injury or death when compared with early CPAP-based stabilization.
- One retrospective comparison in extremely preterm infants (23–26 weeks) found prophylactic surfactant significantly decreased mechanical ventilation duration (74 vs. 171 hours), and non-significant trends toward lower rates of interstitial emphysema, pneumothorax, and chronic lung disease — though this particular study is retrospective/observational, not a primary RCT, so it wouldn't meet your inclusion criteria as a primary trial (flagging this for your screening).
- Related but distinct is the "click test"-guided prophylactic vs. early selective comparison (Osborn 2000), and comparisons of *early* vs. *delayed* selective treatment once RDS is established — a related but separate question from prophylactic-vs-selective.
- One relevant multi-center comparison found no RCTs directly compare prophylactic treatment to early (<2–3 h) selective treatment; only observational cohort studies exist for that specific comparison, so be cautious that some "prophylactic vs. selective" literature is observational rather than randomized (as noted in your exclusion criteria, I've flagged these below).

## Primary studies identified

**Randomized/quasi-randomized controlled trials of prophylactic vs. selective (rescue) surfactant:**

1. **Dunn MS, Shennan AT, Zayack D, Possmayer F.** Bovine surfactant replacement therapy in neonates of less than 30 weeks' gestation: a randomized controlled trial of prophylaxis vs treatment. *Pediatrics* 1991;87:377–386.

2. **Kendig JW, Notter RH, Cox C, Reubens LJ, Davis JM, Maniscalco WM.** A comparison of surfactant as immediate prophylaxis and as rescue therapy in newborns of less than 30 weeks' gestation. (1991)

3. **Merritt TA** et al. (1991 prophylactic vs. rescue trial — part of the Cochrane-included trial set)

4. **Egberts J, de Winter JP, Sedin G, Kleine MJK, Broberger U, Bel F, et al.** Comparison of prophylaxis and rescue treatment with Curosurf in neonates less than 30 weeks' gestation: a randomized trial. *Pediatrics* 1993;92:768–774.

5. **Kattwinkel J, Bloom BT, Delmore P, Davis CL, Farrell E, Friss H, et al.** Prophylactic administration of calf lung surfactant extract is more effective than early treatment of respiratory distress syndrome in neonates of 29 through 32 weeks' gestation. *Pediatrics* 1993;92:90–98.

6. **Walti H, Paris-Llado J, Bréart G, Couchard M** (French Collaborative Multicentre Study Group). Porcine surfactant replacement therapy in newborns of 25–31 weeks' gestation: a randomized, multicentre trial of prophylaxis versus rescue with multiple low doses. *Acta Paediatr* 1995;84:913–921.

7. **Bevilacqua G, Parmigiani S, Robertson B.** Prophylaxis of respiratory distress syndrome by treatment with modified porcine surfactant at birth: a multicentre prospective randomized trial. *J Perinat Med* 1996;24:1–12.

8. **Bevilacqua G, Chernev T, Parmigiani S, Yarakova N, Gaioni L, Volante E, et al.** Use of surfactant for prophylaxis versus rescue treatment of respiratory distress syndrome: experience from an Italian-Bulgarian trial. (1997)

9. **Iarŭkova N, Vakrilova L, Slŭncheva B, Dancheva S, Popivanova A, Emilova Z, et al.** Administration of exogenous surfactant in very low birth weight infants with RDS. *Akuserstvo i Ginekologija* 1999;38:23–26.

10. **Osborn DA.** Study of prophylactic surfactant versus early selective surfactant using the "click test" to determine need for therapy for RDS. (2000)

11. **SUPPORT Study Group of the Eunice Kennedy Shriver NICHD Neonatal Research Network.** Early CPAP versus surfactant in extremely preterm infants. *N Engl J Med* 2010;362:1970–1979.

12. **Sandri F, Plavka R, Ancora G, et al.** Prophylactic or early selective surfactant combined with nCPAP in very preterm infants. *Pediatrics* 2010;125(6):e1402–e1409.

13. **Dunn MS, Kaempf J, de Klerk A, de Klerk R, Reilly M, Howard D, et al.** (Vermont Oxford Network DRM Study Group). Randomized trial comparing 3 approaches to the initial respiratory management of preterm neonates. *Pediatrics* 2011;128(5):e1069–e1076.

**Note on studies to exclude from your primary-study set (flagging, not including as RCTs):**
- The retrospective cohort comparing prophylactic (N=31) vs. selective (N=34) curosurf in 23–26 week infants (PMC2951078) is observational, not randomized — exclude if you require RCT design.
- The Korean 53-center collaborative study (early prophylactic vs. late selective, 2010 vs. 2011 cohorts) is a retrospective multi-center comparison, not a randomized trial — exclude on the same grounds.

A few of these older trials (items 3, 8, 10) I could only verify by title/author from citation lists rather than full independent records — worth double-checking exact journal/year details in PubMed/Cochrane's reference list directly before finalizing your evidence table.
