Here are the individual primary studies (randomized/quasi-randomized controlled trials) comparing prophylactic vs. selective (rescue) surfactant administration in preterm infants at risk of RDS. I pulled this list primarily from the reference list of the Cochrane review on this topic (Rojas-Reyes 2012), which is a good index of the primary trial literature, but every item below is a primary study itself — not the review.

## Core trials directly comparing prophylactic vs. selective/rescue surfactant

1. **Bevilacqua G, Parmigiani S, Robertson B, et al. (1996).** Prophylaxis of respiratory distress syndrome by treatment with modified porcine surfactant at birth: a multicentre prospective randomized trial. *Journal of Perinatal Medicine*, 24:1–12. PMID: 9120744

2. **Bevilacqua G, Chernev T, Parmigiani S, et al. (1997).** Use of surfactant for prophylaxis versus rescue treatment of respiratory distress syndrome: experience from an Italian-Bulgarian trial. *Acta Biomedica Ateneo Parmense*, 68(Suppl 1):47–54. PMID: 10021717

3. **Dunn MS, Shennan AT, Zyack D, Possmayer F (1991).** Bovine surfactant replacement therapy in neonates of less than 30 weeks' gestation: a randomized controlled trial of prophylaxis vs treatment. *Pediatrics*, 87:377–386. PMID: 2000278

4. **Dunn MS, Kaempf J, de Klerk A, et al., for the Vermont Oxford Network DRM Study Group (2011).** Randomized trial comparing 3 approaches to the initial respiratory management of preterm neonates. *Pediatrics*, 128:e1069–e1076. PMID: 22025591

5. **Egberts J, Winter JP, Sedin G, et al. (1993).** Comparison of prophylaxis and rescue treatment with Curosurf in neonates less than 30 weeks gestation: a randomized trial. *Pediatrics*, 92:768–774. PMID: 8233735

6. **Iarŭkova N, Vakrilova L, Slŭncheva B, et al. (1999).** Administration of exogenous surfactant in very low birth weight infants with RDS. *Akusherstvo i Ginekologija*, 38:23–26. PMID: 11965714

7. **Kattwinkel J, Bloom BT, Delmore P, et al. (1993).** Prophylactic administration of calf lung surfactant extract is more effective than early treatment of respiratory distress syndrome in neonates of 29 through 32 weeks' gestation. *Pediatrics*, 92:90–98. PMID: 8516091

8. **Kendig JW, Notter RH, Cox C, et al. (1991).** A comparison of surfactant as immediate prophylaxis and as rescue therapy in newborns of less than 30 weeks' gestation. *New England Journal of Medicine*, 324:865–871. PMID: 2000109
   - Long-term follow-up: **Sinkin RA, Kramer BM, Merzbach JL, et al. (1998).** School-age follow-up of prophylactic versus rescue surfactant trial: pulmonary, neurodevelopmental, and educational outcomes. *Pediatrics*, 101:E11. PMID: 9565444

9. **Merritt TA, Hallman M, Berry C, et al. (1991).** Randomized, placebo-controlled trial of human surfactant given at birth vs rescue administration in very low birthweight infants with lung immaturity. *Journal of Pediatrics*, 118:581–594. PMID: 2007937
   - Follow-up: **Vaucher YE, Harker L, Merritt TA, et al. (1993).** Outcome at twelve months of adjusted age in very low birth weight infants with lung immaturity. *Journal of Pediatrics*, 122:126–132. PMID: 8419599

10. **Walti H, Paris-Llado J, Breart G, Couchard M, French Collaborative Multicentre Study Group (1995).** Porcine surfactant replacement therapy in newborns of 25–31 weeks' gestation: a randomized multicentre trial of prophylaxis versus rescue with multiple low doses. *Acta Paediatrica*, 84:913–921. PMID: 7488817

## Trials on prophylactic/early surfactant in the context of routine CPAP stabilization (later generation of trials, relevant to modern practice)

11. **SUPPORT Study Group of the Eunice Kennedy Shriver NICHD Neonatal Research Network (2010).** Early CPAP versus surfactant in extremely preterm infants. *New England Journal of Medicine*, 362:1970–1979. PMID: 20472939

12. **Sandri F, Plavka R, Ancora G, et al., CURPAP Study Group (2010).** Prophylactic or early selective surfactant combined with nCPAP in very preterm infants. *Pediatrics*, 125:e1402–e1409. PMID: 20439601

13. **Rojas MA, Lozano JM, Rojas MX, et al. (2009).** Very early surfactant without mandatory ventilation in premature infants treated with early continuous positive airway pressure: a randomized, controlled trial. *Pediatrics*, 123:137–142. PMID: 19117872

14. **Morley CJ, Davis PG, Doyle LW, et al., COIN Trial Investigators (2008).** Nasal CPAP or intubation at birth for very preterm infants. *New England Journal of Medicine*, 358:700–708. PMID: 18272893 (Not a surfactant-timing trial per se, but closely related — CPAP vs. intubation/prophylactic-type management — frequently discussed alongside the prophylactic-vs-selective literature.)

## Additional retrospective/observational comparative studies (not RCTs, flag if you want RCTs only)

- **Early prophylactic versus late selective use of surfactant... a collaborative study of 53 multi-center trials in Korea** (2014). PMID: 25120324 — this is a retrospective multicenter cohort comparison, not a randomized trial; included here only in case observational comparative data is in scope, but exclude if you're restricting to RCTs.

A note on scope: items 1–10 are the "classic" trials predating routine CPAP stabilization at birth; items 11–13 reflect the more recent generation of trials conducted in an era of routine antenatal steroids and CPAP, which is why later evidence syntheses (e.g., Rojas-Reyes 2012) found effect modification by era/CPAP use. You may want to stratify your synthesis along that line, since pooling both eras washed out the mortality/air-leak benefit seen in the earlier trials.
