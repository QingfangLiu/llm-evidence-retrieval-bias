Here's what the primary-study evidence shows on prophylactic vs. selective surfactant, based only on individual trials (not the Cochrane reviews, guidelines, or narrative articles that came up in search).

## The two eras of evidence

The trials cluster into two groups, and this matters for interpreting the results: **older trials** (mostly 1991–1999) were done before routine post-birth stabilization on CPAP was standard, so "selective" treatment in those trials often meant an already-intubated, mechanically ventilated infant waiting for RDS to worsen. **Newer trials** (2009–2011) were done in the CPAP era, comparing prophylactic intubation-and-surfactant against stabilizing infants on CPAP first and giving surfactant only if they later needed it.

### Older trials (pre-routine-CPAP era)
These generally found prophylactic surfactant reduced pneumothorax, pulmonary interstitial emphysema, and mortality compared with waiting for RDS to become established in an intubated infant:
- **Kendig et al. 1991 (NEJM)** — immediate prophylaxis vs. rescue therapy in infants <30 weeks.
- **Dunn et al. 1991 (Pediatrics)** — three-arm trial (control, early, late surfactant) in infants <30 weeks; found no significant differences between groups in pneumothorax, pulmonary interstitial emphysema, cerebral hemorrhage, periventricular leukomalacia, patent ductus arteriosus, ventilation duration, oxygen duration, or mortality.
- **Kattwinkel et al. 1993 (Pediatrics)** — prophylactic vs. early-rescue calf lung surfactant extract in 29–32 week infants; severe RDS occurred in 19% of prophylactically treated infants vs. 32% of the rescue-eligible group.
- **Egberts et al. 1993 (Pediatrics)** — prophylaxis vs. rescue with Curosurf in infants <30 weeks.
- **Vaucher/Merritt et al. 1993 (J Pediatr)** — 12-month outcomes in a placebo-controlled human surfactant trial.
- **Walti et al. 1995 (Acta Paediatrica)** — porcine surfactant, prophylaxis vs. rescue with multiple low doses, infants 25–31 weeks.
- **Bevilacqua et al. 1996 (J Perinat Med)** — modified porcine surfactant given prophylactically at birth.
- **Bevilacqua et al. 1997** — Italian-Bulgarian trial of prophylaxis vs. rescue.
- **Iarŭkova et al. 1999** — exogenous surfactant in very-low-birth-weight infants with RDS.

### Modern trials (routine CPAP stabilization era)
These trials reflect current practice more closely, and tend to find less benefit — or even harm — from prophylactic dosing once CPAP is used as first-line stabilization:
- **Rojas et al. 2009 (Pediatrics)**, Colombian Neonatal Research Network — very early surfactant without mandatory ventilation vs. CPAP-first, in infants treated with early CPAP.
- **Sandri et al. 2010 (Pediatrics)**, the CURPAP trial — 208 inborn infants born at 25–28 weeks who were not intubated at birth were randomly assigned to prophylactic surfactant or nCPAP within 30 minutes of birth, comparing prophylactic surfactant-then-CPAP against early CPAP with early selective surfactant; the trial concluded prophylactic surfactant was not superior to nasal CPAP with early selective surfactant in reducing major morbidities.
- **SUPPORT Study Group 2010 (NEJM)** — extremely preterm infants randomized to CPAP-first stabilization vs. intubation/surfactant in the delivery room.
- **Dunn, Kaempf, de Klerk et al. 2011 (Pediatrics)**, Vermont Oxford Network — three-arm trial comparing prophylactic surfactant/rapid extubation, prophylactic surfactant/continued ventilation, and initial CPAP.

### A note on antenatal steroids
One useful pattern raised in the literature searching these trials: the modern CPAP-era trials were conducted when antenatal corticosteroid use was near-universal (90–98%), versus the low rates (often under 30%) in the older prophylaxis-vs-rescue trials, and this is proposed as a reason the modern trials show smaller (or absent) benefit from prophylactic dosing — steroid-matured lungs may need surfactant less urgently.

### One ongoing trial worth knowing about
**Göpel et al., the pro.LISA trial** (protocol published 2023, Trials journal) is a large (n=698 target) RCT specifically testing prophylactic vs. selective *less invasive* surfactant administration (LISA, a thin-catheter, non-intubation method) in 25–29 week infants on CPAP. This is highly relevant to your question but results were not yet published as of the most recent information I could find — it's a trial to watch rather than a completed answer.

One thing worth flagging as you read: these trials use different definitions of "selective/rescue" (some with routine CPAP, some without), different surfactant products and doses, and different eras of antenatal steroid use — so their results aren't directly poolable, and the practical takeaway has shifted over time as CPAP-first strategies became standard.
