Based on the recent primary clinical trials (randomized controlled trials), the consensus regarding blood pressure (BP) control after successful reperfusion therapy—whether via intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) or endovascular thrombectomy (EVT)—has shifted significantly. While intensive blood pressure reduction was historically hypothesized to be protective, recent primary data demonstrates that it offers little to no functional benefit and is associated with substantial risk of overt harm.

Here is a synthesis of the benefits and risks derived strictly from recent pivotal primary studies.

## Hypothesized Benefits of Intensive BP Lowering

The theoretical rationale for intensive blood pressure control (e.g., targeting systolic BP <120 or <140 mm Hg) immediately following reperfusion was to minimize reperfusion injury and reduce the likelihood of hemorrhagic transformation.

* **Reduction in Intracranial Hemorrhage (Post-IVT):** The ENCHANTED trial, which investigated patients receiving intravenous thrombolysis, found that intensive BP lowering (target <130-140 mm Hg) did reduce the rate of intracranial hemorrhage compared to the standard target of <180 mm Hg (Anderson et al., 2019).
* **Lack of Translation to Functional Recovery:** Despite the reduction in hemorrhage seen in ENCHANTED, this did not translate to any significant improvement in 90-day functional outcomes (Anderson et al., 2019). Furthermore, subsequent trials focused specifically on EVT, such as BP-TARGET, failed to show even a radiographic reduction in intraparenchymal hemorrhage rates when targeting intensive ranges (100–129 mm Hg) compared to standard care (Mazighi et al., 2021).

## Identified Risks and Harms

Recent primary data strongly suggests that intensive blood pressure reduction after successful clot retrieval compromises cerebral microcirculation, leading to delayed recovery or sustained disability.

* **Worse Functional Independence:** Two major phase 3 trials—ENCHANTED2/MT and OPTIMAL-BP—were both terminated early due to safety concerns and overt harm in the intensive treatment arms. The ENCHANTED2/MT trial demonstrated that an aggressive systolic target of <120 mm Hg led to a significantly higher likelihood of major disability or death at 90 days compared to a standard 140–180 mm Hg target (Yang et al., 2022). Similarly, OPTIMAL-BP found that targeting a systolic BP <140 mm Hg for 24 hours after EVT resulted in a significantly lower proportion of patients achieving functional independence at 3 months (Nam et al., 2023).
* **Increased Infarct Volume:** The BEST-II trial assessed the safety of multiple BP targets post-EVT (<140, <160, and ≤180 mm Hg). While the lower targets did not fully meet criteria for futility, researchers noted concerning trends: intensive lowering was associated with larger follow-up infarct volumes and poorer utility-weighted modified Rankin Scale scores compared to the standard, guideline-recommended targets (Mistry et al., 2023).
