Here are the individual primary studies (randomized controlled trials) comparing intensive versus standard blood pressure control following reperfusion therapy (intravenous thrombolysis and/or endovascular thrombectomy) for acute ischemic stroke — excluding reviews, meta-analyses, and guidelines.

**Trials after endovascular thrombectomy (EVT):**

- The BP-TARGET trial did not find a benefit to intensive blood pressure lowering regarding the risk of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage after successful endovascular therapy, though it was not powered to detect differences in functional outcomes.

- The OPTIMAL-BP trial randomized 306 patients to intensive blood pressure management (systolic target <140 mm Hg) versus conventional management (target 140–180 mm Hg) after successful EVT; 39.4% of the intensive group versus 54.4% of the conventional group achieved functional independence at 3 months, a significant difference favoring conventional management, and the authors concluded intensive lowering in the first 24 hours after reperfusion may be harmful.

- ENCHANTED2/MT randomized patients to a systolic blood pressure target of less than 120 mm Hg versus 140–180 mm Hg and was the first trial of intensive blood pressure lowering after mechanical thrombectomy adequately powered to detect a difference in functional outcomes.

- BEST-II was a phase 2 randomized, open-label, blinded-endpoint trial that assigned 120 patients 1:1:1 to systolic blood pressure targets of ≤180 mm Hg (control), <160 mm Hg, or <140 mm Hg, maintained for 24 hours after successful thrombectomy.

**Trial after intravenous thrombolysis:**

- The ENCHANTED trial (blood pressure arm) randomly assigned thrombolysis-eligible patients with systolic BP ≥150 mm Hg within 6 hours of stroke onset to intensive (target systolic BP 130–140 mm Hg within 1 hour) or guideline-recommended (target <180 mm Hg) blood pressure lowering over 72 hours, with functional status at 90 days as the primary outcome and any intracranial hemorrhage as the key safety outcome. The trial found that intensive blood pressure lowering reduced intracranial hemorrhage but did not improve functional status.
