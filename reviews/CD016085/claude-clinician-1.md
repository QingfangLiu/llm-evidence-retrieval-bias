Also worth checking the 2026 autoregulation-guided trial and the ENCHANTED trial for thrombolysis-only patients, since the user's question covers reperfusion therapy broadly (which includes thrombolysis, not just thrombectomy).Here's a summary of the primary randomized controlled trials on intensive versus standard blood pressure control after reperfusion therapy (thrombolysis and/or endovascular thrombectomy) for acute ischemic stroke.

## After intravenous thrombolysis

**ENCHANTED (2019)** — International RCT of ~2,196 thrombolysis-treated patients randomized to intensive systolic BP lowering (target ~130–140 mm Hg within 1 hour) versus guideline-recommended control (target <180 mm Hg). Intensive lowering reduced the risk of thrombolysis-related intracerebral hemorrhage but did not improve functional outcome, largely because the population was predominantly mild-to-moderate severity stroke.

## After endovascular thrombectomy (EVT)

**BP-TARGET (2021)** — French multicenter RCT of 324 patients after successful EVT, comparing intensive SBP control (achieved mean 128 mm Hg) versus standard control (achieved mean 138 mm Hg) over 24 hours. More intensive control was similarly safe but did not improve functional outcome compared with standard control.

**ENCHANTED2/MT (2022)** — Chinese multicenter RCT of 821 patients with persistently elevated SBP (≥140 mm Hg) after successful EVT, randomized to intensive (<120 mm Hg) versus less-intensive (140–180 mm Hg) targets sustained for 72 hours. The trial was stopped early: intensive BP lowering to a target below 120 mm Hg led to worse functional outcomes than the less intensive approach. Intensive treatment also worsened the composite outcome of death or early neurological deterioration and of major disability at 90 days, with no differences in symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage, serious adverse events, or death alone.

**OPTIMAL-BP (2023)** — South Korean multicenter RCT (19 centers) of 306 patients with successful reperfusion (mTICI ≥2b) after EVT, randomized to intensive (SBP <140 mm Hg) versus conventional (SBP 140–180 mm Hg) management for the first 24 hours. Intensive versus conventional BP management reduced functional independence at 3 months, leading to early trial termination for safety/futility concerns.

**BEST-II (2023)** — U.S. multicenter, three-arm RCT (1:1:1) randomizing patients after successful EVT to SBP targets of ≤180 mm Hg (control), <160 mm Hg, or <140 mm Hg for 24 hours, using a futility design. Neither lower SBP target differed from the standard <180 mm Hg target in utility score, and the trial did not meet any prespecified criteria for futility, though a future larger trial testing the same hypothesis was judged unlikely to give a definitive answer; a low probability of benefit from lowering SBP targets was found.

**Single-center feasibility/pilot RCT (Blood Pressure Management Following Endovascular Stroke Treatment)** — Randomized participants with persistently elevated BP after successful EVT to intensive (SBP target <140 mm Hg) versus standard (SBP <180 mm Hg) control for 48 hours, primarily testing feasibility, with exploratory neurologic and hemodynamic endpoints. BP normalized in more than half of patients after EVT even without antihypertensive treatment.

## Ongoing/registered trials (not yet reporting primary results)
- **CRISIS I** (NCT04775147)
- **DETECT** (Blood Pressure Management in Stroke Following Endovascular Treatment, NCT04484350)
- **INTENSE** (China, 2024–2026 enrollment)
- **ENCHANTED3-MT** — planned to test minimal/moderate/intensive tiers with a floor of 140 mm Hg
