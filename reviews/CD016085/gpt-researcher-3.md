## Scope

I identified **eight distinct randomized primary studies** published through **July 25, 2026**, plus one long-term follow-up report. They evaluated blood-pressure targets during or immediately after:

* Intravenous alteplase thrombolysis: **ENCHANTED**
* Successful endovascular thrombectomy: **BP-TARGET, ENCHANTED2/MT, BEST-II, OPTIMAL-BP, DETECT, IDENTIFY, and HOPE**

I excluded observational studies, protocols, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, commentaries, and editorials. The DETECT publication also contains a meta-analysis; **only its original randomized pilot-trial component is considered below**.

## Overall interpretation

### After intravenous thrombolysis

More-intensive BP lowering reduced **any intracranial hemorrhage**, but did **not improve 90-day disability**. The achieved difference in mean systolic BP was relatively small, and the reduction involved the broad “any intracranial hemorrhage” outcome rather than a demonstrated improvement in symptomatic hemorrhage or functional recovery.

### After successful thrombectomy

The evidence does **not support universally forcing systolic BP below 120–140 mm Hg**:

* Intensive fixed targets have not consistently reduced symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage.
* Targets below 120 mm Hg and below 140 mm Hg produced clinically important signals of worse functional recovery.
* Reported risks included hypotension, early neurological deterioration, malignant cerebral edema, severe disability, and persistent reduction in functional independence.
* Small feasibility and futility trials were neutral or inconclusive rather than supportive.

The 2026 **HOPE** trial is an important exception, but it did not test universal intensive lowering. It individualized BP according to reperfusion completeness—allowing or supporting higher BP after incomplete reperfusion and lowering BP after near-complete or complete reperfusion.

### Longer-term outcome

The **OPTIMAL-BP one-year extension** found that the reduction in functional independence associated with the intensive target appeared to persist. At one year, functional independence occurred in **40.5% versus 52.7%** in the intention-to-treat analysis; adjusted OR 0.59, 95% CI 0.34–1.00, with a statistically significant difference in the per-protocol analysis. Mortality and changes in mRS between 3 and 12 months did not differ. ([PubMed][9])

## Implications for evidence synthesis

The intervention should not be treated as one homogeneous exposure. At minimum, studies should be separated by:

1. **Reperfusion treatment**

   * Intravenous thrombolysis
   * Endovascular thrombectomy

2. **BP strategy**

   * Fixed very-low target: <120 or 100–129 mm Hg
   * Fixed moderate target: <130, <140, or <160 mm Hg
   * Reperfusion-guided individualized strategy

3. **Reperfusion status**

   * Confirmed successful reperfusion
   * Degree of reperfusion, particularly mTICI 2b versus 2c/3
   * Thrombolysis without documented vessel reopening

4. **Outcome type**

   * Any radiographic hemorrhage
   * Symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage
   * Ordinal mRS
   * Functional independence
   * Neurological deterioration, cerebral edema, hypotension and mortality

The fixed-target trials collectively suggest that the association between higher post-EVT BP and poor outcome seen in nonrandomized studies should not be interpreted as proof that actively forcing BP lower will improve recovery. Elevated BP may partly be a marker of stroke severity, incomplete reperfusion, impaired autoregulation or other poor-prognosis features. Aggressive lowering may reduce perfusion to tissue that remains pressure-dependent.

HOPE suggests that **matching the BP target to reperfusion completeness** may be more promising than applying the same low target to every patient. Because this is one prematurely stopped positive trial whose intervention differs materially from earlier trials, it should be analyzed separately and requires confirmation.
