## Summary

There is now a substantial body of individual randomized controlled trials (RCTs) directly addressing intensive vs. standard blood pressure (BP) control after reperfusion therapy (IV thrombolysis and/or endovascular thrombectomy) for acute ischemic stroke. The trials vary in:
- **Reperfusion modality**: post-thrombolysis (ENCHANTED) vs. post-thrombectomy (BP-TARGET, ENCHANTED2/MT, OPTIMAL-BP, BEST-II, IDENTIFY, DETECT, and the Katsanos feasibility trial)
- **BP targets compared**: e.g., SBP <120, <130, <140, <160 vs. <180 mm Hg
- **Timing/duration of intervention**: first 24–72 hours post-reperfusion

**General pattern across trials**: Several of the larger, more recent trials (ENCHANTED2/MT, OPTIMAL-BP) found that intensive BP lowering after thrombectomy was associated with **worse functional outcomes**, without a clear reduction in symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage — leading to early termination of some trials for harm/futility. Earlier or smaller trials (BP-TARGET, BEST-II) found **no significant benefit or harm** across the targets tested, though BEST-II suggested a low probability that any lower SBP target would prove beneficial in a larger trial. The post-thrombolysis ENCHANTED trial found intensive lowering reduced intracranial hemorrhage but did not improve functional outcome. IDENTIFY (2025) was stopped early for a neutral interim result once other trials reported harm.

Given that multiple relevant RCTs now exist, along with a brand-new Cochrane systematic review (2026) synthesizing exactly this question, this may be a good candidate for a broader literature-mapping exercise if you want full trial-level extraction (sample sizes, arms, precise targets, outcome definitions) — a deeper search could also help you catch any very recent (2026) trial publications or ongoing trials (CRISIS I, CLEVER, HOPE) that may have newly reported results.
