## Summary of what the trials show

Six RCTs have directly randomized patients to intensive vs. standard/conventional systolic BP (SBP) targets in the first 24–72 hours after successful thrombectomy:

- **Trials targeting very low SBP with longer time from symptom onset to recanalization (ENCHANTED2/MT, OPTIMAL-BP)** found intensive lowering was **harmful** — worse functional outcomes (less independence at 90 days) and, in ENCHANTED2/MT, a trend toward more early neurological deterioration. ENCHANTED2/MT was stopped early for safety; OPTIMAL-BP was also halted early after ENCHANTED2/MT's results emerged.
- **Trials with more modest SBP targets and shorter onset-to-recanalization times (BP-TARGET, IDENTIFY)** found intensive control was **neutral** — no clear benefit, but also no significant excess harm.
- **BEST-II**, a three-arm futility trial (SBP <140, <160, or ≤180 mmHg), did **not rule out futility** of lower targets — it suggested a low probability that more intensive control helps, without confirming harm the way the two larger efficacy trials did.
- Across trials, rates of **symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage were generally similar** between intensive and standard groups — the main signal of harm was in functional outcome (disability), not hemorrhage.
- The consistent theme across trials: **more aggressive/lower BP targets, especially achieved in patients treated later after stroke onset, tend to reduce, not improve, favorable outcomes**, likely because very low BP can compromise blood flow to still-vulnerable brain tissue even after the vessel is reopened.

A large follow-on trial (**ENCHANTED3-MT**) is underway to define an optimal intermediate target, since the field remains unsettled on the exact best number rather than the general "don't go too low" conclusion.

## Primary studies (individual RCTs)

1. **Mazighi M, Richard S, Lapergue B, et al. (BP-TARGET Investigators).** Safety and efficacy of intensive blood pressure lowering after successful endovascular therapy in acute ischaemic stroke (BP-TARGET): a multicentre, open-label, randomised controlled trial. *Lancet Neurology*. 2021;20(4):265–274.

2. **Yang P, Song L, Zhang Y, et al.** Intensive blood pressure control after endovascular thrombectomy for acute ischaemic stroke (ENCHANTED2/MT): a multicentre, open-label, blinded-endpoint, randomised controlled trial. *Lancet*. 2022;400(10363):1585–1596.

3. **Mistry EA, Hart KW, Davis LT, et al.** Blood Pressure Management After Endovascular Therapy for Acute Ischemic Stroke: The BEST-II Randomized Clinical Trial. *JAMA*. 2023;330(9):821–831.

4. **Nam HS, Kim YD, Heo J, et al. (OPTIMAL-BP Trial Investigators).** Intensive vs Conventional Blood Pressure Lowering After Endovascular Thrombectomy in Acute Ischemic Stroke: The OPTIMAL-BP Randomized Clinical Trial. *JAMA*. 2023;330(9):832–842.

5. **IDENTIFY Trial Investigators.** Early intensive blood pressure management after endovascular treatment in ischaemic stroke (IDENTIFY): a multicentre, open-label, blinded-endpoint, randomised controlled trial. *Lancet Regional Health – Western Pacific*. 2025.

A useful related earlier trial, in a different reperfusion context (IV thrombolysis rather than thrombectomy), is:

6. **Anderson CS, Huang Y, Lindley RI, et al.** Intensive blood pressure reduction with intravenous thrombolysis therapy for acute ischaemic stroke (ENCHANTED): an international, randomised, open-label, blinded-endpoint, phase 3 trial. *Lancet*. 2019;393(10174):877–888.
