Here are the primary randomized controlled trials directly addressing intensive vs. standard blood pressure control after reperfusion therapy (thrombolysis and/or endovascular thrombectomy) for acute ischemic stroke. I excluded reviews, meta-analyses, and guidelines as requested.

## Key trials

**BP-TARGET** (Mazighi et al., *Lancet Neurology* 2021) — Multicenter, open-label RCT in France, 318 patients with successful endovascular therapy (EVT). Compared an intensive SBP target (100–129 mmHg) to a conventional target (130–185 mmHg) for 24–36 hours. The intensive target did not reduce the rate of intracranial hemorrhage compared with the conventional target, and long-term functional outcome data were limited.

**BEST-II** (Mistry et al., *JAMA* 2023) — Phase 2, open-label, blinded-endpoint futility trial at 3 US centers, 120 patients after successful EVT. Patients were randomized to one of three SBP targets — 140 to less than 140 mmHg, 140 to less than 160 mmHg, or 140 to 180 mmHg or less — starting within 60 minutes of recanalization and maintained for 24 hours (note: targets as reported are <140, <160, and ≤180 mmHg). Lower SBP targets did not meet strict futility criteria, but the trial suggested a low probability that lower targets would show benefit in a larger trial, and the confidence interval could not rule out harm.

**ENCHANTED2/MT** (Yang et al., *Lancet* 2022) — Multicenter, open-label, blinded-endpoint RCT at 44 centers in China, 816 patients (stopped early after 816 of a planned larger sample). Patients with persistently elevated SBP (≥140 mmHg for >10 minutes) after successful EVT were randomized to a more intensive target (SBP <120 mmHg) or a less intensive target (SBP 140–180 mmHg), to be reached within 1 hour and sustained for 72 hours. The trial was stopped early because of harm in the intensive-treatment group.

**OPTIMAL-BP** (Nam et al., *JAMA* 2023) — Multicenter, open-label RCT with blinded endpoint assessment at 19 centers in South Korea, 306 patients after successful EVT. Patients were randomized to intensive BP management (SBP target <140 mmHg) or conventional management (SBP target 140–180 mmHg); 39.4% of the intensive group versus 54.4% of the conventional group achieved functional independence at 3 months, a significant difference favoring conventional management.

**CLEVER** (Jumaa et al., *European Stroke Journal* 2026) — Single-center, open-label RCT in Toledo, Ohio, 80 patients after successful mechanical thrombectomy (mTICI ≥2c), using clevidipine as the first-line agent. Patients were randomized to an SBP goal of 90–120 mmHg (intensive) or 90–160 mmHg (standard) for 24 hours. Intensive control did not reduce hemorrhagic transformation (32.5% vs. 35.0%; adjusted OR 0.93) or symptomatic ICH, and showed a non-significant trend toward worse functional independence at 3 months (47.5% vs. 57.5%) compared with standard management.

A smaller earlier pilot/feasibility RCT (the "BEST" feasibility trial, precursor to BEST-II) also randomized patients to intensive (<140 mmHg) vs. standard (<180 mmHg) SBP targets after EVT, primarily to test feasibility rather than efficacy; I've noted it here for completeness, though its published report also incorporates meta-analytic pooling and so is not purely a primary study.

## Bottom line across these trials
Across BP-TARGET, BEST-II, ENCHANTED2/MT, OPTIMAL-BP, and CLEVER, intensive BP lowering after successful reperfusion has consistently failed to demonstrate benefit and, in two trials (ENCHANTED2/MT, OPTIMAL-BP), was associated with significantly worse functional outcomes, without a clear reduction in hemorrhagic complications in any trial.
