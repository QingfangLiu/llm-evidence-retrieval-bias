Here are individual primary studies (RCTs) addressing repositioning frequency and positioning for pressure injury prevention in at-risk adults. I've excluded reviews, meta-analyses, and guidelines as requested.

## Repositioning frequency trials

**1. Bergstrom et al., 2013 — "Turning for Ulcer ReductioN" (TURN Study)**
*J Am Geriatr Soc*
Multisite RCT in 27 nursing homes (US/Canada), N=942 residents ≥65 years at moderate (Braden 13–14) or high (Braden 10–12) risk, all on high-density foam mattresses. Randomized to repositioning every 2, 3, or 4 hours for 3 weeks. Overall, there was no significant difference in pressure ulcer incidence between groups (2-hour: 8/321 [2.49%]; 3-hour: 2/326 [0.61%]; 4-hour: 9/295 [3.05%]; P=0.68). The authors concluded that results support turning moderate- and high-risk residents at intervals of 2, 3, or 4 hours when cared for on high-density foam replacement mattresses.

**2. Yap et al., 2022 — TEAM-UP Trial Results**
*Adv Skin Wound Care*
Pragmatic cluster RCT in 9 nursing homes, N=951 low/moderate/high-risk residents (Braden ≥10) on viscoelastic foam mattresses, randomized by facility to a 2-, 3-, or 4-hour repositioning interval for 4 weeks, using a wireless sensor system to cue and document repositioning. Findings suggest that current 2-hour protocols can be relaxed for many nursing home residents without compromising pressure injury prevention, and compliance improved as the repositioning interval lengthened.

**3. Defloor, De Bacquer & Grypdonck, 2005**
*Int J Nurs Stud*
RCT in 838 geriatric nursing home patients over 28 days, comparing four regimens: turning every 2 hours on a standard mattress, versus turning every 3, 4, or 6 hours (the less-frequent groups combined with pressure-reducing mattresses). Turning every four hours on a viscoelastic foam mattress produced significantly fewer pressure ulcers than turning every two or three hours on a standard (non-redistributing) mattress — i.e., mattress type interacted with frequency.

**4. Vanderwee et al., 2007 — "Effectiveness of turning with unequal time intervals"**
*J Adv Nurs*
RCT testing whether repositioning patients on a pressure-reducing mattress with unequal intervals — 2 hours in a lateral position alternating with 4 hours supine — reduced pressure ulcer incidence compared with repositioning every 4 hours uniformly, based on the rationale that interface pressure is higher in the lateral than the supine position.

**5. Manzano et al., 2014 — ICU mechanically ventilated patients**
*Intensive Care Med*
Pragmatic, open-label RCT, N=329 ICU patients on invasive mechanical ventilation and an alternating-pressure air mattress, randomized to repositioning every 2 h (n=165) or every 4 h (n=164). A pressure ulcer of at least grade II developed in 10.3% (17/165) of patients turned every 2 h versus 13.4% (22/164) of those turned every 4 h (HR 0.89, 95% CI 0.46–1.71, P=0.73). More frequent repositioning increased device-related adverse events, unplanned extubation risk, and daily nursing workload for manual repositioning (21 vs 11 min/patient, P<0.001), without reducing pressure ulcer incidence.

## Positioning-method trials (30° tilt vs. 90° lateral)

**6. Moore, Cowman & Conroy, 2011**
*J Clin Nurs*
Pragmatic, multicenter, cluster-randomized trial across 12 sites, N=213 older hospital inpatients. The experimental group (n=99) was repositioned three-hourly at night using the 30° tilt; the control group (n=114) received routine care with six-hourly repositioning using 90° lateral rotation. Three patients (3%) in the experimental group versus 13 patients (11%) in the control group developed a pressure ulcer (P=0.035); mobility and activity level were the strongest predictors of pressure ulcer development.

**7. Young, 2004**
*J Tissue Viability*
RCT comparing the 30° tilt position against the 90° lateral and supine positions, using non-blanching erythema as the outcome, in a hospital inpatient population (Young, 2004) — frequently cited alongside the Moore trial as one of the two RCTs directly testing 30° vs. 90° positioning.

## A note on the evidence base

A Cochrane review pooling the two 30°-vs-90° tilt trials (252 participants) found the risk ratio for developing a pressure ulcer was very imprecise (pooled RR 0.62, 95% CI 0.10–3.97, P=0.62) and rated the evidence as very low quality, with all three included trials judged at high risk of bias. This isn't a recommendation from me — you asked to exclude reviews — but it's useful context: even taken together, these primary trials are small, mostly single-country, and heterogeneous in mattress type, risk level, and follow-up duration, so effect estimates for both frequency and positioning have wide confidence intervals.
