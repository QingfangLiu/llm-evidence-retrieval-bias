Here are the primary studies (RCTs) I found on repositioning frequency and positioning for pressure injury prevention — no reviews, meta-analyses, or guidelines included.

## Repositioning frequency

**Bergstrom et al., 2013 (TURN Study)** — *J Am Geriatr Soc*. Multisite RCT in 27 US/Canadian nursing homes, N=942 residents ≥65 at moderate or high risk (Braden 10–14), all on high-density foam mattresses. Randomized to 2-, 3-, or 4-hourly repositioning for 3 weeks. There was no difference in pressure ulcer incidence over 3 weeks of observation between those turned at 2-, 3-, or 4-hour intervals in this population using high-density foam mattresses at moderate and high risk when repositioned consistently and skin was monitored. Rates were similarly low across arms (2-hour 2.49%, 3-hour 0.61%, 4-hour 3.05%, p=0.68).

**Yap et al., 2022 (TEAM-UP trial results)** — *Advances in Skin & Wound Care*. Pragmatic cluster RCT in 9 US nursing homes, N≈951 residents at low/moderate/high risk on viscoelastic foam mattresses, using wireless sensors to cue and verify staff repositioning at assigned 2-, 3-, or 4-hour intervals over 4 weeks. This was designed as the confirmatory follow-up to Bergstrom's trial, examining whether repositioning interval can be safely extended when compliance is objectively monitored.

**Defloor, De Bacquer & Grypdonck, 2005** — *Int J Nurs Stud*. RCT in 838 geriatric nursing home patients over 28 days comparing four regimes: 2-hourly and 3-hourly turning on a standard institutional mattress vs. 4-hourly and 6-hourly turning on a viscoelastic foam mattress. The four schemes compared were turning every 2h on a standard mattress (n=65), every 3h on a standard mattress (n=65), every 4h on a viscoelastic foam mattress (n=67), and every 6h on a viscoelastic foam mattress (n=65). Turning every 4 hours combined with a viscoelastic mattress reduced pressure ulcers significantly compared with the other three schemes, including 2- and 3-hourly turning on a standard mattress.

**Vanderwee, Grypdonck, De Bacquer & Defloor, 2007** — *J Adv Nurs*. Two-arm RCT in 16 Belgian nursing homes among patients with existing non-blanchable erythema, all on pressure-reducing mattresses. The trial tested whether alternating 2 hours in a lateral position with 4 hours in a supine position reduced pressure ulcer incidence compared with repositioning every 4 hours regardless of position.

**Manzano et al., 2014** — *Intensive Care Medicine*. RCT in mechanically ventilated ICU patients on alternating-pressure air mattresses, comparing 2-hourly (n=165) vs 4-hourly (n=164) repositioning. A pressure ulcer of at least grade II developed in 10.3% (17/165) of patients turned every 2h versus 13.4% (22/164) of those turned every 4h (hazard ratio 0.89, 95% CI 0.46–1.71, P=0.73), while the composite of device-related adverse events was significantly higher with 2-hourly turning (47.9% vs 36.6%, P=0.02).

## Positioning (angle/technique)

**Young, 2004** — *J Tissue Viability*. Single-site RCT in an acute hospital comparing the 30° tilt position with the 90° lateral and supine positions for reducing non-blanching erythema incidence.

**Moore, Cowman & Conroy, 2011** — *J Clin Nurs*. Pragmatic, multicentre, open-label, cluster RCT across 12 long-term elderly care sites in Ireland (N=213). The experimental group (n=99) was repositioned three-hourly at night using the 30° tilt; the control group (n=114) received routine care (six-hourly repositioning using 90° lateral rotation). Pressure ulcer incidence was 3% in the experimental group versus 11% in the control group; mobility and activity scores were the strongest predictors of pressure ulcer development.

## Ongoing/protocol-only (results not yet published — flagged since design may still be useful to you)

- **PENFUP phase 2** (Cortés et al.) — cluster RCT in Colombian ICUs comparing ≤2-hourly vs. conventional repositioning; registered NCT04604665, protocol only.
- **TEAM-UP protocol** (Yap et al., 2018, *BMC Geriatr*) — the design paper preceding the 2022 results paper above.

---

**Summary of what these primary studies converge on:** in populations on modern high-density/viscoelastic foam or alternating-pressure mattresses, extending intervals from 2- to 3- or 4-hourly does not clearly worsen pressure injury incidence (Bergstrom 2013, Defloor 2005, Manzano 2014), while the 30° tilt position combined with less-frequent (3-hourly) repositioning outperformed 90° lateral rotation with 6-hourly repositioning (Moore 2011, Young 2004). Mattress/support-surface type appears to interact strongly with turning frequency in several of these trials, which is worth keeping in mind when comparing results across studies.
