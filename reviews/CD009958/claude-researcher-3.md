Here are the primary studies I could locate that directly test repositioning frequency and/or body position for pressure injury (PI) prevention in adults. I've excluded reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, and protocol-only papers where results weren't yet reported.

## Repositioning frequency (interval comparisons)

**Defloor, De Bacquer & Grypdonck (2005)** — *Int J Nurs Stud* 42(1):37–46. Cluster trial in 838 geriatric nursing home patients comparing four regimes over 28 days: turning every 2 h on a standard mattress, every 3 h on a standard mattress, every 4 h on a viscoelastic foam mattress, and every 6 h on a viscoelastic foam mattress, against standard care. This is one of the earliest trials to test turning frequency crossed with support-surface type.

**Vanderwee, Grypdonck, De Bacquer & Defloor (2007)** — *J Adv Nurs* 57(1):59–68. RCT in 16 Belgian nursing homes (patients with non-blanchable erythema). Experimental group (n=122) was repositioned alternately 2 hours in a lateral position and 4 hours in a supine position; control group (n=113) was repositioned every 4 hours, all on pressure-reducing mattresses.

**Bergstrom, Horn, Rapp et al. — the TURN Study (2013)** — *J Am Geriatr Soc* 61(10):1705–1713. Multisite, randomized clinical trial designed to determine optimal repositioning frequency of nursing home residents at risk for pressure ulcers when cared for on high-density foam mattresses, comparing 2-, 3-, and 4-hour turning intervals.

**Manzano, Colmenero, Pérez-Pérez et al. (2014)** — *Intensive Care Med* 40(11):1679–1687. ICU RCT in mechanically ventilated patients on alternating-pressure air mattresses. Eligible participants were randomly assigned to repositioning every 2 h (n=165) or every 4 h (n=164); a pressure ulcer of at least grade II developed in 10.3% vs 13.4% respectively (HR 0.89, 95% CI 0.46–1.71, P=0.73), while device-related adverse events were more frequent with 2-hourly repositioning (47.9% vs 36.6%, P=0.02) and nursing workload was roughly double.

**Yap, Horn, Sharkey et al. — the TEAM-UP Study (2022)** — *Adv Skin Wound Care* 35(6):315–325. Embedded pragmatic cluster RCT in nine nursing homes randomized to 2-, 3-, or 4-hour repositioning intervals, with residents on 7-inch high-density foam mattresses monitored via a wireless sensor system.

## Body positioning

**Young (2004)** — *J Tissue Viability* 14(3):88–96. RCT examining the 30° tilt position (n=23) versus the 90° lateral and supine positions (n=23) in a hospital inpatient population, with incidence of non-blanching erythema as the primary outcome. The 30° tilt did not reduce pressure damage incidence compared with 90° lateral or supine, and 78% of subjects had difficulty adopting/maintaining the 30° position.

**Moore, Cowman & Conroy (2011)** — *J Clin Nurs* 20(17-18):2633–2644. Pragmatic, multi-centre, cluster-RCT across 12 long-term care sites (n=213) comparing the 30° tilt repositioned three-hourly at night (experimental, n=99) against routine 90° lateral rotation repositioned six-hourly (control, n=114). 3% of the experimental group vs 11% of the control group developed a pressure ulcer (p=0.035).

A few additional notes for your synthesis:
- Vanderwee 2007 and the TURN Study are frequently pooled/compared against each other in later work because they used similar 2/3/4-hour comparisons but different mattress types — worth checking for outcome heterogeneity.
- The Cochrane review "Repositioning for pressure ulcer prevention in adults" (Gillespie et al.) pooled the Young 2004 and Moore 2011 30°-vs-90° trials and flagged both as high risk of bias with an imprecise pooled effect — useful context if you want to sanity-check individual study quality, though it's a review itself so I haven't included it as a primary source.
- I did not find a completed/published results paper for the PENFUP ICU repositioning-frequency trial in Colombia (NCT04604665) — only the study protocol, so I excluded it; worth checking ClinicalTrials.gov periodically for results.
