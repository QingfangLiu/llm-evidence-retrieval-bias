Here are the individual primary studies (RCTs and cluster-RCTs) I found on repositioning frequency and positioning for pressure-injury prevention in adults. I've excluded the systematic reviews, meta-analyses, and commentaries that turned up in search results — those are noted only where they helped identify a primary study's citation details.

## Repositioning frequency trials

**Bergstrom N, Horn SD, Rapp MP, Stern A, Barrett R, Watkiss M. Turning for Ulcer ReductioN: a multisite randomized clinical trial in nursing homes.** *J Am Geriatr Soc.* 2013;61(10):1705-13. (the "TURN" study)
Multisite RCT in 27 US/Canadian nursing homes. 942 residents aged 65+ without pressure ulcers, at moderate or high risk per the Braden Scale, were randomly allocated to a 2-, 3-, or 4-hour repositioning schedule for 3 weeks, all on high-density foam mattresses. No significant difference in pressure ulcer formation was found between the three intervals.
- Companion economic analysis: Paulden M, Bergstrom N, Horn SD, et al. "Turning for Ulcer Reduction (TURN) Study: An Economic Analysis." (same trial, cost outcomes)

**Yap TL, Horn SD, Sharkey PD, Zheng T, Bergstrom N, Colon-Emeric C, Sabol VK, Alderden J, Yap W, Kennerly SM. Effect of Varying Repositioning Frequency on Pressure Injury Prevention in Nursing Home Residents: TEAM-UP Trial Results.** *Adv Skin Wound Care.* 2022;35(6):315-25.
Embedded pragmatic cluster-RCT in nine nursing homes randomized to one of three repositioning intervals (2, 3, or 4 hours), using 7-inch high-density foam mattresses and a wireless sensor system that cued staff on repositioning need. Residents at low, moderate, and high risk were included.

**Defloor T, De Bacquer D, Grypdonck MH. The effect of various combinations of turning and pressure reducing devices on the incidence of pressure ulcers.** *Int J Nurs Stud.* 2005;42(1):37-46.
521 participants total (262 relevant to repositioning comparisons), conducted in Belgium, largely in long-term care, comparing different turning/mattress combinations across two 4-week periods with different prevention schemes per ward.

**Vanderwee K, Grypdonck MHF, De Bacquer D, Defloor T. Effectiveness of turning with unequal time intervals on the incidence of pressure ulcer lesions.** *J Adv Nurs.* 2007;57(1):59-68.
Compared alternating turning intervals against standard fixed schedules (cited alongside Defloor 2005 as one of the earlier Belgian repositioning trials).

**Colmenero M, Manzano F, Pérez-Pérez AM, Roldán D, del Mar Fernández-Mondéjar E, et al. (Manzano F et al.) Comparison of two repositioning schedules for the prevention of pressure ulcers in patients on mechanical ventilation with alternating pressure air mattresses.** *Intensive Care Med.* 2014;40(11):1679-87.
Open-label RCT in two ICUs in southern Spain, 329 mechanically ventilated participants, comparing 2-hourly with 4-hourly repositioning, all nursed on alternating air-pressure mattresses using 30° head-of-bed tilt (alternating left/right/supine). A 2-hour schedule was not superior to a 4-hour schedule for preventing pressure ulcers, but required greater nursing workload; the study also suggested compliance with the assigned schedule (achieving >60% of scheduled repositionings) mattered more than the schedule itself.
- ClinicalTrials.gov record: NCT00847665, "Pressure Ulcer Prevention in Ventilated Patients Using Two Repositioning Regimens" (same trial).

**Pickham D, et al. (2018 trial, "LS-HAPI study")** — cited in the Cochrane review as: 1312 ICU participants, testing whether feedback from a wearable patient sensor increased turning compliance with a 2-hourly, 20° tilt regimen. Full citation per reference list: Pickham D, et al. "...for preventing pressure injuries in acutely ill adults: A pragmatic randomized clinical trial (LS-HAPI study)." *Int J Nurs Stud.* 2018;80:12-9.

**Zhou Q, et al. (2014 trial, China)** — conducted in an ICU setting in China, using a randomization table (randomization process itself not well described in the report).

## Positioning/tilt-angle trials

**Moore Z, Cowman S, Conroy RM. A randomised controlled clinical trial of repositioning, using the 30° tilt, for the prevention of pressure ulcers.** *J Clin Nurs.* 2011;20(17-18):2633-44.
Pragmatic, multi-centre, open-label, prospective cluster-RCT comparing two repositioning regimens among older persons; 12 study sites were cluster-randomized. The experimental group (n=99) was repositioned three-hourly at night using the 30° tilt.

**Young T. The 30 degree tilt position vs the 90 degree lateral and supine positions in reducing the incidence of non-blanching erythema in a hospital inpatient population: a randomised controlled trial.** *J Tissue Viability.* 2004;14(3):88,90,92-6.
Single acute-care facility (Wales); compared 30° tilt against 90° lateral and supine positions for incidence of non-blanching erythema.

**Ghezeljeh TN, Kalhor L, Ebadi A, et al. (referred to as Ghezeljeh 2017).** The comparison of the effect of head-of-bed elevation to 30° and 45° on the incidence of ventilator-associated pneumonia and the risk for pressure ulcers: a controlled randomised clinical trial.
Compared 30° head-of-bed tilt with 45° head-of-bed tilt in ICU participants, with a "routine position" control group.

## Ongoing/protocol-stage trials (not yet reporting results)

These are primary-study protocols, included for completeness since they're not reviews, but they don't yet have outcome data:
- Cortés OL, et al. "Frequency of repositioning for preventing pressure ulcers in patients hospitalized in ICU: protocol of a cluster randomized controlled trial." *BMC Nurs.* 2021. Multi-centric, pragmatic, double-blind cluster-RCT comparing repositioning ≤2 hours vs. conventional care across 22 ICUs in Colombia (22 clusters × 150 patients each). Registered as NCT04604665.
- Kennerly SM, Yap TL, Horn SD, et al. "TEAM-UP for quality" protocol paper (precursor to the Yap 2022 results paper above).

## A note on completeness
The Cochrane systematic review by Gillespie et al. (2020, *Cochrane Database Syst Rev*) — which you asked me to exclude as a review — was useful as a map of the primary-study landscape: it identifies eight RCTs (Bergstrom 2013, Defloor 2005, Ghezeljeh 2017, Manzano 2014, Moore 2011, Pickham 2018, Young 2004, Zhou 2014) as the total body of RCT/cluster-RCT evidence on repositioning schedules and positions for pressure injury prevention in adults as of its last search (March 2019). The list above covers all eight, plus the newer TEAM-UP results paper (2022) and the Colombian ICU trial protocol, which postdate that review.
