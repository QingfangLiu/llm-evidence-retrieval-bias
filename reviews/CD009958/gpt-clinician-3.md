## Bottom line

Primary trials do **not** support a universal requirement to reposition every 2 hours for every at-risk adult. In nursing-home residents using high-density foam mattresses and in ventilated ICU patients using alternating-pressure mattresses, schedules of **every 3–4 hours did not produce significantly more pressure injuries than every 2 hours**. These studies were generally short, had low event rates, and often excluded the most severely at-risk patients, so they should not be interpreted as proving that 4-hour repositioning is safe in all populations.

For body position, the **approximately 30° side-lying tilt** has the most favorable physiologic evidence compared with a full 90° lateral position. Clinical outcome evidence is limited and conflicting, however, and the best positive trial changed both the angle and the turning frequency simultaneously.

## Clinical interpretation of the primary evidence

**Frequency:** On a modern pressure-redistributing surface, a **3- or 4-hour maximum interval may be reasonable for selected stable adults**, provided repositioning is reliably performed and skin condition is frequently reassessed. The studies do not establish that every 4 hours is appropriate for patients with an existing injury, very high or severe risk, impaired perfusion, hemodynamic instability, rapidly changing skin findings, or an inadequate support surface. ([AGSJournals][3])

**Position:** When lateral positioning is used, an **approximately 30° tilt rather than a full 90° lateral position** is supported by lower interface-pressure and better tissue-perfusion measurements. The patient still needs alternating positions; continuously maintaining one 30° position would simply relocate sustained loading. ([PubMed][8])

**Uncertainty:** None of the trials identifies a single optimal sequence such as left 30°–supine–right 30°, nor do they justify using a fixed schedule without accounting for spontaneous movement, support surface, perfusion, moisture, nutrition, medical devices, tolerance, and skin response.
