## Bottom line

**Remote pharmacist care can outperform usual care, but the effect depends on what the pharmacist can do.** The clearest clinical benefits occur in hypertension programs that combine transmitted home BP readings with pharmacist-led medication adjustment. Telephone or digital counseling without active treatment modification more consistently improves medication adherence than HbA1c, BP, hospitalization, or quality of life.

## Observational telepharmacy evidence

A retrospective urban study compared people obtaining statins, ACE inhibitors/ARBs or noninsulin diabetes medicines through telepharmacies with traditional-pharmacy users. Telepharmacy use was associated with **lower adherence to statins and ACE inhibitors/ARBs**, while diabetes-medication adherence was similar. This likely reflects important selection and service-model differences and should not be interpreted as a causal estimate of pharmacist clinical care. ([PubMed][12])

## Clinical interpretation

The individual studies suggest:

* **Hypertension:** The best-supported use case. Large trials show clinically meaningful BP improvements when pharmacists receive home measurements and can intensify or adjust treatment. Monitoring alone is less effective. ([PubMed][2])
* **Medication adherence:** Usually improves, although absolute improvements range from modest in large pragmatic programs to larger in targeted telephone services. ([JAMA Network][4])
* **Diabetes:** Adherence and self-care may improve, but HbA1c benefits are inconsistent and often absent compared with usual care. ([PubMed][6])
* **Clinical events and utilization:** Evidence is limited and inconsistent. Large studies generally have not shown clear hospitalization benefits, although one pragmatic trial reported fewer ED visits. ([JAMA Network][4])
* **Safety:** Older adults undergoing active pharmacist-directed BP titration may require explicit hypotension surveillance. ([PubMed][3])

Thus, remote pharmacy care is best viewed as **an effective medication-management model for selected outcomes—particularly BP and adherence—rather than a uniformly superior replacement for usual chronic-disease care.**
