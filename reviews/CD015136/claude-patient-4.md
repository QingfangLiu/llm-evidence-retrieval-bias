Here are individual primary studies (not reviews) comparing remote pharmacy care/telepharmacy with usual care for people with chronic conditions managed outside hospital:

**1. STIC2IT trial** — Choudhry NK, et al. "Effect of a Remotely Delivered Tailored Multicomponent Approach to Enhance Medication Taking for Patients With Hyperlipidemia, Hypertension, and Diabetes." *JAMA Intern Med.* 2018;178(9):1182-1189.
A cluster-randomized pragmatic trial (14 primary care sites, ~4,078 adults) explicitly designed as a "tele-pharmacy intervention." The intervention improved medication adherence but did not significantly improve disease control, using telephone-delivered behavioral interviewing by clinical pharmacists, text messaging, pillboxes, and mailed progress reports compared with usual care.

**2. Pharmaceutical care in T2DM + hypertension** — RCT (2018 cohort), PubMed ID 35685555.
Compared clinical pharmacist access plus phone follow-up and a messaging app against routine outpatient care; adherence and glycemic/BP outcomes were the endpoints. The 3-month medication adherence was higher in the pharmaceutical care group than the routine group (90.0% vs. lower comparator).

**3. Mobile Health Intervention in Patients With Type 2 Diabetes** — RCT, *JAMA Network Open* (PMC10543137), conducted in federally qualified health centers (FQHCs).
Team-based remote care combining clinical pharmacists, health coaches, and telehealth versus usual outpatient care, with HbA1c as the key outcome. Short-term (less than 12 months) improvement in HbA1c was found with clinical pharmacists providing medication management services delivered remotely alongside health coaches and telehealth.

**4. Pharmacist-led remote follow-up for breast cancer patients** — Prospective RCT (PMC12443711).
Chronic, ongoing outpatient drug therapy monitored via an app-based remote pharmacist service versus standard follow-up. Severe adverse drug reactions occurred in 20.2% of the intervention group versus 50.0% of controls, with better medication knowledge, adherence, and quality-of-life scores in the remote pharmacy group.

**5. Alfalfa App warfarin management RCT** — *JMIR* (PMC12112255), post-cardiac valve replacement patients.
Compared an "internet + pharmacy care" mobile app for warfarin dosing against offline, pharmacist-led anticoagulation outpatient clinic visits. Time in therapeutic range was significantly higher in the app group than the offline group (66.46% vs 46.65%), with higher monitoring frequency and greater percentage of INR values within target range.

**6. Internet-based warfarin management after mechanical heart valve replacement** — Multicenter RCT (PMC8398748).
Internet-based anticoagulation management (including pharmacist/clinician remote dose adjustment) versus conventional in-person management. The internet-based group showed significantly higher time in therapeutic range and fewer bleeding/embolic complications (6.94% vs 12.74%) than the conventional group.
