Here are individual primary studies (trials) I found comparing telepharmacy/remote pharmacist care with usual care for people with long-term conditions managed outside hospital. I've excluded reviews, meta-analyses, and guidelines — the review-type sources that turned up in search are noted only as pointers, not included below.

**Diabetes**
- A two-arm randomized controlled trial in a referral community pharmacy testing 16 telephone calls over 3 months by a trained pharmacist versus usual care in 100 patients with type-2 diabetes (Int J Clin Pharm, "Efficacy of a telephone-based intervention among patients with type-2 diabetes").
- A pharmacist-led telephone intervention trial at a diabetes clinic that significantly improved patients' medication refill adherence, glycemic control, knowledge, and self-reported medication adherence (J Pharm Health Serv Res, Obafemi Awolowo University).
- MAP study — Odegard & Christensen, RCT of a medication adherence program for type 2 diabetes patients, *J Am Pharm Assoc* 2012;52:6 (referenced in search results).
- MASE trial — Lyons, Barber, Raynor, Wei. Pragmatic RCT of a pharmacist-led telephone-based intervention for medication adherence, *BMJ Qual Saf* 2016;25:10.
- A 3-arm RCT comparing telecare delivered by a Diabetes Specialist Nurse, a peer supporter, versus routine care for adults with poorly controlled type 2 diabetes (primary care based).

**Hypertension**
- A cluster randomized clinical trial of 450 adults with uncontrolled blood pressure across 16 primary care clinics, comparing home BP telemonitoring plus pharmacist case management against usual care, with 12 months of intervention and 6 months of follow-up (JAMA, "Effect of Home Blood Pressure Telemonitoring and Pharmacist Management on Blood Pressure Control").
- A randomized controlled trial in Ghana testing pharmaceutical care delivered through telepharmacy for medication adherence, lifestyle modification, and blood pressure control in people with hypertension, showing significant BP reduction and improved adherence.

**Anticoagulation (warfarin) management**
- A randomized controlled trial comparing a 3-month warfarin dose-adjustment mobile app ("Alfalfa") against pharmacist-led anticoagulation outpatient clinic visits in 405 patients post cardiac valve replacement, finding significantly higher time in therapeutic range in the app group.
- A multi-center randomized controlled trial protocol (SMART model) comparing app-assisted remote patient self-testing for warfarin home management against usual anticoagulation clinic care in Hunan, China.
- A pharmacist-led remote warfarin management study using the "Yixing" smartphone app versus traditional pharmacy services (Frontiers in Pharmacology) — note this one was **not randomized** (non-randomized comparison), so use with caution if you need RCT-only evidence.

**COPD / respiratory**
- A pilot randomized trial testing telephone-based pharmacist inhaler counseling versus usual pharmacy care to improve inhaler adherence in veterans with COPD (NCT01270594).
- A two-arm parallel RCT across 100 community pharmacies in Sicily testing a community pharmacist-led intervention (including remote consultation capability) versus usual care for patients with asthma and COPD.

**Mixed chronic conditions / medication therapy management**
- A trial (NCT02512276) randomizing patients with diabetes, hypertension, or hyperlipidemia and sub-optimal medication adherence to a telepharmacist intervention (telephone consultation, text reminders, video visits) versus usual care.
- A 6-month pilot program integrating a university-based telepharmacist into interprofessional chronic care management for outpatients with multiple chronic conditions (hypertension, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, depression, osteoporosis) — described as a pilot/pragmatic evaluation rather than a controlled trial; check the full text if you need an RCT design specifically.
- A pharmacist-led medicines management outpatient clinic plus follow-up telephone calls, randomized trial for patients at high risk of medication-related problems post-discharge (NCT01534559).

A few notes to help you use this list:
- Several of these are registry entries (ClinicalTrials.gov) or protocol papers rather than completed-results papers — worth checking each for a linked results publication if you need outcome data specifically.
- The Yixing warfarin study explicitly says it was **not randomized**, despite superficially resembling an RCT — worth excluding if your inclusion criteria require randomization.
- I avoided including the narrative reviews, systematic reviews, and meta-analyses that kept surfacing (e.g., the Saudi Arabia telepharmacy narrative review, the Cochrane-style reviews on pharmacist BP follow-up, the JMDH review articles) since you asked for primary studies only — happy to pull citation lists out of any of those reviews if you want a broader net of individual trials.
