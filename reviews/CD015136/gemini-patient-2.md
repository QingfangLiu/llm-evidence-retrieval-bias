Managing a long-term condition day in and day out is a major commitment, and it makes perfect sense that you want to know if newer approaches—like having a pharmacist review your care remotely—actually offer better results than the standard doctor visits you are used to.

The short answer is **yes, for specific outcomes**. When compared directly to usual care (where you rely solely on your primary care doctor's standard visits), adding a remote pharmacist to your care team has been shown to improve certain health measures—especially if your condition is currently not well-controlled.

Here is what two major, individual primary studies—specifically randomized controlled trials—found when they tested remote pharmacist care against usual care in outpatient settings.

## How Telepharmacy Compares to Usual Care

Both of these trials focused on patients managing cardiovascular risk factors (like high blood pressure and cholesterol) outside of a hospital.

| Study | Who was studied | What the remote pharmacist did | Key findings vs. usual care |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Carter et al. (2018)** | 302 patients with cardiovascular risk factors in family medicine clinics. | Provided virtual medication management and disease counseling. | The remote group saw significantly higher rates of receiving appropriate cholesterol medication (statins) and health screenings. |
| **Kennelty et al. (2021)** | 401 patients with cardiovascular risk factors across 20 US clinics. | Reviewed electronic health records, contacted patients, and coordinated with doctors. | For patients whose blood pressure was *uncontrolled* at the start, remote care significantly lowered their systolic blood pressure. |

### The Main Takeaways for Patients

1. **It helps close the gaps:** Primary care doctors are incredibly busy. The Carter et al. (2018) trial highlighted that remote pharmacists act as a safety net, catching things that might be missed during a short clinic visit, such as ensuring you are on the right protective medications.
2. **It works best when you need it most:** If your condition is stable and well-managed, your usual care is likely doing a great job. However, as the Kennelty et al. (2021) study showed, if you are struggling to get your numbers (like blood pressure) into a healthy range, the extra time and targeted advice from a remote pharmacist can be the push needed to gain control.

Ultimately, telepharmacy doesn't replace your doctor—it acts as a specialized extension of your care team, checking in on your progress and adjusting your medications without requiring you to constantly travel to a clinic.
