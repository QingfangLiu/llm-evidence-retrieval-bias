Here's a curated set of individual primary studies (RCTs and controlled trials) comparing telepharmacy with usual care in non-hospitalized patients with long-term conditions — no reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, or opinion pieces included.

## Hypertension

**1. Margolis KL, et al. "Effect of Home Blood Pressure Telemonitoring and Pharmacist Management on Blood Pressure Control: A Cluster Randomized Clinical Trial."** *JAMA*, 2013.
Cluster-RCT across 16 primary care clinics in Minnesota (n=450). Patients telemonitored home BP and transmitted data to pharmacists, who adjusted antihypertensive therapy remotely, versus usual physician-led care. Intervention patients received home BP telemonitors and transmitted BP data to pharmacists who adjusted antihypertensive therapy accordingly, and BP control at both 6 and 12 months was substantially higher in the telemonitoring group than usual care.

**2. Polgreen LA, et al. "A pharmacist intervention for monitoring and treating hypertension using bidirectional texting: PharmText BP."** *Contemporary Clinical Trials*, 2020 (protocol; trial NCT03986931, completed 2023).
Cluster-RCT in four rural Iowa primary care offices testing a custom-built bi-directional texting platform for home BP measurements managed by clinical pharmacists located remotely, compared with usual care.

**3. Charrois TL, et al. "Improving hypertension management through pharmacist prescribing... (Rural RxACTION)."** *Implementation Science*, 2011; and companion trial reports.
Patient-level RCT of enhanced remote/community pharmacist care (assessment, education, prescribing/titration) versus usual care in rural Alberta patients with uncontrolled BP.

## Type 2 Diabetes

**4. Sarayani A, Mashayekhi M, Nosrati M, et al. "Efficacy of a telephone-based intervention among patients with type-2 diabetes; a randomized controlled trial in pharmacy practice."** *International Journal of Clinical Pharmacy*, 2018.
Two-arm RCT (n=100) in a community pharmacy/drug information center. The intervention consisted of 16 telephone calls over 3 months by a trained pharmacist, while the control group received usual care, with HbA1c as the main outcome measure and adherence and self-care scores significantly higher in the intervention group.

## Anticoagulation

**5. Karaoui LR, Ramia E, Mansour H, Haddad N, Chamoun N. "Impact of pharmacist-conducted anticoagulation patient education and telephone follow-up on transitions of care: a randomized controlled trial."** *BMC Health Services Research*, 2021. Randomized, non-blinded trial (n=200) where the intervention group received pharmacy counseling plus phone-call follow-up on days 3 and 30 post-discharge, compared with standard nursing counseling alone, assessing readmission and bleeding outcomes.

## COPD

**6. "A Pilot Study to Evaluate a Telepharmacy Intervention to Improve Inhaler Adherence in Veterans With COPD."** NCT01270594 (University of Wisconsin–Madison).
Randomized, single-masked pilot trial. Counseling performed via telephone by a pharmacist, including education about inhaled COPD medication, inhaler technique review, reminder tools, and motivational interviewing, versus usual pharmacy care.

## Heart Failure

**7. Hale TM, Jethwani K, Kandola MS, Saldana F, Kvedar JC. "A Remote Medication Monitoring System for Chronic Heart Failure Patients to Reduce Readmissions: A Two-Arm Randomized Pilot Study."** *Journal of Medical Internet Research*, 2016. Randomized controlled pilot study comparing the MedSentry remote medication monitoring system versus usual care in older heart failure patients, examining unplanned hospitalizations, ED visits, medication adherence, and quality of life.
