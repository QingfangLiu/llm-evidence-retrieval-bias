When you're managing a long-term condition from home, the last thing you want is care that feels disconnected. Based on primary clinical trials, the short answer is that telepharmacy—where pharmacists monitor and adjust your care remotely—is generally **just as effective** as usual in-person care for maintaining your health, and for some specific conditions, it actually **works significantly better**.

Here is a breakdown of what the individual clinical trials show regarding remote pharmacy care compared to standard in-person care.

## Where Telepharmacy Outperforms Usual Care

The most compelling evidence for telepharmacy comes from patients who have uncontrolled conditions, particularly high blood pressure (hypertension), that require frequent medication adjustments.

A large randomized controlled trial found that patients using home telemonitoring combined with remote pharmacist management achieved significantly better blood pressure control than those receiving usual in-person care (Margolis et al., 2013). After 12 months, 57.2% of the telepharmacy patients had their blood pressure under control, compared to only 30.0% of the usual care patients. Strikingly, this improvement was maintained even six months after the intervention ended (Margolis et al., 2013).

When researchers dug into *why* this trial was so successful, they found that remote care allowed for much faster action. Because pharmacists were receiving remote data, they could adjust antihypertensive medications frequently over the phone—often achieving blood pressure control within just three months (Beran et al., 2018). Patients noted that frequent phone contact actually helped build a stronger patient-pharmacist relationship than traditional care (Beran et al., 2018).

Similarly, a 2021 cluster-randomized trial evaluating a remote cardiovascular risk service found that while the service didn't drastically change overall clinic guideline scores, it led to a significant improvement in systolic blood pressure specifically for patients whose conditions were uncontrolled at the start of the study (Kennelty et al., 2021).

## Where Telepharmacy is Equal to Usual Care

For general medication adherence and broader disease monitoring, telepharmacy is considered "non-inferior," meaning it works just as well as going to a physical clinic.

A recent trial conducted during the COVID-19 pandemic compared medication adherence in patients with chronic diseases (like diabetes and hypertension) who received either traditional face-to-face visits or telephone-based tele-visits (Norouzi et al., 2023). The study found no statistically significant difference in patient-reported medication adherence between the two groups (Norouzi et al., 2023).

This is actually a very positive finding for outpatient care: it means that patients can avoid the travel, wait times, and physical burden of visiting a clinic without sacrificing their commitment to taking their medications properly.

## The Bottom Line

If your long-term condition is currently stable and you just need routine check-ins to stay on track with your medications, telepharmacy will likely give you the exact same results as usual care, but with added convenience. However, if you are actively struggling to get a condition (like high blood pressure) under control, a telepharmacy program that involves regular remote monitoring and frequent adjustments by a pharmacist can be highly effective and may yield much faster results than standard clinic visits.
