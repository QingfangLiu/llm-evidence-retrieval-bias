As a clinician, you know that tailoring controlled ovarian stimulation (COS) is critical for optimizing IVF outcomes. While recombinant follicle-stimulating hormone (rFSH) is widely used, comparing it directly to other gonadotropins—specifically human menopausal gonadotropin (hMG) and long-acting rFSH (corifollitropin alfa)—reveals distinct pharmacodynamic differences.

Here is a breakdown of how rFSH compares to these alternatives, based exclusively on primary randomized controlled trials (RCTs).

### rFSH vs. Highly Purified hMG (HP-hMG)

Human menopausal gonadotropin preparations contain both FSH and LH activity. The debate between rFSH and hMG largely centers on whether this LH activity improves embryo quality or if pure FSH is superior for total oocyte yield.

* **Oocyte Yield:** RCTs consistently demonstrate that rFSH yields a statistically higher number of retrieved oocytes compared to hMG regimens (Andersen et al., 2006; Chapon et al., 2020). For example, in a large multinational assessor-blind trial using a long GnRH agonist protocol, rFSH yielded an average of 11.8 oocytes compared to 10.0 with HP-hMG (Andersen et al., 2006).
* **Embryo Quality:** Despite the lower total oocyte yield, some trials suggest HP-hMG may improve the proportion of top-quality embryos. Andersen et al. (2006) noted a significantly higher percentage of top-quality embryos with HP-hMG (11.3%) versus rFSH (9.0%). However, other trials utilizing GnRH antagonist cycles have found a lower number of mature (MII) oocytes with hMG and similar overall embryonic development between the two groups (Chapon et al., 2020).
* **Pregnancy Rates:** Both ongoing pregnancy rates and live birth rates appear comparable without overwhelming superiority of either medication. Andersen et al. (2006) established non-inferiority, reporting a 27% ongoing pregnancy rate for HP-hMG and 22% for rFSH, while Chapon et al. (2020) likewise found statistically similar pregnancy rates.

### rFSH vs. Corifollitropin alfa (Long-Acting rFSH)

Corifollitropin alfa is a fusion protein that functions as a sustained follicle stimulant. Because it lacks LH activity but has a significantly extended half-life, a single injection can replace the first seven daily injections of standard rFSH.

* **Oocyte Yield and Maturation:** Single-dose corifollitropin alfa provides a slightly higher or equivalent follicular response compared to daily rFSH. A large non-inferiority RCT found an estimated difference of 1.2 more cumulus-oocyte complexes with corifollitropin alfa compared to rFSH (Devroey et al., 2014). This equivalence holds true even in older patient cohorts (women aged 35–42), where no significant difference in retrieved oocytes was observed between the two protocols (Vuong et al., 2017).
* **Pregnancy Rates:** The ongoing pregnancy rates are virtually identical. Devroey et al. (2014) reported ongoing pregnancy rates of 38.9% for corifollitropin alfa and 38.1% for daily rFSH in normal responders. Similarly, Vuong et al. (2017) found ongoing pregnancy and live birth rates to be statistically indistinguishable between the two treatments in an older demographic, solidifying it as an effective option to reduce patient injection burden.
