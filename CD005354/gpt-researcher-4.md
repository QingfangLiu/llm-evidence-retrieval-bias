# Primary-study evidence map: rFSH versus other gonadotropins in IVF/ICSI

## Scope

I treated **rFSH** as follitropin alfa or beta used for controlled ovarian stimulation before IVF/ICSI. Comparators included:

* Urinary FSH or highly purified urinary FSH
* Human menopausal gonadotropin/menotropins, particularly highly purified hMG
* Other recombinant FSH preparations, including follitropin delta and biosimilars
* Long-acting corifollitropin alfa
* Recombinant LH supplementation

I excluded studies confined to ovulation induction without IVF/ICSI, IUI, final-oocyte-maturation triggers, noncomparative pharmacology studies, and all reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, opinions, and editorials. The search was conducted through **July 26, 2026**. This is a targeted primary-study map rather than a complete PRISMA systematic review.

## Main findings

### 1. rFSH versus urinary or highly purified urinary FSH

The larger early trials generally suggest that rFSH is **more efficient pharmacodynamically**—often requiring a lower total dose or shorter treatment and sometimes producing approximately one or two additional oocytes—but individual trials have not demonstrated a consistent live-birth advantage.

In the 981-participant trial by Out and colleagues, rFSH produced more oocytes than urinary FSH, 10.84 versus 8.95, with a lower total FSH dose and shorter treatment. Ongoing pregnancy per initiated attempt did not differ significantly, although the cumulative ongoing pregnancy rate including frozen transfers was borderline higher with rFSH, 25.7% versus 20.4%. ([PubMed][1])

Other trials were less favorable to rFSH in terms of oocyte yield. Lenton et al. reported 10.2 versus 10.8 oocytes and live-birth rates of 33.8% versus 26.7% for rFSH and highly purified urinary FSH, respectively; none of these differences was statistically significant. ([PubMed][2]) Baker et al. similarly found no meaningful difference in oocyte yield, clinical pregnancy, or live birth; the live-birth rate was 38.2% in both groups. ([PubMed][3])

**Interpretation:** rFSH may provide greater dose efficiency than older urinary preparations, but the primary trials do not establish that this translates into higher live birth.

### 2. rFSH versus hMG or highly purified hMG

This comparison is biologically different because hMG supplies both FSH and LH-like activity, largely through its hCG content. Across the major primary trials:

* rFSH usually produced **more oocytes**.
* HP-hMG often produced fewer oocytes but sometimes a greater proportion of embryos classified as high quality.
* Ongoing pregnancy and cumulative live birth were generally similar in individual trials.
* HP-hMG had a particularly important safety advantage in a trial of predicted high responders.

The European and Israeli phase III trial randomized 781 women and found ongoing pregnancy rates of 25% with HP-hMG and 22% with rFSH, with similar OHSS and miscarriage rates. ([PubMed][4])

In the 731-participant MERIT trial, rFSH produced more oocytes, while HP-hMG produced a higher locally assessed proportion of top-quality embryos. Ongoing pregnancy was 27% with HP-hMG and 22% with rFSH, but the confidence interval included no difference. ([PubMed][5])

MEGASET, which used a GnRH-antagonist protocol and compulsory single-blastocyst transfer, found fresh ongoing pregnancy rates of 29% with HP-hMG and 27% with rFSH. Cumulative live birth after subsequent frozen transfers was 40% versus 38%, establishing noninferiority of HP-hMG. ([PubMed][6])

The most clinically distinctive result came from MEGASET-HR, involving 620 women with AMH ≥5 ng/mL. Cumulative live birth was virtually identical—50.6% with HP-hMG and 51.5% with rFSH—but OHSS was 9.7% with HP-hMG versus 21.4% with rFSH. Early pregnancy loss was also lower with HP-hMG. ([PubMed][7])

**Interpretation:** rFSH’s tendency to produce more oocytes does not consistently translate into better pregnancy or live-birth outcomes. In predicted high responders, HP-hMG may provide comparable cumulative effectiveness with substantially less OHSS, although that finding comes predominantly from one large population-specific trial.

### 3. Follitropin alfa versus follitropin beta

A randomized trial of 348 women found very similar clinical pregnancy rates with follitropin alfa and beta: 33.5% and 32.9% per cycle, respectively. Cumulative ongoing pregnancy after frozen transfers was also similar, 35.4% versus 37.7%. ([PubMed][8])

**Interpretation:** The primary comparative evidence does not identify a clinically important difference between follitropin alfa and beta when given at equivalent starting doses.

### 4. Long-acting corifollitropin alfa versus daily rFSH

Corifollitropin alfa is a long-acting recombinant FSH analogue designed so that one injection replaces the first seven daily rFSH injections.

ENGAGE randomized approximately 1,500 women. Ongoing pregnancy was 38.9% with corifollitropin alfa and 38.1% with daily rFSH. Corifollitropin alfa produced modestly more oocytes; moderate or severe OHSS was numerically higher but not statistically different. ([PubMed][9])

ENSURE, in women weighing ≤60 kg, found 13.3 versus 10.6 oocytes with corifollitropin alfa and rFSH. Moderate or severe OHSS occurred in 3.4% versus 1.6%, respectively. ([PubMed][10])

PURSUE enrolled 1,390 women aged 35–42. Corifollitropin alfa was noninferior for vital pregnancy; live birth was 21.3% versus 23.4%, and OHSS was 1.7% in both groups. ([PubMed][11]) A separate Vietnamese RCT of 400 women aged 35–42 found equivalent oocyte yield and comparable ongoing, cumulative, and live-birth outcomes. ([PubMed][12])

A 2026 Chinese phase III trial of 474 normal-reserve women reported noninferior oocyte yield—12.1 versus 10.9—with generally comparable pregnancy outcomes and OHSS rates of 3.8% versus 2.5%. ([PubMed][13])

**Interpretation:** Corifollitropin alfa principally offers a reduction in injection burden. Pregnancy and live-birth efficacy appears similar to daily rFSH in the studied populations, although some trials found modestly more oocytes and numerically more OHSS.

### 5. Follitropin delta versus follitropin alfa

Follitropin delta is produced in a human cell line. Most trials evaluated it together with an individualized dosing algorithm based on AMH and body weight; consequently, those studies compare both the **molecule and the dosing strategy**.

ESTHER-1 randomized 1,329 women. Ongoing pregnancy was 30.7% with individualized follitropin delta and 31.6% with conventional follitropin alfa; live birth was 29.8% versus 30.7%. Mean oocyte yield was similar, but follitropin delta produced fewer extremely high or low responses and fewer OHSS-prevention interventions. ([Københavns Universitets Forskningsportal][14])

In a 1,009-participant Asian trial, ongoing pregnancy was 31.3% with individualized follitropin delta and 25.7% with follitropin alfa. Live birth was significantly higher, 31.3% versus 24.7%, and early OHSS or preventive intervention was less frequent with follitropin delta. The authors noted that the trial covered one stimulation cycle with fresh cleavage-stage transfer. ([PubMed][15])

ADAPT-1 provides a more direct molecule comparison because both products were administered using conventional starting-dose regimens. Among 300 women, mean oocyte yield was 9.9 with each product, clinical pregnancy was 31.6% versus 31.0%, and early OHSS was 2.5% versus 3.0%. ([PubMed][16])

**Interpretation:** Follitropin delta appears comparable to follitropin alfa for pregnancy efficacy. Its individualized regimen can reduce excessive ovarian response, but the contribution of the molecule itself cannot be separated from the AMH/body-weight dosing algorithm in most trials.

### 6. Follitropin-alfa biosimilars versus reference Gonal-f

Registration trials generally used the number of retrieved oocytes—not live birth—as their primary equivalence endpoint.

Bemfola and Gonal-f produced 10.8 and 10.6 oocytes, respectively, in a 372-participant phase III trial, with similar safety and severe-OHSS rates. ([PubMed][17]) Ovaleap and Gonal-f produced 12.2 and 12.1 oocytes in a 299-participant equivalence trial. ([PubMed][18])

Cinnal-f was noninferior to Gonal-f for the percentage of metaphase-II oocytes in 200 women, with no significant differences in the pregnancy or safety outcomes assessed. ([PubMed][19]) QL1012 produced 14.7 versus 13.4 oocytes, within the prespecified equivalence margin, and had similar OHSS rates. ([PubMed][20])

**Interpretation:** The trials support equivalence in ovarian response and short-term safety. They generally do not have sufficient size or live-birth-centered designs to demonstrate equivalence in cumulative live birth.

### 7. Recombinant LH added to rFSH

This is an adjunct question rather than a substitution comparison. Marrs et al. randomized 431 women undergoing ICSI to rFSH plus 150 IU/day recombinant LH or rFSH alone. The number of metaphase-II oocytes was essentially identical, 10.3 versus 10.4. ([PubMed][21])

**Interpretation:** Routine LH addition did not improve ovarian yield in this broad population. Results from selected older, profoundly suppressed, or poor-response populations should not automatically be generalized to all IVF patients.

## Overall interpretation

The primary studies do **not** support a conclusion that one gonadotropin is uniformly superior across all IVF populations and outcomes.

* Compared with urinary FSH, rFSH often improves dose efficiency or oocyte yield, without a consistent live-birth advantage.
* Compared with HP-hMG, rFSH commonly produces more oocytes, but HP-hMG generally produces similar ongoing-pregnancy and cumulative-live-birth outcomes. HP-hMG may reduce OHSS substantially in predicted high responders.
* Corifollitropin alfa reduces the number of injections while maintaining broadly similar reproductive outcomes.
* Individualized follitropin-delta dosing reduces extreme ovarian responses without sacrificing efficacy, but most trials confound drug and dosing-algorithm effects.
* Biosimilar trials mainly establish equivalence on oocyte yield rather than live birth.

For evidence synthesis, **cumulative live birth per initiated stimulation**, including all fresh and frozen transfers, is more clinically informative than oocyte yield or fresh-cycle pregnancy alone. Important limitations include older agonist protocols, transfer of up to three embryos in early trials, inconsistent OHSS definitions, noninferiority designs, surrogate primary endpoints, different starting doses, and frequent manufacturer sponsorship. ([PubMed][1])
