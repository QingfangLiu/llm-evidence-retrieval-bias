## Summary

Individual primary studies (mostly RCTs) comparing rFSH with other gonadotropins for ovarian stimulation in IVF/ICSI cluster into several comparison types:

**rFSH vs. highly purified hMG (HP-hMG)** — the most heavily studied comparison. Results are mixed: some trials (Andersen/Devroey 2006; the pooled analysis by Devroey et al. 2008 in RBMOnline) found higher live-birth/pregnancy rates with HP-hMG despite rFSH yielding more oocytes, while the large MEGASET antagonist trial (Devroey et al. 2012) found HP-hMG non-inferior to rFSH. The Bosch et al. 2008 antagonist-protocol RCT found comparable outcomes between the two.

**rFSH vs. urinary FSH (FSH-P/FSH-HP)** — older trials (e.g., the 2000 ICSI comparison of Gonal-F vs. Metrodin-HP) generally found no significant differences in pregnancy outcomes, with some PCOS-specific trials reporting mixed findings on embryo quality vs. oocyte yield.

**rFSH vs. rFSH+rLH combinations** — trials in poor responders (Levi Setti et al. 2005; Humaidan/ESPART 2017) and general IVF populations (Baruffi et al. 2004) show LH supplementation may help selected subgroups (e.g., poor responders) more than the general population.

**Corifollitropin alfa vs. daily rFSH** — a well-studied "long-acting" alternative, with multiple large phase 3 RCTs (Ensure, Engage, Pursue trials) showing broad equivalence in efficacy with a more convenient dosing schedule.

**Follitropin delta vs. follitropin alfa** — the newest comparison (ESTHER-1/ESTHER-2, ADAPT-1), showing similar pregnancy rates but improved individualization of ovarian response with follitropin delta.

**HP-hMG vs. rFSH+rLH** — head-to-head studies of these two LH-activity-containing regimens (Ferraretti et al.; retrospective real-life data) remain inconclusive on which LH source performs better.
