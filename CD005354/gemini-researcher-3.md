As an evidence-synthesis researcher, filtering out the noise of meta-analyses and narrative reviews to locate the foundational primary data is critical. Here is a synthesis of the key primary randomized controlled trials (RCTs) directly comparing recombinant follicle-stimulating hormone (rFSH) with other gonadotropins for ovarian stimulation in IVF.

## Recombinant FSH vs. Highly Purified hMG (HP-hMG)

When evaluating standard rFSH against highly purified human menopausal gonadotropin (HP-hMG), the primary evidence base relies heavily on protocol-specific, large-scale RCTs.

* **Long GnRH Agonist Protocols (The MERIT Trial):** Andersen et al. (2006) conducted a multinational, assessor-blind RCT involving 731 women. The study established non-inferiority—but not superiority—of HP-hMG over rFSH in terms of ongoing pregnancy rates (27% vs. 22%). Notably, the trial highlighted distinct pharmacodynamics: rFSH treatment yielded significantly **more oocytes** on average (11.8 vs. 10.0), whereas HP-hMG resulted in a **higher proportion of top-quality embryos** per cohort (11.3% vs. 9.0%) (Andersen et al., 2006).
* **GnRH Antagonist Protocols:** To examine these therapies in antagonist cycles, Bosch et al. (2008) ran an RCT with 280 patients. Consistent with the MERIT trial, they observed no significant differences in ongoing pregnancy rates between rFSH (32.1%) and HP-hMG (35.0%). However, rFSH again generated a significantly higher mean oocyte yield (14.4 vs. 11.3) and drove higher progesterone levels at the end of stimulation, while HP-hMG was associated with higher estradiol levels (Bosch et al., 2008).

## Recombinant FSH vs. Long-Acting Gonadotropins

The **ENGAGE trial** (Devroey et al., 2014) evaluated whether a single injection of corifollitropin alfa—a sustained follicle stimulant lacking LH activity—could safely replace seven daily injections of standard rFSH. In this double-blind, non-inferiority RCT involving 1,506 normal-responder patients on an antagonist protocol, the long-acting corifollitropin alfa demonstrated equivalent ongoing pregnancy rates to daily rFSH (38.9% vs. 38.1%). Both groups displayed comparable safety profiles, with no significant difference in the incidence of moderate-to-severe ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS) (Devroey et al., 2014).

## Standard rFSH vs. Individualized rFSH

Recent primary studies have compared standard rFSH (follitropin alfa) with follitropin delta, a newer rFSH produced from a human cell line that allows for algorithm-driven individualized dosing based on AMH levels and body weight.

Bosch et al. (2019) conducted a Phase 3 controlled, assessor-blind safety trial focusing on patients undergoing repeated ovarian stimulation cycles (cycles 2 and 3) following initial stimulation in the ESTHER-1 trial. The outcomes showed similar efficacies for both formulations across mean oocytes retrieved and live birth rates. Importantly, the trial confirmed the low immunogenicity and safety of follitropin delta across repeated, personalized dosing cycles without boosting immune responses (Bosch et al., 2019).
