I'll search for primary studies comparing recombinant FSH with other gonadotropins in IVF ovarian stimulation.Here's an overview of the primary literature comparing recombinant FSH (rFSH) with other gonadotropin preparations for ovarian stimulation in IVF. I focused on individual RCTs and prospective comparative studies (no reviews/meta-analyses/guidelines).

**rFSH vs. highly purified urinary FSH (HP-uFSH)**
Several multicenter RCTs found comparable outcomes between the two. A four-center trial randomizing 152 patients found no difference in oocyte numbers between HP-hFSH (mean 16.3) and rFSH (mean 17.1), with similar total gonadotropin dose and pregnancy rates. A separate 120-patient ICSI-specific RCT similarly found no significant differences in follicle numbers, oocytes retrieved, mature oocyte counts, fertilization rates, or embryo numbers between r-FSH and u-FSH-HP groups. In PCOS patients specifically, one RCT reported a significantly higher pregnancy rate favoring r-FSH (odds ratio 1.2, 95% CI 1.02–1.42), while another Iranian RCT compared the two preparations' effect on ART outcomes in PCOS patients directly.

**rFSH vs. human menopausal gonadotropin (hMG/HP-hMG)**
This comparison has produced somewhat mixed but generally favorable results for hMG on pregnancy outcomes. A large trial from IVI Valencia (280 patients, GnRH antagonist protocol) found no significant difference in ongoing pregnancy rate between hp-hMG and rFSH (35.0% vs. 32.1%), though rFSH patients yielded more oocytes (14.4 vs. 11.3). The Andersen et al. 2006 trial (Human Reproduction) is a frequently cited randomized assessor-blind comparison of highly purified hMG vs. rFSH. A subsequent integrated analysis of two RCTs (986 total patients) found similar ongoing pregnancy rates between HP-HMG and rFSH (46.1% vs. 46.1%). In PCOS patients, one RCT of 80 women found lower oocyte yield with hMG but no difference in pregnancy rates, with less OHSS in the hMG group.

**Follitropin delta vs. follitropin alfa** (newer rFSH molecules)
The ESTHER-1 trial (1,329 women) found similar ongoing pregnancy rates (30.7% vs. 31.6%) between individualized follitropin delta and conventional follitropin alfa dosing, with fewer excessive responses and OHSS-preventive measures needed in the follitropin delta group. The follow-on ESTHER-2 safety trial in repeat cycles found similar oocyte numbers, ongoing pregnancy, and live birth rates between the two across cycles 2 and 3, with low and similar rates of anti-FSH antibody formation. ADAPT-1 is another RCT directly comparing follitropin delta and follitropin alfa using conventional dosing regimens.

**Corifollitropin alfa (long-acting rFSH) vs. daily rFSH**
The Engage trial (1,506–1,509 patients) is the pivotal double-blind RCT here, finding similar ongoing pregnancy rates between corifollitropin alfa and rFSH, with significantly more oocytes retrieved with corifollitropin alfa. Related individual RCTs include the Ensure trial (lower-body-weight women) and the Pursue trial.

A few practical notes: several of these trials are sponsored by the manufacturers of the products studied (common in this field), and results vary somewhat by protocol (agonist vs. antagonist) and patient population (normo-responders, PCOS, poor responders), so it's worth checking which population each trial enrolled if you're trying to map findings onto your own situation. Your clinic's reproductive endocrinologist will be the best judge of which specific protocol fits your case — this list is meant to help you understand the evidence landscape, not to guide your own dosing decisions.
