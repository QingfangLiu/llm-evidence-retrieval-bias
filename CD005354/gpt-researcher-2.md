## Scope

I interpreted “other gonadotropins” as:

* Urinary FSH and highly purified urinary FSH, which are FSH-only preparations.
* Human menopausal gonadotropin/menotropin, particularly highly purified hMG, which supplies both FSH and LH activity.
* Other recombinant FSH preparations, including biosimilars and follitropin delta, as a related within-class comparison.

I included comparative primary studies of controlled ovarian stimulation for IVF or ICSI. I excluded IUI-only studies, non-IVF ovulation-induction studies, pharmacokinetic studies in volunteers, reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, opinions and editorials. This is a focused trial-level evidence map rather than a PRISMA-complete systematic search.

## Overall interpretation

### rFSH versus urinary or highly purified urinary FSH

The early large randomized trials generally found that rFSH produced a somewhat stronger ovarian response: more follicles or oocytes, a lower total FSH dose, or a shorter stimulation period. This did **not consistently translate into higher pregnancy or live-birth rates**.

For example:

* In the 981-participant Out trial, rFSH produced more oocytes than urinary FSH, 10.84 versus 8.95, while requiring a lower total dose and shorter treatment. Ongoing pregnancy after the fresh attempt was not significantly different; cumulative ongoing pregnancy including frozen transfers was 25.7% versus 20.4%, with *P*=0.05. ([PubMed][1])
* In the 496-participant Feronia/Apis trial, rFSH produced more oocytes, 13.1 versus 11.4, and required fewer treatment days and ampoules. Pregnancy rates were 25.1% versus 20.1%, but the difference was not statistically significant. ([PubMed][2])
* Lenton et al. found broadly comparable ovarian and pregnancy outcomes between follitropin alfa and highly purified urinary FSH. ([PubMed][3])
* In Baker et al., mean oocyte yield was 17.1 with rFSH and 16.3 with highly purified urinary FSH; clinical pregnancy was 44.7% versus 48.7%, and live birth was exactly 38.2% in both groups. ([PubMed][4])

Thus, the more reproducible difference is in **ovarian-response or dosing-efficiency endpoints**, not live birth.

### rFSH versus hMG or highly purified hMG

The large hMG trials show a different pattern. rFSH commonly produces more oocytes, while HP-hMG produces a different endocrine and embryo-development profile. Nevertheless, ongoing pregnancy and cumulative live birth have generally been similar or have met prespecified noninferiority criteria.

* The 2002 European and Israeli phase III trial enrolled 781 women. Ongoing pregnancy was 25% with HP-hMG and 22% with rFSH, with similar OHSS, miscarriage and adverse-event rates. ([PubMed][5])
* In the 731-participant MERIT trial, rFSH produced more oocytes, 11.8 versus 10.0, but HP-hMG produced a higher proportion of top-quality embryos in the local assessment. Ongoing pregnancy was 27% with HP-hMG and 22% with rFSH; HP-hMG superiority was not established, but noninferiority was. ([OUP Academic][6])
* In the 749-participant MEGASET trial using a GnRH-antagonist protocol and compulsory single-blastocyst transfer, ongoing pregnancy was 29% with HP-hMG and 27% with rFSH. Cumulative live birth after fresh and frozen transfer was 40% and 38%, respectively. ([PubMed][7])
* In 620 predicted high responders in MEGASET-HR, fresh ongoing pregnancy was 35.5% with HP-hMG and 30.7% with rFSH, while cumulative live birth was 50.6% and 51.5%. Importantly, OHSS occurred in 9.7% with HP-hMG and 21.4% with rFSH. ([PubMed][8])

The last result should not automatically be generalized to average or poor responders, because MEGASET-HR specifically required AMH ≥5 ng/mL.

### Special populations

Results in selected populations do not demonstrate a clear universal advantage for either preparation:

* In 160 women with PCOS, clinical pregnancy was 41.2% with rFSH and 45.0% with urinary FSH. ([PubMed][9])
* In 508 Chinese women over age 37, urinary FSH produced higher 2PN and grade-I embryo proportions and fewer cycles without a transferable embryo, but the principal clinical outcomes were otherwise similar. ([PubMed][10])
* Another randomized trial of women over age 39 reported better ovarian-response measures with urinary FSH, illustrating that conclusions from younger normoresponder trials may not apply directly to advanced reproductive age. ([PubMed][11])

### Comparisons among recombinant products

These studies address interchangeability or individualized dosing rather than recombinant-versus-urinary production:

* Bemfola and originator Gonal-f produced equivalent oocyte yields, 10.8 versus 10.6, with severe OHSS in 0.8% of each group. The trial was principally powered for oocyte equivalence, not live birth. ([PubMed][12])
* Ovaleap and Gonal-f produced virtually identical mean oocyte yields, 12.2 versus 12.1, with comparable safety. ([PubMed][13])
* In ESTHER-1, individualized follitropin-delta dosing produced similar ongoing pregnancy and live-birth rates to conventional follitropin-alfa dosing while reducing excessive ovarian responses and preventive interventions. ([ScienceDirect][14])
* In a subsequent 1,009-participant Asian trial, ongoing pregnancy was 31.3% with individualized follitropin delta and 25.7% with follitropin alfa; live birth was 31.3% versus 24.7%, with fewer early OHSS events or preventive interventions in the delta group. ([PubMed][15])

## Evidence-synthesis considerations

The trials should probably be stratified rather than treated as one homogeneous “rFSH versus other gonadotropins” comparison:

1. **Urinary FSH and hMG are distinct comparators.** Urinary FSH is an FSH-only preparation, whereas hMG adds LH activity.
2. **Protocol era matters.** Many early urinary-FSH trials used long GnRH-agonist protocols and allowed transfer of as many as three embryos. Modern MEGASET trials used antagonist protocols, blastocyst culture and single-embryo transfer. ([PubMed][1])
3. **Oocyte yield is not equivalent to live birth.** Many trials were designed or powered around oocyte number, ovarian response or noninferiority in ongoing pregnancy rather than cumulative live birth.
4. **Responder phenotype matters.** The comparatively large OHSS reduction with HP-hMG comes from a specifically selected high-AMH population. ([PubMed][8])
5. **Product and dose are partly confounded.** Some early comparisons used unequal nominal starting doses, while others used equal IU doses.
6. **Trial sponsorship warrants extraction.** Several pivotal product-development trials were manufacturer-sponsored or included manufacturer employees; this should be captured in a risk-of-bias assessment. The MERIT publication, for example, explicitly reports Ferring sponsorship. ([OUP Academic][6])

Across these individual studies, there is **no consistent trial-level evidence that rFSH provides a clinically important live-birth advantage over urinary FSH or HP-hMG**. rFSH often increases oocyte yield or reduces dose requirements; HP-hMG generally provides similar pregnancy and cumulative live-birth outcomes and may reduce OHSS among predicted high responders.
