Here's a synthesis of what individual primary studies show when comparing recombinant FSH (rFSH) against other gonadotropins for ovarian stimulation in IVF, followed by a reference list. I excluded systematic reviews, meta-analyses, narrative reviews, guidelines, and editorials — the studies below are original trials/case-control studies only.

## rFSH vs. highly purified/urinary hMG (HP-hMG)

Several RCTs directly randomized women to rFSH or HP-hMG using a long agonist down-regulation protocol. One trial found more oocytes were retrieved after rFSH treatment (11.8) compared with HP-hMG treatment (10.0), though a higher proportion developed into top-quality embryos with HP-hMG, and ongoing pregnancy rate per cycle was 27% with HP-hMG versus 22% with rFSH — a difference the authors said established non-inferiority of HP-hMG rather than superiority.

A pooled patient-level analysis of two RCTs (not a meta-analysis of published summary data, but an integrated raw-data reanalysis) similarly compared live birth rates from ovarian stimulation with HP-HMG versus rFSH alone in 491 and 495 women respectively, following a long down-regulation protocol.

A single-center RCT using the GnRH antagonist protocol looked specifically at embryo quality: 85 patients were randomized to rFSH and 83 to hMG, and there was no difference in pregnancy rates (31% vs. 30.1%, p=0.87).

Another registered RCT (NCT02412904) compared embryonic quality between rFSH and hMG in a GnRH antagonist protocol with 188 patients enrolled.

In women with PCOS specifically, a prospective RCT of 80 women found significantly lower peak estradiol, fewer intermediate-sized follicles, and fewer oocytes/MII oocytes with hMG, but no difference in fertilization rates, embryo quality, or pregnancy rates — and OHSS occurred only in the rFSH group (11.9%), with none in the hMG group.

## rFSH vs. urinary FSH (uFSH)

A multicenter, investigator-blind RCT of 152 patients found no difference in oocyte numbers, clinical pregnancy rate, or live birth rate between highly purified urinary FSH and rFSH (follitropin-alpha).

A smaller RCT in ICSI patients (120 patients) found no significant difference in follicle numbers or oocytes collected between r-FSH and highly purified u-FSH groups.

Another prospective RCT (70 rFSH vs. 61 uFSH) found no difference in total gonadotropin dose or stimulation length, though follicular fluid estradiol and androstenedione levels were significantly higher with rFSH.

## Combined urinary + recombinant FSH vs. rFSH alone

A prospective RCT of 119 ICSI patients found pregnancy and implantation rates were significantly higher with a combined uFSH-then-rFSH protocol than with rFSH alone (43.9% vs. 22.1% pregnancy; 27.5% vs. 13.2% implantation), and MII oocytes and grade-1 embryos also favored the combined group.

## rFSH + recombinant LH vs. hMG (or hMG + rFSH)

A multicenter RCT (Pacchiarotti et al., 2010) compared HMG versus recombinant FSH plus recombinant LH (Pergoveris) in 122 patients undergoing the long GnRH agonist protocol, with 111 reaching oocyte retrieval (58 HMG, 53 rec-LH group).

A separate Iranian RCT of 140 patients compared rFSH plus HMG added on day 6 versus rFSH plus recombinant LH added on day 6, assessing chemical/clinical pregnancy, live birth, and abortion rates.
