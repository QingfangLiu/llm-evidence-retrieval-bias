## Scope

I searched for **direct comparative primary studies** of ovarian stimulation in women undergoing IVF or ICSI. I prioritized randomized controlled trials and excluded systematic reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, opinions, editorials, IUI-only studies, and ovulation-induction studies not involving IVF/ICSI.

The evidence below concerns:

* Conventional rFSH—mainly follitropin alfa or beta—versus urinary FSH.
* rFSH versus human menopausal gonadotropin/menotropin, including highly purified hMG.
* rFSH alone versus regimens adding LH activity.
* Newer recombinant FSH formulations, including follitropin delta and long-acting corifollitropin alfa.

## Overall clinical interpretation

### rFSH versus urinary FSH or highly purified urinary FSH

Large early trials generally found that rFSH produced **approximately one to two additional oocytes**, with a lower total gonadotropin dose or slightly shorter stimulation. However, fresh-cycle ongoing pregnancy differences were usually not statistically significant. In the largest early study, cumulative ongoing pregnancy including frozen transfers was borderline higher with rFSH, but this finding came from an older treatment era with transfer of up to three embryos. ([PubMed][1])

Later comparisons with modern, highly purified urinary FSH found less difference. In a 152-patient investigator-blinded trial, mean oocyte yield was 16.3 with urinary FSH versus 17.1 with rFSH, and the live-birth rate was exactly 38.2% in both groups. ([PubMed][2])

**Interpretation:** rFSH appears more potent per administered IU than older urinary FSH preparations, but this does not consistently translate into a clinically important live-birth advantage.

### rFSH versus hMG or highly purified hMG

Because hMG provides both FSH and LH/hCG activity, the biological response differs somewhat from pure rFSH.

In the 731-patient MERIT trial, rFSH produced more oocytes—11.8 versus 10.0—but HP-hMG produced a greater proportion of top-quality embryos. Ongoing pregnancy was 27% with HP-hMG and 22% with rFSH; superiority was not established, although HP-hMG met the prespecified noninferiority criterion. ([OUP Academic][3])

In the 749-patient MEGASET antagonist trial with compulsory single-blastocyst transfer, fresh ongoing pregnancy was 29% with HP-hMG versus 27% with rFSH, and cumulative live birth after fresh and frozen transfers was 40% versus 38%. HP-hMG was noninferior. ([PubMed][4])

An earlier 727-patient phase III trial likewise found ongoing pregnancy rates of 25% with HP-hMG and 22% with rFSH, with similar OHSS, miscarriage, adverse-event, and tolerability rates. ([PubMed][5])

**Interpretation:** rFSH commonly yields modestly more oocytes, whereas HP-hMG may alter steroidogenesis and embryo-distribution measures. The larger trials do not show a convincing live-birth advantage for either preparation.

### Adding LH activity to rFSH

Routine recombinant LH supplementation has not produced consistent benefits in unselected IVF patients.

In a 431-patient ICSI trial, adding rLH from stimulation day 6 did not change the number of metaphase-II oocytes—10.3 versus 10.4—or overall implantation. An apparent age interaction favoring LH supplementation in women aged at least 35 years was based on nonsignificant subgroup results and should be considered hypothesis-generating. ([PubMed][6])

In 84 anticipated poor responders, adding rLH to rFSH did not improve ongoing pregnancy, implantation, ovarian response, oocyte yield, or embryo measures. ([PubMed][7])

A 610-patient trial comparing HP-hMG plus rFSH with rFSH alone found lower trigger-day progesterone, higher fertilization, and more fresh transfers with the combined regimen, but no significant difference in implantation, clinical pregnancy, or OHSS. ([PubMed][8])

**Interpretation:** routine LH addition is not clearly supported by these individual trials. A selected patient with profound LH deficiency, an unexpected hypo-response, advanced reproductive age, or a reproducible prior-cycle pattern is a different clinical question, but subgroup evidence is less reliable than the overall randomized comparisons.

### Newer recombinant FSH preparations

Follitropin delta is produced in a human cell line and is usually prescribed using an AMH- and body-weight–based algorithm.

In ESTHER-1, involving 1,329 women, individualized follitropin delta and conventional follitropin alfa produced similar ongoing pregnancy—30.7% versus 31.6%—and live birth—29.8% versus 30.7%. Delta produced more women in the target oocyte range, fewer excessive responses among those with higher AMH, and fewer OHSS-prevention interventions. ([ScienceDirect][9])

In a 1,009-patient Asian trial, individualized follitropin delta produced fewer total oocytes but better-calibrated response according to AMH. Early OHSS or preventive interventions occurred in 5.0% versus 9.6% with follitropin alfa. Ongoing pregnancy was noninferior, and live birth was 31.3% versus 24.7%; however, this was a single-cycle, fresh cleavage-stage transfer study sponsored by the manufacturer, so replication and cumulative outcomes matter. ([PubMed][10])

The 2025 ADAPT-1 trial compared conventional starting doses rather than individualized dosing. Both follitropin delta 15 µg and follitropin alfa 225 IU produced a mean of 9.9 oocytes; clinical pregnancy and early OHSS rates were also similar. ([PubMed][11])

### Long-acting corifollitropin alfa

Corifollitropin alfa can replace the first seven daily rFSH injections.

The 1,506-patient ENGAGE trial found ongoing pregnancy rates of 38.9% with one corifollitropin injection and 38.1% with daily rFSH, meeting noninferiority criteria. ([PubMed][12])

Among 1,390 women aged 35–42 years in PURSUE, live birth was 21.3% with corifollitropin alfa versus 23.4% with daily rFSH; oocyte yield and overall OHSS were similar, and noninferiority was established. ([PubMed][13])

A 2026 Chinese phase III trial of another corifollitropin alfa preparation found 12.1 versus 10.9 oocytes compared with daily rFSH, meeting the oocyte-yield noninferiority criterion. Clinical and ongoing pregnancy were numerically lower with corifollitropin, but the study was not reported as demonstrating superiority or noninferiority for those pregnancy endpoints. ([PubMed][14])

## Practical bottom line

For most IVF patients, the choice among conventional rFSH, highly purified urinary FSH, and HP-hMG is more likely to affect **oocyte number, dose consumption, endocrine profile, convenience, and cost** than the probability of live birth.

The primary studies support these conclusions:

* **Conventional rFSH versus urinary FSH:** rFSH is often more efficient for follicular recruitment, particularly versus older urinary preparations, but live-birth superiority is unproven.
* **rFSH versus HP-hMG:** rFSH usually yields slightly more oocytes; HP-hMG produces different endocrine and embryo-quality patterns; pregnancy and live-birth outcomes are broadly comparable.
* **Routine LH supplementation:** no reproducible benefit in unselected patients or consistently defined poor responders.
* **Individualized follitropin delta:** similar pregnancy efficacy with better control of excessive response in several trials, especially in women with higher AMH.
* **Corifollitropin alfa:** comparable efficacy to daily rFSH with fewer injections, provided the patient fits the studied age, weight, ovarian-reserve, and antagonist-protocol populations.

Important limitations include manufacturer sponsorship in many pivotal trials, inadequate power for live birth in older studies, changing embryo-transfer practices, differing GnRH protocols, and inconsistent definitions of OHSS and poor ovarian response.
