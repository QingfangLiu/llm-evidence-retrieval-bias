Here is a synthesis of individual primary studies comparing recombinant follicle-stimulating hormone (rFSH) to other gonadotropins for ovarian stimulation in IVF/ICSI. Per your criteria, this excludes systematic reviews and meta-analyses, focusing entirely on randomized controlled trials (RCTs).

## rFSH vs. Human Menopausal Gonadotropin (hMG)

Human menopausal gonadotropin (hMG) and its highly purified form (hp-hMG) are derived from the urine of postmenopausal women and contain both FSH and LH activity, whereas rFSH provides purely FSH activity. Primary studies frequently compare these two to determine if the LH activity in hMG impacts oocyte yield or pregnancy outcomes.

* **Oocyte Yield:** RCTs consistently demonstrate that rFSH tends to yield a higher number of retrieved oocytes compared to hMG. Hompes et al. (2008) found that patients receiving highly purified hMG had statistically significantly fewer oocytes retrieved than those receiving rFSH. Similarly, a smaller RCT by Chapon et al. (2020) analyzing the GnRH antagonist protocol confirmed that hMG results in fewer oocytes and mature (MII) oocytes compared to rFSH, a phenomenon often attributed to the LH effect in hMG promoting follicular atresia of smaller follicles.
* **Pregnancy Rates:** Despite the difference in oocyte yield, primary trials generally show no statistically significant difference in clinical or ongoing pregnancy rates. Hompes et al. (2008) reported ongoing pregnancy rates per started cycle of 26.3% for highly purified hMG and 25.2% for rFSH. Chapon et al. (2020) also reported similar pregnancy rates between the two protocols, suggesting that the lower oocyte yield with hMG does not negatively compromise the final clinical outcome.

## rFSH vs. Corifollitropin Alfa (Long-Acting Recombinant FSH)

Corifollitropin alfa is a sustained follicle stimulant created by fusing the C-terminal peptide of the hCG beta-subunit to human FSH. A single injection can replace seven days of daily rFSH injections, reducing the treatment burden for patients.

* **Clinical Efficacy & Non-Inferiority:** Large-scale primary studies have established that corifollitropin alfa is non-inferior to daily rFSH. In a major double-blind RCT comprising 1,506 patients, Devroey et al. (2014) found an ongoing pregnancy rate of 38.9% with corifollitropin alfa versus 38.1% for daily rFSH, with identical rates of moderate-to-severe ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS).
* **Oocyte Retrieval:** Interestingly, Devroey et al. (2014) noted a slightly higher follicular response and number of cumulus-oocyte complexes retrieved with corifollitropin alfa. However, in specific subpopulations, such as women aged 35–42, Vuong et al. (2017) found no significant difference in the number of oocytes retrieved between a single dose of corifollitropin alfa and daily rFSH, nor were there any differences in live birth rates (30.5% vs 32.0%).
