Facing surgery for lung cancer is a major event, and it is completely understandable that you want to weigh the pros and cons of adding new treatments like immune checkpoint inhibitors (immunotherapy) to your plan.

Immunotherapy works by taking the "brakes" off your immune system so it can recognize and attack cancer cells. In recent years, primary clinical trials have tested using these drugs either **neoadjuvantly** (before surgery) or **adjuvantly** (after surgery) to reduce the chance of the cancer returning.

Here is a breakdown of the benefits and harms based on major, primary phase 3 randomized controlled trials.

---

## Approach 1: Immunotherapy Before and After Surgery (With Chemotherapy)

One major strategy is giving immunotherapy alongside chemotherapy before surgery, and then continuing the immunotherapy alone after surgery (a perioperative approach).

The **KEYNOTE-671** trial evaluated this approach using the immunotherapy drug **pembrolizumab** in patients with early-stage, resectable non-small-cell lung cancer (Spicer et al., 2024). Half the patients received pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy before surgery and pembrolizumab alone after, while the other half received chemotherapy plus a placebo.

### Benefits

* **Longer survival:** Patients who received pembrolizumab lived significantly longer overall compared to those who only received chemotherapy and a placebo (Spicer et al., 2024).
* **Fewer recurrences:** The immunotherapy group had a much longer "event-free survival," meaning significantly more time passed without the cancer returning, growing, or preventing surgery.
* **More effective tumor shrinkage:** Adding pembrolizumab greatly increased the chances of a major pathological response—meaning that when the surgeon removed the tissue, they found very few active cancer cells remaining.

### Harms

* **Treatment side effects:** Both groups experienced standard side effects from the chemotherapy (such as nausea, fatigue, and lowered white blood cell counts).
* **Immune-related risks:** Because pembrolizumab overstimulates the immune system to fight cancer, it can cause the body to mistakenly attack its own healthy organs. This can lead to conditions like thyroid dysfunction, pneumonitis (lung inflammation), or skin rashes. The trial noted that while the safety profile was manageable, these immune side effects require careful monitoring (Spicer et al., 2024).

---

## Approach 2: Immunotherapy After Surgery (Following Chemotherapy)

Another strategy focuses on using immunotherapy only *after* a patient has fully recovered from surgery and standard post-surgery chemotherapy.

The **IMpower010** trial tested this by giving the immunotherapy drug **atezolizumab** to patients who had successfully undergone surgery and standard chemotherapy, comparing them to patients who received best supportive care (Felip et al., 2023).

### Benefits

* **Delayed recurrence:** Atezolizumab significantly improved "disease-free survival," meaning patients lived longer without the cancer coming back.
* **Targeted overall survival benefits:** The trial found that the overall survival benefit was strongest in patients whose tumors expressed a high level of a specific protein biomarker called PD-L1. For patients with high PD-L1, the risk of death was substantially reduced compared to those receiving only supportive care (Felip et al., 2023).

### Harms

* **Added toxicity:** While this approach avoids complicating the surgery itself, patients taking atezolizumab still faced immune-related side effects. About 10.7% of patients in the immunotherapy group experienced severe (grade 3 or 4) adverse events, and a very small fraction (0.8%) experienced fatal side effects linked to the treatment (Felip et al., 2023).
* **Endocrine and organ issues:** Similar to other immune checkpoint inhibitors, common side effects included thyroid issues, liver enzyme changes, and rash.

---

## Considerations for Older Adults

While these primary trials included older adults and demonstrated clear statistical benefits, deciding whether to add immunotherapy involves looking at your entire health picture. Immunotherapy is generally better tolerated than traditional chemotherapy, but immune-related side effects can sometimes be more taxing if you have pre-existing autoimmune conditions, heart disease, or declining organ function.

Your oncologist can help you look at your tumor's specific biomarkers (like its PD-L1 status) to ensure the potential benefits outweigh the added risks for your specific lifestyle and goals.
