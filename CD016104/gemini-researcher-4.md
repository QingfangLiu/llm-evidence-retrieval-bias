To support your evidence synthesis on the use of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) for resectable (early-stage) non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) in older adults, I have identified the pivotal primary clinical trials.

Because dedicated, standalone randomized trials exclusively enrolling older adults are exceedingly rare in this specific surgical oncology setting, the most robust primary evidence is derived from the pre-specified age subgroup analyses (typically defined as patients ≥65 years of age) within the landmark Phase 3 randomized controlled trials. I have strictly excluded all systematic reviews, meta-analyses, and narrative reviews, limiting this synthesis solely to the primary interventional studies.

### Benefits and Harms: Neoadjuvant and Perioperative Regimens

Recent primary trials have evaluated administering ICIs *before* surgery (neoadjuvant) or extending them *after* surgery (perioperative), comparing them directly against chemotherapy plus placebo or chemotherapy alone.

* **Efficacy (Benefits):** The addition of ICIs (like nivolumab or pembrolizumab) to neoadjuvant chemotherapy has driven significant improvements in event-free survival (EFS) and pathologic complete response (pCR) rates. In the Phase 3 CheckMate 816 trial, neoadjuvant nivolumab plus chemotherapy reduced the risk of disease progression, recurrence, or death compared to chemotherapy alone (Forde et al., 2022). Similarly, the KEYNOTE-671 trial demonstrated that perioperative pembrolizumab (given before and after surgery) significantly prolonged EFS and increased pCR rates compared to neoadjuvant chemotherapy plus placebo (Wakelee et al., 2023). Within the age-stratified subgroup analyses of these primary trials, the survival and pathologic benefits observed in the overall population were generally maintained in older adults (≥65 years).
* **Safety and Toxicity (Harms):** A critical concern in older populations is whether the addition of immunotherapy causes surgical delays or unmanageable toxicity. In CheckMate 816, the addition of nivolumab did not impede the feasibility of surgery, and the incidence of Grade 3 or 4 treatment-related adverse events (AEs) was comparable between the ICI arm and the control arm (Forde et al., 2022). However, perioperative regimens do carry the risk of immune-mediated adverse events (e.g., pneumonitis, hypothyroidism, and severe rash). Single-arm Phase 2 primary studies tracking long-term outcomes have confirmed that while neoadjuvant atezolizumab or pembrolizumab can be safely administered, immune-related toxicities require close monitoring, though they rarely permanently preclude definitive surgery (Henick et al., 2024; Wood et al., 2025).

### Benefits and Harms: Adjuvant Regimens

Other primary trials have focused exclusively on the adjuvant setting, administering ICIs only *after* complete surgical resection and standard adjuvant chemotherapy.

* **Efficacy (Benefits):** The Phase 3 IMpower010 trial evaluated adjuvant atezolizumab compared directly to best supportive care (no active treatment) following resection and chemotherapy. The study established a distinct disease-free survival (DFS) benefit for patients receiving atezolizumab, particularly those whose tumors expressed PD-L1 on 1% or more of tumor cells (Felip et al., 2021). The age subgroups (≥65 years) demonstrated a DFS benefit consistent with the younger cohorts.
* **Safety and Toxicity (Harms):** The primary harms in the adjuvant setting revolve around chronic immune-related AEs. In IMpower010, Grade 3 or 4 AEs were higher in the atezolizumab group (22%) compared to the best supportive care group (12%), with a notable portion of patients requiring treatment discontinuation or systemic corticosteroids due to immune-mediated events (Felip et al., 2021).
