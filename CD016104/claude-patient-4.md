## Immune checkpoint inhibitors around lung surgery: what the trials show

For resectable, localized (stage I–IIIB, non-metastatic) NSCLC, checkpoint inhibitors have been tested in three timing patterns versus chemotherapy-alone or placebo: **before surgery (neoadjuvant)**, **after surgery (adjuvant)**, and **both before and after (perioperative)**. Below is what the major randomized, placebo/control-controlled trials found, organized by approach.

### Neoadjuvant only (given before surgery)
**CheckMate 816** (nivolumab + chemo vs. chemo alone, stage IB–IIIA): Adding nivolumab before surgery reduced the risk of disease recurrence, progression, or death by 37%, with median event-free survival of 31.6 months versus 20.8 months for chemotherapy alone. Long-term follow-up showed this is the only neoadjuvant-only immunotherapy phase 3 trial to demonstrate a statistically and clinically significant overall-survival benefit at 5 years for a resectable solid tumor. Importantly, it had no adverse effect on surgical feasibility or surgical outcomes.

### Adjuvant only (given after surgery)
- **IMpower010** (atezolizumab vs. best supportive care after surgery + chemo, stage IB–IIIA): Showed a disease-free survival benefit with atezolizumab versus best supportive care after adjuvant chemotherapy, with pronounced benefit in patients whose tumors expressed PD-L1 on 1% or more of cells, and no new safety signals.
- **KEYNOTE-091 (PEARLS)** (pembrolizumab vs. placebo after surgery, PD-L1-unselected): Pembrolizumab significantly improved disease-free survival compared with placebo, without new safety signals, in a PD-L1-unselected population. At final analysis, median DFS was 53.8 months with pembrolizumab vs. 43.0 months with placebo, though overall survival benefit was not statistically confirmed.

### Perioperative (immunotherapy both before and after surgery)
- **KEYNOTE-671** (pembrolizumab, stage II–IIIB): perioperative pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy vs. placebo plus chemotherapy, each followed by surgery and continued pembrolizumab/placebo.
- **CheckMate 77T** (nivolumab, stage IIA–IIIB): the addition of perioperative nivolumab to neoadjuvant chemotherapy resulted in significantly longer event-free survival than perioperative placebo (hazard ratio 0.58; P<0.001), with a higher pathological complete response rate (25.3% vs. 4.7%) and no new safety signals; surgical outcomes were similar between groups. However, there were two treatment-related deaths in the nivolumab group, both from pneumonitis occurring after neoadjuvant therapy.
- **AEGEAN** (durvalumab, stage IIA–IIIB[N2]): median event-free survival favored durvalumab (HR 0.69), with a clinically meaningful disease-free survival benefit and a trend toward improved overall survival, and severe (grade 3/4) adverse events occurred in similar proportions in both groups (42.4% with durvalumab vs. 43.2% with placebo).
- **NEOTORCH** (toripalimab, stage II–III, China): among stage III patients, toripalimab reduced the risk of disease recurrence, progression, or death versus chemotherapy alone (HR 0.40), with median EFS not reached vs. 15.1 months for placebo.

### Bottom line on benefits and harms
- **Benefit:** Across these trials, adding a checkpoint inhibitor at some point around surgery consistently lowers the chance of the cancer coming back or progressing (better event-free/disease-free survival), and in the trials with longest follow-up (CheckMate 816, and increasingly AEGEAN/CheckMate 77T), there's a trend toward or statistically confirmed improvement in overall survival. Tumors do not appear to become harder to remove, and pathological complete response rates (no viable cancer left at surgery) are markedly higher with the checkpoint inhibitor added before surgery.
- **Harms:** Checkpoint inhibitors add immune-related side effects (thyroid, skin, lung, colon, liver, and other organ inflammation) on top of chemotherapy toxicity. Most trials report no major increase in serious complications or surgical delays, but rare deaths from immune-related pneumonitis have occurred (CheckMate 77T). Grade 3/4 adverse event rates are similar to chemotherapy alone in some trials (AEGEAN) but higher in others (CheckMate 816 Japanese subgroup: 59% vs. 43%).
