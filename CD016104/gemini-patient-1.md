Facing a lung cancer diagnosis and preparing for surgery is a lot to navigate, and it is completely understandable that you want to carefully weigh the pros and cons of adding new treatments to your plan.

In recent years, immune checkpoint inhibitors—drugs that help your own immune system recognize and attack cancer cells—have completely changed how early-stage (resectable) non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) is treated. Depending on your specific situation, these drugs can be given **neoadjuvantly** (before surgery), **adjuvantly** (after surgery), or **perioperatively** (both before and after).

Here is a breakdown of the benefits and harms of adding immune checkpoint inhibitors to standard chemotherapy and surgery, drawn directly from the major primary clinical trials.

## The Benefits of Adding Immunotherapy

Major clinical trials have compared patients who received standard chemotherapy before or after surgery to those who received chemotherapy plus an immune checkpoint inhibitor. The studies show three main benefits:

* **Clearing the tumor before surgery:** When immunotherapy is given alongside chemotherapy before surgery, patients have a significantly higher chance of a **pathological complete response**. This means that when the surgeon removes the tissue, the pathologist finds zero active cancer cells remaining. For example, the CheckMate 816 trial found that 24% of patients receiving the immunotherapy nivolumab plus chemotherapy achieved this complete response, compared to only 2.2% of those receiving chemotherapy alone (Forde et al., 2022). Another trial testing durvalumab saw a similar leap from 4.3% to 17.2% (Heymach et al., 2023).
* **Preventing the cancer from returning:** A central goal of treatment is extending **event-free survival**—living without the cancer returning, worsening, or preventing surgery. Giving immunotherapy either before surgery (Forde et al., 2022; Heymach et al., 2023) or after surgery (Felip et al., 2023) significantly delays or prevents the cancer from coming back compared to standard chemotherapy or observation alone.
* **Helping patients live longer:** The ultimate goal is extending **overall survival**. Recent data shows that a perioperative approach—giving the immunotherapy pembrolizumab before surgery with chemotherapy, and continuing it after surgery—significantly improves overall survival compared to receiving only chemotherapy and a placebo (Spicer et al., 2024).

## The Harms and Potential Risks

While immunotherapy has proven highly effective, it introduces new risks because it stimulates the immune system, which can sometimes mistakenly attack healthy tissues.

* **Immune-related side effects:** The most common serious side effects are immune-related. These can include severe fatigue, skin rashes, thyroid dysfunction, and immune-mediated pneumonitis (inflammation of the lungs), which requires careful monitoring by your care team (Felip et al., 2023).
* **Increased severe adverse events:** Adding immunotherapy to chemotherapy slightly increases the overall rate of severe (Grade 3 or higher) side effects. For instance, in the KEYNOTE-671 trial, 45% of patients in the combined immunotherapy group experienced severe treatment-related side effects compared to 38% in the placebo group (Spicer et al., 2024).
* **Impact on surgery:** A major concern when giving treatments before surgery is whether side effects will make the patient too sick to actually have the operation. Fortunately, studies have shown that adding immunotherapy to chemotherapy does *not* significantly increase surgery cancellations, nor does it make the surgery harder to perform or recover from (Forde et al., 2022).

## What This Means for Older Adults

Because you are an older adult, evaluating these treatments requires balancing their proven power to eliminate cancer with your own baseline health. While these clinical trials included older patients, the immune system and the body's ability to tolerate aggressive combined treatments can change with age. Your medical oncologist and surgeon will look closely at your lung function, heart health, and any other conditions you might have to ensure the added survival benefits outweigh the risks of immune-related side effects.
