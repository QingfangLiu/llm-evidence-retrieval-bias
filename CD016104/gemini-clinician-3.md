Integrating immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) into the treatment of early-stage, resectable non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) has fundamentally shifted the standard of care. For older adults, the decision often requires carefully balancing the survival benefits of systemic therapy against the risk of surgical delays or life-altering toxicities.

Based strictly on the primary Phase 3 randomized controlled trials evaluating neoadjuvant, adjuvant, and perioperative ICIs, here is a breakdown of the observed benefits and harms.

## Benefits of Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors

### 1. Significant Pathological Response

When ICIs are given *before* surgery (neoadjuvant) alongside platinum-based chemotherapy, they profoundly increase the likelihood of clearing the tumor.

* **Pathological Complete Response (pCR):** Adding an ICI increases pCR (defined as 0% viable tumor in resected tissue) by a substantial margin. For instance, neoadjuvant nivolumab plus chemotherapy yielded a pCR of 24.0% compared to just 2.2% with chemotherapy alone (Forde et al., 2022).
* Similar step-up responses are seen across other agents, such as perioperative pembrolizumab (18.1% vs. 4.0%) (Wakelee et al., 2023) and perioperative durvalumab (17.2% vs. 4.3%) (Heymach et al., 2023).

### 2. Improved Event-Free and Disease-Free Survival

Whether given before surgery, after surgery, or both, ICIs delay disease recurrence.

* **Perioperative and Neoadjuvant:** Approaches utilizing ICIs before and after surgery have shown dramatic improvements in Event-Free Survival (EFS). In the CheckMate 77T trial, 18-month EFS was 70.2% for perioperative nivolumab versus 50.0% for chemotherapy alone (Cascone et al., 2024). Perioperative pembrolizumab similarly demonstrated a 24-month EFS of 62.4% vs. 40.6% for controls (Wakelee et al., 2023).
* **Adjuvant Only:** In patients who undergo primary resection followed by chemotherapy, the addition of up to 1 year of adjuvant atezolizumab significantly improves Disease-Free Survival (DFS), particularly in patients with PD-L1 positive tumors (Felip et al., 2021).

### 3. Efficacy in Older Adults

Age-stratified subgroup analyses within these primary trials indicate that patients aged ≥65 years derive EFS, DFS, and pCR benefits consistent with the broader intention-to-treat populations. While overall survival (OS) data in older subgroups takes longer to mature due to competing non-cancer mortalities, the ability of ICIs to prevent local recurrence and distant metastasis remains robust in this demographic (Heymach et al., 2023; Wakelee et al., 2023).

## Harms and Safety Profile

### 1. Treatment-Related Adverse Events (TRAEs)

Adding immunotherapy to neoadjuvant chemotherapy does not drastically increase the overall rate of severe (Grade 3 or 4) general toxicities compared to chemotherapy alone.

* In the AEGEAN trial, Grade 3 or 4 adverse events occurred in 42.4% of the durvalumab group and 43.2% of the placebo group (Heymach et al., 2023).
* Similarly, the CheckMate 816 trial reported Grade 3 or 4 events in 33.5% of the nivolumab group and 36.9% of the chemotherapy-alone group (Forde et al., 2022).

### 2. Immune-Mediated Toxicities

While overall severe adverse event rates remain stable, ICIs introduce distinct immune-mediated risks (e.g., pneumonitis, thyroiditis, colitis, and adrenal insufficiency) that require active clinical surveillance. These adverse events often dictate whether a patient can tolerate the full adjuvant continuation phase (Felip et al., 2021; Wakelee et al., 2023).

### 3. Surgical Feasibility and Delays

A major clinical concern with neoadjuvant treatment is that toxicity or unexpected tumor progression might cost a patient their window for curative surgery. Reassuringly, the primary data shows that adding an ICI does not compromise surgical feasibility.

* In CheckMate 816, 83.2% of patients receiving neoadjuvant nivolumab plus chemotherapy successfully underwent surgery, compared to 75.4% in the chemotherapy-alone group (Forde et al., 2022).
* Perioperative pembrolizumab data also demonstrated that the surgical cancellation rate was not elevated by the addition of the checkpoint inhibitor (Wakelee et al., 2023).
