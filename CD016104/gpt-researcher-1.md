## Scope and eligibility

I treated the comparator as **absence of an immune checkpoint inhibitor**, permitting identical chemotherapy in both groups. Thus, studies comparing chemo-immunotherapy with chemotherapy alone were eligible, as were adjuvant ICI versus placebo or observation.

I included randomized primary studies with reported clinical results through **July 25, 2026**. I excluded single-arm trials, nonrandomized cohorts, design-only publications, and trials in which every randomized group received an ICI. This yielded **13 randomized studies: 12 with full peer-reviewed reports and one available only as a conference abstract**.

The disease spectrum was resectable stage IB tumors ≥4 cm through selected stage IIIB/N2 disease, depending on the staging edition and trial.

## Main conclusions

### Older-adult applicability

None of the studies was designed specifically for older adults, and none was powered to establish efficacy or safety in a geriatric subgroup. Most enrolled surgically fit participants with ECOG performance status 0–1. Older-adult evidence therefore comes from subgroup forest plots rather than dedicated comparisons.

Adults aged ≥65 made up a substantial proportion of several large trials, but data for those aged ≥75, frailty, cognition, multimorbidity, functional status, or geriatric syndromes were generally unavailable. TD-FOREKNOW excluded patients older than 70. ANVIL is particularly relevant because median ages were 66 and 67 years, but it found no benefit from adjuvant nivolumab. ([PubMed][1])

Across the perioperative trials, age ≥65 subgroup estimates generally followed the favorable overall event-free-survival result, without convincing evidence of an age–treatment interaction. However, these were descriptive, underpowered subgroup analyses with wide confidence intervals. **No study has established a statistically powered age-specific overall-survival benefit**, and harms were rarely reported separately by age. The findings are therefore most applicable to **fit older surgical candidates**, not necessarily frail or highly comorbid older adults. ([PubMed][2])

### Neoadjuvant or perioperative treatment

All eight randomized neoadjuvant/perioperative studies used an ICI **with chemotherapy**; I found no reported randomized trial of neoadjuvant ICI monotherapy versus placebo or no treatment meeting the criteria.

These studies consistently showed large improvements in pathological complete response and event-free survival. Overall-survival benefit is now established for CheckMate 816, KEYNOTE-671, and RATIONALE-315, with supportive but less precise phase 2 evidence from NADIM II. Overall survival remains immature in several other trials.

## Benefit–harm interpretation for older adults

For **fit older adults selected for surgery**, the most consistent evidence supports adding an ICI to neoadjuvant chemotherapy, with or without postoperative ICI. The improvement is clearest for pathological response and event-free survival, and several regimens now also have overall-survival evidence.

Adjuvant ICI started only after upfront surgery has a less dependable benefit. IMpower010 suggests benefit concentrated in PD-L1-positive—particularly PD-L1-high—disease, and KEYNOTE-091 improved DFS in the overall population. In contrast, BR.31 and ANVIL were convincingly negative. The results should therefore not be interpreted as a class-wide adjuvant effect.

The main added harms are immune-mediated pneumonitis, colitis, hepatitis, endocrinopathies, dermatitis, and rare myocarditis or fatal immune events. When ICIs are combined with chemotherapy, myelosuppression and other chemotherapy toxicities dominate many grade ≥3 event counts. Across perioperative phase 3 trials, severe-event rates were often similar to or moderately higher than control, but discontinuation and prolonged immune toxicity remain clinically relevant. Age-specific rates of hospitalization, functional decline, postoperative recovery, loss of independence, and quality of life were generally not reported. ([PubMed][11])
