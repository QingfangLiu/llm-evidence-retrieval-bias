## Neoadjuvant only (given before surgery, none after)

**CheckMate 816** (nivolumab + chemo before surgery vs. chemo alone, stage IB–IIIA): This phase 3 trial found that neoadjuvant nivolumab with chemotherapy reduced the risk of disease recurrence, progression, or death by 37% compared with chemotherapy alone, with median event-free survival of about 31.6 months versus 20.8 months. At the final 5-year analysis, nivolumab plus chemotherapy continued to improve event-free survival, with median EFS of 59.6 months versus 21.1 months and 5-year EFS rates of 49% versus 34%, and it was the only neoadjuvant-only immunotherapy phase 3 trial to show a statistically and clinically significant overall survival benefit at 5 years for a resectable solid tumor. Surgery itself was not compromised by the added drug.

## Perioperative (given both before and after surgery)

**KEYNOTE-671** (pembrolizumab, stage II–IIIB): Neoadjuvant pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy, followed by surgery and adjuvant pembrolizumab, significantly improved event-free survival compared with chemotherapy plus placebo, with a 42% relative improvement, and median EFS was not reached versus 17 months in the placebo group. Overall survival was also significantly better with pembrolizumab. In a subgroup limited to stage II disease, event-free survival, overall survival, and pathological response rates all favored pembrolizumab, with grade 3–4 treatment-related adverse events in 50.0% versus 40.5% of patients — relevant since stage II disease is more common in older patients being evaluated for surgery.

**AEGEAN** (durvalumab, stage II–IIIB): In this double-blind trial of 802 patients randomized to durvalumab plus chemotherapy or placebo plus chemotherapy before surgery, followed by adjuvant durvalumab or placebo, perioperative durvalumab significantly increased event-free survival and pathological complete response, with a safety profile consistent with the individual agents. A pathologic complete response occurred in 17.2% of patients on durvalumab versus a much lower rate on placebo.

**CheckMate 77T** (nivolumab, stage IIA–IIIB): Perioperative nivolumab produced a statistically significant event-free survival benefit versus placebo (median EFS not reached vs. 18.4 months; HR 0.58), with improved pathological complete response as well and FDA approval based on these results. An exploratory analysis in patients with N2 (mediastinal lymph node) involvement found nivolumab improved event-free survival (1-year rate 70% vs. 45%) and pathological complete response (22.0% vs. 5.6%) even in this higher-risk group. Quality-of-life data showed perioperative nivolumab did not compromise health-related quality of life.

**NEOTORCH** (toripalimab, stage II–III, China): Toripalimab plus chemotherapy reduced the risk of disease recurrence, progression, or death by 60% compared with chemotherapy alone (median EFS not reached vs. 15.1 months).

## Neoadjuvant/perioperative in stage III specifically (smaller randomized phase 2 trials)

**NADIM II** (nivolumab, stage IIIA–IIIB, Spain): This randomized phase 2 trial found an overall survival rate of 85.3% at 24 months with neoadjuvant nivolumab plus chemotherapy versus 64.8% with chemotherapy alone, and a pathologic complete response rate of 36.2% versus 6.8%.

## Adjuvant only (started after surgery, no drug given beforehand)

**IMpower010** (atezolizumab after adjuvant chemo, stage IB–IIIA): Adjuvant atezolizumab improved disease-free survival compared with best supportive care after chemotherapy, with a pronounced benefit in patients whose tumors expressed PD-L1 on at least 1% of cells. In the PD-L1 ≥1% stage II–IIIA subgroup, the hazard ratio for disease-free survival was 0.66.

**PEARLS/KEYNOTE-091** (pembrolizumab, stage IB–IIIA, any PD-L1 level): Pembrolizumab significantly improved disease-free survival compared with placebo without new safety signals, regardless of PD-L1 expression level, with median disease-free survival of 53.6 months versus 42.0 months. Benefit in the very-high PD-L1 (≥50%) subgroup was directionally favorable but did not reach the predefined statistical threshold at the primary analysis.

## What this generally means for benefits and harms

Across these trials, adding a checkpoint inhibitor (alone or with chemo, before and/or after surgery) fairly consistently:
- **Improves** event-free/disease-free survival and, in several trials (CheckMate 816, KEYNOTE-671, CheckMate 77T, NADIM II), overall survival
- **Increases** the chance of a pathological complete or major response
- **Does not appear to compromise** the ability to have surgery or surgical safety in most trials
- **Increases** grade 3–4 treatment-related and immune-related adverse events compared with chemotherapy alone (roughly 10 percentage points higher in several trials), including immune-mediated toxicities affecting the lungs, thyroid, skin, GI tract, or other organs that can occasionally be serious

None of these trials specifically restricted enrollment by age, though older adults with adequate performance status were generally eligible; age-specific subgroup and toxicity data exist in some trial supplements but weren't the focus of these searches. This is general information from published trials, not personalized advice — your oncology and surgical team can help weigh these trade-offs against your specific health status, tumor stage, and PD-L1 status.
