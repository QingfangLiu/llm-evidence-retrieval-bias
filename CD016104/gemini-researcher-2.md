As an evidence-synthesis researcher, you are navigating a rapidly evolving space. The paradigm for early-stage (resectable) non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) has fundamentally shifted over the last few years from surgery and chemotherapy alone to the integration of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs).

Because you strictly require primary data—avoiding meta-analyses and reviews—the best available evidence comes from the landmark phase 3 randomized controlled trials. In these primary studies, older adults (typically defined in the prespecified subgroup analyses as patients aged $\geq$ 65 years) generally make up 35% to 50% of the study populations.

Here is a breakdown of the benefits and harms of ICIs in the neoadjuvant, adjuvant, and perioperative settings based entirely on primary trial data.

## Neoadjuvant (Before Surgery)

Using ICIs prior to surgery aims to leverage the intact primary tumor to stimulate a robust systemic immune response, reducing micrometastatic disease.

* **Benefits:** The CheckMate 816 trial evaluated neoadjuvant nivolumab plus platinum-based chemotherapy against chemotherapy alone (Forde et al., 2022). The primary data showed a profound increase in the pathologic complete response (pCR) rate—meaning no viable tumor remained at the time of surgery—jumping from 2.2% with chemotherapy to 24.0% with the nivolumab combination. This translates directly to significantly longer event-free survival (EFS). Crucially, the prespecified subgroup analysis for age showed that the EFS and pCR benefits favored the chemoimmunotherapy arm for patients both under and over age 65.
* **Harms:** The addition of nivolumab did not significantly impede the feasibility of surgery (83.2% in the experimental arm underwent surgery vs. 75.4% in the control arm) or significantly increase grade 3 or 4 treatment-related adverse events (33.5% vs. 36.9%) (Forde et al., 2022). However, for older or frail adults, managing preoperative immune-related adverse events (irAEs)—such as pneumonitis or endocrinopathies—remains a critical clinical concern, as severe toxicity can cause surgical delays or render a patient medically inoperable.

## Adjuvant (After Surgery)

Adjuvant immunotherapy is administered to eradicate residual microscopic disease after the physical tumor bulk has been surgically removed.

* **Benefits:** The IMpower010 trial evaluated adjuvant atezolizumab against best supportive care following resection and adjuvant chemotherapy (Felip et al., 2021). It demonstrated a significant disease-free survival (DFS) benefit, particularly in patients whose tumors expressed PD-L1 ($\geq$ 1%). Subgroup analyses confirmed that the risk reduction for disease recurrence extended to older adults ($\geq$ 65 years).
* **Harms:** The safety profile in IMpower010 was consistent with known atezolizumab toxicities, but the primary data emphasize that patients subjected to the compounding toll of surgery, platinum chemotherapy, and then up to a year of immunotherapy face cumulative fatigue, thyroid dysfunction, and skin toxicities (Felip et al., 2021). For older populations, this prolonged treatment duration requires careful monitoring of quality of life and functional decline.

## Perioperative (Before and After Surgery)

The most recent wave of primary studies investigates a "sandwich" approach: giving chemoimmunotherapy before surgery, followed by adjuvant immunotherapy after recovery.

* **Benefits:** Three massive primary trials—KEYNOTE-671, CheckMate 77T, and AEGEAN—have defined this space. KEYNOTE-671 demonstrated that perioperative pembrolizumab significantly improves overall survival (OS) and EFS compared to neoadjuvant chemotherapy alone (Spicer et al., 2024). Similarly, CheckMate 77T showed an 18-month EFS of 70.2% for perioperative nivolumab versus 50.0% for chemotherapy (Cascone et al., 2024), and the AEGEAN trial confirmed significant pCR and EFS improvements using perioperative durvalumab (Heymach et al., 2023). Across all three primary studies, forest plots for EFS consistently show benefit for older subgroups ($\geq$ 65 years).
* **Harms:** The primary concern with the perioperative approach in older adults is the cumulative toxicity burden. In CheckMate 77T, grade 3 or 4 treatment-related adverse events occurred in 32.5% of the nivolumab group (Cascone et al., 2024). In KEYNOTE-671, the pembrolizumab arm experienced higher rates of serious immune-mediated adverse events, including pneumonitis and severe skin reactions, which require aggressive corticosteroid management—a particular vulnerability for older adults with comorbidities (Spicer et al., 2024).
