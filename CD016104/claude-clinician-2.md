## Overview of the evidence base

The primary evidence for perioperative immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) in resectable, limited-stage (stage IB–IIIB) NSCLC comes from a set of large, randomized phase II/III trials. They fall into three treatment strategies:

**1. Neoadjuvant-only ICI + chemotherapy (vs. chemotherapy alone)**

- CheckMate 816 showed that three cycles of neoadjuvant nivolumab plus chemotherapy had a significant benefit over chemotherapy alone with respect to event-free survival (EFS) and pathological complete response (pCR), with no adverse effect on surgical feasibility or outcomes. The final analysis showed a nearly 30% reduction in risk of death compared with chemotherapy alone. Common immune-related adverse events included rash (8.5%) and low-grade pneumonitis (1.1%); surgery delays and cancellations were similar between arms.
- NADIM II (randomized phase II, stage IIIA) — neoadjuvant nivolumab plus chemotherapy produced a 24-month overall survival rate of 85.3% vs. 64.8% with chemotherapy alone, and a pathologic complete response rate of 36.2% vs. 6.8%.

**2. Adjuvant-only ICI (after surgery ± chemotherapy, vs. placebo/best supportive care)**

- IMpower010 showed a disease-free survival benefit with adjuvant atezolizumab versus best supportive care after adjuvant chemotherapy in resected stage II–IIIA NSCLC, with pronounced benefit in patients whose tumors expressed PD-L1 ≥1%, and no new safety signals. A later analysis found overall survival remained immature in the ITT population but showed a positive trend favoring atezolizumab, primarily in the PD-L1 TC ≥50% subgroup.
- PEARLS/KEYNOTE-091 found pembrolizumab significantly improved disease-free survival compared with placebo, without new safety signals, in completely resected, PD-L1-unselected, stage IB–IIIA NSCLC, though the magnitude of benefit did not clearly track with PD-L1 expression, and benefit in PD-L1 <1% tumors is uncertain.

**3. Perioperative ICI (neoadjuvant + adjuvant, vs. placebo/chemotherapy alone)**

- KEYNOTE-671 showed significant improvements in event-free survival, major pathological response, and pathological complete response among participants who received neoadjuvant pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy followed by surgery and adjuvant pembrolizumab. At a later analysis, 36-month overall survival was 71% vs 64% (HR 0.72), and median EFS was 47.2 vs 18.3 months; grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events occurred in 45% vs 38%.
- AEGEAN — a phase 3, international, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial — investigated durvalumab given perioperatively along with neoadjuvant chemotherapy in resectable NSCLC. Perioperative durvalumab plus neoadjuvant chemotherapy was associated with significantly greater event-free survival and pathological complete response than neoadjuvant chemotherapy alone, with a safety profile consistent with the individual agents.
- CheckMate 77T found that adding perioperative nivolumab to neoadjuvant chemotherapy resulted in significantly longer event-free survival than perioperative placebo (HR 0.58, P<0.001), with higher pathological complete response (25.3% vs 4.7%) and major pathological response (35.4% vs 12.1%); no new safety signals were reported and surgical outcomes were similar between groups.
- NEOTORCH compared toripalimab plus platinum-based chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone on event-free survival and major pathological response in resectable stage III NSCLC, reducing risk of recurrence, progression, or death by 60% (HR 0.40) in the stage III population.

**Age-specific evidence**: Trial-level primary publications generally under-represent older/frailer patients, but age subgroup data have been reported for pCR across trials, with a significant pCR benefit maintained in patients ≥65 years (RR 5.26) as well as those <65 years, and a pooled analysis of 8 RCTs (1,561 patients aged ≥65) found significant benefit for pCR (RR 5.26) and EFS (HR 0.64) with perioperative immunotherapy vs. placebo — note this last one is a meta-analysis, included here only to flag where age-specific primary subgroup data can be found (KEYNOTE-671 and NADIM II report elderly-subgroup OS data directly).
