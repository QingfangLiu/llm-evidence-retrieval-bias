## Scope

I identified **11 randomized comparative primary trials** in resectable stage IB–IIIB non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC):

* **2 neoadjuvant-only trials**
* **6 perioperative trials**—checkpoint inhibitor before and after surgery
* **3 adjuvant-only trials**

For the preoperative studies, the control was generally the **same chemotherapy without a checkpoint inhibitor**, rather than literally no systemic therapy. I excluded single-arm studies, nonrandomized studies, reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, editorials, and commentaries.

There is **no mature randomized study of neoadjuvant checkpoint-inhibitor monotherapy versus placebo/no checkpoint inhibitor**. All randomized preoperative evidence used immunotherapy with chemotherapy.

## Main conclusions

For fit surgical candidates, adding a PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor to neoadjuvant chemotherapy consistently:

* Increased pathologic complete response, often from approximately **1%–9% to 17%–37%**.
* Reduced the hazard of an event or recurrence by approximately **32%–60%**.
* Generally did not reduce the chance of reaching surgery or obtaining an R0 resection.

An overall-survival benefit has now been demonstrated in the phase 3 **CheckMate 816**, **KEYNOTE-671**, and **RATIONALE-315** trials. The smaller phase 2 **NADIM II** trial also reported encouraging survival, but with considerably less precision. ([PubMed][1])

Postoperative-only immunotherapy is less consistent: atezolizumab and pembrolizumab improved disease-free survival in their respective trials, while durvalumab did not. A broad overall-survival advantage from adjuvant-only immunotherapy has not yet been established. ([PubMed][2])

## Before-surgery treatment only

### CheckMate 816: nivolumab plus chemotherapy

This phase 3 trial randomized 358 patients with stage IB–IIIA disease to three cycles of nivolumab plus platinum-doublet chemotherapy or chemotherapy alone.

**Benefits:** Median event-free survival was 31.6 versus 20.8 months, HR 0.63. Pathologic complete response was 24.0% versus 2.2%. At 68.4 months’ median follow-up, 5-year overall survival was 65.4% versus 55.0%, HR for death 0.72. Surgery was performed in 83.2% versus 75.4%.

**Harms:** Grade 3–4 treatment-related adverse events occurred in 33.5% versus 36.9%; thus, adding nivolumab did not increase the overall severe-treatment-toxicity rate in this trial. Immune-mediated toxicities and rare serious events remain possible, but no new late safety signal emerged in the survival update. ([PubMed][3])

### TD-FOREKNOW: camrelizumab plus chemotherapy

This Chinese randomized phase 2 trial enrolled 94 patients, aged 18–70, with resectable stage IIIA or selected IIIB disease; 88 received allocated treatment.

**Benefits:** Pathologic complete response was 32.6% with camrelizumab plus chemotherapy versus 8.9% with chemotherapy alone. Major pathologic response was 65.1% versus 15.6%. Event-free-survival data were immature and not statistically conclusive, HR 0.52, 95% CI 0.21–1.29.

**Harms:** Grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events occurred in 25.6% versus 11.1%. Immune-mediated events occurred in 53.5% of camrelizumab-treated patients but were all grade 1–2 in this report. One postoperative death occurred in the experimental arm and was considered unrelated to treatment. ([JAMA Network][4])

## Treatment before and after surgery

These trials test the **entire perioperative strategy**. They do not establish how much of the benefit comes from preoperative immunotherapy versus continued postoperative immunotherapy.

### NADIM II: perioperative nivolumab plus chemotherapy

This randomized phase 2 trial included 86 patients with resectable stage IIIA or IIIB disease. Patients received neoadjuvant nivolumab plus chemotherapy or chemotherapy alone; patients in the nivolumab arm with an R0 resection then received six months of adjuvant nivolumab.

**Benefits:** Pathologic complete response was 37% versus 7%. Surgery occurred in 93% versus 69%. At 24 months, progression-free survival was 67.2% versus 40.9%, HR 0.47, and overall survival was 85.0% versus 63.6%, HR 0.43.

**Harms:** Grade 3–4 adverse events during neoadjuvant treatment occurred in 19% versus 10%. There was one postoperative death after pneumonectomy in the nivolumab group, judged unrelated to study treatment. Because of the small sample, survival estimates are less secure than those from phase 3 trials. ([PubMed][5])

### KEYNOTE-671: perioperative pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy

This double-blind phase 3 trial randomized 797 patients with stage II–IIIB N2 disease to four cycles of pembrolizumab plus cisplatin-based chemotherapy followed by surgery and 13 cycles of pembrolizumab, or matched placebo plus chemotherapy and postoperative placebo.

**Benefits:** At the second interim analysis, 36-month overall survival was 71% versus 64%, HR 0.72. Median event-free survival was 47.2 versus 18.3 months, HR 0.59. Surgery was performed in 82.1% versus 79.4%, and R0 resection in 92.0% versus 84.2% of operated patients.

**Harms:** Grade 3–5 treatment-related adverse events occurred in 45% versus 38%. Treatment-related deaths occurred in four patients versus three. In a surgical analysis, six versus two patients died within 30 days from surgery-related adverse events. ([PubMed][6])

### AEGEAN: perioperative durvalumab plus chemotherapy

This phase 3 trial randomized approximately 800 patients with stage II–IIIB N2 disease to durvalumab or placebo with four cycles of neoadjuvant platinum chemotherapy, followed by postoperative durvalumab or placebo.

**Benefits:** Event-free survival favored durvalumab, HR 0.68. Twelve-month event-free survival was 73.4% versus 64.5%. Pathologic complete response was 17.2% versus 4.3%. Overall survival was not mature in the primary report.

**Harms:** Grade 3–4 adverse events from any cause occurred in 42.4% versus 43.2%. Despite similar overall severe-event rates—largely driven by chemotherapy—immune-mediated adverse events were more frequent with durvalumab. Patients with documented EGFR or ALK alterations were excluded from the principal efficacy population. ([PubMed][7])

### CheckMate 77T: perioperative nivolumab plus chemotherapy

This double-blind phase 3 trial randomized 461 patients with stage IIA–IIIB disease to four cycles of neoadjuvant nivolumab plus chemotherapy followed by one year of nivolumab, or chemotherapy plus placebo followed by placebo.

**Benefits:** Eighteen-month event-free survival was 70.2% versus 50.0%, HR 0.58. Pathologic complete response was 25.3% versus 4.7%; major pathologic response was 35.4% versus 12.1%. Overall survival remained immature.

**Harms:** Grade 3–4 treatment-related adverse events occurred in 32.5% versus 25.2%. Treatment discontinuation was more common with nivolumab. Two treatment-related deaths from pneumonitis were reported in the nivolumab arm. ([PubMed][8])

### Neotorch: perioperative toripalimab plus chemotherapy

This Chinese phase 3 trial tested three preoperative cycles and one postoperative cycle of toripalimab plus platinum chemotherapy, followed by toripalimab maintenance, against matched placebo. The published interim efficacy analysis included 404 patients with stage III disease; stage II patients were not included in that analysis.

**Benefits:** Median event-free survival was not reached versus 15.1 months, HR 0.40. Two-year event-free survival was 64.7% versus 38.7%. Major pathologic response was 48.5% versus 8.4%, and pathologic complete response was 24.8% versus 1.0%. Surgery was more frequent in the experimental group.

**Harms:** Grade ≥3 adverse events occurred in approximately 63% versus 54%. Immune-related events were more frequent with toripalimab. Fatal adverse events occurred in approximately 3% versus 2%, although not all were treatment related. Generalizability is limited by the age ceiling of 70, treatment exclusively in China, and a study population that was 92% male. ([PubMed][9])

### RATIONALE-315: perioperative tislelizumab plus chemotherapy

This Chinese double-blind phase 3 trial randomized 453 patients with stage II–IIIA disease to tislelizumab or placebo with neoadjuvant chemotherapy, followed by surgery and postoperative tislelizumab or placebo.

**Benefits:** In the final analysis, overall survival favored tislelizumab, HR 0.65; 36-month survival was 79.3% versus 69.3%. Median event-free survival was not reached versus 30.6 months, HR 0.58. The interim analysis found major pathologic response rates of 56% versus 15%.

**Harms:** In the interim safety report, grade ≥3 adverse events occurred in 72% versus 66%, while serious treatment-related adverse events occurred in 15% versus 8%. Much of the severe toxicity was hematologic and related to chemotherapy. Forty percent of participants were 65 or older, but 91% were male and all were treated in China. ([ScienceDirect][10])

## Treatment after surgery only

### IMpower010: adjuvant atezolizumab

After complete resection and cisplatin-based adjuvant chemotherapy, 1,005 patients with stage IB–IIIA disease were randomized to one year of atezolizumab or best supportive care.

**Benefits:** Among stage II–IIIA patients with tumor-cell PD-L1 ≥1%, disease-free survival favored atezolizumab, HR 0.66. The HR was 0.79 in all stage II–IIIA patients and 0.81 in the full stage IB–IIIA intention-to-treat population. Benefit appeared most pronounced with higher PD-L1 expression. Overall-survival follow-up has produced a signal in the PD-L1-high population, but not definitive evidence of a broad OS benefit.

**Harms:** Atezolizumab-related grade 3–4 adverse events occurred in 11%, and grade 5 treatment-related events in four patients, approximately 1%. Unlike the perioperative studies, the control patients had no year-long placebo infusions. ([PubMed][2])

### PEARLS/KEYNOTE-091: adjuvant pembrolizumab

This triple-blind phase 3 trial randomized 1,177 patients with completely resected stage IB–IIIA disease to pembrolizumab or placebo for up to 18 cycles. Adjuvant chemotherapy was strongly recommended for stage II–IIIA but was not required for every patient.

**Benefits:** In the overall population, median disease-free survival was 53.6 versus 42.0 months, HR 0.76. Unexpectedly, the dual-primary analysis in patients with PD-L1 tumor proportion score ≥50% was not statistically significant, HR 0.82. Overall survival remained immature.

**Harms:** Grade ≥3 adverse events occurred in approximately 34% versus 26%; serious adverse events in 24% versus 15%. Four treatment-related deaths occurred with pembrolizumab and none with placebo, including immune-mediated causes such as myocarditis. ([PubMed][11])

### BR.31: adjuvant durvalumab

This phase 3 trial randomized 1,415 patients with completely resected stage IB ≥4 cm–IIIA disease to durvalumab or placebo after optional adjuvant chemotherapy.

**Benefits:** There was no disease-free-survival benefit in the primary EGFR/ALK-negative, PD-L1 ≥25% population: HR 0.93, 95% CI 0.71–1.25. No benefit emerged in the hierarchically tested broader PD-L1 or EGFR/ALK-negative populations.

**Harms:** Grade 3–4 adverse events occurred in 26% versus 20%, meaning additional toxicity without demonstrated efficacy. ([PubMed][12])

## Applicability to older adults

The evidence is most applicable to **fit older adults selected for major lung resection and platinum-based chemotherapy**, rather than the general geriatric NSCLC population.

Median ages were usually in the early-to-mid 60s, and some phase 3 trials enrolled substantial numbers aged 65 or older—for example, 40% in RATIONALE-315. However, patients aged 75–80 or older were much less well represented. TD-FOREKNOW and Neotorch capped enrollment at age 70. Participants generally had ECOG performance status 0–1, adequate organ function, and no active autoimmune disease, significant interstitial lung disease, or need for substantial immunosuppression. ([JAMA Network][4])

Consequently, the trials provide limited direct evidence for patients with frailty, ECOG ≥2, cognitive or functional impairment, significant multimorbidity, severe renal impairment, baseline steroid use, or marginal surgical reserve. Age subgroup analyses have generally not demonstrated a definitive age-by-treatment interaction, but they are underpowered and should not be interpreted as proving equal net benefit in very old or frail adults.

Other clinically important limitations are:

* Perioperative trials do not determine whether postoperative immunotherapy is necessary after effective neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy.
* Pathologic complete response is strongly prognostic but does not substitute for mature survival results.
* Several pivotal trials excluded EGFR- or ALK-altered tumors or removed them from the principal efficacy analysis.
* Additional months of postoperative therapy increase cumulative exposure, clinic burden, immune toxicity, and the possibility of persistent endocrinopathy or less common pneumonitis, colitis, hepatitis, neurologic toxicity, myocarditis, or nephritis.
