## Summary of evidence

**Neoadjuvant-only immunotherapy plus chemotherapy vs. chemotherapy alone**

- **CheckMate 816** (nivolumab, 3 pre-op cycles): In 358 patients, adding nivolumab significantly improved pathological complete response and event‑free survival, without adverse effect on surgical feasibility.On the basis of this trial, nivolumab in combination with chemotherapy has been approved as neoadjuvant treatment for adult patients with resectable NSCLC, and overall CheckMate 816 showed neoadjuvant nivolumab plus chemotherapy had a significant benefit over chemotherapy alone with respect to event-free survival and pathological complete response and had no adverse effect on surgical feasibility or surgical outcomes. The final analysis reported an overall-survival benefit as well, described as a statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvement in overall survival, making it the first and only neoadjuvant-only immuno-oncology therapy to demonstrate this in resectable NSCLC. Harms: the most common immune-mediated adverse event was rash (8.5%), with grade 1–2 pneumonitis in 1.1%; adverse events led to surgery delay in 3.4% vs 5.1% and surgery cancellation in 1.1% vs 0.6% of patients.

**Perioperative (neoadjuvant + adjuvant) immunotherapy plus chemotherapy vs. chemotherapy/placebo**

- **KEYNOTE-671** (pembrolizumab): 797 patients were randomly assigned between May 2018 and December 2021 to pembrolizumab (n=397) or placebo (n=400) plus cisplatin-based chemotherapy for 4 cycles before surgery, followed by adjuvant pembrolizumab or placebo for 13 cycles. Overall survival results indicated significant benefit of neoadjuvant pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy followed by adjuvant pembrolizumab, with a manageable safety profile. A subsequent stage-II subgroup analysis found improved event-free survival (HR 0.50), overall survival (HR 0.69), major pathological response, and pathological complete response, with grade 3-4 treatment-related adverse events in 50.0% (pembrolizumab) vs 40.5% (chemo alone), and no treatment-related deaths.
- **CheckMate 77T** (nivolumab): 461 patients with resectable stage IIA to IIIB disease were randomly assigned to neoadjuvant nivolumab plus chemotherapy (n=229) or chemotherapy plus placebo (n=232), followed by surgery and adjuvant nivolumab or placebo for 1 year. Median event-free survival was not reached in the nivolumab arm vs 18.4 months with placebo (HR 0.58).
- **AEGEAN** (durvalumab): 802 patients with stage II to IIIB disease were randomly assigned to neoadjuvant durvalumab (n=400) or placebo (n=402) plus chemotherapy, followed by adjuvant durvalumab or placebo for 12 cycles; perioperative durvalumab was associated with significantly greater event-free survival and pathological complete response than chemotherapy alone, with a safety profile consistent with the individual agents.
- **Neotorch** (toripalimab, China): A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial of toripalimab plus perioperative chemotherapy vs chemotherapy alone in resectable stage II or III NSCLC; among 404 stage III patients, adding toripalimab led to significant improvement in event-free survival.
- **NADIM II** (nivolumab, phase 2 randomized): In resectable stage IIIA NSCLC, the addition of neoadjuvant nivolumab to chemotherapy significantly improved overall survival compared with chemotherapy alone (85.3% vs 64.8% at 24 months), with pathologic complete response of 36.2% vs 6.8%.

**Adjuvant-only immunotherapy after surgery ± chemotherapy vs. placebo/best supportive care**

- **IMpower010** (atezolizumab): A randomised, multicentre, open-label phase 3 study showed disease-free survival benefit with atezolizumab vs best supportive care after adjuvant chemotherapy in resected stage II–IIIA NSCLC, with pronounced benefit in tumours expressing PD-L1 ≥1%, and no new safety signals.
- **PEARLS/KEYNOTE-091** (pembrolizumab): 1,177 patients with completely resected stage IB–IIIA NSCLC were randomly assigned to pembrolizumab or placebo for up to 18 cycles; pembrolizumab led to significant disease-free survival improvement vs placebo in the intention-to-treat population (HR 0.76).
- **CCTG BR.31** (durvalumab) — a **negative** trial: Adjuvant durvalumab did not lead to a statistically significant improvement in disease-free survival compared with placebo after complete resection in stage IB–IIIA NSCLC with PD-L1 ≥25%. Grade 3/4 adverse events occurred in 23.5% vs 19.6%, and adverse events leading to discontinuation in 14.0% vs 5.1%, in the durvalumab vs placebo arms.

**On age/older adults specifically:** none of these pivotal trials was designed or powered for an older-adult population; older patients (especially ≥75) are underrepresented, but pre-specified age subgroup analyses in most of these trials have generally shown directionally consistent efficacy across age strata, and a review noted subgroup analyses regarding performance status, age, disease stage, histology, choice of platinum agent, and smoking history demonstrated a consistent benefit in EFS rates across all subgroups.
