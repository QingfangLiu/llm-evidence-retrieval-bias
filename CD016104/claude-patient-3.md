## Neoadjuvant only (before surgery)

**CheckMate 816** (nivolumab + chemo vs. chemo alone) — Patients with resectable stage IB–IIIA NSCLC were randomized to three cycles of nivolumab plus platinum chemo or chemo alone before surgery. Neoadjuvant nivolumab plus chemotherapy had a significant benefit over chemotherapy alone in event-free survival and pathological complete response, with no adverse effect on surgical feasibility or surgical outcomes. The combination reduced the risk of disease recurrence, progression, or death by 37%, with median event-free survival of 31.6 months vs. 20.8 months for chemo alone. A later update showed a statistically significant and clinically meaningful overall survival benefit as well. Harms were generally consistent with each drug's known toxicity profile, with no major increase in surgical complications.

## Adjuvant only (after surgery)

**IMpower010** (atezolizumab vs. best supportive care, after surgery + chemo) — This randomized, open-label phase III trial compared atezolizumab against best supportive care following platinum-based chemotherapy in resected stage IB–IIIA NSCLC. It showed a disease-free survival benefit with atezolizumab, with pronounced benefit in patients whose tumors expressed PD-L1 on 1% or more of tumor cells, and no new safety signals. At longer follow-up, it continued to show benefit with atezolizumab versus best supportive care, particularly in the PD-L1–selected population, though overall survival data remain immature.

**PEARLS/KEYNOTE-091** (pembrolizumab vs. placebo, after surgery ± chemo) — This randomized, triple-blind, placebo-controlled phase III trial enrolled patients with resected stage IB–IIIA NSCLC across 196 centers in 29 countries. Pembrolizumab significantly improved disease-free survival compared with placebo and was not associated with new safety signals, regardless of PD-L1 expression. Notably, unlike the metastatic setting, PD-L1 expression did not show a consistent pattern predicting benefit in this adjuvant setting.

## Perioperative (both before and after surgery)

**KEYNOTE-671** (pembrolizumab, neoadjuvant + adjuvant, vs. placebo) — Patients with resectable stage II, IIIA, or IIIB(N2) NSCLC received neoadjuvant pembrolizumab or placebo with chemotherapy for 4 cycles, followed by surgery and then adjuvant pembrolizumab or placebo for up to 13 cycles, testing whether a "sandwich" approach adds benefit beyond either neoadjuvant or adjuvant treatment alone.

**CheckMate 77T** (nivolumab, neoadjuvant + adjuvant, vs. placebo) — Patients with resectable stage II–IIIB NSCLC were randomized to nivolumab or placebo plus chemotherapy before surgery, followed by nivolumab or placebo alone after surgery. The trial showed a statistically significant and clinically meaningful event-free survival benefit and improved pathologic complete response versus placebo, including benefit in the harder-to-treat N2 nodal disease subgroup. A quality-of-life analysis also found nivolumab did not negatively affect health-related quality of life and reduced the risk of its deterioration compared with placebo.

**AEGEAN** (durvalumab, neoadjuvant + adjuvant, vs. placebo) — This phase 3, international, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial investigated durvalumab given perioperatively along with neoadjuvant chemotherapy in resectable stage II–IIIB NSCLC. Perioperative durvalumab plus neoadjuvant chemotherapy was associated with significantly greater event-free survival and pathologic complete response than neoadjuvant chemotherapy alone, with a safety profile consistent with the individual agents. On the harms side, grade 3–4 severe adverse events occurred in 42.4% of durvalumab patients versus 43.2% of placebo patients — similar overall — but immune-related adverse events were more common with durvalumab than placebo (23.7% vs. 9.3%), though most were mild (grade 1–2).

**NEOTORCH** (toripalimab, neoadjuvant + adjuvant, vs. placebo) — This randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial evaluated toripalimab combined with perioperative chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone in resectable stage II or III NSCLC, showing significant improvement in event-free survival, particularly analyzed in stage III patients. A more recent post-hoc surgical-outcomes analysis found toripalimab plus chemotherapy significantly reduced the surgery cancellation rate (17.8% vs. 26.7%) without increasing perioperative complications. (Note: toripalimab is not approved everywhere outside China/certain markets, so relevance depends on your location.)

### A note on age
None of these searches turned up age-specific subgroup restrictions excluding older adults — all trials enrolled broad adult populations (with ECOG performance status criteria), and some report subgroup data by age, but I haven't pulled those specific numbers here. If age-related fitness for surgery or immunotherapy toxicity is a particular concern for you, that's worth raising directly with your oncology team, since individual comorbidities and functional status often matter more than age alone in decision-making.
