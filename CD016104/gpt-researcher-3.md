## Scope

I interpreted the population as **older adults—principally age 65 years or older—with completely resected or potentially resectable, nonmetastatic NSCLC**, generally stage IB with tumors ≥4 cm through selected stage IIIB/N2 disease. I included randomized trials in which the experimental and control groups differed by receipt of an immune-checkpoint inhibitor; identical chemotherapy could be given in both groups.

I found **12 named randomized trials representing 13 randomized comparisons** with published primary results through July 25, 2026. CheckMate 816 contributed two separate comparisons. Single-arm studies, protocols, unresectable stage III studies, and studies without a no-immunotherapy control were excluded.

Abbreviations: **EFS**, event-free survival; **DFS**, disease-free survival; **OS**, overall survival; **pCR**, pathological complete response; **MPR**, major pathological response; **TRAE**, treatment-related adverse event.

Thus, **adjuvant-only checkpoint inhibition is not a uniform class effect**. IMpower010 and KEYNOTE-091 improved DFS in their respective primary overall or biomarker-defined analyses, whereas BR.31 and ANVIL were negative. No adjuvant-only trial has yet demonstrated a convincing overall-population OS benefit.

## Applicability to older adults

The evidence is most applicable to **fit adults in their mid-60s who are candidates for major lung resection**, not to an unselected geriatric population.

* Median ages were commonly about 61–67 years; ANVIL had median ages of 66 and 67 years, providing particularly direct representation of conventional “older adult” populations. ([JAMA Network][14])
* Nevertheless, the trials generally required ECOG performance status 0–1 and adequate organ function. TD-FOREKNOW and Neotorch excluded anyone older than 70, directly limiting applicability to adults in their 70s and 80s. ([JAMA Network][3])
* No included trial was designed around frailty, geriatric assessment, multimorbidity, cognitive impairment, polypharmacy, or functional vulnerability.
* The principal reports rarely provided adverse-event results stratified by age. Therefore, they cannot reliably determine whether pneumonitis, endocrinopathy, myocarditis, treatment discontinuation, perioperative complications, or functional decline are more frequent in adults aged 75 or older.
* In KEYNOTE-671, the OS point estimate appeared less favorable or less clear in participants aged ≥65, but confidence intervals overlapped the overall effect. The authors explicitly characterized all subgroup analyses as descriptive, unadjusted for multiplicity, and underpowered for treatment-effect comparisons. ([PMC][15])

**Evidence-synthesis conclusion:** For fit surgical candidates aged approximately 65–70, perioperative chemoimmunotherapy likely retains its substantial pCR and EFS benefits. Confidence is lower for OS within the older subgroup specifically, and considerably lower for adults aged ≥75, frail adults, and those with substantial cardiopulmonary or autoimmune comorbidity. Age-specific net benefit cannot be calculated from the published primary reports because age-stratified harms are largely absent.
