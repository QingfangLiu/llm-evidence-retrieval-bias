## What the primary studies show

Across many randomized controlled trials, hospitalized patients are assigned to a **restrictive** transfusion strategy (lower hemoglobin trigger, e.g., 7–8 g/dL) or a **liberal** strategy (higher trigger, e.g., 9–10 g/dL).

**Benefits of restrictive transfusion (giving less blood):**
- In critically ill patients, a restrictive strategy (threshold 7.0 g/dL) was at least as effective as, and possibly superior to, a liberal strategy (threshold 10.0 g/dL), with a trend toward decreased 30-day mortality, and fewer red-cell units transfused (TRICC trial).
- In patients with acute upper GI bleeding, a restrictive strategy (transfuse below 7 g/dL) was compared with a liberal strategy (transfuse below 9 g/dL), and the restrictive strategy significantly improved outcomes — fewer transfusions, higher 6-week survival, less further bleeding, and fewer adverse effects.
- In septic shock, patients transfused at a threshold of 7 g/dL, versus 9 g/dL, received fewer transfusions and had similar 90-day mortality, use of life support, and days alive out of hospital, with similar rates of ischemic events and adverse reactions (TRISS trial).
- In hip-fracture surgery patients at cardiovascular risk, withholding transfusion until hemoglobin fell below 8 g/dL (or symptoms occurred) did not increase death or inability to walk at 60 days, nor increase in-hospital complications, compared with a liberal 10 g/dL threshold (FOCUS trial).

**Signals of possible harm from restrictive strategies:**
- In a cardiac surgery trial (TITRe2), the primary clinical outcome was similar between restrictive (transfuse <7.5 g/dL) and liberal (<9 g/dL) groups, but a secondary analysis found a borderline-significant difference in 90-day mortality: 4.2% died in the restrictive group vs. 2.6% in the liberal group, creating uncertainty for this population.

**Where results were more neutral:**
- In hematopoietic cell transplant patients, a restrictive strategy (threshold <70 g/L) was as effective as a liberal strategy (<90 g/L), with similar quality-of-life and clinical outcomes but fewer transfusions.

11. **Jairath V, et al.** (TRIGGER Trial). Restrictive versus liberal blood transfusion for acute upper gastrointestinal bleeding: a pragmatic, open-label, cluster randomised feasibility trial. *Lancet.* 2015;386:137–144.
