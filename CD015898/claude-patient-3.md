I have enough to give you a solid, focused set of primary studies. A key finding: this is a genuinely under-studied question. Most transfusion trials compare *when to transfuse* (hemoglobin thresholds — restrictive vs. liberal), not *how much to give per transfusion event*. The Cochrane group itself notes that almost no threshold trials specified RBC volume per transfusion, which is why a dedicated Cochrane protocol on this exact question was only registered in 2024. Below are the individual primary studies I could locate that directly randomized or otherwise experimentally compared **larger vs. smaller RBC volume/dose given per transfusion episode** (not threshold trials), across neonates, children, and adults.

**Neonates / infants**
- Larger volumes produced greater Hb/Hct rises without pulmonary harm in a 10 vs. 20 mL/kg RCT in VLBW infants.
- A high-volume (20 mL/kg) vs. standard (15 mL/kg) RCT in premature infants found no adverse events with the higher volume.
- A double-blind randomized crossover trial (15 vs. 20 mL/kg aliquots) in VLBW infants again showed higher Hb/Hct with the larger volume and good tolerance.
- A birth-weight-based (15 mL/kg) vs. formula-based volume RCT in preterm neonates found the formula-based (generally larger) approach produced a greater hematocrit rise.

**Children**
- The TRACT trial (Uganda/Malawi) randomized children with severe anemia to 20 vs. 30 mL/kg whole-blood-equivalent transfusion; effects on mortality diverged sharply by fever status — 30 mL/kg roughly halved mortality in afebrile children but nearly doubled it in febrile children, with no difference overall.
- An earlier phase II trial in Ugandan children tested standard vs. increased transfusion volume ahead of TRACT.
- A weight-based vs. formula-based PRBC dosing RCT in transfusion-dependent thalassemic children found the formula-based approach lowered annual RBC requirement and donor exposures.

**Adults**
- The FUEL trial (thalassemia major) randomized patients to red cell units with different Hb content/volume per bag, finding higher-Hb-content units produced a larger Hb increment.
- The "1versus2" trial randomized adults with hematologic malignancies/HSCT to single- vs. double-unit transfusion per episode, finding single-unit non-inferior for major complications while modestly reducing units used.
- The SMaRT Blood trial (postpartum anemia) randomized women to 1- vs. 2-unit initial transfusion protocols.

Given how thin this evidence base is, especially in general adult hospital populations, this may be a good candidate for a deeper literature search across databases (MEDLINE/Embase trial registries) rather than web search alone, since several relevant trials I found are only indexed as conference abstracts, protocols, or trial registry entries rather than full-text publications.
