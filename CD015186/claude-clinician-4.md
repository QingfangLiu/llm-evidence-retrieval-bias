## Overview of SC-MIGS evidence base

SC-MIGS procedures share a common mechanism — bypassing or ablating the trabecular meshwork/inner wall of Schlemm's canal to restore conventional outflow — but differ in approach: stents that scaffold the canal (iStent, iStent inject, Hydrus), circumferential viscodilation/catheterization (ab interno canaloplasty, OMNI, iTrack), incisional trabeculotomy/goniotomy (Trabectome, Kahook Dual Blade, GATT, BANG), and combinations of these.

**Benefits reported across primary studies:**
- **iStent/iStent inject + phaco (RCTs):** The pivotal iStent inject trial randomized 3:1 to stent+phaco vs. phaco alone in mild-moderate POAG; at 2 years, stent eyes had clinically and statistically greater reductions in IOP without medication after iStent inject implantation with cataract surgery versus cataract surgery alone, with excellent safety through 2 years. A separate 5-year multi-surgeon RCT compared two trabecular stents against prostaglandin monotherapy as first-line treatment in newly diagnosed POAG.
- **Hydrus Microstent + phaco (HORIZON trial, RCT):** 369 eyes were randomized to Hydrus Microstent and 187 to no microstent; at 24 months, unmedicated diurnal IOP was reduced by ≥20% in 77.3% of Hydrus eyes vs. 57.8% of controls. Three-year data showed the cumulative probability of incisional glaucoma surgery was lower in the microstent group (0.6% vs. 3.9%; hazard ratio, 0.156), and 5-year data extended these findings with adverse events including peripheral anterior synechiae at a cumulative rate of 14.6% vs 3.7% for cataract surgery alone, plus partial or complete device obstruction (8.4%) and device malposition (1.4%).
- **GATT (360° trabeculotomy):** Case series report substantial IOP and medication reduction across POAG, secondary OAG, and even angle-closure and pediatric/juvenile populations, including in eyes with prior failed surgery and in advanced disease, though nearly all are retrospective single-surgeon or multicenter cohorts rather than RCTs.
- **Trabectome (ab interno trabeculectomy):** Multiple prospective and retrospective cohorts (Berlin study group, Trabectome Study Group database analyses) show durable IOP lowering out to 3–8 years, with somewhat less IOP reduction than trabeculectomy but a more favorable safety profile.
- **OMNI/ab interno canaloplasty + trabeculotomy (ROMEO study):** Retrospective multicenter cohorts show 12-month IOP reductions of roughly 20–35% with medication reductions, fairly consistent across mild-to-advanced glaucoma severity strata.
- **Head-to-head/comparative data:** The TVC study (RCT, canaloplasty vs. trabeculectomy) now has 11-year follow-up showing lower long-term complete success with canaloplasty than trabeculectomy. A registered RCT protocol (BANG vs. GATT) aims to generate head-to-head data between two ab interno trabeculotomy techniques, since most available MIGS publications are case series or retrospective studies with different protocols.

**Harms/limitations noted:** hyphema/blood reflux (near-universal with canal-based procedures, generally self-limited), transient IOP spikes, peripheral anterior synechiae, device obstruction/malposition (stent-specific), and variable long-term durability compared with trabeculectomy in more advanced disease.

A broad literature synthesis across dozens of these primary studies (effect sizes, complication rates, comparative effectiveness by device) would benefit from a systematic search across PubMed/Embase rather than the ~15 studies surfaced here.
