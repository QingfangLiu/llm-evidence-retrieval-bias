I searched for primary studies on Schlemm's canal–targeting MIGS (trabecular micro-bypass stents, ab interno trabeculotomy/goniotomy devices, and ab interno canaloplasty), excluding reviews, meta-analyses, and guidelines. Below is a set of primary studies (RCTs, prospective cohorts, and retrospective case series) organized by device/technique.

**What I found across categories:**

For the **Hydrus Microstent**, the pivotal randomized trials are the HORIZON studies, which found that a 24-month multicenter RCT demonstrated superior reduction in diurnal IOP and medication use among mild-to-moderate POAG patients who received a Schlemm canal microstent combined with phacoemulsification compared with phacoemulsification alone, with follow-up extending through 3 years in 556 eyes.

For **iStent/iStent inject**, pivotal RCTs similarly found that clinically and statistically greater reductions in IOP without medication were achieved after iStent inject implantation with cataract surgery versus cataract surgery alone, with excellent safety through 2 years, building on earlier single-stent trials by the iStent Study Group.

Head-to-head comparisons include the randomized **COMPARE** study, where standalone MIGS with the Hydrus resulted in a higher surgical success rate and fewer medications compared with a 2-iStent procedure, with similar safety profiles between the two devices, and the randomized **INTEGRITY** study comparing iStent infinite to Hydrus.

For **GATT**, most evidence is retrospective/prospective cohort data, e.g., one large series found GATT was performed on 126 eyes with cumulative 1-year success of 0.88 for GATT combined with cataract extraction, 0.96 for GATT alone, and IOP decreasing from a mean of 20.65 to 14.1 mmHg, and a 4-year outcomes study.

For **Trabectome**, evidence includes a small RCT (n=19) comparing it against trabeculectomy, where Phaco-AIT achieved similar IOP lowering at 6 and 12 months compared with phaco-trabeculectomy, with no serious complications identified in the Phaco-AIT group, plus larger retrospective cohorts.

For **Kahook Dual Blade goniotomy**, evidence is primarily prospective/retrospective case series, such as a multicenter series where mean IOP was reduced from 16.8 mmHg at baseline to 12.4 mmHg at month 12, a 26.2% reduction.

For **ab interno canaloplasty (OMNI system)**, the ROMEO study reported 12-month outcomes, and additional prospective single-arm studies exist.
