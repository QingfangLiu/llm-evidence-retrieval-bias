Schlemm's canal minimally invasive glaucoma surgery (SC-MIGS) encompasses procedures designed to enhance the eye's natural aqueous humor outflow pathway. These techniques—such as stenting the canal (e.g., iStent inject, Hydrus) or incising the trabecular meshwork (e.g., Gonioscopy-Assisted Transluminal Trabeculotomy [GATT])—aim to bypass the diseased trabecular meshwork to lower intraocular pressure (IOP) in patients with open-angle glaucoma.

Here is a synthesis of the benefits and harms of SC-MIGS based on recent primary literature, with the studies listed at the end.

## Benefits of SC-MIGS

* **Significant IOP Reduction:** SC-MIGS procedures effectively lower IOP with high success rates. In a prospective, unmasked randomized controlled trial evaluating the iStent inject against medical therapy (latanoprost/timolol), over 94% of the stented eyes achieved an unmedicated IOP reduction of 20% or greater from baseline at 12 months (Giamporcaro et al., 2014).
* **Decreased Medication Burden:** By restoring physiological outflow, these procedures reduce or eliminate the need for topical hypotensive drops. A retrospective cohort study analyzing GATT outcomes in patients with medically uncontrolled open-angle glaucoma found that the mean number of glaucoma medications fell from 3.5 to 1.2 after one year, achieving an 87% overall success rate (Faria et al., 2022).

## Harms and Complications

While SC-MIGS devices and procedures avoid the major risks associated with traditional filtering surgeries (like trabeculectomy), they carry specific, usually transient, adverse events:

* **Hyphema:** Because these procedures manipulate the highly vascularized angle structures, bleeding into the anterior chamber is the most frequent complication. In the GATT cohort, 39% of patients experienced transient hyphema; however, this typically resolved within a median of 5 days without sight-threatening consequences (Faria et al., 2022).
* **Device Malposition and Uveitis:** Implantable canal stents (such as the Hydrus microstent) can occasionally become malpositioned. A recent case series documented instances where stents shifted or were incorrectly inserted, causing them to rub against the iris. This can trigger uveitis-glaucoma-hyphema (UGH) syndrome, necessitating surgical explantation of the device up to 11 months post-surgery to control chronic inflammation and pressure spikes (Sachdeva et al., 2024).
