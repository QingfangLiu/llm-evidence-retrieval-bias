## Summary of findings

I searched specifically for individual primary studies (RCTs, prospective/retrospective cohort studies, and case series) on Schlemm's canal-based MIGS — trabecular bypass stents (iStent, iStent inject), a Schlemm's canal scaffold (Hydrus Microstent), trabecular ablation/goniotomy devices (Trabectome, Kahook Dual Blade), and circumferential trabeculotomy (GATT). I excluded reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, and editorials as requested.

**Efficacy/safety pattern across studies:** RCTs of Hydrus and iStent/iStent inject combined with cataract surgery consistently show greater IOP and medication reduction versus cataract surgery alone, with favorable safety profiles (endothelial cell loss similar to cataract surgery alone, low serious adverse event rates) as demonstrated in the HYDRUS II study, where implantation of the Hydrus Microstent in patients undergoing cataract surgery provided a significant reduction in IOP and medication use compared with cataract surgery alone for 2 years after surgery. The HORIZON trial similarly found superior reduction in modified diurnal IOP and medication use among subjects receiving the Hydrus microstent, and endothelial cell loss with iStent inject was similar between treatment and control groups through 24 months. Head-to-head data are more limited but include the COMPARE trial, and GATT/Trabectome literature is largely retrospective/prospective cohort data showing moderate success rates with a trabeculectomy-related need for reoperation over time in a meaningful minority of eyes.
