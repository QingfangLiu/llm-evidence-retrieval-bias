## Scope

I treated **SC-MIGS** as ab-interno procedures that improve conventional aqueous outflow through the trabecular meshwork, Schlemm’s canal, or collector channels:

* Trabecular/Schlemm’s canal stents: Hydrus and iStent devices
* Goniotomy or trabeculotomy: KDB, Trabectome, microhook, BANG, and GATT
* Canaloplasty or viscodilation: ABiC and OMNI

I excluded subconjunctival and suprachoroidal procedures. The evidence below is a clinically focused set of randomized, prospective, and important long-term primary analyses available through **July 25, 2026**, rather than every published uncontrolled case series.

## Main conclusions

### 1. The most reliable benefit is fewer medications, with a modest additional IOP reduction

The strongest evidence comes from large multicenter trials in people with **mild-to-moderate open-angle glaucoma undergoing cataract surgery**.

In HORIZON, adding Hydrus to cataract surgery produced an additional mean unmedicated IOP reduction of **2.3 mmHg** versus cataract surgery alone at two years. A ≥20% unmedicated IOP reduction occurred in 77.3% versus 57.8%, and patients used 0.3 versus 0.7 medications. ([PubMed][1])

With iStent inject, the additional two-year mean IOP benefit was **1.6 mmHg**. A ≥20% unmedicated reduction occurred in 75.8% versus 61.9%, while medication-free IOP ≤18 mmHg was achieved by 63.2% versus 50.0%. ([PubMed][2])

Thus, the **average pressure advantage beyond cataract surgery itself is generally modest**, but the chance of reaching a medication-free or lower-medication outcome improves meaningfully.

### 2. Hydrus has the best long-term randomized effectiveness data

At five years in HORIZON:

* 66% of Hydrus eyes versus 46% of cataract-only eyes were medication-free.
* 49.5% versus 33.8% had IOP ≤18 mmHg without medication.
* Subsequent incisional glaucoma surgery occurred in 2.4% versus 6.2%. ([PubMed][3])

A post hoc visual-field analysis also found slower mean progression with Hydrus plus cataract surgery: **−0.26 versus −0.49 dB/year**. Because this was a secondary analysis rather than a prespecified primary endpoint, it is encouraging but less definitive than the original IOP and medication outcomes. ([PubMed][4])

### 3. Results cannot be generalized automatically to advanced glaucoma

Most pivotal trials enrolled people with mild-to-moderate disease, cataract, and washed-out IOP in approximately the low-to-mid 20s. They do not establish that SC-MIGS can reliably achieve the very low target IOP sometimes needed with advanced or rapidly progressive glaucoma. ([PubMed][1])

Small trials comparing angle surgery with trabeculectomy reported fewer serious complications with the angle procedures, but they were far too small to establish equivalent efficacy. One enrolled 32 eyes; another stopped early after enrolling only 19. ([PubMed][5])

## Harms to consider

### Bleeding and hyphema

Blood reflux from Schlemm’s canal is expected when trabecular tissue is cut. Clinically visible hyphema is particularly common with circumferential GATT and less common with stents or viscodilation-only procedures. In the randomized studies above, reported rates ranged from approximately **6% with OMNI** to **41%–87% with GATT or needle goniotomy**, depending on the technique and definition. Most episodes were transient, but blood can temporarily blur vision, increase IOP, or occasionally require anterior-chamber washout. ([PMC][15])

### Early IOP elevation

Transient IOP spikes occurred in 2% of GEMINI participants. Spikes may arise from retained viscoelastic, blood, inflammation, steroid response, or incomplete outflow improvement and can be more consequential in an eye with advanced optic-nerve damage. ([PMC][15])

### Stent position, synechiae, and obstruction

Implanted procedures can be affected by malposition or peripheral anterior synechiae. The six-month INTEGRITY trial reported more placement problems and synechiae with Hydrus than with iStent infinite, although longer follow-up is necessary before treating that difference as definitive. ([PubMed][10])

### Corneal endothelial cells

The findings depend partly on how endothelial loss is analyzed. HORIZON reported no significant difference in the **mean rate** of endothelial loss from months 3–60. However, a separate post hoc analysis found that more Hydrus eyes crossed a threshold of approximately ≥30% endothelial-cell loss than controls—20.8% versus 10.6%. The analogous iStent inject difference, 9.4% versus 6.3%, was not significant. No persistent corneal edema was reported for Hydrus or iStent inject in that analysis. ([PubMed][3])

### Treatment failure or need for further surgery

SC-MIGS does not prevent later laser, medication escalation, or filtration/tube surgery. The reassuring point is that it generally preserves the conjunctiva for those later procedures. In the five-year HORIZON trial, subsequent incisional surgery was required in 2.4% after Hydrus and 6.2% after cataract surgery alone. ([PubMed][3])

## How I would interpret this for a treatment discussion

The evidence is most favorable when the objectives are:

* Reducing drops or improving adherence
* Achieving an IOP in the mid-to-high teens
* Adding glaucoma treatment during already-planned cataract surgery
* Avoiding or postponing bleb-forming surgery

The evidence is less reassuring when the eye has advanced damage, demonstrated rapid progression, or requires a consistently very low target pressure. The most useful questions for your surgeon are the target IOP for your specific optic nerve, whether surgery is being combined with cataract extraction, and what the planned next step would be if the SC-MIGS does not reach that target.
