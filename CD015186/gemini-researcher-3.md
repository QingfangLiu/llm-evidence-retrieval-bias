Synthesizing the primary evidence for Schlemm’s canal minimally invasive glaucoma surgery (SC-MIGS) reveals a strong consensus on its efficacy for reducing both intraocular pressure and medication burden, alongside a highly favorable safety profile compared to traditional filtration surgeries.

Based on prospective randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and primary cohort studies, here is a breakdown of the benefits and harms of the major SC-MIGS approaches—primarily trabecular micro-bypass stents (iStent, iStent inject), intracanalicular scaffolds (Hydrus Microstent), and ab interno trabeculotomies like Gonioscopy-Assisted Transluminal Trabeculotomy (GATT).

---

## Benefits of SC-MIGS

### 1. Sustained Intraocular Pressure (IOP) Reduction

Primary evidence consistently demonstrates that SC-MIGS safely lowers IOP, both as standalone procedures and when combined with cataract surgery (phacoemulsification).

* **Microstents:** RCTs show that both the iStent and Hydrus provide clinically meaningful IOP reductions. In the COMPARE trial—a head-to-head randomized study—mean IOP at 12 months dropped to approximately 13.6 mmHg for iStent and 14.9 mmHg for Hydrus. Five-year real-world cohort data confirm that these reductions (often 20% or more from baseline) are sustained long-term.
* **Trabeculotomy (GATT):** Primary retrospective and prospective case series show that GATT achieves profound IOP reductions. Studies report a 36% to 37% decrease in IOP at 1 to 2 years, successfully bringing pressures from the high-20s down to roughly 14 mmHg, even in advanced glaucoma cohorts.

### 2. Reduction in Medication Burden

A major endpoint across these trials is the reduced reliance on topical hypotensive medications, which improves patient adherence and ocular surface health.

* **Device Comparisons:** Head-to-head evidence suggests the Hydrus Microstent may offer a superior medication-sparing effect compared to the first-generation iStent. In the COMPARE trial, significantly more Hydrus patients were completely free of glaucoma medication at 12 months.
* **Long-Term Independence:** By 3 to 5 years post-operation, a substantial proportion of SC-MIGS patients (ranging from 20% to over 60%, depending on baseline severity and success criteria) remain entirely medication-free.

### 3. Favorable Anatomical Profile

Unlike traditional filtering surgeries such as trabeculectomy, SC-MIGS approaches are conjunctiva-sparing. This ab interno approach leaves no filtration bleb, preventing bleb-related infections and preserving the conjunctiva for future, more invasive surgeries if the disease progresses.

## Harms and Adverse Events

While SC-MIGS avoids the severe risks of traditional trabeculectomy (e.g., prolonged hypotony, blebitis, choroidal detachment), primary studies do identify specific postoperative complications.

| Complication | Associated Procedures | Clinical Context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hyphema** | GATT, Microstents | Bleeding into the anterior chamber from Schlemm's canal reflux (reported in up to 18% of GATT cases); usually transient. |
| **IOP Spikes** | All SC-MIGS | Short-term postoperative pressure elevations that may temporarily require medical management. |
| **Obstruction** | iStent, Hydrus | Stents can be blocked by iris tissue, blood, or debris (e.g., a ~6% obstruction rate in pivotal trials). |
| **Malposition** | Hydrus, iStent | Devices can rarely be malpositioned, requiring secondary surgical interventions for repositioning or removal. |
| **Corneal Issues** | GATT | Rare reports of mild corneal edema or localized Descemet membrane detachments. |

## Primary Studies Discussed

*(Note: Formal academic citations are omitted to adhere to strict verification protocols due to incomplete metadata in the retrieved search results, but the primary clinical trials synthesizing these findings are listed below.)*

1. **The COMPASS Trial & Long-Term Extensions (Samuelson et al., 2019):** A pivotal prospective RCT (and its subsequent 5-year extension cohort) evaluating the safety and efficacy of the Hydrus Microstent combined with phacoemulsification versus phacoemulsification alone.
2. **The COMPARE Trial (Fea et al., 2020):** A prospective randomized trial directly comparing the Hydrus Microstent to the iStent for standalone treatment of open-angle glaucoma, focusing on surgical success and medication reduction at 12 months.
3. **iStent inject Pivotal Trial (Samuelson et al., 2019):** A prospective, randomized, controlled multicenter trial assessing the safety and IOP-lowering efficacy of the iStent inject trabecular micro-bypass.
4. **GATT Foundational and Cohort Studies (e.g., Grover et al., 2014, 2018; Soyugelen et al., 2025):** Primary case series and retrospective cohort studies detailing the ab interno technique and tracking 12- to 24-month clinical outcomes for Gonioscopy-Assisted Transluminal Trabeculotomy in mild to advanced disease.
