**Benefits seen across studies**

Randomized trials of trabecular stents combined with cataract surgery consistently show clinically meaningful IOP reduction and reduced medication burden compared with cataract surgery alone — for example, the iStent inject pivotal trial found significantly more treated eyes reached target pressure without medication versus controls, with benefit sustained through two years, and the HORIZON trial for the Hydrus Microstent showed similar sustained IOP and medication reduction through 5 years, with fewer patients needing further glaucoma surgery. A head-to-head RCT (COMPARE) found the Hydrus outperformed two iStents for surgical success and medication reduction as a standalone procedure. Ab interno trabeculotomy techniques (Trabectome, Kahook Dual Blade, microhook) and GATT — which don't implant a device but instead open the canal/meshwork directly — show comparable IOP and medication reductions in prospective cohorts and case series, including in more advanced disease and in eyes with prior failed surgery. The OMNI system (canaloplasty plus trabeculotomy) shows similar patterns in prospective multicenter studies (GEMINI, ROMEO) with benefit maintained out to 3 years.

**Harms/safety signals**

Across these studies, SC-MIGS procedures are generally associated with a favorable safety profile relative to traditional filtering surgery (trabeculectomy) — low rates of hypotony, serious adverse events, and endothelial cell loss. The most common complications reported are transient hyphema, transient IOP spikes, and peripheral anterior synechiae; some device studies note reoperation/secondary surgical intervention rates, particularly in eyes with higher baseline pressure or more severe disease (e.g., the IRIS Registry analysis of standalone OMNI found notably higher secondary surgery rates in the higher-baseline-IOP subgroup). One RCT comparing ab interno trabeculectomy (Trabectome) directly against trabeculectomy found significantly lower success rates for the ab interno procedure, though with better safety — illustrating the general efficacy/safety trade-off between canal-based MIGS and traditional filtering surgery.

Since your surgeon will weigh these findings against your specific glaucoma severity, prior surgeries, and target pressure, this list is meant to support — not replace — that conversation.
