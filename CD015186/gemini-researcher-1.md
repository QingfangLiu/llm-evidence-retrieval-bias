MIGS targeting Schlemm’s canal (SC-MIGS)—whether through stenting (e.g., Hydrus, iStent) or excisional goniotomy (e.g., Kahook Dual Blade)—demonstrates a strong efficacy-to-safety ratio for patients with primary open-angle glaucoma (OAG). Based exclusively on primary clinical literature, these procedures yield substantial reductions in both intraocular pressure (IOP) and medication burden, with a safety profile that closely mirrors standard cataract surgery.

## Clinical Benefits

* **Significant IOP Reduction:** SC-MIGS consistently lowers unmedicated diurnal IOP. In the HORIZON randomized controlled trial (RCT), 77.3% of eyes receiving a Hydrus Microstent alongside phacoemulsification achieved a ≥20% reduction in unmedicated IOP at 24 months, compared to 57.8% of eyes undergoing phacoemulsification alone (Samuelson et al., 2019). Earlier trial data confirms this magnitude, showing an 80% success rate for the Hydrus device versus 46% for the control group over two years (Pfeiffer et al., 2015).
* **Reduced Medication Burden:** A major quality-of-life benefit is the decreased reliance on topical hypotensive drops. Stenting procedures have been shown to reduce average medication use from 1.7 drops at baseline to 0.3 at 24 months, with over 70% of patients becoming entirely medication-free (Pfeiffer et al., 2015; Samuelson et al., 2019). Excisional approaches yield similar independence; 6-year longitudinal data on the Kahook Dual Blade revealed an average 30.8% reduction in medication burden from baseline (Vasu et al., 2024).
* **Preservation of Visual Function:** SC-MIGS safely lowers pressure without accelerating visual field decay. Comparative data evaluating bypass stents (iStent and iStent inject W) combined with cataract surgery found that best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) improves rapidly post-operation, and visual field indices remain stable without post-operative worsening (Morita et al., 2024).
* **Long-Term Durability:** Excisional goniotomy procedures maintain efficacy over extended periods. Six-year retrospective data demonstrated an overall surgical success rate of 46.2% with the Kahook Dual Blade, maintaining an average 28% reduction in IOP over the long term, independent of concurrent phacoemulsification (Vasu et al., 2024).

## Harms and Safety Profile

The safety profile of SC-MIGS is one of its primary advantages over traditional penetrating surgeries like trabeculectomy.

* **Low Rates of Severe Adverse Events:** Adding an SC-MIGS device to standard cataract surgery does not significantly increase the frequency of serious sight-threatening complications. Across multiple RCTs, there were no serious ocular adverse events directly attributed to the microstents, and overall safety parameters remained statistically similar to standalone cataract surgery (Samuelson et al., 2019).
* **Anatomical Alterations (Synechiae):** The most notable device-related finding following microstent implantation is the development of focal peripheral anterior synechiae (PAS), typically measuring 1–2 mm in length. However, this anatomical finding is generally benign and does not correlate with decreased visual acuity or procedural failure (Pfeiffer et al., 2015).
* **Transient Postoperative Complications:** Excisional and tissue-sparing procedures can occasionally result in mild, transient complications such as temporary IOP spikes or gravity-dependent hyphema (blood settling in the anterior chamber) during the first postoperative week. These typically resolve rapidly without requiring surgical reintervention (Vasu et al., 2024).
