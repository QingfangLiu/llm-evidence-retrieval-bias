## Scope

I treated **SC-MIGS** as ab-interno procedures that improve conventional aqueous outflow by:

* bypassing or removing trabecular meshwork to access Schlemm’s canal;
* dilating/catheterizing Schlemm’s canal and collector channels; or
* scaffolding Schlemm’s canal with an intracanalicular implant.

This includes iStent-family devices, Hydrus, GATT, Kahook Dual Blade goniotomy, Tanito microhook trabeculotomy, Trabectome, ab-interno canaloplasty, OMNI canaloplasty/trabeculotomy, and excimer-laser trabeculotomy. I excluded subconjunctival and suprachoroidal procedures. Traditional trabeculectomy or SLT appears only when it was the comparator.

All evidence summarized below comes from **individual randomized trials, trial extensions, prospective cohorts, or retrospective cohorts**, searched through **July 25, 2026**. This is a focused evidence map rather than a fully reproducible systematic review of every small case series.

## Overall conclusions

**The clearest evidence supports adding a trabecular/Schlemm-canal stent to cataract surgery in mild-to-moderate POAG.** Hydrus has the most complete long-term comparative evidence. At two years, Hydrus added approximately **2.3 mmHg of unmedicated diurnal IOP reduction** relative to cataract surgery alone and reduced medication use. Benefits in medication freedom and avoidance of further incisional surgery persisted through five years. ([PubMed][1])

The pivotal iStent inject trial also found an incremental benefit, although smaller: unmedicated IOP fell by 7.0 mmHg with iStent inject plus cataract surgery versus 5.4 mmHg with cataract surgery alone. An independent trial found fewer medications but **no significant between-group IOP difference** at two years. ([PubMed][2])

For **trabecular cutting, excision, or canaloplasty procedures**, results are heterogeneous. Tanito microhook trabeculotomy has a positive two-year randomized trial, whereas a small randomized KDB trial found no incremental benefit over cataract surgery alone in patients whose glaucoma was already relatively well controlled. OMNI, GATT, and KDB have numerous favorable before–after studies, but comparatively little controlled evidence separating the procedure’s effect from cataract surgery, regression to the mean, medication changes, or selection. ([ScienceDirect][3])

Most trials evaluated **IOP and medication burden**, not patient-important visual outcomes. HORIZON is the main exception: a post-hoc five-year analysis found slower visual-field mean-deviation loss with Hydrus plus cataract surgery than with cataract surgery alone, −0.26 versus −0.49 dB/year. This finding is promising but was a secondary/post-hoc analysis rather than the original primary endpoint. ([PubMed][4])

## Comparative primary evidence

### Hydrus

**Hydrus II randomized trial — Hydrus plus cataract surgery versus cataract surgery alone, 100 eyes**

At 24 months, 80% versus 46% achieved at least a 20% washed-out IOP reduction; mean washed-out IOP was 16.9 versus 19.2 mmHg, and 73% versus 38% were medication-free. Focal peripheral anterior synechiae were the principal device-related finding; other adverse-event rates were similar. ([PubMed][5])

**HORIZON randomized trial — 556 eyes**

At two years:

* ≥20% unmedicated IOP reduction: 77.3% with Hydrus versus 57.8% with cataract surgery alone.
* Mean unmedicated reduction: 7.6 versus 5.3 mmHg.
* Medication count: 0.3 versus 0.7.
* Overall serious ocular-event rates were not significantly different. ([PubMed][1])

At five years:

* ≥20% unmedicated IOP reduction: 54.2% versus 32.8%.
* Medication-free: 66% versus 46%.
* Subsequent incisional glaucoma surgery: 2.4% versus 6.2%.
* No significant difference in endothelial-cell loss from months 3–60. ([PubMed][6])

The visual-field analysis favored Hydrus, although it was post hoc. ([PubMed][4])

### First-generation iStent

**Fea randomized trial — iStent plus cataract surgery versus cataract surgery alone, 36 participants**

At 15 months, mean IOP was 14.8 versus 15.7 mmHg and medication use was 0.4 versus 1.3. After medication washout, mean IOP was 16.6 versus 19.2 mmHg. The four-year follow-up retained a washed-out IOP advantage, although only 24 participants returned. ([PubMed][7])

**US iStent pivotal trial — 240 eyes**

At one and two years, a higher proportion of iStent-treated eyes met medication-free IOP targets than eyes receiving cataract surgery alone. Safety was broadly similar to cataract surgery alone. The Samuelson and Craven publications are successive reports from the same trial rather than separate cohorts. ([PubMed][8])

### iStent inject and iStent infinite

**iStent inject pivotal randomized trial — 505 eyes**

At 24 months:

* ≥20% unmedicated diurnal IOP reduction: 75.8% versus 61.9%.
* Mean unmedicated reduction: 7.0 versus 5.4 mmHg.
* Medication-free IOP ≤18 mmHg: 63.2% versus 50.0%.
* Safety was similar to cataract surgery alone. ([PubMed][2])

A five-year endothelial-safety extension found similar endothelial-cell loss with iStent inject plus cataract surgery and cataract surgery alone. Only 227 of the original 505 patients entered the extension, which limits certainty. ([PubMed][9])

**Independent assessor-masked randomized trial — 104 eyes**

At two years, medication use averaged 0.7 with iStent inject plus cataract surgery versus 1.5 after cataract surgery alone; 57% versus 36% were medication-free. There was no significant IOP difference beyond four weeks and no difference in ocular-surface or glaucoma-related quality-of-life scores. Safety was similar. ([PubMed][10])

**iStent inject versus fixed latanoprost/timolol — 192 participants**

At one year, the proportions achieving at least 20% IOP reduction were similar, 94.7% and 91.8%. One surgical patient developed IOP of 48 mmHg, and one apparently obstructed stent required Nd:YAG treatment. Medication intolerance occurred in the drug group. ([PMC][11])

**INTEGRITY randomized trial — iStent infinite versus Hydrus, 180 eyes, six-month interim analysis**

Mean washed-out IOP reductions were similar, approximately 7.4 and 7.2 mmHg. Reported surgical complications occurred in 3.3% with iStent infinite and 16.9% with Hydrus. Improper placement was reported in 1.1% versus 7.9%, and peripheral anterior synechiae over 1 mm in 1.1% versus 4.5%. This was a short-term, manufacturer-involved interim analysis; longer follow-up is needed. ([PubMed][12])

### Head-to-head stent comparison

**COMPARE randomized trial — standalone Hydrus versus two first-generation iStents, 152 eyes**

Hydrus produced a higher complete-success rate, 0.6 fewer medications on average, and a 22.6-percentage-point higher medication-free proportion at 12 months. Two eyes in the iStent group and none in the Hydrus group required secondary glaucoma surgery. Overall safety profiles were considered similar. ([PubMed][13])

### Trabecular excision and trabeculotomy

**Kahook Dual Blade randomized trial — KDB plus cataract surgery versus cataract surgery alone, 42 eyes**

In relatively well-controlled mild-to-moderate glaucoma, there was no significant between-group difference in IOP or medication use at 12 months. Both groups had similar visual, endothelial, and perimetric outcomes. ([PubMed][14])

**Tanito microhook trabeculotomy randomized trial — 114 eyes**

At 24 months, mean IOP was 15.2 mmHg after microhook trabeculotomy plus cataract surgery versus 18.2 mmHg after cataract surgery alone. Percentage reduction was 42.6% versus 28.1%; medication use was 0.2 versus 1.5. Hyphema occurred in four microhook patients during the first year, with one anterior-chamber washout. Baseline medication counts differed between groups, which complicates medication comparisons. ([PubMed][15])

**GATT versus ab-interno canaloplasty randomized trial — 77 eyes**

At 12 months, mean IOP was 16.0 mmHg after GATT versus 19.0 mmHg after canaloplasty. Medication-free proportions were 77.8% versus 57.2%, but this difference did not reach conventional statistical significance. GATT caused more hyphema, approximately 87% versus 47%, and more supraciliary effusion on imaging, approximately 92% versus 71%. Four eyes required further glaucoma surgery. ([PubMed][16])

**Trabectome versus trabeculectomy, both with cataract surgery — 19-patient randomized trial**

The study stopped early and was severely underpowered. IOP and medication use were similar at one year; severe complications occurred only in the trabeculectomy group. One Trabectome patient needed another glaucoma procedure. ([PubMed][17])

**Excimer-laser trabeculotomy versus SLT — 30-eye randomized trial**

At two years, complete success was 53.3% after ELT and 40% after SLT, a nonsignificant difference. Mean IOP fell from 25.0 to 17.6 mmHg after ELT and from 23.9 to 19.1 mmHg after SLT. The trial was too small to exclude clinically important differences in benefits or harms. ([PubMed][18])

**Three canaloplasty variants plus cataract surgery — 48-patient randomized study**

Ab-externo canaloplasty, mini-canaloplasty, and ab-interno canaloplasty all reduced IOP and medication use through three years. With only 16 eyes per group, the study provides little precision for comparisons between approaches or uncommon harms. ([PubMed][19])

## Important nonrandomized evidence

**OMNI GEMINI prospective cohort**

Among 149 operated eyes, with 120 in the principal effectiveness analysis, unmedicated diurnal IOP fell from 23.8 to 15.6 mmHg at 12 months and medications from 1.8 to 0.4; 80% were medication-free. Transient hyphema occurred in 6% and transient IOP elevation in 2%; no secondary glaucoma surgery occurred during the first year. In the optional extension, 66 participants were followed, with 74% medication-free at 36 months. Because every patient received OMNI plus cataract surgery, the incremental OMNI effect cannot be separated from cataract surgery. ([PMC][20])

**OMNI ROMEO cohorts**

Separate retrospective cohorts evaluated combined and standalone OMNI. Results generally showed lower medication use and lower IOP when baseline IOP was elevated. At extended follow-up, 6 of 72 eyes required another laser or surgical intervention after the first postoperative year. ([PubMed][21])

**GATT cohort**

In the 198-patient 24-month report, the POAG subgroup had an average IOP reduction of 9.2 mmHg and used 1.43 fewer medications. Cumulative failure varied substantially by glaucoma subgroup. Because this was an uncontrolled chart review, these reductions should not be interpreted as a randomized treatment effect. ([PubMed][22])

**Prospective KDB cohort**

In 52 eyes undergoing KDB plus cataract surgery, mean IOP fell from 16.8 to 12.4 mmHg at 12 months. The absence of a concurrent cataract-only group is important, particularly with a relatively low baseline IOP. ([PubMed][23])

**iStent infinite prospective single-arm trial**

Among 72 eyes with glaucoma uncontrolled after prior treatment or maximal medical therapy, 76.1% met the prespecified 12-month responder endpoint and mean diurnal IOP declined by 5.9 mmHg. No hypotony, infection, explantation, or device-related intervention was reported. ([PubMed][24])

**Ab-interno canaloplasty long-term cohort**

In 27 eyes followed for up to four years, mean IOP declined from 19.8 to 14.6 mmHg and medications from 1.9 to 0.9. The cohort was small, uncontrolled, and largely underwent simultaneous cataract surgery. ([PubMed][25])

## Harms across SC-MIGS

The harm profile differs by mechanism:

* **Trabecular cutting or circumferential trabeculotomy:** transient hyphema is common and can be very frequent after GATT; retained blood occasionally requires washout. IOP spikes are also reported. ([PubMed][16])
* **Intracanalicular implants:** peripheral anterior synechiae, malposition, obstruction, focal iris changes, and occasional hyphema are characteristic device-related events. ([PubMed][5])
* **Canaloplasty/viscodilation:** Descemet detachment or limited descemetolysis, hyphema, inflammation, and IOP spikes are reported, generally infrequently in the small cohorts. ([PubMed][26])
* **Further glaucoma intervention:** rates vary with baseline severity and the definition of failure. In controlled mild-to-moderate populations they are usually low, but they reach approximately 5%–13% in some uncontrolled or real-world cohorts. ([PubMed][27])
* **Corneal endothelium:** available five-year comparative data for Hydrus and iStent inject do not indicate excess endothelial-cell loss over cataract surgery alone. ([PubMed][6])

The absence of many serious events should not be interpreted as proof that they cannot occur. Most studies were not powered to detect uncommon endophthalmitis, persistent hypotony, cyclodialysis, major hemorrhage, severe endothelial failure, or permanent vision loss.

## Main evidence limitations

1. Most randomized evidence concerns **mild-to-moderate POAG with cataract**, not severe glaucoma, very low target pressures, normal-tension glaucoma, or young patients.
2. Cataract surgery is an active IOP-lowering cointervention; uncontrolled combined-surgery studies cannot determine the incremental MIGS effect.
3. Definitions of “success,” medication washout, hyphema, IOP spike, and reoperation vary considerably.
4. Medication reduction may partly reflect postoperative prescribing decisions rather than purely physiological efficacy.
5. Long-term comparative evidence is concentrated in HORIZON; most other procedures have one- to two-year evidence.
6. Visual-field preservation, quality of life, ocular-surface outcomes, adherence, and patient preferences are infrequently measured.
7. Several pivotal studies involved device manufacturers in sponsorship, study conduct, authorship, or analysis, making independent replication particularly important.
