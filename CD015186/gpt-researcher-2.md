## Scope

I treated **SC-MIGS** as non-implant procedures that directly open, excise, incise, or viscodilate the trabecular meshwork–Schlemm’s canal pathway: GATT/suture trabeculotomy, ab interno canaloplasty, Kahook Dual Blade goniotomy, microhook trabeculotomy, Trabectome/ab interno trabeculectomy, and excimer laser trabeculotomy. Implanted trabecular bypass devices such as iStent appear only as comparators.

This is a targeted map of **comparative primary studies**, emphasizing randomized trials. I excluded reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, opinions, editorials, and uncontrolled case series from the main synthesis.

## Main findings

### Overall interpretation

SC-MIGS generally lowers IOP and/or medication burden, but the incremental benefit depends strongly on the comparator and baseline IOP:

* Compared with **phacoemulsification alone**, substantial additional pressure lowering was demonstrated in one relatively large microhook trial with baseline IOP around 25–27 mmHg, while two smaller KDB or suture-trabeculotomy trials with lower baseline IOP found little or uncertain incremental benefit.
* Compared with **trabeculectomy**, the very small trials did not demonstrate a clear difference in medium-term IOP control, but severe or numerous postoperative complications were concentrated in the trabeculectomy arms.
* Compared with an **iStent**, average IOP and medication outcomes were similar. One KDB trial favored KDB on a composite endpoint, but not on continuous IOP or medication outcomes.
* In head-to-head SC-MIGS trials, **circumferential GATT tended to lower IOP more than canaloplasty or limited goniotomy**, at the cost of more hyphema.
* The evidence predominantly concerns IOP, medications, and short-term surgical success—not visual disability, quality of life, or prevention of glaucomatous progression.

## Evidence by comparison

### 1. SC-MIGS added to cataract surgery versus cataract surgery alone

**Microhook trabeculotomy:** In 114 randomized patients, phaco-microhook reduced mean IOP from 26.5 to 12.5 mmHg at 12 months, versus 25.3 to 20.0 mmHg after phaco alone. At 24 months, among 99 analyzed patients, IOP was 15.2 versus 18.2 mmHg and medication use was 0.2 versus 1.5. The groups were imbalanced in baseline medication use. Four combined-surgery patients developed hyphema during the first year, with one anterior-chamber washout; no additional complications occurred from months 12–24. ([PubMed][1])

**Suture trabeculotomy:** A pilot trial randomized only 18 eyes. At 24 months, IOP was 11.8 mmHg after combined surgery versus 14.6 mmHg after phaco alone, with higher protocol-defined success after combined surgery. Medication use did not fall materially, and the sample was too small for reliable between-group safety estimates. ([PubMed][2])

**KDB goniotomy:** In 42 eyes from 33 participants with well-controlled glaucoma or ocular hypertension, adding KDB did not improve 12-month IOP or medication outcomes: IOP was 16.0 versus 15.0 mmHg and median medication use was zero in both groups. Because some participants contributed both eyes and the trial was retrospectively registered, its estimates warrant caution. ([PubMed][3])

**Interpretation:** Incremental benefit over phaco appears most convincing in the higher-baseline-IOP microhook population. Cross-trial differences could reflect baseline pressure, medication washout, glaucoma subtype, procedure extent, or study methods rather than true device differences.

### 2. SC-MIGS versus conventional glaucoma surgery

**Ab interno goniotrabeculotomy versus MMC trabeculectomy:** In 32 patients, trabeculectomy produced lower IOP at months 1 and 3, but there was no significant difference from months 6–24. At 24 months, IOP was ≤14 mmHg in 87.5% versus 81.3%. More postoperative complications occurred with trabeculectomy. ([PubMed][4])

**Phaco-Trabectome versus phaco-trabeculectomy:** Recruitment stopped early after only 19 patients. Twelve-month IOP was 16.8 versus 17.1 mmHg and medication use 0.44 versus 0.75, with no statistically significant differences. Mild or moderate complications occurred in both arms, but severe complications occurred only after phaco-trabeculectomy; one Trabectome patient required further glaucoma surgery. ([PubMed][5])

**Ab interno versus external canaloplasty variants:** Forty-eight patients were randomized to ABiC, minicanaloplasty, or conventional ab externo canaloplasty, all with phaco. Median post-washout IOP was approximately 15 mmHg in every group at three years, with no significant between-group difference. A ≥20% unmedicated reduction occurred in 56.2%, 68.8%, and 75.0%, respectively. One minicanaloplasty patient required reoperation. ([PubMed][6])

**Interpretation:** These trials suggest that some ab interno procedures may offer comparable medium-term pressure control with fewer severe complications than filtering surgery, but the evidence is far too small to establish equivalence or reliably compare uncommon harms.

### 3. SC-MIGS versus trabecular micro-bypass stents

**KDB versus single iStent, both with phaco:** Among 164 analyzed eyes, 12-month IOP was 15.4 versus 16.1 mmHg and medication use 0.3 versus 0.4—neither difference was statistically significant. The composite outcome of either ≥20% IOP reduction or reduction of at least one medication occurred in 93.7% versus 83.3%, favoring KDB. Safety profiles were described as similar. ([Mayo Clinic][7])

**STREAMLINE canaloplasty versus iStent inject W, both with phaco:** Interim six-month results from 72 randomized eyes showed nearly identical IOP—16.52 versus 16.08 mmHg—and medication-free proportions of 81.8% versus 78.4%. Adverse events were mild and self-limited. This remains an interim analysis rather than the planned complete 24-month trial. ([PMC][8])

**Interpretation:** No convincing average IOP advantage over trabecular stenting has been demonstrated. The KDB composite advantage was driven by a combined endpoint and was not accompanied by significant differences in the individual continuous outcomes.

### 4. One SC-MIGS procedure versus another

**GATT versus ABiC:** At 12 months, mean IOP was lower with GATT—16.0 versus 19.0 mmHg—but medication-free status, medication count, and complete success did not differ statistically. Hyphema occurred in 87% versus 47%, and imaging-detected supraciliary effusion in 92% versus 71%, respectively. Further glaucoma surgery was required in one GATT and three ABiC eyes. ([PubMed][9])

**GATT versus bent ab interno needle goniotomy:** In 45 pseudophakic patients, average IOP and medication differences at approximately 11 months were not statistically significant, but GATT produced substantially higher complete and qualified success rates. Transient hyphema occurred in 56.5% after GATT and 40.9% after needle goniotomy. ([PubMed][10])

**KDB versus microhook trabeculotomy:** In 58 analyzed patients, KDB produced a persistently larger trabecular opening but no significant advantage in IOP, medications, endothelial-cell density, or complications through one year. Anterior-chamber flare was higher with KDB at 12 months. ([PubMed][11])

**Extent of suture trabeculotomy:** Ninety-nine patients were randomized to 360°, upper-180°, or lower-180° incision. At five years, average IOP had fallen from 18.8 to 13.5 mmHg and medications from 3.3 to 2.0, but no significant differences were found among incision extents in IOP, success, or visual-field slope. Only 56.5% completed five years, creating considerable attrition uncertainty. ([PubMed][12])

**Interpretation:** Circumferential GATT may provide stronger pressure lowering than canal dilation or limited goniotomy in some populations, but it consistently produces more postoperative bleeding. Evidence that a 360° incision is superior to a 180° incision is absent.

### 5. SC-MIGS versus laser treatment

In 30 medically uncontrolled POAG patients, excimer laser trabeculotomy reduced mean IOP from 25.0 to 17.6 mmHg at 24 months, versus 23.9 to 19.1 mmHg with 180° SLT. However, complete success—53.3% versus 40.0%—and qualified success—33.3% versus 26.6%—did not differ statistically. With only 15 eyes per arm, harms and reinterventions are very imprecisely estimated. ([PubMed][13])

## Harms across the studies

The characteristic adverse effect is **blood reflux or hyphema**, especially after circumferential trabeculotomy. Most episodes resolved without intervention, but anterior-chamber washout was occasionally required. GATT generally produced more hyphema than canaloplasty or limited microhook/needle goniotomy. ([PubMed][9])

Other reported events included transient IOP spikes, supraciliary or ciliochoroidal effusion detected on imaging, postoperative inflammation or flare, and occasional need for further glaucoma surgery. Severe postoperative events were uncommon in SC-MIGS arms, but the trials are too small to exclude rare complications such as persistent hypotony, infection, clinically important endothelial loss, or sight-threatening hemorrhage.

Compared with trabeculectomy, the small direct trials favored SC-MIGS regarding severe or overall complications, but they cannot provide precise comparative risk estimates. ([PubMed][4])

## Outcomes that remain uncertain

* **Visual-field preservation:** Only the five-year incision-extent trial provided a substantive longitudinal field analysis, and it compared three variants of the same operation rather than SC-MIGS with no SC-MIGS. ([PubMed][12])
* **Quality of life and patient-reported outcomes:** These were not meaningfully addressed in the identified comparative reports.
* **Rare or delayed harms:** Sample sizes and follow-up are inadequate.
* **Advanced glaucoma or very low target IOP:** Most contemporary studies enrolled mild-to-moderate disease.
* **Stand-alone versus combined effects:** Many procedures were performed with phacoemulsification, so findings should not automatically be extrapolated to standalone SC-MIGS.
