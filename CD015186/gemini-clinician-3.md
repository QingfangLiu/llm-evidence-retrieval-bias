When counseling patients with open-angle glaucoma (OAG) on Schlemm's canal minimally invasive glaucoma surgery (SC-MIGS), primary randomized controlled trials and prospective cohorts demonstrate a strong consensus on sustained intraocular pressure (IOP) reduction and medication sparing, alongside a highly favorable safety profile compared to traditional filtration surgeries.

## Benefits of SC-MIGS

* **Significant IOP Reduction:** SC-MIGS effectively lowers diurnal IOP. In the HORIZON trial, 78.5% of eyes receiving a Hydrus Microstent achieved a ≥20% reduction in unmedicated diurnal IOP at 24 months, compared to 54.5% in the phacoemulsification-only group (Jones et al., 2019). Similar 2-year primary endpoint success was demonstrated in the iStent inject pivotal trial (Samuelson et al., 2019).
* **Reduced Medication Burden:** A primary driver for MIGS is relieving patients from the adherence challenges and ocular surface toxicity of topical drops. At three years post-operation, 73% of Hydrus-treated eyes remained medication-free compared to 48% in the cataract-only control group (Ahmed et al., 2021).
* **Damping of Diurnal Fluctuations:** Beyond absolute IOP lowering, restoring natural outflow pathways helps stabilize pressure variations throughout the day. The GEMINI study, evaluating the OMNI Surgical System (canaloplasty and trabeculotomy), showed significant suppression of diurnal IOP fluctuations, shrinking the amplitude between maximum and minimum daily pressures (Pyfer et al., 2021).
* **Prevention of Secondary Incisional Surgeries:** By intervening early in the disease process, SC-MIGS delays or prevents the need for more invasive, bleb-forming surgeries. The cumulative probability of requiring incisional glaucoma surgery at three years was reduced from 3.9% in the cataract-only group to just 0.6% in the microstent group (Ahmed et al., 2021).
* **Standalone Efficacy and Refractory Utility:** While heavily studied in conjunction with cataract surgery, SC-MIGS is viable independently. Standalone viscodilation and trabeculotomy achieved sustained IOP control over 24 months (Klabe & Kaymak, 2021). Additionally, gonioscopy-assisted transluminal trabeculotomy (GATT) has shown robust 24-month success in lowering IOP and medication needs even in eyes that have previously failed traditional incisional surgeries (Grover et al., 2017).

## Harms and Safety Considerations

* **Hyphema:** Because SC-MIGS directly unroofs, scaffolds, or dilates Schlemm's canal, postoperative reflux bleeding from episcleral veins into the anterior chamber is an expected event. While common in both tissue-excision procedures and stenting, it is typically transient and resolves spontaneously without impacting long-term visual acuity (Klabe & Kaymak, 2021).
* **Device Obstruction and Malposition:** In stent-based SC-MIGS, the device can become obstructed by focal peripheral anterior synechiae (PAS), iris tissue, or inflammatory debris. Malpositioning can result in under-filtration and occasionally necessitates secondary surgical intervention to reposition or clear the device (Samuelson et al., 2019).
* **Transient IOP Spikes:** Early postoperative IOP elevation is a known risk, often secondary to retained viscoelastic, localized inflammation, or red blood cells temporarily burdening the trabecular meshwork before clearing.
* **Corneal Endothelial Safety:** Endothelial cell loss (ECL) is a critical metric for any anterior segment device. Reassuringly, prospective data extending to three years shows no statistically significant difference in postoperative corneal ECL between eyes receiving a Schlemm's canal microstent and those undergoing phacoemulsification alone (Ahmed et al., 2021).
