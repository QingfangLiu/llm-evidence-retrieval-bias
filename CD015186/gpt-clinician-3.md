## Scope

I used a **narrow, nonimplant definition of Schlemm’s-canal MIGS**: ab interno procedures that incise, excise, or dilate the trabecular meshwork/Schlemm’s canal, including GATT or suture trabeculotomy, microhook or Kahook Dual Blade goniotomy, Trabectome/ab interno goniotrabeculotomy, ab interno canaloplasty, and excimer-laser trabeculotomy.

I did **not** include iStent or Hydrus trials, which are generally evaluated as trabecular micro-bypass implant studies. I prioritized randomized comparative studies; observational primary studies are identified separately. Search current through **July 25, 2026**.

## Overall interpretation

### Benefits

SC-MIGS generally lowers IOP and medication burden, but the incremental benefit varies considerably by baseline IOP and comparator.

* **When added to phacoemulsification:** A small 18-eye trial of 360-degree suture trabeculotomy found a 24-month IOP of 11.8 mmHg versus 14.6 mmHg with phaco alone. A larger 114-eye microhook trial with baseline IOP around 25–27 mmHg found a 24-month IOP of 15.2 versus 18.2 mmHg. ([PubMed][1])
* **In relatively well-controlled glaucoma:** A 42-eye KDB trial with baseline IOP around 17–18 mmHg found no incremental benefit over phaco alone at 12 months: IOP was 16.0 versus 15.0 mmHg, and both groups had a median of zero medications. ([PubMed][2])
* **Versus trabeculectomy:** The two randomized trials were extremely small. They suggested similar mean IOP by 12–24 months, with more postoperative or severe complications after trabeculectomy, but they cannot establish equivalence—especially for advanced glaucoma or very low target pressures. ([PubMed][3])
* **Between SC-MIGS procedures:** GATT produced approximately 3 mmHg lower IOP than ab interno canaloplasty at 12 months, but with more hyphema and supraciliary effusion. GATT also achieved higher protocol-defined success than limited bent-needle goniotomy. ([PubMed][4])

The benefit appears more convincing when preoperative IOP is high than when the main goal is medication reduction in already-controlled, mild disease. That conclusion is an inference from the contrasting Maheshwari and Ventura-Abreu trials rather than a prespecified cross-trial comparison. ([ScienceDirect][5])

### Harms

The characteristic adverse effect is **blood reflux or postoperative hyphema**.

* Hyphema occurred in 87% after GATT versus 47% after ABiC in one trial, and in 57% versus 41% after GATT and bent-needle goniotomy in another. Most episodes were transient. ([PubMed][4])
* In the trial comparing 360-degree with 180-degree suture trabeculotomy, layered hyphema occurred in 26% after 360-degree treatment versus 6% after lower-180-degree treatment. One eye required anterior-chamber washout. ([BMJ Best Practice][6])
* **Early IOP spikes** can be frequent. In that same trial, IOP of at least 30 mmHg occurred in 26%–47%, depending on incision group; transient hypotony occurred in 0%–9%. ([PMC][7])
* **Additional glaucoma surgery remains possible:** 3 of 38 ABiC eyes and 1 of 39 GATT eyes underwent another glaucoma operation within 12 months; in the incision-extent study, 3%–9% required additional surgery. ([PubMed][4])
* Imaging-detected **supraciliary effusion** was common in the ABiC-versus-GATT trial—71% and 92%, respectively—although the clinical importance and generalizability of these imaging findings are uncertain. ([PubMed][4])

Serious infection, persistent hypotony, or major permanent vision loss was uncommon in these trials, but the studies were much too small to exclude rare severe complications. Reporting of complications was inconsistent across studies.

## Outcomes that remain uncertain

The randomized evidence does **not adequately answer**:

* Whether SC-MIGS prevents visual-field progression, disability, or blindness better than medication, laser, or conventional surgery.
* Effects on vision-related quality of life and patient satisfaction.
* Long-term comparative effectiveness beyond two years; only one randomized cohort has reported five-year results, with substantial attrition.
* Safety in advanced glaucoma, very low target-pressure eyes, prior angle surgery, and patients at elevated bleeding risk.
* Rare complications, because most trials enrolled fewer than 100 participants.

Thus, the best-supported patient-facing expectation is: **a reasonable chance of a moderate IOP and/or medication reduction, with rapid conjunctiva-sparing surgery, but a meaningful chance of transient hyphema or an early pressure spike and no guarantee that filtration surgery will be avoided later.**

## Supportive observational primary evidence

Observational data provide larger safety samples but are more susceptible to selection bias and regression to the mean.

* A 531-eye retrospective ABiC series reported mean IOP reductions of approximately 3.3–4.5 mmHg over an average 20 months; intraoperative complications occurred in 1.6%, while 13.1% subsequently needed additional glaucoma surgery. ([PubMed][16])
* A 197-eye KDB cohort reported transient day-one hyphema in 17.3% and a one-week IOP spike greater than 10 mmHg in 10.2%. ([PubMed][17])
* A 27-eye ABiC series with a mean follow-up of approximately 6.6 years reported IOP reduction from 19.9 to 14.7 mmHg; only seven eyes reached ten-year follow-up. ([PubMed][18])
* A retrospective comparison of ab interno and ab externo circumferential suture trabeculotomy found large IOP reductions with both approaches, with hyphema and transient pressure spikes as the common complications. ([PubMed][19])
