Deciding on the right surgical approach for open-angle glaucoma is a significant step. Procedures targeting Schlemm’s canal—known as SC-MIGS—work by addressing the eye's natural drainage pathway to bypass the fluid resistance typically found in the trabecular meshwork.

Here is a breakdown of the benefits and harms of SC-MIGS procedures, drawn exclusively from primary clinical trials and comparative studies.

## Benefits of SC-MIGS

Based on primary clinical data, the benefits center around lowering intraocular pressure and reducing the daily burden of prescription eye drops:

* **Significant Intraocular Pressure (IOP) Reduction:** SC-MIGS devices consistently lower IOP over multi-year periods. In the HORIZON randomized controlled trial, 77.3% of patients receiving a Schlemm canal microstent achieved an unmedicated IOP reduction of at least 20% after two years, compared to just 57.8% of patients who only had cataract surgery (Samuelson et al., 2019). Similarly, the GEMINI study on the OMNI surgical system (which performs canaloplasty and trabeculotomy) demonstrated a 35% reduction in mean unmedicated IOP at 12 months, which was sustained through 36 months (Gallardo et al., 2022; Greenwood et al., 2023).
* **Reduced Medication Burden:** A major quality-of-life benefit is a decreased reliance on topical glaucoma medications. The HORIZON study showed patients dropped from an average of 1.7 daily medications to just 0.3 at the 24-month mark (Samuelson et al., 2019). Patients undergoing OMNI canaloplasty and trabeculotomy experienced an 80% reduction in medication use over 12 months (Gallardo et al., 2022).
* **Performance Beyond Traditional Expectations:** Stents placed in Schlemm's canal, such as the iStent *inject*, have demonstrated the ability to lower postoperative IOP beyond the expected floor limits typically ascribed to traditional trabecular devices, occasionally driving pressures down into the low teens (Guedes et al., 2019).

## Harms and Risks of SC-MIGS

Primary data shows that SC-MIGS procedures generally carry fewer severe risks than traditional, more invasive glaucoma surgeries (like trabeculectomy), but they are not without drawbacks:

* **Waning Efficacy and Retreatment:** The most common drawback is that a device may fail to control IOP over the long term. A prospective survival analysis comparing micro-bypass stents found an increased incidence of failure beyond 5 months for some devices, meaning those patients had to restart their glaucoma medications to maintain safe pressures (Hooshmand et al., 2019).
* **General Surgical Risks:** Large-scale trials report that these procedures have an excellent overall safety profile with no serious ocular adverse events directly caused by the microstents. Generally, the complication rate and safety profile remain equivalent to the risks of undergoing standard cataract surgery alone (Gallardo et al., 2022; Samuelson et al., 2019).
