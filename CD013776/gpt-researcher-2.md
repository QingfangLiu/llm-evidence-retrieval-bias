## Scope

I located **17 distinct randomized trial families** directly comparing fluorescence/blue-light-assisted TURBT with white-light TURBT. Multiple publications from the same randomized cohort are grouped together to prevent double counting. I also list several informative comparative cohort studies, particularly newer studies addressing progression or complications.

“Blue-light TURBT” generally meant conventional white-light inspection/resection **plus an additional fluorescence-guided inspection and resection**, using intravesical 5-aminolevulinic acid (5-ALA) or hexaminolevulinate/hexaminolevulinic acid (HAL). Newer Japanese studies have used **oral 5-ALA**, which has a different safety profile.

I excluded secondary literature, noncomparative series, surveillance-only flexible blue-light cystoscopy, narrow-band imaging/SPIES studies, guidelines, opinions, and editorials.

## Interpretation from the individual studies

**Recurrence:** The results are genuinely inconsistent. Several older, relatively small or single-center trials reported large recurrence reductions, particularly the Riedl/Daniltchenko, Filbeck/Denzinger, Babjuk, Geavlete, Karaolides, Rolevich, and Drăgoescu studies. Conversely, several multicenter or more pragmatic trials—Schumacher, Stenzl 2011, O’Brien, Kelloniemi, and especially PHOTO—found no meaningful recurrence advantage. The large HAL trial reported a modest early benefit that persisted as a longer median time to recurrence, but not a statistically significant reduction in cystectomy. ([PubMed][6])

**Progression:** Progression was usually a secondary outcome with few events and inconsistent definitions. Rolevich reported a statistically significant reduction, while Stenzl 2011 and Kelloniemi did not; the long-term Danish cohort found no progression advantage. Results using broadened “IBCG progression” definitions should not be equated with progression to muscle-invasive or metastatic disease. ([PubMed][14])

**Complications:** Comparative reporting was sparse in the older trials. The best modern intravesical-HAL evidence, PHOTO, found nearly identical adverse-event frequencies, and O’Brien reported no HAL-related events. Intravesical 5-ALA likewise produced no local or systemic reactions in Kelloniemi. In contrast, **oral 5-ALA should be treated as a separate safety intervention**: comparative Japanese studies consistently identify substantially more intraoperative hypotension and vasopressor use than with WL-TURBT. ([NCBI][25])

Overall, the individual-study record supports improved lesion detection and sometimes more complete resection, but it does **not demonstrate a consistently reproducible reduction in recurrence across settings**, and convincing evidence for preventing conventional progression remains limited. Intravesical HAL/5-ALA does not appear to materially increase ordinary surgical complications in the studies that reported them; oral 5-ALA carries a clinically important hypotension risk.
