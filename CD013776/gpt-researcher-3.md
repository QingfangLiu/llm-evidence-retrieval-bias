## Scope

I found **17 randomized index-TURBT trial cohorts** comparing fluorescence/blue-light-assisted TURBT—using intravesical 5-aminolevulinic acid (5-ALA) or hexaminolevulinate/hexylaminolevulinate (HAL)—with conventional white-light TURBT. I merged publications that were clearly follow-ups or subgroup analyses of the same randomized cohort.

I excluded systematic reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, consensus statements, editorials, and comments from the study list below.

## What the individual studies suggest

**Recurrence:** The findings are inconsistent. Numerous older, generally smaller or single-center trials reported substantial reductions in early residual disease or recurrence. However, several multicenter, blinded, specialist-surgeon, or contemporary trials—including Schumacher, the placebo-controlled Stenzl trial, O’Brien, Gkritsios, Neuzillet, and PHOTO—did not demonstrate a recurrence advantage. The large international Stenzl/Grossman HAL trial falls between these groups, showing a modest early reduction and longer median time to recurrence. ([PubMed][3])

**Progression:** Evidence is much less developed. Rolevich reported a statistically significant progression reduction, whereas the placebo-controlled Stenzl trial and the T1 high-grade Denzinger subgroup found no progression benefit. Most other trials had few progression events, short follow-up, or did not adequately report the outcome. ([PubMed][15])

**Complications:** Available primary-study reporting does not show a clear increase in complications from blue-light-assisted TURBT. Kriegmair reported no side-effect difference, O’Brien reported no HAL-related adverse events, the large Stenzl trial found similar overall adverse-event patterns, and PHOTO reported rare and similar events. Nevertheless, most studies were not designed or powered to exclude clinically meaningful differences in uncommon complications such as bladder perforation, major bleeding, or readmission. ([ScienceDirect][4])

The most important extraction issue is that **tumor detected at a 6-week or 3-month re-TURBT is frequently labeled “recurrence,” although it may primarily represent residual disease from the index operation**. Those endpoints should not automatically be pooled with pathologically confirmed later recurrences. There is also substantial heterogeneity in prior bladder-cancer history, risk category, immediate postoperative chemotherapy, subsequent BCG/chemotherapy, operator expertise, photosensitizer, and post-randomization exclusion of patients without confirmed NMIBC.
