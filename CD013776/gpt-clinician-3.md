## Clinical takeaway

Across individual comparative studies, blue-light/photodynamic diagnosis–assisted TURBT **more consistently improves tumor detection and completeness of resection than it improves long-term outcomes**.

* **Recurrence:** Several earlier or single-center randomized trials reported substantial reductions, but other well-conducted trials—including the large contemporary PHOTO trial—found no benefit. The effect may be greatest where conventional TURBT quality is variable, in multifocal/recurrent disease, or when occult CIS is likely.
* **Progression:** Evidence is much less consistent. Most trials were underpowered for progression, and the largest pragmatic trial showed no reduction.
* **Complications:** Intravesical hexaminolevulinate or 5-ALA has not produced a consistent increase in TURBT complications. Reported events were primarily typical post-TURBT urinary symptoms.

The studies below are **individual primary studies only**. Companion follow-ups from the same randomized cohort are grouped so they are not mistaken for independent trials.

## Relevant contemporary nonrandomized primary studies

These are more vulnerable to confounding but add information on contemporary TURBT and adjuvant-treatment practice.

* **Miyake et al., 2023:** Propensity-matched 495 PDD and 495 white-light primary NMIBC patients. Recurrence-free survival and a broad “IBCG progression” endpoint incorporating grade-up and stage-up favored PDD, but there was **no difference in conventional progression to MIBC/metastasis, MIBC-free survival, metastasis-free survival or overall survival**. ([PMC][16])
* **Hoogeveen et al., 2023:** Single-institution historical cohort of 215 newly suspected NMIBC patients found no meaningful recurrence difference at 6 months, 12 months or 5 years; recurrence-free survival HR **1.12, 95% CI 0.70–1.79**, numerically favoring white light. ([PubMed][17])
* **BRIGHT follow-up, 2024:** Prospective oral 5-ALA PDD cohort compared with a retrospectively assembled white-light cohort; after propensity matching, one-year recurrence after second TURBT was lower with PDD, HR **0.44, 95% CI 0.25–0.77**. The nonconcurrent control and oral rather than intravesical 5-ALA limit applicability to standard intravesical HAL blue-light practice. ([PubMed][18])
* **Hjort et al., 2026:** Nationwide Danish registry cohort of 4,318 BCG-treated patients. PDD was associated with modestly lower one-year recurrence, adjusted RR **0.85**, but not five-year recurrence, RR **0.94**, and there was no long-term progression or BCG-response advantage. ([PubMed][19])

## How I would interpret this for counseling

**Recurrence prevention is plausible but not guaranteed.** The largest positive HAL trial produced about a 9% absolute reduction at nine months, whereas PHOTO—arguably the most pragmatic contemporary test of routine use in first intermediate/high-risk tumors—found essentially no difference at three years. ([PMC][6])

The variability between trials suggests that the incremental value of blue light may be greatest when it compensates for missed lesions or incomplete conventional resection. It may add less when TURBT is already performed by experienced bladder-cancer teams, with meticulous inspection, adequate detrusor sampling, appropriate repeat TURBT and consistent immediate/adjuvant intravesical therapy. The surgeon-interaction analysis from Rolevich and the null specialist-team and PHOTO trials support—but do not definitively prove—this explanation. ([PMC][13])

**Progression prevention remains unproven.** One single-center factorial trial found a significant progression reduction, but several other randomized studies, including PHOTO, found no benefit, and the events were sparse. ([PubMed][12])

**Safety appears comparable to white-light TURBT for intravesical HAL/5-ALA.** The better-reported trials found no clinically important increase in overall or serious adverse events; hematuria, dysuria, bladder spasm and retention predominated and were largely attributable to TURBT itself. ([PMC][6])
