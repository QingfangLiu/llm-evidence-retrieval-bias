## Bottom line

**Blue-light-assisted TURBT generally finds more tumors—particularly flat carcinoma in situ—but the evidence that this consistently prevents recurrence is mixed.** Several older randomized studies found meaningful reductions in recurrence, while several other randomized studies—including the large, modern PHOTO trial—found no benefit.

For **progression to muscle-invasive or metastatic cancer**, no individual randomized trial has demonstrated a clear, statistically convincing reduction. For **complications**, blue-light-assisted TURBT appears broadly comparable with white-light TURBT; most adverse events are attributable to TURBT itself rather than the blue-light agent.

In most studies, “blue-light TURBT” meant **white-light inspection followed by additional blue-light inspection and resection**, rather than blue light replacing white light.

## How I would interpret this evidence

### Preventing recurrence

The evidence supports a **possible recurrence benefit**, but not a guaranteed one. The positive trials frequently came from earlier eras, smaller centers or populations containing recurrent and multifocal tumors. The negative PHOTO and O’Brien trials studied newly presenting tumors in settings with experienced surgeons and contemporary postoperative management. One plausible interpretation is that blue light provides its greatest incremental benefit when ordinary visualization or completeness of resection is more challenging; excellent white-light TURBT may narrow the difference.

The PHOTO result is particularly important because it was large, multicenter and designed around a clinically meaningful three-year recurrence endpoint. Its confidence interval does not exclude a modest benefit or harm, but it excludes the large reduction the investigators originally expected. ([PubMed][1])

### Preventing progression

Progression is substantially less common than recurrence, so most trials were too small or too short to answer this reliably. The available individual studies show:

* No progression benefit in the PHOTO trial.
* No benefit in the randomized 5-ALA trials.
* No reduction in muscle-invasive progression in the long-term T1 high-grade subgroup.
* A suggestive—but not definitive—signal in a secondary reanalysis of the Stenzl/Grossman cohort.

Therefore, **blue light should not be considered proven to prevent muscle-invasive progression, cystectomy or bladder-cancer death**.

### Complications

The best directly comparative safety data show **similar rates of urinary symptoms, bleeding, retention and serious adverse events**. These are mainly consequences of cystoscopy and TURBT. Blue light can lead to additional biopsies or resection because more suspicious areas are seen, but the randomized studies did not demonstrate a meaningful overall increase in serious surgical complications. ([PMC][2])

## Practical patient-level conclusion

Blue-light assistance is a reasonable technique for improving lesion detection and completeness of resection, particularly when **CIS, multifocal disease or difficult-to-see lesions are suspected**. Its strongest established advantage is detection; its recurrence advantage varies between trials, and a progression advantage remains unproven. The quality and completeness of the TURBT, obtaining adequate detrusor muscle, appropriate repeat resection when indicated, and subsequent intravesical treatment may be at least as consequential as the light technology itself.

These studies apply only after pathology confirms NMIBC. If the TURBT identifies muscle-invasive disease, the recurrence comparisons above no longer describe the relevant treatment pathway.
