## Evidence map

I found the following **individual comparative primary studies** of blue-light/photodynamic-diagnosis-assisted TURBT versus standard white-light TURBT. I excluded reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, opinions, editorials, and studies limited to diagnostic accuracy or surveillance without treatment outcomes.

This is a targeted evidence map rather than a formally registered systematic review. “Blue light” encompasses both **intravesical hexaminolevulinate/5-ALA** and, in several Japanese studies, **oral 5-ALA**; their adverse-effect profiles differ.

## Interpretation by outcome

### Recurrence

The individual trials are **inconsistent**. Several earlier, generally smaller or single-/two-center studies reported large reductions in residual tumor and recurrence. ([PubMed][1]) The large international HAL trial found a modest early benefit and longer median recurrence-free time, whereas the multicenter placebo-controlled 5-ALA trial and the contemporary pragmatic PHOTO trial found no benefit. ([PMC][6])

Important effect modifiers or sources of heterogeneity include:

* Primary versus recurrent disease.
* Solitary versus multifocal tumors.
* Intravesical HAL versus intravesical or oral 5-ALA.
* Surgeon experience and the thoroughness of WL TURBT.
* Immediate mitomycin and later BCG use.
* Whether the comparator received an initial systematic WL inspection and mapping.
* Whether “recurrence” at an early repeat TURBT actually represents residual tumor.
* Exclusion after randomization of patients without pathologically confirmed NMIBC.

### Progression

Individual-study progression data are much sparser and generally **do not demonstrate a statistically convincing reduction**. The long-term T1 high-grade subgroup, the placebo-controlled 5-ALA trial, and PHOTO all failed to show a progression advantage. ([PubMed][2]) Observational studies sometimes report lower expanded or composite progression measures, but conventional progression to muscle-invasive/metastatic disease is less consistently affected. ([PubMed][18])

### Complications

For **intravesical HAL or 5-ALA**, randomized evidence suggests no meaningful increase in overall TURBT-related complications: hematuria, dysuria, bladder spasm, retention, serious adverse events and postoperative grade ≥3 events were generally comparable with WL TURBT. ([PMC][6])

For **oral 5-ALA**, the safety conclusion is different. Comparative Japanese studies identify clinically relevant risks of **intraoperative hypotension and transaminase elevation**, particularly in older patients and those receiving general anesthesia. ([ScienceDirect][16])

## Bottom line

The primary-study evidence supports that blue-light assistance detects additional lesions and often reduces residual tumor. Whether this translates into fewer later recurrences depends heavily on the study setting: the benefit was substantial in several earlier explanatory trials but absent in the large contemporary PHOTO trial and several other well-controlled or real-world comparisons. A consistent reduction in progression has not been demonstrated by individual trials. Intravesical HAL appears to add little perioperative toxicity, whereas oral 5-ALA carries a distinct hypotension and liver-enzyme risk.
