Here are individual primary studies (RCTs and prospective comparative studies) comparing blue-light/photodynamic-diagnosis (PDD) TURBT with standard white-light TURBT for NMIBC. I've excluded reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, and editorials/comments.

**Large phase III RCTs — recurrence/progression as endpoints**

- **Stenzl et al., J Urol 2010** — Multicenter RCT combining pooled data; found hexaminolevulinate-guided fluorescence cystoscopy reduced recurrence in patients with non-muscle invasive bladder cancer.

- **Grossman et al.** (long-term follow-up, 551 patients) — Prospective randomized study of BLC vs WLC for Ta/T1 disease. After median follow-up of 53.0 months (WLC) and 55.1 months (BLC), recurrence-free rate was 31.8% vs 38% (P=0.14); median time to recurrence was 9.4 months for WLC vs 16.4 months for BLC (P=0.04).

- **Grossman et al., J Urol 2007** — Phase III multicenter comparison of hexaminolevulinate fluorescence cystoscopy vs white light cystoscopy for detecting superficial papillary lesions (cited in reference lists above, PMC7136177/Frontiers article).

**UK pragmatic RCT — negative for recurrence benefit**

- **PHOTO trial (Heer et al.)** — Pragmatic, open-label, parallel-group randomized trial in 22 UK NHS hospitals; participants with suspected first diagnosis of NMIBC at intermediate/high risk were assigned 1:1 to PDD-guided TURBT or standard white-light TURBT, with time to recurrence at 3 years as primary outcome. Recurrences occurred in 86/209 in the PDD group vs 84/217 in the white-light group (HR 0.94, P=.7) — no significant difference at 3 years. Companion cost-effectiveness analysis: 493 participants randomized to PDD-TURBT (n=244) or WL-TURBT (n=249); no evidence of a difference in costs or QALYs over 3-year follow-up. Original protocol: PHOTO is a pragmatic multicentre RCT comparing PDD-guided TURBT with standard white light resection in newly diagnosed intermediate/high-risk NMIBC.

- **Guy's Hospital trial (O'Brien et al./Kausch group)** — Prospective randomized trial of hexylaminolevulinate PDD-assisted TURBT plus single-shot mitomycin C vs conventional white-light TURBT plus mitomycin C in 249 newly presenting NMIBC patients (Guy's Hospital, 2005–2010); despite more accurate diagnostic assessment, HAL-PDD did not lead to lower recurrence rates versus standard care.

**High-risk/re-resection focused RCT**

- **HAL blue-light re-TURBT study** — 446 patients randomized to blue-light or white-light cystoscopy/resection (Dec 2007–Nov 2009); high-risk patients underwent standard re-TURBT 6 weeks later. Short-term recurrence rate at re-TURBT was 11.1% (blue-light) vs 31.2% (white-light), with blue-light superior across CIS, pTaG3, and pT1 subgroups.

**Multicenter non-inferiority RCT with adjuvant chemotherapy**

- **HELENA study** — Prospective randomized controlled open-label multicenter non-inferiority trial (129 patients, EORTC intermediate-risk NMIBC, 2010–2016) comparing Hexvix-TURB with immediate intravesical chemotherapy alone vs white-light TURB with immediate instillation plus maintenance intravesical chemotherapy; primary endpoint was recurrence-free survival at 12 months.

**Surveillance-setting phase III RCT (flexible cystoscopy, not resection per se, but often included in this literature)**

- **Daneshmand et al., surveillance phase III study** — 304 high-risk patients received hexaminolevulinate before white-light flexible cystoscopy and randomization to blue-light flexible cystoscopy; 103 were referred to OR, including 63 with confirmed malignancy. In 13/63 patients (20.6%), recurrence was seen only with blue-light flexible cystoscopy (p<0.0001), including 5 CIS cases.

**Detection-focused prospective randomized/controlled trials**

- **China bridging phase III study (Hu et al., 2025/2026, NCT05600322)** — Prospective, comparative, within-patient controlled multicenter phase III trial at 7 Chinese tertiary hospitals (Nov 2022–Jul 2023); patients received intravesical HAL, underwent WLC, then were randomized to high-definition BLC; primary endpoint was proportion of patients with histology-confirmed tumors found by BLC but not WLC. Of 114 BLC patients, 13 (11.4%) had CIS; 84.6% had additional lesions detected by BLC and 61.5% were diagnosed solely by BLC.

- **UK single-center cohort study (histopathology/recurrence)** — Single UK center study; in the re-resection arm, blue-light detected significantly more CIS than white light (51.7% vs 20.7%, wording as reported, p=0.0277), with 3.4% upstaged to muscle-invasive disease; recurrence rates were 33.3% (primary arm) and 37.5% (secondary arm) at 24 months.

**Safety-focused primary studies (not reviews)**

- **Combined analysis of registration trials + postmarketing data (Daneshmand et al.)** — pooled analysis of individual trial datasets on hexaminolevulinate safety, reporting 41–58% of patients had at least one adverse event, predominantly mild-to-moderate, across 73–93% of patients with concomitant diseases.

- **Repeat-dosing safety study** — Phase III pre-trial planned analysis prospectively assessing safety of repeated HAL administration; of patients receiving HAL at office cystoscopy, 103 (33.2%) received a second dose weeks later at TURBT, comparing adverse event rates between the two settings.

A few notes for your synthesis: the Stenzl 2010 and Grossman studies are the foundational registration-trial data most guidelines rely on; PHOTO and the Guy's Hospital trial are the two pragmatic, negative UK RCTs that complicate the "recurrence benefit" narrative; and the re-TURBT study is the strongest primary evidence specifically for high-risk patients.
