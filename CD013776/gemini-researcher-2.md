By filtering out the extensive meta-analyses and systematic reviews in this space, we can look directly at the highest-quality primary evidence: the large-scale Randomized Controlled Trials (RCTs).

Currently, the primary clinical evidence is somewhat divided. While the foundational, earlier trials demonstrated a clear advantage in delaying and preventing recurrence with blue-light (photodynamic) TURBT using hexaminolevulinate, the most recent pragmatic trial found no significant difference between the two techniques.

Here is a high-level comparison of the three most definitive RCTs, followed by a deeper breakdown of their findings regarding your outcomes of interest.

## The Case for Efficacy: Earlier Foundational RCTs

A major multi-center phase III RCT established the initial clinical support for blue-light TURBT, randomizing over 800 patients with suspected non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC).

* **Recurrence:** The initial 9-month follow-up showed that blue-light cystoscopy significantly improved tumor detection and reduced the rate of tumor recurrence compared to the white-light group, dropping the rate from 56% to 47% (Stenzl et al., 2010).
* **Long-Term Follow-Up:** A subsequent extension study tracked these patients for over four years (median follow-up of ~54 months). It confirmed a long-term benefit, with the median time to recurrence extended to 16.4 months in the blue-light group compared to 9.4 months in the white-light group (Grossman et al., 2012).
* **Progression and Complications:** While there was a trend toward fewer patients progressing to T2–T4 (muscle-invasive) cancer in the blue-light group (3.1% vs 6.1%), this difference did not reach statistical significance. Similarly, rates of cystectomy and bladder cancer-related deaths were comparable across both groups, and neither trial noted an increase in serious surgical complications (Grossman et al., 2012).

## The Case for Effectiveness: The Pragmatic PHOTO Trial

Despite the earlier efficacy findings, the largest recent pragmatic RCT—the UK's PHOTO trial—challenged these conclusions when blue-light TURBT was applied in real-world clinical settings. This trial randomized 538 patients with a suspected first diagnosis of intermediate- to high-risk NMIBC across 22 hospitals.

* **Recurrence:** Over a 3-year follow-up period, the trial found **no difference** in recurrence rates. The 3-year recurrence-free survival rate was 57.8% in the blue-light group and 59.1% in the white-light group (Heer et al., 2022).
* **Progression & Complications:** Adverse event frequencies were both low and statistically identical between the two approaches (5.7% for blue light vs. 5.5% for white light). The trial concluded that the photodynamic intervention offered no measurable clinical benefit regarding recurrence and was not cost-effective for initial management (Heer et al., 2022).

## Summary

In short, while blue-light TURBT undoubtedly improves visual detection during surgery, the primary RCTs disagree on whether this translates to long-term oncological benefits. Older phase III trials show delayed and reduced recurrence, whereas the recent pragmatic PHOTO trial shows no difference at 3 years. Importantly, no primary RCT has demonstrated a statistically significant reduction in disease progression to muscle-invasive stages, and neither technique appears to carry a higher risk of adverse events.
