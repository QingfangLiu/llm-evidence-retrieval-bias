## Bottom line

Blue-light/photodynamic-diagnosis–assisted TURBT **consistently detects additional tumors and often leaves less residual disease**, especially CIS and multifocal tumors. Whether that translates into fewer subsequent recurrences is less consistent:

* Several older randomized trials reported clinically important recurrence reductions.
* The large international HAL trial found a modest early and longer-term delay in recurrence.
* Other randomized trials—including the recent, pragmatic 538-patient PHOTO trial conducted with contemporary care—found **no recurrence benefit**.
* A reduction in progression to muscle-invasive disease has **not been convincingly demonstrated**.
* Blue-light TURBT does **not appear to cause more serious complications**, although adverse-event reporting in older trials was limited.

The studies below are individual primary studies; I have not included reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, opinions, or editorials.

## How I would interpret these studies clinically

**Recurrence:** Blue light can reduce early residual/recurrent tumor rates, particularly where standard resection might otherwise miss CIS, multifocal disease or small papillary lesions. The magnitude has varied from large reductions in older, smaller studies to no benefit in trials using experienced surgeons, contemporary white-light equipment and postoperative intravesical treatment.

**Progression:** The evidence is insufficient to tell a patient that blue-light TURBT prevents muscle-invasive progression. Progression events were uncommon, most trials were underpowered, and the strongest favorable signal arose from a post hoc reanalysis. The PHOTO trial and the high-grade subgroup of the Filbeck/Denzinger trial did not demonstrate progression prevention.

**Complications:** Available randomized data do not show a meaningful increase in serious harm. In PHOTO, postoperative and grade ≥3 events were similar, and O’Brien et al. reported no HAL-attributable events. Blue light does require catheter instillation of the photosensitizer and additional operative logistics, but the principal perioperative risks remain those of TURBT itself. ([NCBI][18])

**Most applicable conclusion:** For a patient with suspected primary intermediate- or high-risk NMIBC, blue light is best presented as a technique that may improve visualization and completeness of resection. It **may reduce recurrence in selected settings**, but a recurrence benefit is not guaranteed, and there is currently no reliable proof that it prevents progression or major complications.
