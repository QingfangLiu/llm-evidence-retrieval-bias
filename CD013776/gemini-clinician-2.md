When planning a transurethral resection for a suspected non-muscle-invasive bladder tumor (NMIBC), achieving a complete initial resection is your best defense against recurrence. Standard white-light cystoscopy (WLC) is the traditional baseline, but it is known to occasionally miss flat, multifocal lesions like carcinoma in situ (CIS). Blue-light cystoscopy (BLC), typically using the photosensitizing agent hexaminolevulinate (HAL), addresses this by causing neoplastic tissue to fluoresce bright pink under blue light, significantly enhancing visual contrast.

Based strictly on individual, primary randomized controlled trials (RCTs), here is how blue-light TURBT compares to standard white-light TURBT across your clinical endpoints.

## Impact on Recurrence

Primary clinical trials demonstrate a clear, statistically significant advantage for blue-light-assisted TURBT in reducing recurrence rates, driven by the improved detection and complete excision of tumors during the initial operation.

* **Short-Term Recurrence in High-Risk Patients:** A prospective, randomized trial evaluated patients with high-risk NMIBC undergoing re-TURBT (Geavlete et al., 2010). The study found that the overall short-term recurrence rate was substantially lower in the blue-light arm (**11.1%**) compared to the standard white-light arm (**31.2%**). The difference was particularly stark for flat lesions, with CIS recurring in only 4.3% of the blue-light group versus 27.8% of the white-light group.
* **Long-Term Recurrence-Free Survival:** An international, multicenter RCT evaluated patients with suspected Ta or T1 urothelial tumors with long-term follow-up (Grossman et al., 2012). The trial confirmed that HAL-enabled fluorescence cystoscopy significantly improved the detection of both papillary bladder cancer and CIS. Crucially, this enhanced detection translated to a significant increase in the time to recurrence for the blue-light cohort compared to the white-light cohort.

## Impact on Progression

While blue-light TURBT excels at reducing recurrence, demonstrating a definitive reduction in stage *progression* to muscle-invasive disease within individual primary trials is more challenging.

Because progression events are relatively rare and require years of follow-up to detect, most primary individual RCTs (including the landmark trials cited above) are powered primarily to detect differences in diagnostic yield and recurrence, rather than progression. However, because blue-light TURBT identifies significantly more high-grade disease and CIS—the primary drivers of stage progression—it clinically supports progression-free survival by ensuring these aggressive tumors are accurately staged and appropriately treated with adjuvant therapies like BCG.

## Complications and Safety

The safety and complication profile of blue-light TURBT is highly comparable to standard white-light TURBT.

* **Surgical Risks:** The primary operative risks (such as bleeding, infection, or bladder perforation) are driven entirely by the mechanical TURBT procedure itself, not by the optical modality used.
* **Contrast Agent Tolerability:** The only physiological difference is the intravesical instillation of the HAL contrast agent approximately one hour prior to surgery. In primary trials, HAL is well-tolerated. Adverse events are largely limited to mild, transient local genitourinary reactions, such as bladder spasms, dysuria, or mild bladder pain.
