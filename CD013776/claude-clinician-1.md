Here's a rundown of individual primary studies (RCTs and prospective/retrospective cohorts) directly comparing blue-light (photodynamic/hexaminolevulinate-guided) TURBT to standard white-light TURBT. I've deliberately excluded the reviews and guidelines that dominate this literature, since several exist and consistently summarize these same trials with somewhat different pooled estimates.

**Large multicenter RCTs (positive for blue light)**

- **Stenzl et al., J Urol 2010** (NCT00233402) — 814 patients at increased recurrence risk, randomized to hexaminolevulinate (HAL) fluorescence-guided vs. white-light detection/resection. The study assessed the impact that improved detection of nonmuscle invasive bladder cancer with hexaminolevulinate fluorescence cystoscopy may have on early recurrence rates. At 9 months, tumor recurrence occurred in 128 of 271 patients (47%) in the fluorescence group versus 157 of 280 (56%) in the white-light group (p = 0.026), a 16% relative reduction in recurrence rate.

- **Grossman et al., J Urol 2012** — long-term follow-up of the above cohort, showing a sustained decrease in recurrence with HAL fluorescence cystoscopy (frequently cited alongside the 2010 paper as the "Stenzl/Grossman" dataset).

**Trials focused on recurrence with adjuvant intravesical therapy held constant**

- **O'Brien et al. (Guy's Hospital trial), BJU Int 2013** — 249 newly presenting NMIBC patients randomized to HAL photodynamic-assisted TURBT plus single-shot mitomycin C vs. white-light TURBT plus single-shot mitomycin C. The objective was to determine if photodynamic 'blue-light'-assisted resection leads to lower recurrence rates in newly presenting NMIBC; of 249 patients, 209 (84%) had cancer and in 185 (89%) it was NMIBC.

- **HELENA study, World J Urol 2021 (PMC8521513)** — phase III non-inferiority RCT, 129 EORTC intermediate-risk patients. Patients were randomized to white-light TURB with immediate and maintenance intravesical mitomycin, or HAL (Hexvix) TURB with immediate instillation only. Recurrence-free survival at 12 months was the primary endpoint. The Hexvix group had numerically more recurrence/death events (19 vs. 10), giving an HR of 1.29 in the per-protocol analysis; non-inferiority was not demonstrated, and overall survival did not differ. This is a useful "negative" data point showing blue light did not compensate for reduced maintenance chemotherapy.

- **FinnBladder 9 (NCT01675219)**, reported 2026 — 255 patients with pTa low-grade, high-recurrence-risk NMIBC in a 2×2 design (white-light ± optimized mitomycin C vs. blue-light ± optimized mitomycin C). The arm receiving blue-light TURBT plus adjuvant optimized mitomycin C had a significant 58% lower recurrence risk (HR 0.42, 95% CI 0.22–0.81, p=0.010) versus white-light TURBT alone; recurrence risk did not differ significantly among the other arms. This suggests the benefit was tied to the combination with optimized chemotherapy, not blue light alone.

**Large pragmatic RCT (negative — important counterpoint)**

- **PHOTO trial, Heer et al., NEJM Evidence 2022 / HTA monograph** — the largest and most recent pragmatic RCT, 22 UK NHS hospitals, 426 patients (209 PDD, 217 white light) with newly diagnosed intermediate/high-risk NMIBC. Recurrences occurred in 86 patients in the PDD group and 84 in the white-light group (HR 0.94) — no meaningful difference. The main clinical outcome, time to recurrence, showed no significant difference, cost-effectiveness analysis showed PDD-TURBT was not more cost-effective, and secondary cancer-control outcomes including progression also showed no significant differences; the authors concluded PDD-TURBT is not supported for management of primary intermediate/high-risk NMIBC. A follow-up analysis presented at AUA 2023 explored whether surgeon learning curve/experience explained the null result.

**Other negative RCTs** (useful for showing the evidence isn't uniform):
- Karaolides et al., Urology 2012 — HAL-induced fluorescence vs. white light, questioning whether it reduces recurrences.
- Gkritsios et al., Int Urol Nephrol 2014 — prospective randomized trial finding HAL-guided TURBT did not reduce recurrence rates after 2-year follow-up.

**Prospective/retrospective comparative cohort studies**

- **Gallagher et al., World J Urol 2017 (PMC5693980)** — "real-life" prospective controlled cohort, 345 NMIBC patients undergoing quality-controlled ("good quality") resections, comparing PDD-TURBT (2009–10) to white-light TURBT (2007–8), with 1:1 matching. Recurrence at 3 years was significantly lower for PDD overall (39.0% vs. 53.3%, OR 0.56, p=0.02), and in the matched-pair analysis (24.6% vs. 50.0%, OR 0.33, p<0.001), with the greatest benefit in high-risk patients.

- Retrospective monocentric cohort (Life journal, 2022) comparing extended TURBT with white-light vs. PDD, reporting improved recurrence-free survival and CIS detection with PDD.

- **Chan et al., BJUI Compass 2023** — single UK-center study of HAL blue-light TURBT/biopsy, reporting histopathologic characteristics (including higher secondary CIS detection: 51.7% with blue-light re-resection vs. comparator) and recurrence rates.

**On complications specifically:** none of the individual trials above were designed with surgical complications as a primary endpoint, but where reported (e.g., Stenzl 2010, and product-safety literature), adverse events such as hematuria, dysuria, and bladder spasm occurred at similar rates between blue-light and white-light arms and were attributed to the resection itself rather than the fluorescence agent — you'd want to check each trial's safety tables directly if perioperative complication rates are your main question, since this wasn't consistently a powered outcome.

**Bottom line for your own reading:** the earlier, mostly industry-associated, moderate-sized RCTs (Stenzl/Grossman, O'Brien, Gallagher) show consistent recurrence benefit with blue light, while the largest and most recent pragmatic, non-industry RCT (PHOTO) found no benefit — a discrepancy worth weighing given PHOTO's size, real-world design, and recency. The HELENA and FinnBladder 9 trials suggest that adjuvant intravesical chemotherapy quality/regimen may be a bigger driver of recurrence-free survival than the light source itself.
