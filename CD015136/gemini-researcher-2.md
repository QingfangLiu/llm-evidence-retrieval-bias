As an evidence-synthesis researcher, getting past the noise of narrative reviews and meta-analyses to find the raw, primary trial data is critical.

Here are three individual primary studies that directly evaluate remote pharmacy interventions against standard/baseline care for ambulatory patients managing long-term conditions:

## Primary Studies on Telepharmacy vs. Usual Care

* **Hypertension (Randomized Clinical Trial):** A two-arm trial conducted in the United Arab Emirates evaluated 239 outpatients with uncontrolled hypertension. The intervention arm (119 patients) received telepharmacy services coordinated through community pharmacies, while the control arm (120 patients) received standard face-to-face pharmacy care to evaluate the impact on blood pressure management (Ibrahim et al., 2022).
* **Diabetes and Hypertension (Pragmatic Trial):** A pragmatic before-and-after trial involving 314 outpatients with chronic conditions compared remote tele-visits to routine face-to-face hospital visits. The study found no statistically significant difference in patient-reported medication adherence between the two care delivery methods, suggesting that remote care successfully maintained the baseline adherence standards of usual face-to-face visits (Norouzi et al., 2023).
* **Type 2 Diabetes (Interventional):** A study based in community pharmacies evaluated pharmacist-led, telephone-initiated adherence support for 120 patients with type 2 diabetes who were late for their oral medication refills. The intervention yielded a modest but statistically significant reduction in medication refill gaps over a 12-month follow-up period compared to their baseline adherence behaviors (Odegard et al., 2013).
