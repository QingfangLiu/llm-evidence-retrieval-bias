## Individual primary studies: telepharmacy vs usual care for non-hospitalized patients with long-term conditions

Here's what I found — actual trials, not reviews:

**Hypertension**

- **Al Hail et al. (UAE), 2-arm RCT, 16 community pharmacies, n=239** — Compared telepharmacy services against traditional pharmaceutical care for uncontrolled hypertension over 12 months, tracking blood pressure, drug-related problems, adherence, and knowledge. This was a 2-arm, randomised, clinical trial conducted among 16 community pharmacies and 239 patients with uncontrolled HTN in the U.A.E over a period of 12 months, with one arm receiving telepharmacy services and the other traditional pharmaceutical services. (PMC9891772)

- **Ghana RCT, two hospitals, 2022** — A randomized control trial conducted among individuals with hypertension recruited from two Ghanaian hospitals from May 2022 to December 2022, testing pharmaceutical care delivered via telepharmacy against usual outpatient care, with outcomes of adherence, lifestyle modification, and BP control.

- **Green et al., "e-BP" trial, JAMA 2008, 3-arm RCT, Group Health, Seattle** — A 3-group randomized controlled trial based on the Chronic Care Model, testing whether patient web services, home BP monitoring, and pharmacist-assisted care improves BP control compared with usual care. Study arms included usual care with a home blood pressure monitor alone versus usual care plus web-based pharmacist care. ClinicalTrials.gov NCT00158639.

- **Magid et al., Kaiser Permanente Colorado, Circ Cardiovasc Qual Outcomes 2013, n=348** — A randomized controlled trial conducted in 10 Kaiser Permanente Colorado clinics testing a pharmacist-led, Heart360-enabled home blood pressure monitoring intervention against usual care; at 6 months, 54.1% of the intervention group reached BP goal versus 35.4% of usual care.

**Type 2 diabetes**

- **Maxwell et al., Pharmacotherapy 2016** — evaluated a pharmacist-led telehealth clinic's impact on diabetes-related therapy goals in a U.S. veteran population (cited within PMC9383369's reference list; primary source, not itself a review).

- **UAE, RCT, n=192, ambulatory diabetes center** — A randomised controlled trial examining a multifactorial pharmacist-led intervention protocol including medication therapy management with counselling, medication booklets, and a mobile app, in 192 patients with type 2 diabetes.

**Home health / hospitalization prevention**

- **Zillich et al., Health Serv Res 2014** — a pragmatic RCT of telephonic medication therapy management aimed at reducing hospitalization among home health patients (cited within PMC's tandfonline reference list; itself a primary trial).

**Oncology (remote pharmacy follow-up)**

- **Wu et al., Front Pharmacol 2025, n=178** — 178 patients with non-metastatic breast cancer were randomly assigned to an intervention group receiving remote pharmaceutical follow-up via an app or a control group receiving only routine treatment, over a 6-month follow-up, with severe adverse drug reactions significantly lower in the intervention group (20.2% vs 50.0%, P<0.01).

A few notes on scope and quality:
- Most of these are open-label RCTs (patients/pharmacists usually can't be blinded to a remote-vs-in-person format), so watch for that limitation when appraising.
- Several are small, single-country, single-condition trials — worth checking sample size and follow-up duration before drawing firm conclusions.
