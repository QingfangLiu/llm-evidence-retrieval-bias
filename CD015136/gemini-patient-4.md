Yes, evidence from primary research studies indicates that remote pharmacy care (telepharmacy) generally outperforms usual care for non-hospitalized patients managing long-term conditions. The most significant benefits are seen in improved medication adherence and better clinical control of conditions like hypertension.

Here is a breakdown of three individual randomized controlled trials that directly compared telepharmacy to usual care in outpatient settings.

## 1. The HyperLink Trial: Blood Pressure Control

A large cluster-randomized clinical trial evaluated the impact of home telemonitoring paired with remote pharmacist management for outpatients with uncontrolled hypertension.

* **The Intervention:** Patients measured their blood pressure at home and transmitted the data to a remote pharmacist, who then adjusted their antihypertensive medications accordingly.
* **The Results:** At the 12-month mark, **57.2%** of the telepharmacy group had successfully brought their blood pressure under control, compared to only **30.0%** of the usual care group. Furthermore, the telepharmacy group saw significantly larger reductions in both systolic and diastolic blood pressure (Margolis et al., 2013).

## 2. The STIC2IT Trial: Medication Adherence

This pragmatic cluster-randomized trial focused on outpatients who were nonadherent to their medications and had poorly controlled diabetes, hypertension, or hyperlipidemia.

* **The Intervention:** Pharmacists delivered a tailored, multicomponent remote intervention using telephone behavioral interviewing, text messaging, and mailed progress reports to help patients overcome individual barriers to taking their medication.
* **The Results:** The telepharmacy intervention led to a statistically significant **4.7% increase** in medication adherence compared to usual care. While the overall odds of achieving combined clinical disease control did not significantly differ in the primary intention-to-treat analysis, patients receiving remote pharmacy care were significantly less likely to require an emergency department visit (Choudhry et al., 2018).

## 3. Community Telepharmacy for Hypertension

A more recent 12-month randomized clinical trial tested the effectiveness of telepharmacy services delivered through 16 community pharmacies for patients with uncontrolled hypertension.

* **The Intervention:** One group received remote telepharmacy care (virtual services), while the control group received traditional, in-person standard pharmacy care.
* **The Results:** Patients in the telepharmacy group maintained significantly lower systolic and diastolic blood pressures across the 12-month follow-up compared to the standard care group. Additionally, the remote pharmacists were significantly more effective at identifying and preventing drug-related problems before they escalated (Ibrahim et al., 2022).
