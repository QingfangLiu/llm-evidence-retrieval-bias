Here are individual primary studies (RCTs and pilot trials) comparing telepharmacy against usual/face-to-face care in non-hospitalised patients with long-term conditions. I've excluded reviews, meta-analyses, and guidance documents.

**Hypertension**

- **Margolis et al., 2013, *JAMA* (HyperLink trial).** Cluster-randomised trial in 16 primary care clinics; 450 adults with uncontrolled blood pressure were assigned to home BP telemonitoring combined with pharmacist case management or to usual care. Home BP telemonitoring and pharmacist case management achieved better BP control compared with usual care during 12 months of intervention, and the effect persisted during 6 months of postintervention follow-up.

- **Margolis et al., 2018, *JAMA Network Open* (HyperLink follow-up).** Follow-up of the same cluster-randomised trial found significantly greater reductions in systolic BP in the intervention group than usual care at 6, 12, and 18 months, with effects examined out to 54 months.

- **Margolis et al. (Hyperlink 3), *Hypertension*.** A pragmatic cluster-randomized trial comparing pharmacist-led telehealth care with clinic-based care for uncontrolled high blood pressure.

- **Pilot study, 2022** — Evaluation of pharmacist-led telemedicine medication management for hypertension in established patients during the COVID-19 pandemic (single-arm pilot rather than RCT — useful for feasibility, weaker for comparative effectiveness).

**Diabetes**

- **Cohen, Taveira, Wu & Pirraglia, 2020, *J Telemed Telecare*.** RCT in veterans with type 1/2 diabetes and depression; patients were randomized to pharmacist-led or nurse-led telehealth, with the pharmacist group receiving an added in-depth medication review. After six months, the pharmacist-led telehealth arm showed significant improvements in cardiovascular medication adherence, antidepressant adherence, and overall combined medication adherence. Note: comparator here is nurse-led telehealth, not conventional in-person usual care.

**Anticoagulation**

- **Karaoui, Ramia, Mansour, Haddad & Chamoun, 2021, *BMC Health Services Research*.** RCT of 200 patients discharged on oral anticoagulants; the control group received standard nursing counseling while the intervention group additionally received pharmacist counseling, with phone-call follow-ups on day 3 and 30 post-discharge, assessing bleeding and readmission outcomes.

**Chronic kidney disease**

- **Telehealth by an Interprofessional Team in Patients With CKD, *American Journal of Kidney Disease* (Ishani et al.).** Randomized trial at Minneapolis and St. Cloud VA health care systems comparing an interprofessional team (including a clinical pharmacy specialist) delivering care via a telehealth device against usual care in patients with CKD (eGFR <60), with 451 in the intervention arm and 150 in usual care.

**COPD/asthma**

- **"Better Outcomes for Everybody" trial, medRxiv/community pharmacy setting (Sicily).** A two-arm parallel randomized controlled trial across 100 community pharmacies testing a community pharmacist-led intervention (using telephone or other remote consultation tools) to improve chronic respiratory condition management in adults with COPD or asthma, with a published cost-effectiveness analysis plan.

A few caveats worth flagging as a clinician appraising this evidence:

1. Several of these trials (Cohen 2020) compare telepharmacy against a different remote-care model rather than face-to-face usual care — check the comparator arm carefully before pooling findings.
2. Some (the COVID hypertension pilot) are single-arm feasibility studies, not comparative trials.
3. There's an actively recruiting trial, **CELESTE (NCT06108674)**, directly comparing telepharmacy vs face-to-face pharmaceutical care in elderly hypertensive patients — worth watching for future results but has none yet.
