If you are trying to isolate the primary data from the sea of systematic reviews and practice guidelines, it helps to focus strictly on clinical trials and observational cohorts.

Here are four individual, primary studies (randomized controlled trials and pragmatic trials) that evaluate the effectiveness of telepharmacy interventions against standard, face-to-face care for managing long-term health conditions in outpatient and community settings:

## 1. Hypertension Management in the UAE (2022)

A two-arm randomized clinical trial compared remote telepharmacy services to traditional standard pharmacy care for adults with uncontrolled hypertension. Across a 12-month follow-up period, researchers found that the intervention group receiving remote virtual services delivered by pharmacists experienced significantly better blood pressure control and lifestyle modifications compared to the usual care group (Ibrahim et al., 2022).

## 2. Text Messaging and Consultations for Hypertension (2020)

A cluster randomized controlled trial evaluated an intervention combining automated SMS text messaging and personal telephone consultations provided by pharmacy students. Compared to a control group receiving usual care, the remote intervention group showed significant improvements in systolic blood pressure, medication adherence, and general knowledge about hypertension after three months, though differences in diastolic blood pressure were not statistically significant (Zhai et al., 2020).

## 3. Asthma Care in Underserved Rural Populations (2012)

A pilot randomized controlled trial evaluated the "Patient And phaRmacist Telephonic Encounters" (PARTE) intervention for rural patients with asthma. Patients in the control group received standard care (mailed prescription refills with written instructions), while the intervention group additionally received telephonic pharmacist consultations focused on self-management and medication use. The study established that this telepharmacy model was highly feasible and showed positive initial indicators for improving asthma control without the need for in-person visits (Young et al., 2012).

## 4. Tele-visits vs. Face-to-Face Care During COVID-19 (2023)

A pragmatic before-after trial examined the medication adherence of patients with chronic conditions (specifically diabetes and hypertension) who were transitioned from routine face-to-face visits to telephone-based tele-visits during the pandemic. The study found no statistically significant difference in patient medication adherence between the in-person and remote models. This demonstrated that telepharmacy can serve as an equally effective method for maintaining continuity of care when physical visits are not viable (Norouzi et al., 2023).
