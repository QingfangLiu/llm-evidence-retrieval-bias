### Postdischarge studies that may or may not meet your “outside hospital” rule

These interventions were delivered remotely after discharge, but participants were recruited during hospital care:

* **Wang et al., 2022:** 80 patients with type 2 diabetes and hypertension; clinical pharmacist access, WeChat discussion and telephone follow-up. Three-month adherence was 90.0% versus 52.5%, with better glucose and BP target attainment. DOI: 10.1155/2022/7681404. ([PubMed][17])
* **Wang et al., 2024:** 445 people with chronic heart failure; pharmacist-led WeChat education and monthly follow-up versus monthly nurse follow-up. Proportion of days covered was 8.1 percentage points higher at 52 weeks. DOI: 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2024.53976. ([PubMed][18])

## Overall signal

The most convincing evidence of clinical benefit is for **uncontrolled hypertension**, especially when pharmacists receive transmitted home BP readings and can actively intensify treatment. The Green and Margolis trials produced large, statistically clear improvements in BP control.

For **diabetes**, results are mixed. Several small hybrid studies reported HbA1c improvements, but the much larger, predominantly telephone-based ENGAGE-DM trial was null in its intention-to-treat analysis. Telephone interventions more consistently improved **medication adherence or self-care** than HbA1c.

For **asthma**, the evidence consists mainly of very small or pilot trials. For **CKD**, remote pharmacist intervention improved care processes but not BP. Across conditions, interventions involving medication adjustment or close clinical management appear more effective than isolated counseling calls.

This is a targeted identification exercise rather than a database-complete systematic search; the remote/hybrid distinction should be applied during full-text screening according to your protocol.
