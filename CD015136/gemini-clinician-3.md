Based on recent randomized controlled trials (RCTs), remote pharmacy care (telepharmacy) is generally **as effective as—and frequently superior to—usual clinic-based care** for managing chronic conditions in the ambulatory setting.

The primary advantage of pharmacist-led telehealth lies in its ability to overcome "clinical inertia" and drive medication adherence. Because pharmacists can follow up frequently without requiring the patient to travel, they excel at optimizing drug therapy, catching drug-related problems early, and supporting lifestyle modifications.

## Evidence from Primary RCTs

Here is a breakdown of recent, highly cited randomized controlled trials that directly compare telepharmacy to usual care for non-hospitalized patients.

### 1. Superior Blood Pressure Control and Adherence

Mozu et al. (2023) conducted an RCT involving 118 patients with hypertension, comparing standard clinical care against standard care supplemented with telepharmacy via phone calls.

* **The Findings:** After six months, the telepharmacy group achieved a statistically significant reduction in mean blood pressure (dropping from 148.1/85.8 to 134.8/79.5 mm Hg).
* **The Difference:** The proportion of patients achieving adequate blood pressure control jumped to **66.1%** in the telepharmacy group, compared to just **39.0%** at baseline/usual care. Medication adherence and positive lifestyle modifications (such as sodium reduction and exercise) were also significantly higher.

---

### 2. Equal Clinical Efficacy with Higher Patient Satisfaction

The large-scale Hyperlink 3 pragmatic cluster-randomized trial by Margolis et al. (2022) evaluated 3,071 patients across 21 primary care clinics. They compared pharmacist-led telehealth (which integrated home BP telemonitoring) directly against best-practice, face-to-face clinic care for moderately severe hypertension.

* **The Findings:** Both groups saw massive and nearly identical reductions in blood pressure over 12 months (systolic drops of 18–19 mm Hg).
* **The Difference:** While clinical outcomes were matched, the telepharmacy group reported significantly higher satisfaction. They rated their care more highly and found the remote management far more convenient, proving that telepharmacy can match optimized in-person care while drastically reducing the time burden on the patient.

### 3. Outperforming Nurse-Led Telehealth in Complex Patients

Cohen et al. (2019) focused on complex patients managing both diabetes and depression. They randomized veterans to either usual care (which, in this specific VA system setting, was nurse-led telehealth) or pharmacist-led telehealth.

* **The Findings:** Over six months, the pharmacist-led arm demonstrated significantly better improvements in medication adherence for cardiovascular medications, antidepressants, and overall medication regimens compared to the nurse-led arm.
* **The Difference:** While depression scores themselves did not significantly differ between the two groups, the trial highlighted the specific value of a pharmacist's expertise in conducting deep medication reviews and driving adherence for polypharmacy patients.
