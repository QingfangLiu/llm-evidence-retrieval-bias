Here are individual primary studies (RCTs and prospective comparative studies) evaluating telepharmacy/remote pharmacist care vs. usual care for people with long-term conditions in non-hospital settings. I've excluded reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, and commentaries.

## Hypertension

**Margolis et al., JAMA 2013** — Cluster RCT, 16 primary care clinics, 450 adults with uncontrolled BP. Eight clinics were randomized to provide usual care to patients (n = 222) and 8 clinics were randomized to provide a telemonitoring intervention (n = 228). Intervention patients received home BP telemonitors and transmitted BP data to pharmacists who adjusted antihypertensive therapy accordingly. Result: significantly greater reductions in systolic BP in the intervention group than in the usual care group at 6, 12, and 18 months (-10.7, -9.7, and -6.6 mm Hg, respectively).
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23821088/

**Margolis et al., JAMA Network Open 2018** (long-term follow-up of the same trial) — assessed durability of the effect through 54 months.
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7381046/ (linked via search index; primary PMID 30646139)

**Margolis et al., Hypertension 2020** — cardiovascular events and cost outcomes from the same cohort (HyperLink trial family).
https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/full/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.120.15492

**"Hyperlink 3" pragmatic cluster-randomized trial** — compares pharmacist-led telehealth care with clinic-based care for uncontrolled high blood pressure.
https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.122.19816

## Diabetes / multiple risk factors (pharmacist-involved remote care)

**Pharmacist Management of Diabetes Mellitus RCT** (NCT00869076) — patients with poorly controlled diabetes randomized to pharmacist management (in collaboration with primary care physician) versus usual medical care for one year, testing whether pharmacist management produces greater improvement in blood sugar control.
https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00869076

**TECHNOMED trial** — 3-arm pragmatic RCT in seniors with diabetes and hypertension: usual care, home BP telemonitoring alone, or home BP telemonitoring plus protocolized pharmacist case management, with pharmacist case managers reviewing teletransmitted data and adjusting BP management.
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4938881/

## Anticoagulation (warfarin) management

**Telephone follow-up service for warfarin, Thailand (TCTR20180614006)** — prospective study of discharged warfarin patients; the telephone follow-up service in recently discharged patients helped them achieve and maintain their INR target.
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6109538/

**Tele-pharmacy Anticoagulation Clinic during COVID-19, Saudi Arabia** — prospective study of 270 patients followed for 3 months in a pharmacist-managed telepharmacy anticoagulation clinic, comparing outcomes with historical face-to-face care.
https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8459665

**Alfalfa App RCT** — randomized controlled trial evaluating efficacy, safety, and cost-effectiveness of a 3-month warfarin dose adjustment mobile app versus offline pharmacist-led anticoagulation clinic management after cardiac valve replacement; time in therapeutic range was significantly higher in the app group (66.46% vs 46.65%, P<.001).
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12112255/

**Internet-based warfarin management RCT, multicenter (mechanical heart valve replacement)** — 721 patients enrolled; the internet-based group showed significantly higher time in therapeutic range and lower anticoagulation complications (6.94% vs 12.74%, P=.01) than the conventional group.
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8398748/

**Video vs. face-to-face pharmacist anticoagulation counseling RCT** — prospective, parallel-group study randomizing patients to warfarin discharge education by video or face-to-face pharmacist counseling.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25788517/

**Hospital anticoagulation clinic vs. online anticoagulation clinic (comparative cohort)** — compared clinical outcomes between patients managed via hospital anticoagulation clinic and those managed via online anticoagulation clinic; patients managed in the hospital clinic had a higher percentage of INR values within target range (80.2% vs 71.9%, P=0.005).
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29956134/

## Heart failure

**Murray et al., Ann Intern Med 2007** — "Pharmacist intervention to improve medication adherence in heart failure: a randomized trial."
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38035317/ (indexed reference; original PMID 17502632)

**Hale et al., J Med Internet Res 2016** — "A Remote Medication Monitoring System for Chronic Heart Failure Patients to Reduce Readmissions: A Two-Arm Randomized Pilot Study."
PMID 27154462 (cited above)

## Complex/multimorbid populations

**Pragmatic RCT, Norway (telemedicine-based intervention for chronic conditions)** — pragmatic randomized controlled trial comparing telemedicine-based intervention (patients using tablets and home telemonitoring devices to report point-of-care measurements and answer health questions at a follow-up center) with usual care in six local centers, measuring HRQoL, patient experience, and healthcare utilization.
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11377648/

A few notes for your review:
- Several of these are RCTs with published protocol papers and multiple follow-up publications (e.g., the Margolis/HyperLink hypertension trial series) — I've listed the primary results papers.
- Some anticoagulation studies are prospective comparative or retrospective cohort designs rather than RCTs; I've flagged the design for each so you can weight the evidence accordingly.
- Coverage is heaviest for hypertension and anticoagulation, where dedicated pharmacist-led telehealth trials are most common; diabetes, heart failure, and general chronic-disease telepharmacy RCTs are sparser and sometimes bundle pharmacists into broader multidisciplinary telemonitoring teams rather than isolating pharmacist-delivered remote care.
