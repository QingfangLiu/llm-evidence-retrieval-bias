Here's a set of individual primary studies (RCTs, cluster-RCTs, and cohort studies) comparing pharmacist-delivered remote/telepharmacy care with usual care for non-hospitalized patients with long-term conditions. I've excluded reviews, meta-analyses, and guidelines as requested.

**Hypertension**

- **Margolis et al., JAMA 2013 (HyperLink trial)** — Cluster-RCT, 16 primary care clinics, 450 adults with uncontrolled BP. Eight clinics were randomized to provide usual care to patients and eight clinics were randomized to provide a telemonitoring intervention, where intervention patients received home BP telemonitors and transmitted BP data to pharmacists who adjusted antihypertensive therapy accordingly. Home BP telemonitoring and pharmacist case management achieved better BP control compared with usual care during 12 months of intervention, an effect that persisted during 6 months of postintervention follow-up.

- **Margolis et al., JAMA Network Open 2018** — Long-term follow-up of the same cluster-RCT. Home blood pressure telemonitoring with pharmacist management lowered blood pressure more than usual care in the first 18 months, but this was not sustained through 54 months, though routine clinical measurements suggested significantly lower blood pressure in the intervention group for up to 24 months.

- **Pilot study, PMC9800920 (China, COVID-19 era)** — Evaluated pharmacist-led telemedicine medication management for established hypertension patients, using WeChat/telephone contact, against usual care.

**Type 2 diabetes**

- **PMC10543137, JAMA Network Open-style RCT** — 221 African American or Latinx patients with type 2 diabetes and elevated HbA1c (≥8%) were randomized to receive mHealth diabetes support, including remote pharmacist review of glucose levels and medication intensification via video telehealth, versus usual care for a year followed by usual care in year two. The remote pharmacist-support arm demonstrated an HbA1c reduction of 0.79 percentage points, compared with 0.42–0.45 points in an earlier in-person pharmacist support trial.

- **Butt et al., Saudi Pharm J 2016** — RCT of pharmacist-led diabetes intervention (cited within Seamon et al. 2021, JACCP) assessing impact of pharmacist led diabetes mellitus intervention on HbA1c, medication adherence and quality of life.

- **Seamon et al., JACCP 2021** — A pharmacist-led diabetes telemedicine program, using a pharmacist-led phone call initiative, was associated with a reduction in HbA1c for patients with uncontrolled diabetes in a rural family practice.

- **PMC11332646** — Evaluation of remote pharmacist intervention on HbA1c reduction within an accountable care organization (real-world comparative study).

**Anticoagulation (warfarin management)**

- **Saudi Med J 2024 (PMC/SMJ 45(12):1374)** — Retrospective cohort of 191 patients newly started on warfarin. The primary outcome, time in therapeutic range, was not significantly different between the telepharmacy and on-site clinic groups (50% vs 53.8%, p=0.455), and remained so after adjustment.

- **PMC8398748 (multicenter RCT, mechanical heart valve patients)** — 721 patients were randomized; the internet-based pharmacy management group showed significantly higher time in therapeutic range (mean 0.53 vs 0.46, P<.001) and lower rates of bleeding and embolic complications (6.94% vs 12.74%, P=.01) than the conventional/office-based group.

- **PMC12112255 ("Alfalfa App" RCT, cardiac valve replacement)** — 405 participants were randomized to warfarin management via a pharmacy app versus a pharmacist-led in-person anticoagulation clinic; time in therapeutic range was significantly higher in the app group (66.46% vs 46.65%, P<.001), with higher monitoring frequency and more INR values in range.
