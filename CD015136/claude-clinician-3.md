## Hypertension

**Margolis et al. (2013), JAMA** — Cluster RCT (n=450) testing home BP telemonitoring plus pharmacist case management versus usual care in primary care clinics; compared pharmaceutical care via telepharmacy with in-person care. The pharmacist-managed telemonitoring arm produced markedly better BP control than usual care.

**Margolis et al. (2018), JAMA Network Open** — 12-month follow-up of the above cohort (n=326) examining whether BP gains from remote pharmacist care persisted after the intervention ended, with the pharmaceutical care group showing significantly reduced BP versus usual care at follow-up.

**UAE telepharmacy hypertension trial (2021–2022)** — Two-arm RCT across 16 community pharmacies (n=239) comparing telepharmacy services (119 patients, 8 pharmacies) against standard in-person pharmacy care (120 patients, 8 pharmacies) for uncontrolled hypertension, assessing drug-related problems and BP control (PMC9891772).

**Ghana telepharmacy hypertension RCT (2022)** — RCT (n=118) from two Ghanaian hospitals testing telepharmacy pharmaceutical care added to standard clinical care versus standard care alone over six months; the intervention group showed a statistically significant reduction in blood pressure and improved adherence, with controlled BP rising from 39.0% to 66.1% (ScienceDirect).

**Pharmaceutical care via telepharmacy vs. in-person pharmaceutical care in elderly hypertensive patients** — Registered RCT (NCT06108674) directly comparing a teleconsultation pharmaceutical care protocol against face-to-face pharmacist care (not usual care alone) in primary care elderly patients with uncontrolled hypertension, evaluating BP control and satisfaction.

## Diabetes

**TelePharmaceutical Care Diabetes Trial (NCT05380596)** — Unblinded RCT (planned n=124) comparing three monthly pharmacist teleconsultations by phone against usual care (medication dispensing only) in type 2 diabetes patients, following CONSORT guidelines.

## Anticoagulation (warfarin)

**Yixing smartphone app study (Jiang et al., 2021), Frontiers in Pharmacology** — Trial (64 intervention, 66 control, propensity-matched to 50/50) of a pharmacist-led remote warfarin management app versus traditional pharmacy services after mechanical valve replacement; the intervention group had a higher fraction of time in therapeutic range (80.3% vs 72.1%, p=0.033) and higher patient awareness scores, though medication-taking adherence and complication rates did not differ significantly.

**Alfalfa App trial (ChiCTR1900021920)** — Single-center RCT comparing app-based ("Internet + pharmacy care") anticoagulation management against offline pharmacist-led anticoagulation clinic care after cardiac valve replacement, powered for time-in-therapeutic-range differences (~73% web-based vs 60% offline in prior data).

## Heart failure

**Pharmacist-Led Management Model RCT (Wang et al., 2024), JAMA Network Open** — Multicenter RCT (n=445) across 5 Chinese hospitals comparing a pharmacist-led intervention delivered via WeChat messaging (weekly reminders, education, monthly pharmacist follow-up) against nurse-led usual care in chronic heart failure. The intervention group had a significantly higher proportion of days covered by heart failure medication at 52 weeks and a greater proportion achieving adherence ≥80%, with improved quality-of-life scores, but no significant difference in readmissions, all-cause mortality, or adverse drug events.

## Warfarin (postal/telephone delivery — retrospective but primary data)

**Chiang Mai postal warfarin telepharmacy study** — Retrospective cohort of outpatients in a postal warfarin delivery program with pharmacist telephone follow-up; found the prevalence of therapeutic INR was 52.3% before and 45% after starting postal delivery, with 29.5% of patients maintaining therapeutic INR continuously throughout.

---

**Notes on scope and quality:**
- Most direct "telepharmacy vs usual care" evidence with RCT designs clusters around hypertension and heart failure.
- Some studies compare telepharmacy against *in-person pharmacist care* rather than true "usual care" (no pharmacist involvement) — I've flagged these explicitly, since it changes what the comparison tells you.
- The China Yixing and Alfalfa warfarin studies compare telepharmacy to standard pharmacy/anticoagulation clinic services, not no-pharmacist usual care.
- Sample sizes are mostly small-to-moderate (100–450), so effect estimates should be interpreted cautiously.
