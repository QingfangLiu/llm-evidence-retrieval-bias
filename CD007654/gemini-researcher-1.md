As an evidence-synthesis researcher, you are exactly right to bypass the narrative summaries and go straight to the primary clinical data. The recent wave of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) for incretin-based weight-loss medications—specifically GLP-1 and GLP-1/GIP receptor agonists like semaglutide and tirzepatide—provides robust primary evidence that these drugs significantly improve health outcomes and lower blood pressure in populations where hypertension is highly prevalent.

While early obesity trials often excluded severe cardiovascular disease, recent landmark RCTs and their pre-specified sub-analyses explicitly capture the health effects of these medicines on blood pressure and cardiovascular endpoints.

## Hard Health Endpoints: Cardiovascular Outcomes

The most definitive primary evidence linking weight-loss medications to improved clinical health in hypertensive populations comes from the SELECT trial (Lincoff et al., 2023). This Phase III event-driven RCT investigated cardiovascular outcomes in over 17,000 overweight or obese patients with preexisting cardiovascular disease (but without diabetes).

* **The Hypertension Overlap:** Over 80% of the SELECT trial participants had a history of clinical hypertension at baseline.
* **The Outcome:** Semaglutide (2.4 mg) reduced the risk of a composite primary endpoint (cardiovascular death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, or nonfatal stroke) by 20% compared to placebo over an average follow-up of nearly 40 months.

## Blood Pressure Reduction and Medication Load

Beyond hard cardiovascular endpoints, primary analyses of other major RCTs reveal significant, direct reductions in blood pressure, which appear to be heavily mediated by the weight loss itself.

* **SURMOUNT-1 (Tirzepatide):** A stratified analysis of the SURMOUNT-1 RCT evaluated the blood pressure effects of tirzepatide over 72 weeks (Krumholz et al., 2024). Tirzepatide treatment yielded a net reduction of 6.8 mm Hg in systolic blood pressure and 4.2 mm Hg in diastolic blood pressure. Notably, mediation analysis showed that 68% of the systolic reduction and 71% of the diastolic reduction was directly explained by the participants' weight loss.
* **STEP 1 and 4 (Semaglutide):** Exploratory analyses of the STEP 1 and STEP 4 trials demonstrated that semaglutide significantly improved cardiometabolic risk factors compared to placebo (Kosiborod et al., 2022). Crucially, the participants taking semaglutide not only saw greater reductions in systolic and diastolic blood pressure, but they also had a net reduction in their use of concurrent anti-hypertensive medications.

These primary studies collectively demonstrate that weight-loss medicines do improve health in people with high blood pressure, functioning both through direct cardiovascular protection and weight-mediated blood pressure reductions that allow for de-escalation of blood pressure therapies.
