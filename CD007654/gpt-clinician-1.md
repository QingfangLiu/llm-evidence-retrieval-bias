## Bottom line

Weight-loss pharmacotherapy can improve blood pressure and, for selected drugs and populations, clinically important cardiovascular outcomes. The evidence is **not a uniform class effect**:

* **Tirzepatide produces the largest demonstrated BP reductions**, including reductions in 24-hour ambulatory BP. Hard-outcome benefit has been shown in patients with obesity-related HFpEF, but not yet for uncomplicated hypertension.
* **Semaglutide has the strongest evidence for preventing atherosclerotic cardiovascular events**, specifically in patients with established cardiovascular disease and overweight/obesity without diabetes. It also improves symptoms and function in obesity-related HFpEF.
* **Orlistat and liraglutide improve BP and metabolic risk factors**, but obesity trials have not shown the same level of cardiovascular-event evidence.
* **Phentermine/topiramate lowers systolic BP but increases heart rate somewhat.** Phentermine alone did not lower ambulatory BP in a recent short trial.
* **Naltrexone/bupropion is less attractive when BP is a major concern:** early BP increases occur, and definitive cardiovascular safety was not established.
* Historical experience matters: **sibutramine increased cardiovascular events despite weight loss**, demonstrating that weight loss or BP surrogates cannot automatically be assumed to improve health outcomes.

I conducted a targeted search for randomized primary studies and original analyses of individual trials. I excluded systematic reviews, meta-analyses, narrative reviews, guidelines, committee statements and editorials.

## Studies measuring clinical health outcomes

### Semaglutide: SELECT

SELECT randomized 17,604 adults with established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, BMI ≥27 kg/m² and no diabetes. Over a mean follow-up of approximately 40 months, cardiovascular death, nonfatal MI or nonfatal stroke occurred in **6.5% with semaglutide versus 8.0% with placebo**, HR 0.80. The absolute reduction was 1.5 percentage points—an approximate NNT of 67 over 3.3 years. Discontinuation because of adverse events was more common with semaglutide, primarily because of gastrointestinal effects. ([PubMed][9])

**Applicability:** This is the best evidence that an obesity medication can reduce atherosclerotic cardiovascular events. It applies to **secondary prevention**, not to an otherwise low-risk patient whose only comorbidity is hypertension.

### Tirzepatide: SUMMIT

SUMMIT randomized 731 patients with obesity-related HFpEF. Over a median 104 weeks, cardiovascular death or worsening HF occurred in **9.9% versus 15.3%**, HR 0.62—an absolute difference of 5.4 percentage points and an approximate NNT of 19. KCCQ clinical summary scores improved by 6.9 points more with tirzepatide. The event benefit was driven principally by fewer worsening-HF events; cardiovascular deaths were few and not reduced. ([New England Journal of Medicine][10])

**Applicability:** Strong evidence for patients with obesity and symptomatic HFpEF, a group in which hypertension is commonly part of the phenotype. It should not be extrapolated as proof of mortality or general ASCVD benefit in uncomplicated hypertension.

### Semaglutide: STEP-HFpEF

In 529 patients with obesity-related HFpEF, semaglutide improved KCCQ scores by **7.8 points more than placebo**, increased six-minute walk distance by 20.3 m and produced greater weight loss. Serious adverse events occurred in 13.3% versus 26.7%. The trial was designed primarily around symptoms, physical limitation and function rather than cardiovascular events. ([PubMed][11])

**Applicability:** Clinically meaningful patient-centered benefit for symptomatic HFpEF, beyond merely lowering BP or weight.

### Orlistat: XENDOS

XENDOS randomized 3,305 adults with obesity to lifestyle intervention plus orlistat or placebo for four years. New diabetes occurred in **6.2% versus 9.0%**, a 37.3% relative reduction; the detectable benefit was concentrated among participants with impaired glucose tolerance at baseline. Completion rates were low—52% with orlistat and 34% with placebo. ([Diabetes Journals][12])

**Applicability:** Evidence for diabetes prevention in obesity with impaired glucose tolerance, rather than a hypertension-specific cardiovascular benefit.

## Studies that temper or contradict benefit assumptions

### Naltrexone/bupropion: LIGHT

LIGHT randomized 8,910 high-risk adults; more than 90% had hypertension. The trial was terminated prematurely after inappropriate disclosure of confidential interim data. At 50% of planned events, MACE occurred in 2.0% versus 2.3%, HR 0.88, but the wide adjusted confidence interval did not establish the prespecified cardiovascular-safety margin. The investigators concluded that cardiovascular safety remained uncertain. ([PubMed][13])

### Sibutramine: SCOUT

Among 9,804 high-risk adults randomized after a sibutramine run-in, sibutramine produced more weight loss but BP was approximately **1.2/1.4 mmHg higher** than with placebo. The primary cardiovascular outcome occurred in **11.4% versus 10.0%**, HR 1.16, including increases in nonfatal MI and stroke. ([New England Journal of Medicine][14])

Sibutramine was subsequently removed from the US market. ([U.S. Food and Drug Administration][15])

### Lorcaserin: CAMELLIA–TIMI 61

This trial randomized 12,000 high-risk adults, 90.4% of whom had hypertension. Lorcaserin produced small BP improvements and was cardiovascularly noninferior, but not superior: MACE HR 0.99 and extended MACE HR 0.97. ([New England Journal of Medicine][16])

Lorcaserin is now historical rather than a treatment option: the FDA requested its withdrawal after a cancer imbalance was identified in the trial data. ([U.S. Food and Drug Administration][17])

## Clinical interpretation

For a patient with obesity and hypertension:

* **Tirzepatide or semaglutide will usually lower BP rather than raise it**, with tirzepatide showing particularly large ambulatory SBP effects. BP can fall enough to require reassessment of existing antihypertensive doses, especially with substantial weight loss or orthostatic symptoms. ([PMC][1])
* **Semaglutide’s cardiovascular-event benefit is established when the patient already has ASCVD.**
* **Tirzepatide and semaglutide offer meaningful health-status benefits in obesity-related HFpEF**, and tirzepatide also reduces worsening-HF events.
* For **hypertension without ASCVD or HFpEF**, the evidence mainly establishes lower BP, weight and metabolic risk—not yet fewer MI, strokes or deaths.
* Phentermine/topiramate may lower SBP, but its pulse effect and the exclusion of uncontrolled BP from the ABPM trial matter when selecting patients.
* Naltrexone/bupropion does not offer a reassuring BP or definitive cardiovascular-outcome advantage when hypertension is a central concern.

Most obesity trials enrolled patients with controlled rather than severe uncontrolled hypertension, and antihypertensive therapy generally continued. The results therefore should not be interpreted as evidence that a weight-loss medicine replaces appropriate antihypertensive treatment.
