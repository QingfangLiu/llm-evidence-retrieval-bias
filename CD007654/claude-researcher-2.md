## Summary

Weight-loss medicines generally lower blood pressure alongside weight loss, and one large modern trial (SELECT) shows this translates into fewer cardiovascular events — but the evidence is mixed by drug class, and one older agent (sibutramine) actually *increased* cardiovascular events in high-risk patients despite promoting weight loss. Here's what the primary literature shows, organized by drug class:

**GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide, liraglutide)** — In the SELECT trial, semaglutide 2.4 mg reduced major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) by 20% in 17,604 adults with overweight/obesity and established cardiovascular disease (most had hypertension), alongside sustained weight loss over 4 years. Semaglutide showed a 20% reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events in 17,604 adults with preexisting cardiovascular disease, overweight or obesity, without diabetes, with weight loss sustained for up to 4 years. A separate small RCT tested liraglutide specifically in hypertensive patients with type 2 diabetes: participants with pre- and stage 1 hypertension and inadequately controlled type 2 diabetes were randomized 1:1 to liraglutide or placebo for 5 weeks to assess effects on 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure.

**Phentermine/topiramate** — The CONQUER trial randomly assigned overweight or obese adults with a BMI of 27-45 kg/m2 and two or more comorbidities including hypertension to placebo, low-dose, or high-dose phentermine/topiramate for 56 weeks, with a dedicated subanalysis evaluating changes in blood pressure in the hypertensive population, stratified by treatment and magnitude of weight loss. Pooled data across trials found at the two highest doses, phentermine/topiramate produced a 5.2 mmHg reduction in systolic blood pressure and about a 3.0 mmHg reduction in diastolic blood pressure, though heart rate rose slightly.

**Orlistat** — Multiple dedicated RCTs exist in hypertensive populations. One trial randomized hypertensive, overweight/obese patients to orlistat plus a hypocaloric diet versus diet alone, finding the orlistat group had greater reductions in weight and diastolic blood pressure. A larger 1-year multicenter trial randomized obese hypertensives inadequately controlled on medication to orlistat or placebo, finding greater weight loss and greater diastolic BP reduction with orlistat, with more orlistat-treated patients reaching goal diastolic BP.

**Sibutramine (withdrawn from market)** — The SCOUT trial is a key contrasting finding: despite promoting weight loss, the risk of a primary cardiovascular outcome event was increased by 16% in the sibutramine group compared with placebo, driven by higher rates of nonfatal MI and stroke, in a population where 97% had cardiovascular disease and 88% had hypertension.
