As an evidence-synthesis researcher, you need the original trial data stripped of secondary interpretations. The current primary literature strongly indicates that modern weight-loss medications—specifically GLP-1 and dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonists—independently improve both blood pressure parameters and hard cardiovascular outcomes in patients with cardiometabolic risks.

Here is a breakdown of the primary clinical trial data evaluating these outcomes, specifically focusing on randomized controlled trial (RCT) results rather than systemic or narrative reviews.

## Blood Pressure Reduction and Medication De-escalation

Both semaglutide and tirzepatide have demonstrated clinically significant blood pressure-lowering effects in primary RCTs, often allowing patients to safely step down their existing antihypertensive therapies.

* **Tirzepatide (SURMOUNT-1):** In stratified analyses of the SURMOUNT-1 trial, 72 weeks of tirzepatide treatment was associated with a net reduction of 6.8 mm Hg systolic and 4.2 mm Hg diastolic blood pressure in participants with obesity or overweight. Notably, 58.0% of the participants receiving the active drug achieved normal blood pressure by the end of the observation period, compared to only 35.2% in the placebo group (Krumholz et al., 2024).
* **Semaglutide (STEP 1 & 4):** Exploratory analyses of the STEP 1 and STEP 4 phase III trials found that 2.4 mg of semaglutide yielded significant reductions in both systolic and diastolic blood pressure compared to a placebo. Beyond these biometric improvements, semaglutide treatment led to a quantifiable net reduction in the use of baseline antihypertensive and lipid-lowering medications (Kosiborod et al., 2022).

## Hard Cardiovascular Outcomes

While blood pressure reduction is an important surrogate marker, recent primary data confirms that these medications effectively reduce the incidence of actual cardiovascular events.

* **Semaglutide (SELECT):** The SELECT trial evaluated people with overweight or obesity and established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, explicitly excluding those with diabetes. The primary trial data showed that once-weekly semaglutide reduced the relative risk of the first major adverse cardiovascular event (MACE)—a composite of cardiovascular mortality, nonfatal myocardial infarction, or stroke—by 20% compared to a placebo. This benefit was observed irrespective of the patients' baseline glycemic status (Lingvay et al., 2024).
