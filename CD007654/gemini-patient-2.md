It makes complete sense that you're looking into how weight-loss medicines might affect your blood pressure. Because weight and hypertension are so tightly linked, finding a treatment that helps with both is a very common and practical goal.

The short answer is **yes**—recent clinical trials show that modern weight-loss medications, specifically GLP-1 and GLP-1/GIP receptor agonists (the classes of drugs that include semaglutide and tirzepatide), provide significant cardiovascular benefits and actively lower blood pressure.

Because you're looking for the original data, I've bypassed the reviews and pulled the findings directly from the primary clinical studies. Here is a breakdown of what those trials have found:

## Blood Pressure Reduction with Semaglutide

Semaglutide (often known by brand names like Wegovy) has been extensively studied for its metabolic effects. In the landmark STEP 1 randomized controlled trial, adults with overweight or obesity who took semaglutide achieved an average body weight reduction of about 15% over 68 weeks. Alongside this weight loss, the study reported that participants experienced significant improvements in both systolic and diastolic blood pressure compared to those taking a placebo (Wilding et al., 2021).

## Major Cardiovascular Benefits

Beyond just lowering the numbers on a blood pressure cuff, weight-loss medications have been shown to actively protect the heart. The SELECT trial specifically evaluated semaglutide in patients who were overweight or obese and had preexisting cardiovascular disease—a group where high blood pressure is extremely common. The trial demonstrated that a 2.4 mg weekly dose of semaglutide reduced the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (including cardiovascular death, nonfatal heart attacks, and nonfatal strokes) by 20% compared to standard care alone (Lincoff et al., 2023).

## Blood Pressure Reduction with Tirzepatide

Tirzepatide (known by brand names like Zepbound) has also shown strong results for blood pressure management. A stratified analysis of the SURMOUNT-1 clinical trial tracked patients taking tirzepatide over 72 weeks. The data revealed a rapid decline in both systolic and diastolic blood pressure during the first 24 weeks, which then stabilized. On average, patients saw a net reduction of 6.8 mm Hg in systolic blood pressure. The researchers determined that the vast majority of this blood pressure reduction (68% to 71%) was directly driven by the weight loss the medication induced (Krumholz et al., 2024).

> **A quick note:** While these trials show that weight-loss medications can be a powerful tool for improving cardiovascular health, your doctor is the best person to help you weigh these options against your current blood pressure medications and medical history.
