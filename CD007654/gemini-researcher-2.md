Yes, primary clinical trials consistently demonstrate that modern weight-loss medications—specifically GLP-1 and dual GLP-1/GIP receptor agonists—significantly improve cardiovascular health and lower blood pressure in individuals with overweight or obesity.

Here is a breakdown of the foundational randomized controlled trials (RCTs) that provide primary data on these outcomes, avoiding secondary synthesis like meta-analyses or reviews.

## Pivotal Primary Studies

**1. The SELECT Trial (Semaglutide & Cardiovascular Events)**
This landmark, double-blind RCT assessed the cardiovascular outcomes of semaglutide in over 17,000 patients with overweight or obesity and preexisting cardiovascular disease, specifically excluding those with diabetes. The trial demonstrated that a 2.4 mg weekly dose of semaglutide was superior to a placebo, reducing the incidence of a primary composite cardiovascular endpoint—death from cardiovascular causes, nonfatal myocardial infarction, or nonfatal stroke—by 20% (Lincoff et al., 2023).

**2. The SURMOUNT-1 Trial (Tirzepatide & Blood Pressure)**
In a stratified analysis of the phase 3 SURMOUNT-1 randomized, placebo-controlled trial, researchers evaluated blood pressure changes in adults with obesity or overweight. Tirzepatide administration triggered a rapid and sustained decline in blood pressure over 72 weeks. The treatment resulted in a net reduction of 6.8 mm Hg in systolic pressure and 4.2 mm Hg in diastolic pressure. Participants taking tirzepatide were significantly more likely to achieve normal blood pressure levels than those on the placebo, an effect driven primarily by substantial weight loss (Krumholz et al., 2024).

**3. The STEP 1 Trial (Semaglutide & Cardiometabolic Risk)**
This 68-week, double-blind randomized trial enrolled 1,961 adults with obesity or overweight (without diabetes) to test semaglutide as an adjunct to lifestyle interventions. Alongside a massive 14.9% mean reduction in body weight, participants receiving semaglutide experienced significantly greater improvements in multiple cardiometabolic risk factors—including notable reductions in systolic blood pressure—compared to the placebo cohort (Wilding et al., 2021).
