Yes, modern anti-obesity medications—specifically GLP-1 receptor agonists (like semaglutide) and dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonists (like tirzepatide)—demonstrate robust improvements in both blood pressure and broader cardiovascular outcomes for patients with overweight or obesity.

Here is the evidence from recent landmark primary randomized controlled trials (RCTs).

## Blood Pressure Reductions

Primary trial data shows that substantial weight loss achieved via these newer agents translates directly to clinically meaningful reductions in resting blood pressure:

* **Tirzepatide:** The SURMOUNT-1 trial evaluated tirzepatide in non-diabetic adults with obesity or overweight and at least one weight-related complication. The primary study demonstrated unprecedented, sustained weight reduction alongside significant improvements across virtually all measured cardiometabolic risk factors, including blood pressure (Jastreboff et al., 2022).
* **Specific Blood Pressure Analysis:** A targeted stratified analysis of the SURMOUNT-1 trial focused exclusively on blood pressure outcomes. It found that over 72 weeks, tirzepatide led to a net reduction of 6.8 mm Hg in systolic blood pressure and 4.2 mm Hg in diastolic blood pressure. Crucially for your practice, participants receiving tirzepatide were significantly more likely to achieve a normal blood pressure by the end of the trial compared to those on placebo (58.0% vs. 35.2%). The researchers noted that approximately 68-71% of this blood pressure reduction was directly mediated by the weight loss itself (Krumholz et al., 2024).
* **Semaglutide:** The STEP 1 trial similarly demonstrated that a 2.4 mg weekly dose of semaglutide, combined with lifestyle intervention, drove sustained weight loss. Alongside the weight reduction, the semaglutide cohort achieved statistically significant, greater improvements in both systolic and diastolic blood pressure compared to the placebo group (Wilding et al., 2021).

## Hard Cardiovascular Outcomes

Improving intermediate markers like blood pressure is important, but preventing downstream complications is the ultimate goal. The SELECT trial provided a definitive look at hard clinical endpoints.

In a massive cohort of over 17,000 non-diabetic patients with overweight or obesity and established cardiovascular disease, weekly semaglutide (2.4 mg) proved superior to a placebo in reducing major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE). Patients on semaglutide saw a 20% relative reduction in the incidence of cardiovascular death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, or nonfatal stroke over a mean follow-up of nearly 40 months (Lincoff et al., 2023).
