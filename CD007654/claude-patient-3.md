## GLP-1/GIP receptor agonists

**Semaglutide – SELECT trial (Lincoff et al., NEJM 2023).** This was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in adults ≥45 years with overweight/obesity and established cardiovascular disease but without diabetes (n=17,604). Semaglutide was associated with changes in multiple biomarkers of cardiovascular risk, including blood pressure, waist circumference, glycemic control, nephropathy, and levels of lipids and C-reactive protein. A pre-specified subgroup analysis found semaglutide significantly and consistently reduced systolic blood pressure across baseline BP categories compared with placebo, and among the 17,604 randomized patients, 14,392 (82%) had a history of hypertension.

**Semaglutide – SUSTAIN 6 (in people with type 2 diabetes).** In this RCT, injectable semaglutide decreased HbA1c, body weight, and systolic blood pressure compared to placebo, and reduced rates of major adverse cardiovascular events, with a hazard ratio of 0.74 (95% CI 0.58–0.95).

**Semaglutide – NCT01720446 (2-year phase 3 trial in type 2 diabetes).** This trial reported vital-sign changes over 104 weeks; the higher-dose semaglutide arm showed larger reductions in diastolic and systolic blood pressure than placebo (e.g., 1.0 mg semaglutide: −5.37 mmHg SBP vs. −2.78 mmHg for the corresponding placebo arm).

**Tirzepatide – SURMOUNT-1 (post hoc blood pressure analysis).** In this randomized, placebo-controlled trial of adults with obesity/overweight, tirzepatide treatment was associated with a rapid decline in systolic and diastolic blood pressure over the first 24 weeks, followed by stabilization, resulting in a net reduction by 72 weeks of about 6.8 mmHg systolic and 4.2 mmHg diastolic versus placebo. Participants assigned to tirzepatide were more likely than those on placebo to have normal blood pressure at week 72 (58.0% vs. 35.2%), with effects broadly consistent across baseline BP subgroups.

**Tirzepatide – SURMOUNT-1 ambulatory BP monitoring substudy.** This substudy enrolled 600 patients who underwent 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure monitoring before randomization and again at week 36. Average 24-hour systolic BP was reduced significantly with tirzepatide versus placebo, ranging from 7.4 to 10.6 mmHg across dose arms, with diastolic BP also reduced, and heart rate increased 2.1–5.4 bpm.

**Tirzepatide – SURMOUNT-3.** In this trial testing tirzepatide after an intensive lifestyle lead-in, blood pressure data were tracked across the run-in and double-blind phases (mean systolic BP fell from roughly 126 to 121 mmHg during lead-in, with further changes during randomized treatment).

## Phentermine/topiramate

**CONQUER trial (Gadde et al., Lancet 2011).** This 56-week phase 3 placebo-controlled trial randomized overweight/obese adults (BMI 27–45) with two or more comorbidities — including hypertension, dyslipidemia, diabetes/prediabetes, or abdominal obesity — to placebo, low-dose, or high-dose phentermine/topiramate extended-release, with primary endpoints of percent weight loss and proportion achieving ≥5% weight loss. Significant improvements were noted in blood pressure, waist circumference, lipids, glycemia, and inflammatory/adipokine markers versus placebo.

**EQUIP trial.** In this RCT, obese patients (mean BMI 42) on a lifestyle program were randomized to phentermine/topiramate ER at two doses or placebo; after 1 year, weight loss was 1.6% with placebo versus 5.1% and 10.9% with the two active doses.

**Cardiovascular risk sub-analysis of CONQUER (Bays et al.).** This analysis specifically evaluated changes in blood pressure among the hypertensive subgroup of CONQUER participants, stratified by treatment and magnitude of weight loss.

**SEQUEL (2-year extension of CONQUER).** A randomized, placebo-controlled extension study evaluating sustained weight loss and metabolic (including blood pressure) benefits over 2 years.

**Ambulatory BP monitoring RCT (NCT05215418, published 2024).** This randomized, multicenter, double-blind ABPM study found phentermine/topiramate reduced systolic blood pressure compared to either placebo or phentermine 30 mg alone. Participants were stratified by hypertensive status (none, treated with 0–2 medications, treated with 3+ medications), and SBP reductions were consistent across these strata regardless of how many background antihypertensive medications patients were on.

**QUEEN's study (Hong et al., 2025, Korea).** A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 4 trial of phentermine/topiramate in Korean adults with obesity, evaluating blood pressure and metabolic outcomes.
