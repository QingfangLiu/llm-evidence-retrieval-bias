To determine whether weight-loss medications improve health outcomes in patients with high blood pressure, we must look directly at primary randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and their predefined sub-analyses. The primary literature demonstrates that GLP-1 and dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonists consistently lower blood pressure and significantly reduce the incidence of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) in these populations.

## Primary Findings on Blood Pressure and Cardiovascular Health

* **Hard Cardiovascular Outcomes:** The landmark SELECT trial evaluated the cardiovascular effects of semaglutide specifically in patients with preexisting cardiovascular disease and obesity (without diabetes)—a population heavily burdened by hypertension. The trial found a 20% relative risk reduction in the incidence of MACE, which included death from cardiovascular causes, nonfatal myocardial infarction, and nonfatal stroke (Lincoff et al., 2023).
* **Direct Blood Pressure Reduction:** In the foundational STEP 1 trial, participants taking once-weekly semaglutide experienced sustained improvements in cardiometabolic risk factors. Alongside an average 14.9% reduction in body weight, the treatment group showed statistically significant and clinically relevant reductions in systolic blood pressure compared to placebo (Wilding et al., 2021).
* **Normalization of Blood Pressure:** Recent stratified analyses of the SURMOUNT-1 trial evaluating the dual-agonist tirzepatide revealed rapid and sustained declines in both systolic and diastolic blood pressure. By 72 weeks, 58.0% of the tirzepatide group had achieved normal blood pressure, compared to 35.2% of the placebo group. Mediation analyses indicated that approximately 70% of this blood pressure reduction was directly driven by the weight loss itself, with the remainder likely mediated by weight-independent vascular mechanisms (Krumholz et al., 2024).
* **Historical Baselines:** Earlier post hoc primary analyses of the older GLP-1 agonist liraglutide (the SCALE trial program) established the initial framework for these cardiovascular benefits, demonstrating early signals of reduced hazard ratios for cardiovascular death and nonfatal events in populations with overweight or obesity (Davies et al., 2017).
