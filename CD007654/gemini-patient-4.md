It makes complete sense to consider how weight-loss medications might interact with or improve your high blood pressure. The short answer is highly encouraging: recent primary clinical trials show that modern weight-loss medications (specifically GLP-1 and dual GLP-1/GIP receptor agonists like semaglutide and tirzepatide) not only lead to significant weight loss but also substantially lower blood pressure and improve overall cardiovascular health.

Here is what the primary clinical studies have found:

## Significant Blood Pressure Reduction

These medications have a direct, measurable impact on your cardiovascular metrics. In the primary SURMOUNT-1 trial, participants taking tirzepatide lost an average of up to 20.9% of their body weight over 72 weeks (Jastreboff et al., 2022). Alongside this weight loss, researchers observed sweeping improvements in nearly all prespecified cardiometabolic risk factors, including significant reductions in both systolic and diastolic blood pressure.

## Around-the-Clock Benefits

The improvements in blood pressure aren't just limited to daytime office visits. A detailed analysis of the SURMOUNT-1 trial used 24-hour ambulatory monitoring to track patients with obesity and overweight. The trial found that tirzepatide provided consistent, clinically meaningful blood pressure reductions during both the day and the night (Krumholz et al., 2024). This is particularly important because a decrease in nighttime blood pressure is a strong predictor of long-term cardiovascular health and lower mortality risk.

## Protection Against Cardiovascular Events

Beyond simply lowering blood pressure numbers, these medications actively protect the heart. The landmark SELECT trial looked at patients who were overweight or obese and had a history of cardiovascular disease, but did not have diabetes. The trial found that semaglutide reduced the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events—specifically cardiovascular death, nonfatal heart attacks, and nonfatal strokes—by 20% compared to a placebo (Lincoff et al., 2023).
