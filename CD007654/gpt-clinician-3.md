## Clinical take-home

**Weight-loss pharmacotherapy can improve health in adults with hypertension, but the certainty and type of benefit depend strongly on the patient’s comorbidities and the drug.**

* **Blood pressure:** Tirzepatide and semaglutide produce clinically meaningful BP reductions, generally proportional to weight loss. Phentermine/topiramate and orlistat produce smaller reductions.
* **Major cardiovascular events:** Semaglutide 2.4 mg has the clearest evidence, but specifically in adults with **established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease**, overweight/obesity, and no diabetes—not uncomplicated primary-prevention hypertension.
* **Heart failure:** Tirzepatide improves outcomes in patients with obesity-related HFpEF.
* **Diabetes prevention:** Tirzepatide, and to a lesser extent liraglutide, substantially reduce progression from prediabetes to diabetes while treatment continues.
* **Important uncertainty:** For patients with obesity and hypertension but without ASCVD, HFpEF, or prediabetes, most evidence remains limited to weight, BP, metabolic markers, and medication burden rather than fewer myocardial infarctions, strokes, or deaths.

I included randomized trials and original participant-level analyses of randomized trials. I excluded reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, opinions, and editorials.

## Evidence for patient-important outcomes

### Semaglutide: established ASCVD

SELECT randomized 17,604 adults with BMI ≥27, established cardiovascular disease, and no diabetes to semaglutide 2.4 mg or placebo. Hypertension was present in approximately 81.7%, making the population highly relevant to hypertensive patients with secondary-prevention indications. Over a mean 39.8 months, cardiovascular death, nonfatal MI, or nonfatal stroke occurred in 6.5% with semaglutide versus 8.0% with placebo, HR 0.80 (95% CI 0.72–0.90). This was a 1.5-percentage-point absolute reduction. Treatment discontinuation because of adverse events was more frequent, 16.6% versus 8.2%. ([PubMed][1])

**Interpretation:** This is strong evidence that semaglutide improves cardiovascular health in a patient who has hypertension **and established ASCVD**. SELECT does not establish the same benefit for primary prevention in someone whose principal comorbidity is hypertension.

### Tirzepatide: obesity-related HFpEF

SUMMIT randomized 731 patients with obesity and symptomatic HFpEF. Cardiovascular death or worsening heart failure occurred in 9.9% with tirzepatide versus 15.3% with placebo, HR 0.62 (95% CI 0.41–0.95). The reduction was principally in worsening-HF events—8.0% versus 14.2%—rather than demonstrated reduction in cardiovascular death. Health status also improved: the between-group difference in KCCQ clinical summary score was 6.9 points. ([PubMed][2])

**Interpretation:** Tirzepatide has outcome-level benefit when hypertension and obesity coexist with HFpEF. It does not yet provide general MACE evidence comparable to SELECT for patients with uncomplicated hypertension.

### Prevention of diabetes

Among 1,032 SURMOUNT-1 participants with obesity and prediabetes treated for 176 weeks, diabetes developed in 1.3% of tirzepatide-treated participants versus 13.3% receiving placebo, HR 0.07. After a 17-week off-treatment period, the corresponding figures were 2.4% and 13.7%, suggesting considerable benefit but some loss of protection after discontinuation. ([PubMed][3])

In the three-year SCALE extension, diabetes developed in 2% of participants receiving liraglutide 3 mg versus 6% receiving placebo, HR 0.21. An important limitation was that only 50% completed 160 weeks and participants who withdrew were not subsequently followed for diabetes outcomes. ([PubMed][4])

**Interpretation:** For a hypertensive patient who also has prediabetes, diabetes prevention is a meaningful additional health benefit, especially with tirzepatide.

## Direct blood-pressure evidence

### Tirzepatide

In the original SURMOUNT-1 trial, tirzepatide produced mean weight reductions of 15.0%–20.9% at 72 weeks and improved prespecified cardiometabolic measures. ([PubMed][5])

An original participant-level BP analysis found placebo-adjusted reductions of:

* **SBP: 6.8 mm Hg**
* **DBP: 4.2 mm Hg**

at 72 weeks. Normal BP was present in 58.0% of tirzepatide-treated participants versus 35.2% receiving placebo. Mediation analysis estimated that weight loss explained 68% of the SBP and 71% of the DBP effect. Low-BP adverse events were uncommon but occurred more frequently with tirzepatide. ([PubMed][6])

In the 600-participant ambulatory-BP substudy, placebo-adjusted 24-hour SBP reductions at 36 weeks were 7.4, 10.6, and 8.0 mm Hg with the 5-, 10-, and 15-mg doses, respectively. However, participants generally had controlled baseline BP and stable antihypertensive therapy, limiting extrapolation to severely uncontrolled hypertension. ([PubMed][7])

### Semaglutide

STEP 1 randomized 1,961 adults without diabetes. Semaglutide produced 14.9% mean weight loss versus 2.4% with placebo and improved cardiometabolic risk factors and physical functioning. Gastrointestinal events caused discontinuation in 4.5% versus 0.8%. ([PubMed][8])

Original analyses of STEP 1 and STEP 4 data found greater SBP and DBP reductions and a net reduction in antihypertensive-medication use with semaglutide. In STEP 4, BP and other risk-factor improvements deteriorated when participants were randomized to discontinue semaglutide. ([PubMed][9])

**Interpretation:** Semaglutide lowers BP and may permit antihypertensive deprescribing, but monitoring is necessary because weight loss and reduced intake can make an established regimen excessive.

### Phentermine/topiramate and phentermine

In an eight-week ambulatory-BP RCT of 565 participants—approximately two-thirds of whom had diagnosed or treated hypertension—24-hour SBP changed by:

* Placebo: −0.1 mm Hg
* Phentermine 30 mg: +1.4 mm Hg
* Phentermine/topiramate 15/92 mg: −3.3 mm Hg

The phentermine/topiramate versus placebo difference was −3.2 mm Hg. The combination caused a small increase in heart rate, while phentermine alone produced a larger heart-rate rise and did not lower ambulatory SBP. Participants with screening BP above 140/90 mm Hg were excluded. ([PubMed][10])

CONQUER demonstrated substantial weight loss with phentermine/topiramate in adults selected for multiple comorbidities, including hypertension, but it was not a cardiovascular-outcomes trial. ([PubMed][11])

**Interpretation:** The combination appears BP-neutral to modestly BP-lowering in controlled hypertension, but phentermine monotherapy should not be assumed to lower BP simply because it causes weight loss.

### Orlistat

A 12-week RCT directly enrolled 204 overweight or obese adults with hypertension. Orlistat plus diet produced 3.7 kg weight loss versus 2.0 kg with diet alone. SBP fell by 15.3 versus 11.6 mm Hg—a nonsignificant between-group difference—while DBP fell by 11.4 versus 5.2 mm Hg, a significant difference. The large reductions in both groups emphasize the contribution of the accompanying dietary intervention. ([PubMed][12])

**Interpretation:** Orlistat may modestly augment DBP and metabolic improvements, but its BP evidence is short-term and substantially weaker than that for tirzepatide or semaglutide.

## Uncertain or negative cardiovascular evidence

### Naltrexone/bupropion

The LIGHT cardiovascular trial randomized 8,910 high-risk participants but was terminated prematurely after confidential interim results were publicly released. At the 50% interim analysis, MACE occurred in 2.0% with naltrexone/bupropion versus 2.3% with placebo, HR 0.88, but the very wide adjusted confidence interval did not establish the prespecified noninferiority margin. The investigators concluded that cardiovascular safety remained uncertain. ([PubMed][13])

### Historical cautionary drugs

Sibutramine is an important reminder that weight reduction and improved metabolic measures do not guarantee cardiovascular benefit. In SCOUT, sibutramine increased nonfatal MI and nonfatal stroke in high-risk patients. ([New England Journal of Medicine][14])

Lorcaserin was cardiovascularly neutral rather than beneficial in CAMELLIA–TIMI 61: MACE HR 0.99 and extended MACE HR 0.97 despite modest improvements in BP and other risk factors. ([PubMed][15])

CRESCENDO found no cardiovascular benefit from rimonabant, HR 0.97, and was stopped early because of psychiatric toxicity and suicides. ([PubMed][16])

## Practical interpretation for hypertensive patients

For a patient with **established ASCVD**, semaglutide has the strongest direct evidence for preventing MI, stroke, or cardiovascular death.

For a patient with **HFpEF and obesity**, tirzepatide has strong evidence for reducing worsening-HF events and improving symptoms and quality of life.

For a patient with **prediabetes**, tirzepatide has especially compelling diabetes-prevention data; liraglutide also has supportive evidence.

For a patient with **hypertension alone**, tirzepatide and semaglutide offer the largest and best-documented BP improvements, but a reduction in hard cardiovascular events has not yet been established specifically for that lower-risk phenotype. BP should be monitored during titration and substantial weight loss, with reassessment of diuretics and other antihypertensives if orthostasis, low home readings, reduced oral intake, or volume depletion occurs.
