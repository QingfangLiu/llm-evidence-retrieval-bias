## GLP-1 / GIP receptor agonists

**Semaglutide** has the most robust primary-outcome data. The SELECT trial randomized 17,604 adults with overweight/obesity and established cardiovascular disease (no diabetes) to semaglutide 2.4 mg or placebo; semaglutide reduced major adverse cardiovascular events by 20% (HR 0.80). A prespecified subgroup analysis found among 17,604 randomized patients, 82% had a history of hypertension, and patients with hypertension who received placebo had a higher rate of cardiovascular events than those on semaglutide (2.6 vs 2.1 per 100 patient-years), with consistent cardiovascular benefit regardless of hypertension history or baseline blood pressure category, and consistent reductions in systolic blood pressure across all blood pressure categories.

**Tirzepatide (SURMOUNT-1)** randomized 2,539 adults with obesity to placebo or tirzepatide 5/10/15 mg. A stratified post hoc analysis found a net reduction by 72 weeks of 6.8 mmHg systolic and 4.2 mmHg diastolic blood pressure versus placebo, with effects consistent across baseline blood pressure and hypertension subgroups, and participants on tirzepatide were more likely than placebo to have normal blood pressure at week 72 (58.0% vs 35.2%). A required ambulatory BP monitoring substudy of the same trial similarly found BP reductions on 24-hour monitoring. SURMOUNT-3, in participants who first completed a 12-week intensive lifestyle lead-in, also tracked blood pressure changes with treatment.

## Phentermine/topiramate

The CONQUER trial randomized overweight/obese adults with ≥2 comorbidities (including hypertension) to placebo or two doses of phentermine/topiramate ER for 56 weeks; it found significant improvements in blood pressure, waist circumference, lipids, glycemia, and inflammatory markers with phentermine/topiramate compared with placebo. A dedicated post hoc cardiovascular-risk analysis of CONQUER examined changes in blood pressure specifically in the hypertensive population, stratified by treatment and magnitude of weight loss. A more recent randomized ambulatory-BP-monitoring trial directly compared phentermine/topiramate, phentermine alone, and placebo on 24-hour BP.

## Orlistat

Two dedicated randomized trials tested orlistat specifically in hypertensive populations. One placebo-controlled trial in obese patients with inadequately controlled hypertension found diastolic BP decreased more with orlistat than placebo (−11.4 vs −9.2 mmHg), and orlistat-treated patients were more likely to reach goal diastolic BP. A second pragmatic RCT in a hypertension clinic found systolic and diastolic blood pressure decreased more with orlistat plus diet than diet alone (15.3/11.4 mmHg vs 11.6/5.2 mmHg). The XENDOS trial (primarily a diabetes-prevention trial) also tracked BP as a secondary outcome with long-term orlistat use.

## Clinical takeaway

Across drug classes, weight loss achieved pharmacologically is associated with meaningful blood pressure reductions, and for semaglutide there is direct evidence of reduced cardiovascular events in patients with a high prevalence of hypertension. Effect sizes and durability vary, and none of these trials had "cure hypertension" as their primary endpoint — BP was usually a secondary or subgroup outcome.
