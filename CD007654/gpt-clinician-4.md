## Bottom line

**Weight-loss medicines often improve blood pressure and cardiometabolic risk in adults with obesity and hypertension, but benefits differ substantially by medication.** The strongest evidence for actual clinical outcomes—not merely weight or blood-pressure changes—is:

* **Semaglutide 2.4 mg** reduced major cardiovascular events in people with overweight/obesity and established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease.
* **Tirzepatide** reduced worsening heart-failure events in people with obesity-related HFpEF.
* These outcome trials were **not limited to people with hypertension**, so they support treatment particularly when hypertension coexists with established ASCVD or HFpEF. I did not identify a cardiovascular-outcome trial restricted to otherwise uncomplicated obesity-associated hypertension. ([PubMed][1])

For blood-pressure control itself, individual trials support reductions with **semaglutide, tirzepatide, orlistat, phentermine/topiramate, topiramate, and liraglutide**. **Naltrexone/bupropion does not show a consistent BP benefit**, and its cardiovascular-outcome trial was inconclusive. The older drug **sibutramine increased nonfatal MI and stroke**, illustrating that weight loss alone does not guarantee cardiovascular benefit. ([PMC][2])

I searched PubMed and trial-linked primary publications through **July 18, 2026**. I included randomized trials, analyses using data from a single randomized trial, and one relevant primary cohort study. I excluded systematic reviews, meta-analyses, pooled multiple-trial analyses, guidelines, opinions, editorials, and narrative reviews.

## Studies directly examining blood pressure

### Semaglutide

A prespecified/exploratory analysis of the individual **STEP 1** randomized trial reported BP reductions in both controlled and uncontrolled hypertension:

* Controlled hypertension: SBP **−6.38 vs −1.47 mmHg** and DBP **−2.96 vs −0.72 mmHg**.
* Uncontrolled hypertension: SBP **−4.67 vs +1.59 mmHg** and DBP **−2.31 vs +1.43 mmHg**.
* An ACC/AHA BP level below 130/80 mmHg was reached by **51.5% vs 38.2%**. ([PMC][2])

Thus, semaglutide produced approximately a **5–6 mmHg placebo-adjusted SBP benefit**, including among participants whose hypertension was uncontrolled at baseline. This analysis was based on a subgroup of a weight-loss trial rather than a dedicated hypertension trial. ([PMC][2])

### Tirzepatide

The individual-trial analysis of **SURMOUNT-1** found a placebo-adjusted reduction at 72 weeks of:

* SBP: **−6.8 mmHg**
* DBP: **−4.2 mmHg**

Normal BP at week 72 was present in **58.0% of tirzepatide-treated participants versus 35.2% with placebo**. Effects were consistent across baseline BP and hypertension categories. Mediation analysis estimated that weight loss accounted for approximately 68% of the SBP and 71% of the DBP effect. ([PubMed][8])

This is a post hoc analysis, but it uses participant data from one randomized trial and directly evaluates hypertension categories.

### Orlistat

In a pragmatic RCT confined to **204 overweight or obese hypertensive patients**, 12 weeks of orlistat plus diet versus diet alone produced:

* Weight change: **−3.7 vs −2.0 kg**
* SBP change: **−15.3 vs −11.6 mmHg**; between-group difference was not statistically significant.
* DBP change: **−11.4 vs −5.2 mmHg**; statistically greater reduction with orlistat.
* Greater reductions in glucose and cholesterol were also observed. ([PubMed][9])

A separate 54-week randomized trial in 531 adults with cardiovascular risk factors found SBP changes of **−6.0 vs −2.3 mmHg** and DBP changes of **−5.5 vs −3.1 mmHg** with orlistat versus placebo. ([PubMed][10])

### Phentermine/topiramate and phentermine

A 2024 randomized ambulatory-BP trial enrolled 565 adults; approximately two-thirds had treated hypertension. At eight weeks, mean 24-hour SBP changed by:

* Placebo: **−0.1 mmHg**
* Phentermine 30 mg: **+1.4 mmHg**
* Phentermine 15 mg/topiramate ER 92 mg: **−3.3 mmHg**

The phentermine/topiramate-placebo difference was **−3.2 mmHg**, while phentermine monotherapy did not lower BP. Participants with screening BP above 140/90 mmHg were excluded, so these findings apply chiefly to controlled hypertension. ([PubMed][11])

In the **CONQUER** hypertension subgroup, phentermine/topiramate produced greater weight loss and BP improvement than placebo. Across treatment assignments, hypertensive participants losing at least 5% of body weight had SBP reductions of approximately **7.5–11.8 mmHg**, but this analysis does not isolate a medication effect from the effect of achieved weight loss. ([PubMed][12])

### Topiramate monotherapy

A hypertension-specific RCT randomized 531 adults with obesity and established hypertension to placebo or topiramate:

* Weight loss: **1.9% with placebo vs 5.9% and 6.5%** with topiramate 96 and 192 mg/day.
* DBP reduction: **2.1 vs 5.5 and 6.3 mmHg**, respectively.

The study was terminated earlier than originally planned when development shifted to a controlled-release formulation. ([PubMed][13])

### Liraglutide

The principal SCALE obesity trial enrolled 3,731 people without diabetes; a BMI of 27–29.9 required a comorbidity such as treated or untreated hypertension. Liraglutide produced substantially greater weight loss than placebo and improved cardiometabolic measures, but it was not a hypertension-specific trial. ([PubMed][14])

In **SCALE Insulin**, involving 396 adults with obesity/overweight and insulin-treated diabetes, SBP fell **5.6 mmHg with liraglutide versus 1.6 mmHg with placebo**, an estimated treatment difference of **−4.0 mmHg**; DBP was not significantly different. ([PMC][15])

### Naltrexone/bupropion

In **COR-I**, participants could qualify through obesity alone or overweight with hypertension/dyslipidemia. Naltrexone/bupropion produced approximately 4–5 percentage points more weight loss than placebo. BP, however, initially increased by about **1.5 mmHg**, then declined to approximately 1 mmHg below baseline; the trial did not demonstrate a convincing antihypertensive effect. ([PubMed][16])

Its prematurely terminated LIGHT trial means that BP-neutrality or modest weight loss should not be interpreted as proven cardiovascular safety or benefit. ([PubMed][5])

## Clinical interpretation

For a patient with obesity and hypertension but no established cardiovascular disease or HF:

* **Semaglutide and tirzepatide have the most convincing contemporary evidence for lowering BP**, generally by roughly 5–7 mmHg systolic relative to placebo in hypertension-category analyses. ([PMC][2])
* **Orlistat and phentermine/topiramate also lower BP**, although the magnitude of weight loss and supporting outcome evidence are less robust. ([PubMed][9])
* **Liraglutide probably lowers SBP modestly**, but the hypertension-specific evidence is less developed than for semaglutide or tirzepatide. ([PMC][15])
* **Naltrexone/bupropion should not be selected for an expected BP benefit**; BP and pulse require attention, and definitive cardiovascular-outcome evidence is lacking. ([PubMed][16])
* **Phentermine alone may raise ambulatory SBP**, even when hypertension is controlled; the combination with topiramate behaved differently in the short-term ABPM trial. ([PubMed][11])

For patients with additional disease, the evidence is stronger and more specific: semaglutide reduced MACE in established ASCVD, semaglutide improved symptoms and function in obesity-related HFpEF, and tirzepatide reduced worsening HF in obesity-related HFpEF. ([PubMed][1])
