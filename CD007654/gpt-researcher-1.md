## Scope

I conducted a targeted search through **July 18, 2026** for human primary studies of pharmacologic weight management in adults with overweight/obesity and hypertension. I prioritized:

* Trials in which all participants had hypertension.
* Prespecified or post-hoc hypertension/BP subgroup analyses from randomized trials.
* Cardiovascular-outcome trials when hypertension was highly prevalent, but I classify these as indirect unless a hypertension-specific effect was reported.

The synthesis and reference list below cite only primary study reports. This is an evidence map rather than a fully reproducible PRISMA systematic review.

## Main conclusion

**Weight-loss medicines generally produce weight loss in people with hypertension, and several also lower blood pressure modestly. However, direct evidence that they prevent myocardial infarction, stroke, heart failure, kidney failure, or death specifically in the hypertensive population remains limited.**

The evidence is most convincing for:

* **Weight and BP reduction:** tirzepatide, semaglutide, phentermine/topiramate, topiramate and orlistat.
* **Hard clinical benefit in broader high-risk obesity populations:** semaglutide in established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease and tirzepatide in obesity-related HFpEF.
* **Uncertain cardiovascular safety/benefit:** naltrexone/bupropion, because its cardiovascular trial was terminated prematurely.
* **Potential harm despite weight loss:** sibutramine, which increased nonfatal cardiovascular events.

## Do the medicines improve health beyond BP and weight?

### Direct hypertension evidence

The only large cardiovascular-outcome study in which almost everyone had hypertension was **LIGHT**, testing naltrexone/bupropion. Its interim results did not show an excess of MACE, but early termination made the planned cardiovascular-safety conclusion impossible. It therefore does **not** demonstrate cardiovascular benefit. ([PubMed][9])

None of the hypertension-focused orlistat, topiramate, phentermine/topiramate, semaglutide or tirzepatide analyses was adequately powered to establish fewer strokes, myocardial infarctions, kidney-failure events or deaths specifically among participants with hypertension.

### Stronger, but indirect, clinical-outcome evidence

Two primary outcome trials show that newer incretin medicines can improve clinically important outcomes in selected obesity populations, although neither provides a clean hypertension-specific treatment estimate:

* **SELECT:** In 17,604 people with established cardiovascular disease, overweight/obesity and no diabetes, semaglutide reduced cardiovascular death, nonfatal MI or nonfatal stroke from 8.0% to 6.5%; HR 0.80. Discontinuation because of adverse events was 16.6% versus 8.2%. ([PubMed][13])
* **SUMMIT:** In 731 people with obesity and HFpEF, tirzepatide reduced cardiovascular death or worsening heart failure from 15.3% to 9.9%; HR 0.62, and improved KCCQ health status by an additional 6.9 points. The benefit was driven mainly by fewer worsening-HF events; the trial was not powered to determine cardiovascular mortality alone. ([PubMed][14])

These results support genuine health benefits in **secondary cardiovascular prevention** and **obesity-related HFpEF**, but should not automatically be generalized to a younger person whose only comorbidity is uncomplicated hypertension.

### Evidence that weight loss does not guarantee health benefit

In **SCOUT**, sibutramine produced additional weight loss but BP decreased less than with placebo. The composite cardiovascular outcome occurred in 11.4% versus 10.0%, HR 1.16; nonfatal MI and nonfatal stroke were also increased. ([New England Journal of Medicine][15])

This is an important counterexample: drug mechanism, heart-rate/BP effects and adverse events matter independently of kilograms lost.

## Overall assessment

**For an adult with obesity and hypertension:**

* **Tirzepatide and semaglutide** provide the greatest weight loss and generally lower BP, but hypertension-specific morbidity and mortality data remain sparse.
* **Phentermine/topiramate** produces substantial weight loss and modest BP reduction, though pulse, neuropsychiatric effects and long-term cardiovascular uncertainty require attention.
* **Orlistat** produces smaller weight loss and modest BP/metabolic improvements; GI adverse effects are frequent.
* **Topiramate alone** lowers weight and DBP but has considerable cognitive and neurologic tolerability limitations.
* **Naltrexone/bupropion** gives modest weight loss, does not lower BP and lacks a completed definitive cardiovascular-safety trial.
* **Sibutramine** demonstrates that a weight-reducing drug can worsen cardiovascular outcomes.

Thus, the best-supported conclusion is that several medicines improve **intermediate health measures**, especially weight, BP and glycemic risk. Proof of fewer serious clinical events **specifically attributable to treatment in people with hypertension** is substantially weaker.
