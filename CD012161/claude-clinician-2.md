Here's a summary of primary studies addressing this question, organized by comparison type.

## Faster-acting insulin aspart vs. conventional insulin aspart (ONSET program)

The **ONSET 1 trial** (Russell-Jones et al., 2017) randomized adults with T1D on MDI to double-blind mealtime faster aspart, postmeal faster aspart, or conventional insulin aspart, all with insulin detemir. Faster aspart effectively improved HbA1c, with noninferiority to conventional insulin aspart confirmed, and superior postprandial glucose control for mealtime faster aspart versus conventional aspart. A companion paper (Mathieu et al., 2018) reported the extended 52-week results.

A related trial (Buse et al., 2018) compared fast-acting insulin aspart versus insulin aspart in the setting of insulin degludec-treated type 1 diabetes in a randomized double-blind design.

The **ONSET 8 trial** randomized individuals with T1D to faster aspart or insulin aspart, both combined with insulin degludec; noninferiority to mealtime insulin aspart was confirmed for mealtime or post-meal faster aspart on HbA1c change, and superiority was shown for mealtime faster aspart on the 1-hour postprandial glucose increment, with statistically significantly lower hypoglycemia rates 3–4 hours after a meal in the faster aspart arm.

## Ultra-rapid lispro (URLi) vs. lispro (PRONTO program)

The **PRONTO-T1D trial** (Klaff et al., 2020) was a 26-week, treat-to-target, phase 3 trial in which patients were randomized to double-blind mealtime URLi or lispro, or open-label post-meal URLi, after an 8-week lead-in to optimize basal insulin. Both mealtime and post-meal URLi demonstrated noninferiority to lispro for HbA1c change, though post-meal URLi had a significantly higher endpoint HbA1c versus lispro. Its 52-week extension (Bue-Valleskey et al.) found similar proportions with HbA1c <7% at week 52, statistically significantly lower 1-hour and 2-hour postmeal glucose with URLi, similar rates of severe/documented/postprandial hypoglycemia overall but a 31% lower rate more than 4 hours after meals with URLi, and more injection site reactions with URLi (3.3% vs 0.9%).

## Insulin lispro vs. regular human insulin (older analogue-vs-human-insulin trials)

Anderson et al. and related twice-daily lispro/NPH trials found that in 707 randomized patients with type 1 or type 2 diabetes, twice-daily insulin lispro produced lower postprandial glucose levels and smaller postprandial glucose excursions than regular human insulin, with overall glycemic control, hypoglycemia frequency, and total insulin dose not differing between groups.

Raskin et al. (2001) conducted a 24-week multicenter, randomized, two-way crossover, open-label trial comparing insulin lispro with buffered regular human insulin via CSII pump in patients with type 1 diabetes.

## Insulin aspart vs. regular/soluble human insulin (older trials)

The **UK Insulin Aspart Study Group trial** (Home et al., 1998) was a multicenter randomized double-blind crossover study of 90 male subjects with type 1 diabetes, comparing insulin aspart or soluble human insulin before meals with NPH at bedtime. 24-hour glucose control was superior with aspart (22% reduction in excursions outside the normal range), and hypoglycemic episodes requiring third-party intervention were significantly fewer with aspart (20 vs 44 events), though nighttime glycemic control was inferior with aspart.

Lindholm et al. (1999) performed a double-blind, double-dummy, three-way crossover study in 22 subjects with type 1 diabetes comparing insulin aspart with human insulin given immediately or 30 minutes before a meal, finding superior postprandial glucose control with aspart.

Home et al. (2000) conducted a further randomized controlled trial of insulin aspart vs. human insulin for long-term blood glucose control in T1D, and Raskin et al. (2000) reported a trial titled "Use of insulin aspart, a fast-acting insulin analog, as the mealtime insulin in the management of patients with type 1 diabetes" (Diabetes Care 2000;23:583-8).

## Insulin glulisine vs. insulin lispro

Dreyer et al. (2005) conducted a multinational, multicenter, controlled, open-label, randomized, parallel-group study comparing insulin glulisine to insulin lispro in adults with type 1 diabetes (Horm Metab Res 2005;37:702–707).

## Pump/CSII-focused faster-aspart trials (relevant pharmacologic/safety data, though not strictly MDI)
