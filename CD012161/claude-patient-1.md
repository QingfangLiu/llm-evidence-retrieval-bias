## What the evidence shows

**Rapid-acting analogues vs. regular human insulin (older generation comparisons):**
Across multiple RCTs in adults with T1D on MDI, insulin lispro, aspart, and glulisine consistently show modestly better or equivalent HbA1c control compared with regular human insulin, plus lower postprandial glucose excursions. In one trial, insulin aspart was superior to human insulin with a HbA1c difference of 0.12% and lower post-prandial glucose after all main meals, though pre-prandial glucose was somewhat higher before breakfast and dinner. A UK crossover trial found no significant difference in overall symptomatic hypoglycemia frequency between lispro and human soluble insulin, though timing of hypoglycemic episodes shifted (less overnight, more morning-to-midday). A separate trial found glulisine and lispro produced similar HbA1c reductions over 26 weeks in adults with T1D, suggesting rough equivalence between analogues.

**Ultra-rapid/faster-acting analogues vs. standard analogues (newer generation):**
Trials of faster aspart and ultra-rapid lispro (URLi) against their standard-analogue predecessors generally show noninferiority for HbA1c with modest improvements in early postprandial glucose control. In the onset 1 trial, mealtime faster aspart statistically significantly reduced HbA1c versus insulin aspart, with noninferiority confirmed and superior postprandial glucose control for mealtime faster aspart. In PRONTO-T1D, both mealtime and post-meal URLi demonstrated noninferiority to lispro for HbA1c, though post-meal dosing had a statistically significantly higher endpoint HbA1c than lispro. On safety, longer follow-up data are reassuring: the rate and incidence of severe, documented, and postprandial hypoglycemia were similar between URLi and lispro over 52 weeks, and URLi showed a lower rate of hypoglycemia more than 4 hours after meals, though injection site reactions were more frequent with URLi.

Overall, the pattern across these primary studies is: modern rapid-acting analogues (lispro, aspart, glulisine) offer small but real advantages over regular human insulin in postprandial control and sometimes HbA1c, with mixed effects on hypoglycemia timing rather than overall frequency; and the newest "ultra-fast" formulations (faster aspart, URLi) offer further modest improvements in early postprandial glucose over standard analogues, generally without increased hypoglycemia risk, though local injection-site reactions appear somewhat more common.
