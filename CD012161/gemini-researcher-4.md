For an evidence-synthesis project focused on adults with type 1 diabetes (T1D) using multiple daily injections (MDI), the most relevant primary literature compares the newer "faster-acting" or "ultra-rapid" insulin analogues (e.g., fast-acting insulin aspart [Fiasp] and ultra-rapid lispro [Lyumjev]) directly against their standard rapid-acting predecessors (standard aspart and lispro). Direct comparisons against regular human insulin (RHI) in modern Phase 3 MDI trials are rare, as rapid-acting analogues have long replaced RHI as the standard of care for baseline comparisons.

Here is a synthesis of the benefits and harms extracted strictly from key primary randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and their sub-studies, followed by the requested reference list.

## Benefits of Faster-Acting Analogues

**Postprandial Glucose (PPG) Excursions**
The most significant and consistent benefit of faster-acting analogues is superior control of post-meal blood glucose spikes.

* **Ultra-rapid lispro:** In the PRONTO-T1D trial, mealtime ultra-rapid lispro was superior to standard lispro in reducing both 1-hour and 2-hour PPG excursions during a standardized meal test (Klaff et al., 2020).
* **Fast-acting aspart:** The onset 1 trial demonstrated that fast-acting insulin aspart provided statistically significant improvements in 1-hour and 2-hour PPG increments compared to standard insulin aspart (Russell-Jones et al., 2017).

**Time in Range (TIR)**
Continuous Glucose Monitoring (CGM) substudies show that the accelerated absorption of these insulins improves daytime glycemic control.

* A CGM substudy of the PRONTO-T1D trial found that mealtime ultra-rapid lispro significantly increased daytime time in range (71–180 mg/dL) by roughly 43.6 minutes compared to standard lispro (Malecki et al., 2020).

**HbA1c Non-Inferiority and Pharmacodynamics**

* **HbA1c Reduction:** Both fast-acting insulin aspart and ultra-rapid lispro successfully confirmed non-inferiority to their standard counterparts for overall HbA1c reduction over 26 weeks (Klaff et al., 2020; Russell-Jones et al., 2017).
* **Pharmacodynamics:** These outcomes are driven by a left-shifted pharmacokinetic profile. For example, ultra-rapid lispro appears in the bloodstream about 6 minutes faster than standard lispro, delivering a 7.2-fold greater insulin exposure over the first 15 minutes post-dose in adults with T1D (Linnebjerg et al., 2020).

## Harms and Safety Profile

**Severe Hypoglycemia**
The overall rates and incidence of severe, documented, and blood glucose-confirmed hypoglycemia are fundamentally similar between faster-acting analogues and standard rapid-acting analogues (Klaff et al., 2020; Russell-Jones et al., 2017).

**Late Postprandial and Nocturnal Hypoglycemia**
Because faster-acting analogues leave the bloodstream sooner than standard analogues, they have a shorter duration of action, which actually presents a minor safety benefit regarding late hypoglycemia:

* Mealtime ultra-rapid lispro demonstrated a 37% lower rate of hypoglycemia occurring more than 4 hours after meals compared to standard lispro (Klaff et al., 2020).
* CGM data also indicated a significant decrease in nighttime time spent in hypoglycemia (≤70 mg/dL) with ultra-rapid lispro (Malecki et al., 2020).

**Adverse Events and Tolerability**
While the overall incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events is comparable, the novel excipients used to accelerate absorption (such as citrate and treprostinil in ultra-rapid lispro, or niacinamide in fast-acting aspart) can affect injection site tolerability.

* In the PRONTO-T1D trial, injection site reactions were reported by 2.9% of patients using mealtime ultra-rapid lispro, compared to just 0.2% of patients using standard lispro (Klaff et al., 2020).
