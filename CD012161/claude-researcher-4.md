## What the primary evidence covers

**Faster-acting analogue vs. regular human insulin:**
- The original 1990s multicentre trials established that insulin lispro reduced postprandial glucose excursions and hypoglycemia frequency compared with regular human insulin insulin lispro was compared with regular human insulin in the mealtime treatment of 1,008 patients with IDDM in a 6-month randomized multinational and multicenter clinical trial with an open-label crossover design.
- Insulin aspart vs. regular/soluble human insulin was tested in several adult T1D trials (Tamas 2001, Home 2006, Heller 2004, Raskin 2000).
- Insulin glulisine vs. regular human insulin (with glargine as basal) was tested by Garg et al. 2005.

**Faster-acting analogue vs. faster-acting analogue (head-to-head):**
- The Novo Nordisk "ONSET" program compared faster aspart vs. conventional insulin aspart in adults with T1D on MDI (ONSET 1, ONSET 8).
- The Lilly "PRONTO" program compared ultra-rapid lispro (URLi/Lyumjev) vs. conventional lispro in adults with T1D on MDI (PRONTO-T1D and its extension/substudies).
- Glulisine vs. lispro and glulisine vs. aspart/lispro (van Bon 2011, pump-based) were also compared, though some of these trials are pediatric or pump-based rather than adult MDI — flagged below.
- A biosimilar aspart trial (GEMELLI 1) compared a biosimilar faster-acting aspart to originator aspart in adults on MDI.

I did not include the CopenFast pregnancy trial in the core list since it's not specific to typical adult T1D MDI management (a special population), but have noted it for reference in case it's useful.
