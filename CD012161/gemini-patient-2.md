Deciding whether to switch your mealtime insulin is an important step, and looking directly at the primary clinical trials helps separate the actual clinical outcomes from general marketing.

For adults with type 1 diabetes managing their blood sugar via multiple daily injections (MDI), clinical trials have tracked the differences between regular human insulin, standard rapid-acting analogues (like insulin aspart or lispro), and the newest "ultra-rapid" formulations (like fast-acting aspart/Fiasp or ultra-rapid lispro/Lyumjev).

Here is a breakdown of the benefits and harms based on direct evidence from randomized controlled trials (RCTs).

## Benefits of Faster-Acting Insulin Analogues

**1. Superior Post-Meal Blood Sugar Control**
The primary advantage of rapid and ultra-rapid analogues is their ability to reduce postprandial (post-meal) blood sugar spikes, as they are absorbed into the bloodstream much faster than regular human insulin.

* **Versus regular human insulin:** In a large 6-month RCT involving adults with type 1 diabetes on MDI, standard insulin aspart yielded significantly lower glucose levels after all main meals compared to regular human insulin (Home et al., 2000).
* **Versus other rapid-acting analogues:** The newest "ultra-rapid" formulations improve upon this further. A 52-week Phase III RCT (the ONSET 1 trial) demonstrated that fast-acting insulin aspart (Fiasp) provided superior control of 1-hour postprandial glucose levels compared to conventional insulin aspart (Mathieu et al., 2018). Similarly, the 26-week PRONTO-T1D RCT found that ultra-rapid lispro (Lyumjev) was superior to conventional lispro (Humalog) in reducing both 1-hour and 2-hour post-meal glucose excursions (Klaff et al., 2020).

**2. Overall Glycemic Control (HbA1c)**
Faster-acting analogues tend to provide small but statistically significant improvements in long-term blood glucose control metrics.

* Insulin aspart lowered HbA1c significantly more than regular human insulin over a 6-month period (Home et al., 2000).
* When comparing ultra-rapid analogues to their standard counterparts, fast-acting aspart achieved a significantly greater reduction in HbA1c at 52 weeks compared to conventional aspart (Mathieu et al., 2018). Ultra-rapid lispro was shown to be non-inferior (equally effective) to conventional lispro in HbA1c reduction (Klaff et al., 2020).

**3. Treatment Satisfaction and Convenience**
Because rapid-acting insulins begin working in 10 to 15 minutes (and ultra-rapid in even less), they can be injected right at the start of a meal—or sometimes immediately after—rather than 30 to 45 minutes beforehand. Adults utilizing insulin aspart reported significantly higher treatment satisfaction compared to those using regular human insulin due to this flexibility (Home et al., 2000).

## Harms and Safety Considerations

**1. Hypoglycemia Risks and Shifts in Timing**
The overall rates of severe hypoglycemia are generally comparable across different insulin types, but faster-acting analogues alter the *timing* of when lows are most likely to occur.

* **Reduced late/nocturnal lows:** Because regular human insulin has a longer duration of action (up to 8 hours), it stays in the system long after the meal has digested. Insulin aspart significantly reduced the frequency of major nighttime hypoglycemic events requiring parenteral treatment, as well as late post-meal lows (4–6 hours after eating), compared to regular human insulin (Home et al., 2000). Ultra-rapid lispro also yielded a 37% lower rate of hypoglycemia occurring more than 4 hours after meals compared to standard lispro (Klaff et al., 2020).
* **Overall severe lows:** Fast-acting aspart showed no significant difference in the overall rate of severe or blood-glucose-confirmed hypoglycemia compared to standard aspart (Mathieu et al., 2018).

**2. Pre-Prandial Glucose Variability**
Because regular human insulin lasts longer, it inadvertently provides some residual "basal" coverage between meals. Switching to a rapid-acting analogue with a shorter action profile can sometimes leave gaps in coverage if the basal insulin dose isn't adjusted, which can result in slightly higher pre-meal glucose levels (as was observed before breakfast and dinner when comparing insulin aspart to human insulin) (Home et al., 2000).

**3. Injection Site Reactions**
While general adverse events are broadly similar across modern insulin analogues, some of the newer ultra-rapid formulations utilize added ingredients (excipients) to speed up absorption, which can cause local irritation. In the PRONTO-T1D trial, injection site reactions were reported by 2.4% to 2.9% of patients using ultra-rapid lispro, compared to just 0.2% of those using standard lispro (Klaff et al., 2020).
