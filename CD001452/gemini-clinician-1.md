As a clinician, it makes sense that you want to minimize distress for your neonates while efficiently getting the blood samples you need. Based on the primary randomized controlled trials, venepuncture is generally considered superior to a heel lance for full-term newborns.

Here is how the primary literature breaks down the comparison:

## Pain and Distress

Studies consistently demonstrate that venepuncture is significantly less painful than a heel lance. When pain is measured using standardized neonatal scoring systems (such as the Premature Infant Pain Profile or the Neonatal Facial Coding System) alongside crying time, infants undergoing venepuncture show far less distress (Eriksson et al., 1999; Larsson et al., 1998; Ogawa, 2005). The mechanics of a heel lance often require squeezing or "milking" the heel to maintain blood flow, which prolongs the procedure and compounds the tissue pain.

## Procedural Efficacy

Venepuncture is generally more effective for drawing standard lab volumes (like a newborn metabolic screen or a serum bilirubin check). Phlebotomy via a vein has a higher first-attempt success rate and takes less total time to collect the necessary blood volume compared to lancing (Larsson et al., 1998).

## The Role of Sweet Solutions

While administering oral glucose or sucrose before either procedure effectively reduces pain, the baseline difference between the techniques remains. For instance, one study found that venepuncture *without* a sweet solution caused less distress than a heel lance *with* one (Ogawa, 2005). However, combining venepuncture with an oral sweet solution provides the most optimal pain relief overall (Eriksson et al., 1999).

## The Clinical Caveat: Volume Matters

The main exception to these findings comes down to the volume of blood required. If you only need a single drop of blood—such as for a bedside point-of-care glucose check—the extensive setup for venepuncture isn't warranted. In those single-drop scenarios, a heel lance is faster to perform, and the difference in pain is negligible since prolonged squeezing isn't necessary.
