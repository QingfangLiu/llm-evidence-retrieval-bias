## Primary studies found

1. **Shah VS, Taddio A, Bennett S, Speidel BD.** Neonatal pain response to heel stick vs venepuncture for routine blood sampling. *Arch Dis Child Fetal Neonatal Ed.* 1997;77(2):F143–F144. — RCT, n=27, term neonates, NIPS pain scores compared between groups.

2. **Larsson BA, Tannfeldt G, Lagercrantz H, Olsson GL.** Venipuncture is more effective and less painful than heel lancing for blood tests in neonates. *Pediatrics.* 1998;101(5):882–886. — RCT, n=120 full-term PKU-screening infants, 3 arms (venepuncture, small lancet, large lancet), NFCS and crying outcomes.

3. **Logan PW.** Venepuncture versus heel prick for the collection of the Newborn Screening Test. *Aust J Adv Nurs.* 1999;17(1):30–36. — trial comparing heel prick vs. dorsal hand vein venepuncture for newborn screening collection, conducted across two maternity centres.

4. **Eriksson M, Gradin M, Schollin J.** Oral glucose and venepuncture reduce blood sampling pain in newborns. *Early Hum Dev.* 1999;55(3):211–218. — RCT, n=120 healthy full-term newborns, 2×2 design (venepuncture vs. heel stick, with/without oral glucose), crying time, PIPP score, heart rate.

5. **Ogawa S, Ogihara T, Fujiwara E, et al.** Venepuncture is preferable to heel lance for blood sampling in term neonates. *Arch Dis Child Fetal Neonatal Ed.* 2005;90(5):F432–F436. — Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, n=100 full-term newborns, 4 arms (VP/HL × sucrose/placebo), NFCS and crying.

6. **Taksande AM, Vilhekar KY, Jain M, Chitre D.** Pain response of neonates to venipuncture. *Indian J Pediatr.* 2005;72(9):751–753. — Physiological/behavioral pain response study in neonates undergoing venepuncture.

7. **Saththasivam P, Umadevan D, Ramli N, Voralu K, Naing NN, Ilias MI, Shuib N, Tan BG, van Rostenberghe H.** Venipuncture versus heel prick for blood glucose monitoring in neonates. *Singapore Med J.* 2009;50(10):1004–1007. — RCT comparing pain/physiological response and accuracy for glucose monitoring via the two methods.

A few notes for your synthesis work:
- Studies 1, 2, and 5 are the three trials most consistently cited as the core RCT evidence base in this area (they were the trials meta-analysed in the Cochrane review), and all found lower pain scores with venepuncture.
- Study 4 (Eriksson et al.) is a 2×2 factorial design, so you may want to extract only the "no glucose" arms if you want a "pure" VP vs. HL comparison uncontaminated by a co-intervention.
- Studies 6 and 7 focus more on physiological/glucose-monitoring outcomes rather than validated pain scales — worth checking eligibility against your review's outcome criteria.
- I was not able to fully verify from search alone whether all of these strictly enrolled *only* full-term infants (versus mixed-gestation samples) — I'd recommend pulling full texts to confirm inclusion criteria before final inclusion in your synthesis.
