Based on the evidence from primary randomized controlled trials, venepuncture is generally considered superior to a heel lance for blood sampling in full-term neonates when performed by trained practitioners. Trials consistently show that it is both less painful for the baby and more effective at securing a successful sample.

Here is a breakdown of the specific findings from the primary literature.

## Pain Reduction

Across various validated neonatal pain scales, venepuncture consistently produces significantly lower pain scores than heel lancing:

* **Neonatal Infant Pain Scale (NIPS):** Venepuncture yields lower NIPS scores during routine blood sampling than a heel stick (Shah et al., 1997).
* **Premature Infant Pain Profile (PIPP):** When no sweet solution is given, neonates undergoing venepuncture have significantly lower mean PIPP scores compared to those receiving a heel lance (Eriksson et al., 1999).
* **Neonatal Facial Coding System (NFCS):** Venepuncture results in a considerably lower median NFCS score compared to heel lancing, even before analgesic solutions like sucrose are introduced (Ogawa, 2005).

## Crying Time and Efficiency

The mechanical trauma of squeezing the heel to extract blood typically makes the heel lance a lengthier and more distressing process.

* **Duration of Crying:** Neonates undergoing venepuncture exhibit significantly shorter crying times. For example, one trial found that without glucose, mean crying time dropped from 57.3 seconds for a heel stick to 26.8 seconds for venepuncture (Eriksson et al., 1999).
* **Procedure Efficacy:** Venepuncture frequently results in a faster total procedure time and fewer repeated skin punctures to obtain the necessary blood volume (Ogawa, 2005).

## The Role of Sweet Solutions

While oral sucrose or glucose provides a well-documented analgesic effect for neonatal procedures, venepuncture paired with a sweet solution remains the optimal combination. Even when sucrose is administered, infants receiving a heel lance still record higher pain scores than those undergoing venepuncture *without* sucrose (Ogawa, 2005). However, providing a sweet solution before a skin puncture reduces the pain gap between the two methods (Eriksson et al., 1999).
