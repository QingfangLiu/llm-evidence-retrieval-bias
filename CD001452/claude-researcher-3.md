## Summary

The core body of primary research directly comparing venepuncture and heel lance for blood sampling in full-term neonates consists of a small set of randomised (or quasi-randomised) controlled trials, most conducted between 1997 and 2009, plus a smaller number of more recent related trials. These are the studies underpinning the well-known Cochrane review by Shah & Ohlsson (which itself is a synthesis, not a primary study, so it's excluded from your list per your criteria). Across these trials, venepuncture — when performed by a skilled operator — was generally found to be less painful and more likely to succeed on the first attempt than heel lance, though study sizes were mostly small and heterogeneous in their pain-scoring tools.

## Primary studies (venepuncture vs. heel lance, full-term/term neonates)

1. **Shah VS, Taddio A, Bennett S, Speidel BD.** Neonatal pain response to heel stick vs. venepuncture for routine blood sampling. *Archives of Disease in Childhood Fetal and Neonatal Edition.* 1997;77(2):F143–144.

2. **Larsson BA, Tannfeldt G, Lagercrantz H, Olsson GL.** Venipuncture is more effective and less painful than heel lancing for blood tests in neonates. *Pediatrics.* 1998;101(5):882–886.

3. **Eriksson M, Gradin M, Schollin J.** Oral glucose and venepuncture reduce blood sampling pain in newborns. *Early Human Development.* 1999;55(3):211–218.

4. **Logan PW.** Venepuncture versus heel prick for the collection of the newborn screening test. *Australian Journal of Advanced Nursing.* 1999;17(1):30–36.

5. **Kvist LJ, Jonsson K, Tornestrand BM, Edwinson Mansson M.** Can venepuncture reduce the pain of neonatal PKU-sampling? A randomised study. *Vård i Norden.* 2002;22(3):27–30.

6. **Ogawa S, Ogihara T, Fujiwara E, Ito K, Nakano M, Nakayama S, et al.** Venepuncture is preferable to heel lance for blood sampling in term neonates. *Archives of Disease in Childhood Fetal and Neonatal Edition.* 2005;90(5):F432–436.

7. **Saththasivam P, Umadevan D, Ramli N, Voralu K, Naing NN, Ilias MI, Shuib N, Tan BG, van Rostenberghe H.** Venipuncture versus heel prick for blood glucose monitoring in neonates. *Singapore Medical Journal.* 2009;50(10):1004–1007.
