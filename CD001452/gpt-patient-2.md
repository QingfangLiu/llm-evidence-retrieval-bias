## Bottom line

For a **full-term newborn who needs more than a single drop of blood**, the individual comparative studies generally found that **venepuncture caused less immediate pain than heel lancing**. Several also found that venepuncture required fewer repeat punctures and, when performed by experienced neonatal staff, could obtain the sample faster.

The important exception was blood-glucose testing requiring only one drop: one study found no significant pain difference after oral dextrose and found the heel prick substantially faster. Thus, “better” depends on the required sample volume, the test being performed, use of sucrose/glucose or other comfort measures, and the clinician’s experience.

## Interpretation for a parent

**Venepuncture is probably preferable when:**

* A moderate-sized blood sample is required.
* An experienced neonatal nurse, phlebotomist, or clinician is available.
* The alternative heel collection would require squeezing the heel, prolonged collection, or several punctures.

**Heel lance may be preferable when:**

* Only one small capillary drop is required, as with some bedside glucose measurements.
* The specific laboratory or newborn-screening test requires a capillary heel specimen.
* Suitable venous access or an experienced venepuncture operator is unavailable.

Sweet-tasting solutions altered the comparison: glucose eliminated the statistically significant difference in one trial, whereas another trial still found venepuncture less painful even when the heel-lance group received sucrose. ([PubMed][3])

The studies mainly evaluated **immediate pain, crying, sampling time, and repeat punctures**. They were not large enough to establish meaningful differences in rare complications or long-term outcomes. Many were small, relatively old, and involved specially trained personnel, so results may not be identical when venepuncture is attempted by a less experienced operator.

A useful question for the treating team is: **“What volume and type of sample is required, and who would perform the venepuncture?”**
