I'll search for primary research studies comparing venipuncture and heel lance (heel stick) methods for blood sampling in full-term newborns.Based on this search, here are the primary studies (individual trials, not reviews) that directly compare venipuncture with heel lance for blood sampling in full-term newborns:

**What the evidence generally shows:** Across these trials, venipuncture was associated with less pain (lower pain scale scores, shorter crying) and, in several studies, better sample adequacy/fewer repeat pricks compared with heel lance — though results depend on operator skill with venipuncture.

Key trials:

- A study of 120 healthy, full-term infants tested for phenylketonuria randomized to venipuncture (n=50), heel lance with a small lancet (n=50), or heel lance with a large lancet (n=20), assessing pain reactions with the Neonatal Facial Coding System, concluded that venipuncture is more effective and less painful than heel lancing for blood tests in neonates. (Larsson et al., Pediatrics, 1998)

- A randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of 100 healthy full-term newborns being screened for inborn errors of metabolism, randomly allocated to venepuncture or heel lance with or without sucrose, found that without sucrose the neonatal facial coding system pain score was higher in the heel lance group than the venepuncture group during blood sampling. (Ogawa et al., Archives of Disease in Childhood – Fetal & Neonatal Edition, 2005)

- Shah VS, Taddio A, Bennett S, Speidel BD. "Neonatal pain response to heel stick vs. venepuncture for routine blood sampling." *Archives of Disease in Childhood – Fetal & Neonatal Edition*, 1997;77(2):F143-4.

- Eriksson M, Gradin M, Schollin J. "Oral glucose and venepuncture reduce blood sampling pain in newborns." *Early Human Development*, 1999;55(3):211-8.

- Kvist LJ, Jonsson K, Tornestrand BM, Edwinson Mansson M. "Can venepuncture reduce the pain of neonatal PKU-sampling? A randomised study." *Vård i Norden*, 2002;22:27-30.

- Logan PW. "Venepuncture versus heel prick for the collection of the newborn screening test." *Australian Journal of Advanced Nursing*, 1999;17:30-6.
