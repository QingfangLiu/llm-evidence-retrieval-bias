Here are individual primary studies (not reviews) comparing venipuncture with heel lance/prick for blood sampling in full-term neonates. All are randomized controlled trials that directly measured pain and/or procedural outcomes:

**1. Larsson et al. (1998), *Pediatrics*** — 120 healthy full-term infants randomized to venipuncture (dorsum of hand), small-lancet heel stick, or large-lancet heel stick for PKU screening. Pain assessed with the Neonatal Facial Coding System (NFCS) and cry duration. Venipuncture produced significantly lower pain scores than either heel-lance method and had a higher first-attempt success rate.

**2. Shah, Taddio, Bennett & Speidel (1997), *Arch Dis Child Fetal Neonatal Ed*** — 27 term infants randomized to venipuncture or heel stick for routine blood sampling. Pain scored with the Neonatal Infant Pain Scale (NIPS) by nurses; maternal anxiety and pain perception also recorded. NIPS scores were higher (more pain) in the heel-stick group.

**3. Ogawa et al. (2005), *Arch Dis Child Fetal Neonatal Ed*** — Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of 100 full-term newborns undergoing metabolic screening, allocated to venipuncture or heel lance, each with or without oral sucrose. Pain measured via NFCS and crying. Without sucrose, heel lance produced significantly higher pain scores than venipuncture.

**4. Logan (1999), *Australian Journal of Advanced Nursing*** — 36 infants compared heel prick vs. venipuncture (dorsal hand vein) for collection of the Newborn Screening Test, performed by midwives at two centers. Outcomes included collection time and crying parameters (amount, percentage, latency, duration of first cry). Venipuncture showed significantly better results on all crying measures and shorter collection time.

**5. Saththasivam et al. (2009), *Singapore Medical Journal*** — 66 term neonates randomized to venipuncture or heel prick for blood glucose monitoring. Outcomes included NFCS score, cry latency/duration, procedure duration, and number of skin punctures needed.

**6. Eriksson, Gradin & Schollin (1999), *Early Human Development*** — Compared venipuncture and heel lance (with oral glucose as a co-intervention) in newborns, measuring pain-related behavioral/physiological responses during blood sampling.

A few notes for your own reading:
- Several of these are small trials (n = 27–120), often from a single center, so effect sizes should be interpreted cautiously.
- All measure pain using validated but different tools (NFCS, NIPS, cry parameters), which limits direct comparison across studies — this is why the Cochrane systematic review exists, but you specifically asked for primary trials rather than that synthesis.
