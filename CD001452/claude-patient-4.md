Here's what the evidence looks like when you go straight to the primary research (not reviews) comparing venepuncture (vein draw) with heel lance in full-term newborns.

**What the studies generally found:** across these trials, venepuncture done by a trained/skilled person consistently produced lower pain scores than heel lance, and required fewer repeat pricks. One trial found maternal anxiety was somewhat higher during venepuncture. A trial specifically testing sucrose alongside each method found venepuncture was still less painful than heel lance, and adding sucrose reduced pain further with either method.

A caveat worth knowing as a parent: these are all small studies (dozens to ~120 babies each), pain was measured with several different scoring tools, and results depend a lot on how skilled and practiced the person doing the procedure is. Heel lance remains far more common in practice partly because it doesn't require a skilled phlebotomist and doesn't risk the babies' limited usable veins — that's a practical tradeoff worth discussing with your care team rather than something the pain-score data alone settles.

**Primary studies (individual RCTs, no reviews):**

1. Larsson BA, Tannfeldt G, Lagercrantz H, Olsson GL. Venipuncture is more effective and less painful than heel lancing for blood tests in neonates. *Pediatrics*. 1998;101(5):882-886.

2. Larsson BA, Tannfeldt G, Lagercrantz H, Olsson GL. Alleviation of the pain of venepuncture in neonates. *Acta Paediatrica*. 1998;87(7):774-779.

3. Shah VS, Taddio A, Bennett S, Speidel BD. Neonatal pain response to heel stick vs. venepuncture for routine blood sampling. *Archives of Disease in Childhood – Fetal and Neonatal Edition*. 1997;77(2):F143-F144.

4. Ogawa S, Ogihara T, Fujiwara E, Ito K, Nakano M, Nakayama S, et al. Venepuncture is preferable to heel lance for blood sampling in term neonates. *Archives of Disease in Childhood – Fetal and Neonatal Edition*. 2005;90(5):F432-F436.

These four (really three research groups, since the two Larsson 1998 papers report different outcomes from the same trial) are the individual randomized trials most commonly cited as the direct head-to-head comparisons of venepuncture vs. heel lance specifically in full-term neonates.
