## Primary studies: advanced vs. standard sperm selection in ICSI/IVF

### PICSI / hyaluronan-binding selection

- **Miller et al. 2019, *The Lancet* (HABSelect)** — RCT, 2,772 couples, 16 UK clinics. Term live birth: 27.4% PICSI vs. 25.2% standard ICSI (OR 1.12, 95% CI 0.95–1.34, p=0.18) — **not significant**. Miscarriage significantly *lower* with PICSI (OR 0.61, 95% CI 0.43–0.84). Compared with ICSI, PICSI does not significantly improve term livebirth rates. The wider use of PICSI, therefore, is not recommended at present.
- **Ainsworth/Fair et al. 2022, *Human Reproduction*** (HABSelect DNA-quality substudy) — secondary analysis of the HABSelect cohort; found HA-based sperm selection improved live birth outcomes specifically among older couples, tied to sperm DNA quality.
- **Alegre et al. 2025, *Biomedicines*** — Triple-blinded RCT comparing PICSI vs. ICSI on embryo quality, cumulative pregnancy rate, and live birth rate (cited in Cochrane CENTRAL listing above).
- **Korosi et al. 2017, *Eur Rev Med Pharmacol Sci*** — RCT of PICSI with oral myo-inositol pretreatment in oligoasthenoteratozoospermic men.

### MACS (magnetic-activated cell sorting)

- **[Author not shown] 2021, *J Assist Reprod Genet*** (PubMed 34076869) — RCT, 302 patients with abnormal sperm DNA fragmentation, 4 arms (DGC, testicular sperm, PICSI, MACS). ### IMSI (high-magnification morphological selection)

- **Leandri et al. 2013, *Andrology*** — Multicentric RCT, 255 couples, first ART attempt. IMSI did not provide any significant improvement in the clinical outcomes compared with ICSI neither for implantation (24% vs. 23%), nor clinical pregnancy (31% vs. 33%) nor live birth rates (27% vs. 30%).
- **Gatimel et al. 2016, *J Assist Reprod Genet*** — Study after two successive ICSI failures. IMSI did not significantly improve clinical outcomes compared with ICSI, for implantation (12 vs 10%), clinical pregnancy (23 vs 21%), or live birth rates (20 vs 19%).
- **Balaban et al. (year per citation above)** — Comparative study in repeated implantation failure (RIF) patients; live birth significantly higher with IMSI in teratozoospermic RIF men (38% vs. 20%).
- **[Author] , *Fertil Steril / RBM Online*, PMC3156729** — Prospective comparative study of IMSI in women with repeated implantation failure; trends toward lower miscarriage and higher live birth with IMSI, not statistically significant.
- **[Study, 2016–2017] on IMSI vs ICSI first cycle** — Open prospective study, 446 ICSI vs. 79 IMSI cycles (PMC8560014); ICSI statistically outperformed IMSI on pregnancy/live-birth in first-attempt patients.
- **Cohort study, *Fertil Steril* (PubMed 23357455)** — 1,891 IVF-ICSI vs. 577 IVF-IMSI cycles; comparable first-cycle results, IMSI favored in second cycle after ICSI failure.

### Note on evidence pattern
Across all four techniques, individual RCTs are mostly **negative or neutral for primary clinical endpoints** (live birth, clinical pregnancy) in unselected populations — PICSI (HABSelect), IMSI (Leandri, Gatimel), and microfluidic sorting (Quinn) all failed their primary comparative endpoint against standard selection. Positive signals cluster in **secondary outcomes** (lower miscarriage with PICSI) or **selected subgroups** (elevated sperm DNA fragmentation, older maternal age, repeated implantation failure, teratozoospermia), where several studies (MACS RCTs, IMSI in RIF/teratozoospermia, microfluidic sorting in high-DSB patients) do show benefit.
