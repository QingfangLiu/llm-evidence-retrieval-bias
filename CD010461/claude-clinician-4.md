Here's a curated set of primary studies (RCTs and prospective/comparative studies) on advanced sperm selection techniques vs. standard selection (density gradient centrifugation/swim-up + morphology-based ICSI) in IVF/ICSI, organized by technique. I've excluded reviews, meta-analyses, and guidelines as requested.

## Hyaluronic acid–based selection (PICSI)

**HABSelect (Lancet, 2019)** — the largest PICSI RCT to date. Of 2752 couples randomized, PICSI did not significantly increase the term livebirth rate compared with standard ICSI, but a significant decrease was observed in miscarriage rates among couples in the PICSI group. A related mechanistic follow-up analysis found older women randomized to the PICSI arm had live birth rates equal to younger women, likely due to better avoidance of sperm with damaged DNA, and RCTs including HABSelect have consistently shown reduced miscarriage in the PICSI arm.

**Alegre et al., Biomedicines 2025** — a triple-blinded prospective RCT conducted in an egg-donation population (removing female-factor bias). The trial tested whether PICSI increases live birth rate and improves IVF outcomes in couples in an oocyte donation program.

**PICSI vs. MACS vs. DGC vs. testicular sperm (RCT, 302 patients with abnormal SDF)** — this RCT randomized patients into DGC, testicular sperm, PICSI, and MACS groups; the testicular-sperm group had significantly lower cleavage and blastulation rates than PICSI, DGC, or MACS, and the highest pregnancy rate was in the PICSI group (69.6%) versus the lowest in DGC (51.4%), p=0.025.

**PICSI vs. MACS (prospective RCT, n=413, abnormal SDF)** — 413 ICSI cases with abnormal sperm DNA fragmentation (>20.3% by TUNEL) were randomized to PICSI or MACS; there were no significant differences between the two groups in pre-implantation embryological data, implantation, or clinical outcomes.

## Magnetic-activated cell sorting (MACS)

**MACS in IUI (Cells, 2022, RCT)** — a randomized trial of MACS sperm selection specifically in donor IUI cycles (González-Ravina et al.), referenced in the search results as a clinical trial comparator to the ICSI-focused MACS literature above.

## Microfluidic sperm sorting

**Comparative RCT, "unselected" IVF-ICSI population (PMC10145657, 2023)** — 239 couples were randomized 1:1 to standard density-gradient sperm processing (n=119) or microfluidic sperm preparation (ZyMot, n=120), all undergoing ICSI cycles.

**Microfluidic sorting in high sperm DNA double-strand breaks (retrospective cohort, ScienceDirect)** — in 28 infertile couples with elevated DNA double-strand breaks, couples underwent a first ICSI cycle with conventional sperm preparation and a second cycle with the ZyMot microfluidic device; biochemical pregnancy rate rose 28.3%, clinical pregnancy rate rose 35.6%, and live births rose 35.3% with microfluidic sorting versus the first cycle.

**Microfluidic sperm sorting for embryo quality (registered RCT, NCT03085433)** — couples undergoing IVF were randomized to microfluidic sperm sorting (space-constrained chip using laminar flow, no centrifugation) versus standard processing prior to IVF/ICSI fertilization.

## Intracytoplasmic morphologically selected sperm injection (IMSI)

**Leandri et al., Andrology 2013 (multicentric RCT, n=255)** — in a prospective randomized trial of couples with severe male infertility, IMSI did not significantly improve implantation (24% vs 23%), clinical pregnancy (31% vs 33%), or live birth rates (27% vs 30%) compared with ICSI, regardless of sperm DNA fragmentation, nuclear immaturity, or morphology.

**Antinori et al., Reprod Biomed Online 2008 (prospective RCT, n=446)** — 446 couples with severe oligoasthenoteratozoospermia were randomized to conventional ICSI (n=219) or IMSI (n=227) using ×6600 magnification sperm selection, comparing pregnancy, miscarriage, and implantation rates; patients with two or more previous failed attempts benefited most from IMSI.

**IMSI in unexplained infertility (Fertility and Sterility, prospective RCT)** — 36 cycles per arm (ICSI vs IMSI) were randomized in couples with unexplained infertility; IMSI resulted in higher implantation and pregnancy rates than conventional ICSI, with a roughly 4-fold increase in pregnancy rate.

**IMSI in advanced maternal age (prospective RCT)** — 33 cycles per arm were randomized (ICSI vs IMSI) in women ≥37 years old; IMSI cycles showed significantly higher implantation and pregnancy rates, with roughly a 9-fold increase in clinical pregnancy rate.

**IMSI after two failed ICSI cycles (J Assist Reprod Genet, Gatimel et al.)** — in this study, IMSI did not significantly improve implantation (12% vs 10%), clinical pregnancy (23% vs 21%), or live birth rates (20% vs 19%) compared with ICSI in patients with prior ICSI failures.

---

**Pattern across techniques:** Results are mixed and technique/population-dependent. PICSI's largest, most rigorous trial (HABSelect) showed a miscarriage benefit but not a live-birth benefit; smaller PICSI/MACS comparisons show inconsistent pregnancy-rate differences. IMSI RCTs are split — some show large benefits in specific subgroups (unexplained infertility, advanced maternal age, repeated failure), while the largest multicentric RCT found no benefit for a first ART attempt. Microfluidic sorting has fewer large RCTs so far, with promising signals in observational/smaller randomized comparisons, particularly in men with elevated sperm DNA damage.
