## Bottom line

Across the individual primary studies, **advanced sperm selection has not consistently increased live-birth rates compared with standard sperm preparation and conventional ICSI**.

The clearest findings are:

* **PICSI/hyaluronic-acid selection:** The largest randomized trial found no significant live-birth benefit, although miscarriage was reduced.
* **IMSI/high-magnification selection:** Several randomized trials found no meaningful improvement in pregnancy or live birth.
* **Microfluidic selection, including ZyMōt/Fertile Chip:** Small randomized trials conflict—one reported improved live birth, while others, including a 2026 trial, did not.
* **MACS:** Some small studies in patients with elevated sperm DNA fragmentation reported better pregnancy outcomes, but live-birth evidence remains inconsistent.
* **Zeta-potential and birefringence selection:** Positive results come from small or subgroup-based studies and have not been adequately replicated.

I treated live birth as the most important effectiveness outcome. Every cited item below is an individual primary study—not a review, guideline, committee opinion, editorial or comment.

## 1. Hyaluronic-acid binding: PICSI/HA-ICSI

### Miller et al., 2019 — HABSelect trial

**Design:** Multicentre randomized trial; 2,772 couples undergoing ICSI.

**Results:** Term live birth occurred in 27.4% with PICSI and 25.2% with standard ICSI—an absolute difference of 2.2 percentage points that was not statistically significant. Clinical pregnancy was also similar, 35.2% versus 35.7%. Miscarriage per randomized couple was lower with PICSI, 4.3% versus 7.0%, but this did not translate into a statistically significant live-birth improvement. This is the largest and most influential trial of any advanced sperm-selection technique. PMID 30712901; DOI 10.1016/S0140-6736(18)32989-1. ([PubMed][1])

### Worrilow et al., 2013

**Design:** Multicentre, double-blind randomized trial. Of 802 consented couples, randomization focused on 318 couples whose initial hyaluronan-binding score was 65% or lower.

**Results:** Clinical pregnancy was not significantly higher with HA selection. In the subgroup with low hyaluronan binding, pregnancy loss was lower—for example, 3.3% versus 15.1% in one prespecified stratum. The trial stopped early for financial reasons and was not powered for live birth. PMID 23203216; DOI 10.1093/humrep/des417. ([PubMed][2])

### Majumdar and Majumdar, 2013

**Design:** Prospective randomized trial of 156 couples with unexplained infertility and normal semen parameters.

**Results:** Fertilization, embryo quality and clinical pregnancy were essentially identical. Live birth was 31% with PICSI versus 26.3% with standard ICSI; the study was too small for this difference to be conclusive. Pregnancy loss was numerically lower, 12% versus 25%, but not statistically significant. PMID 24085466. ([PMC][3])

**Interpretation:** PICSI may reduce miscarriage in some settings, but the large HABSelect trial does not demonstrate that routine PICSI increases the chance of taking home a baby.

## 2. Microfluidic sperm selection

### Yetkinel et al., 2019

**Design:** Randomized trial of 122 couples with unexplained infertility; microfluidic selection versus conventional swim-up before ICSI.

**Results:** Live birth was 38.3% versus 36.2%, and clinical pregnancy was 48.3% versus 44.8%; neither difference was statistically significant. More grade-1 embryos were produced with microfluidics. PMID 30542782. ([PubMed][4])

### Saylan and Ata, 2022

**Design:** Randomized study of 128 couples undergoing ICSI for male-factor infertility; Fertile Chip versus swim-up.

**Results:** Live birth was significantly higher with the microfluidic device, 46.8% versus 25%. Clinical pregnancy was 59.4% versus 35.9%. Fertilization and overall embryo-quality measures were not different. This was a small, single-centre trial and its unusually large clinical effect requires independent replication. PMID 35263457; DOI 10.1111/and.14415. ([PubMed][5])

### Huong et al., 2026

**Design:** Randomized trial of 119 couples comparing microfluidic selection with density-gradient centrifugation.

**Results:** Microfluidics reduced sperm DNA fragmentation more effectively, particularly when baseline fragmentation was elevated. Nevertheless, there were no significant differences in fertilization, embryo quality, clinical pregnancy, ongoing pregnancy or live birth. PMID 41757843; DOI 10.5935/1518-0557.20250190. ([PubMed][6])

### Thanapongpibul et al., 2026

**Design:** Randomized sibling-oocyte study involving 55 ICSI cycles with abnormal semen parameters.

**Results:** Microfluidic selection increased fertilization, 82.3% versus 74.7%. This embryo-level study was not designed to establish whether the technique increases pregnancy or live birth because embryos from the two methods were generated within the same patients. PMID 42230899; DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-55209-1. ([PubMed][7])

**Interpretation:** Microfluidic devices often improve sperm DNA-fragmentation measurements and occasionally embryo outcomes, but randomized evidence for higher live birth is conflicting.

## 3. IMSI: ultra-high-magnification morphology selection

### Leandri et al., 2013

**Design:** Multicentre randomized trial of 255 couples having their first ART attempt for male-factor infertility.

**Results:** IMSI did not improve implantation, 24% versus 23%; clinical pregnancy, 31% versus 33%; or live birth, 27% versus 30%. No benefit appeared when patients were stratified by sperm DNA fragmentation, nuclear maturity or morphology. PMID 23788532; DOI 10.1111/j.2047-2927.2013.00104.x. ([PubMed][8])

### La Sala et al., 2015

**Design:** Triple-blind randomized trial in first ICSI cycles for male-factor infertility; stopped early after 121 participants because of concerns about IMSI efficacy and a lower fertilization rate at interim analysis.

**Results:** Live birth per initiated cycle was 18.8% with IMSI versus 15.1% with conventional ICSI, not statistically significant. Clinical pregnancy was also not significantly different. ([PMC][9])

### Shakeri et al., 2026

**Design:** Double-blind randomized trial of 100 couples with severe teratozoospermia.

**Results:** No significant differences were found in fertilization, clinical or ongoing pregnancy, miscarriage or live birth. Live birth was numerically 30.23% versus 15.55%, but the estimate was imprecise and not statistically significant. DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-57250-6. ([Nature][10])

### El Khattabi et al., 2013

**Design:** Prospective but nonrandomized study of 478 patients.

**Results:** Among patients with severe teratozoospermia, live birth was 38% with IMSI versus 20% with ICSI. However, IMSI provided no benefit in patients with repeated ICSI failure without severe male-factor infertility, 21% versus 22%. Because treatment was not randomized, patient selection and other differences could explain the favorable severe-teratozoospermia result. PMID 23548938. ([PubMed][11])

**Interpretation:** The randomized trials do not establish a live-birth advantage for IMSI. Positive results are more prominent in nonrandomized studies or selected subgroups.

## 4. Magnetic-activated cell sorting: MACS

MACS attempts to remove sperm displaying apoptotic markers, usually using annexin-V magnetic beads.

### Troya et al., 2015

**Design:** Prospective randomized three-group study of 136 ICSI patients assigned to standard ICSI, PICSI or MACS.

**Results:** Clinical pregnancy was 58.1% with MACS, 40.4% with PICSI and 27.3% with standard ICSI. Fertilization and the number of available embryos were similar. Live birth was not reported, making it uncertain whether the clinical-pregnancy difference translated into more births. PMID 27206090; DOI 10.5935/1518-0557.20150015. ([PubMed][12])

### Mei et al., 2022

**Design:** Randomized study of approximately 80 patients with sperm DNA-fragmentation index of at least 30%.

**Results:** First-transfer live birth was 63.2% with MACS versus 53.9% with standard preparation, and cumulative live birth was 79.5% versus 70.7%. Neither difference was statistically significant. Clinical pregnancy, implantation and miscarriage were also similar. PMID 34708718; DOI 10.4103/aja202161. ([PubMed][13])

### Hozyen et al., 2022

**Design:** Randomized four-group trial of 302 ICSI patients with abnormal sperm DNA fragmentation: density gradient, testicular sperm, PICSI or MACS.

**Results:** Ongoing pregnancy was higher with PICSI, 62.8%, and MACS, 62%, than with density-gradient preparation, 45.8%. Miscarriage did not differ. Live-birth results were not provided in the abstract. PMID 34076869; DOI 10.1007/s43032-021-00642-y. ([PubMed][14])

### Gil Juliá et al., 2022

**Design:** Large retrospective multicentre study of donor-oocyte ICSI cycles—1,240 MACS cycles and 40,157 standard-processing cycles.

**Results:** MACS did not improve cumulative live birth after accounting for the number of embryos transferred or number of donor oocytes used. The donor-oocyte setting reduces—but does not eliminate—variation from egg quality. PMID 35184950; DOI 10.1016/j.rbmo.2021.09.024. ([PubMed][15])

**Interpretation:** MACS is biologically plausible and may help selected patients with high sperm DNA fragmentation, but the available clinical trials do not yet demonstrate a reliable live-birth advantage.

## 5. Zeta-potential selection

### Nasr-Esfahani et al., 2016

**Design:** Double-blind randomized trial of 203 ICSI cycles with male-factor infertility; density gradient plus zeta selection versus density gradient alone.

**Results:** Clinical pregnancy was 39.2% with zeta selection versus 21.8% with standard preparation. Chemical pregnancy and top-quality embryo rates were also higher. Fertilization was unchanged. The study additionally reported a different offspring sex ratio between groups, an unexpected finding that increases the importance of independent replication. PMID 27441060; DOI 10.22074/ijfs.2016.4917. ([PMC][16])

### Ziarati et al., 2020

**Design:** Randomized single-blind trial involving approximately 205 evaluable couples with male-factor infertility.

**Results:** Chemical and clinical pregnancy and several blastocyst-quality measures were higher with zeta selection, but fertilization was not improved. The publication did not provide strong replicated evidence for cumulative live birth. PMID 31606966; DOI 10.22074/cellj.2020.6525. ([PubMed][17])

**Interpretation:** The results are promising but come from a small number of trials in closely related clinical settings. Zeta selection cannot currently be considered a proven method for increasing live birth.

## 6. Sperm-head birefringence/PolScope selection

### Gianaroli et al., 2008

**Design:** Prospective randomized study: 112 couples received birefringence-selected sperm and 119 underwent conventional ICSI.

**Results:** Overall fertilization and cleavage were similar. Higher clinical and ongoing pregnancy and implantation were reported only in severe male-factor subgroups, including immotile or testicular sperm. Live birth was not the main reported outcome. PMID 18023436; DOI 10.1016/j.fertnstert.2007.05.078. ([PubMed][18])

### Ghosh et al., 2012

**Design:** Randomized study of 96 cycles involving azoospermia or complete asthenozoospermia.

**Results:** Birefringence selection improved embryo and pregnancy outcomes in the completely asthenozoospermic subgroup but not clearly in the azoospermic subgroup. The small specialized population and absence of a definitive live-birth endpoint limit applicability. PMID 22369032; DOI 10.1111/j.1439-0272.2011.01258.x. ([PubMed][19])

## Practical interpretation for an IVF/ICSI patient

The evidence does **not** support assuming that an advanced method is better simply because it produces sperm with lower DNA fragmentation or more high-grade embryos. Those are intermediate outcomes; the important question is whether the method increases **cumulative live birth per egg-retrieval cycle**.

The best-supported conclusion is:

* Routine PICSI, IMSI, microfluidics or MACS has not been proven to increase live birth for an unselected IVF/ICSI population.
* A possible benefit in narrowly defined groups—such as very high sperm DNA fragmentation, severe teratozoospermia, completely immotile sperm or repeated treatment failure—remains plausible but uncertain.
* When a clinic recommends an add-on, it is reasonable to ask for its live-birth results in patients matching your specific diagnosis, rather than sperm-quality or embryo-grading results alone.
