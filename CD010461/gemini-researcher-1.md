Here is a curated selection of notable primary studies (randomized controlled trials and prospective/retrospective cohort studies) directly comparing advanced sperm selection techniques to standard methods (like density gradient centrifugation [DGC] or swim-up). Per your request, all systematic reviews, meta-analyses, and secondary literature have been strictly excluded.

## Hyaluronic Acid Binding (PICSI)

Physiological intracytoplasmic sperm injection (PICSI) selects sperm based on their ability to bind to hyaluronic acid, mimicking natural selection in the cumulus complex.

* **The HABSelect Trial (Miller et al., 2019):** This was a large, parallel, two-group, multicenter RCT comparing PICSI against standard ICSI in 2,752 couples. The primary outcome was full-term live birth. The study found that PICSI did *not* significantly improve term live birth rates compared to standard ICSI, though secondary analysis noted a reduction in miscarriage rates in the PICSI arm.
* **Mechanistic Follow-up to HABSelect (West et al., 2022):** A subsequent analysis of the HABSelect trial data looked at maternal age and sperm DNA quality. It demonstrated that using hyaluronic acid selection equalized the live birth outcomes among older couples to match those of younger couples, suggesting the intervention effectively avoided sperm with damaged DNA.

## Magnetic-Activated Cell Sorting (MACS)

MACS uses annexin V-conjugated magnetic beads to identify and filter out apoptotic sperm (which express phosphatidylserine on their external membrane).

* **Pacheco et al. (2020):** This retrospective cohort study analyzed 724 ART cycles involving patients with high levels of sperm DNA fragmentation (SDF). Cycles were split between MACS applied after DGC versus DGC alone. The MACS group demonstrated significantly lower miscarriage rates in autologous cycles and increased live-birth rates across both autologous and oocyte-donation cycles, suggesting MACS is beneficial for targeting high SDF.

## Microfluidic Sperm Sorting

Microfluidic devices (such as the ZyMōt chip) use micro-channels to isolate highly motile sperm without the need for potentially damaging centrifugation.

* **Leisinger et al. (2021):** This prospective, randomized, sibling-oocyte study evaluated 86 couples in a general unselected IVF population. Oocytes were split in half and injected with sperm processed via either a microfluidic device or standard processing. Interestingly, in this unselected population, there were no significant differences in fertilization, blastocyst utilization, ploidy, or clinical pregnancy rates between the microfluidic and control methods.

## Intracytoplasmic Morphologically Selected Sperm Injection (IMSI)

IMSI evaluates sperm under ultra-high magnification (≥6000x compared to ~400x for standard ICSI) to identify subtle subcellular organelle anomalies, like sperm head vacuoles.

* **Antinori et al. (2008):** This prospective randomized trial evaluated 446 couples with severe oligoasthenoteratozoospermia and repeated implantation failures. Comparing standard ICSI to IMSI, the researchers found that IMSI yielded a significantly higher clinical pregnancy rate (39.2% vs. 26.5%) and a reduced miscarriage rate (17.4% vs. 37.5%). Patients with multiple previous failures benefited the most from this ultra-high magnification selection.

## Surface Charge Selection (Zeta Potential)

This technique exploits the fact that mature sperm with intact chromatin carry a highly negative surface electrical charge.

* **Duarte et al. (2017):** This prospective study evaluated the Zeta potential technique on non-normospermic samples to see if it reduced DNA fragmentation index (DFI) and improved embryo development. Comparing DGC alone against DGC combined with Zeta potential, the combined approach successfully isolated sperm with properly compacted chromatin, which favored better embryo quality and higher pregnancy rates.
