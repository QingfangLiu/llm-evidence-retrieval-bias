## Bottom line

The primary human studies do **not show a consistent improvement in live-birth rates from routinely adding an advanced sperm-selection technique to IVF/ICSI**. The clearest evidence is for hyaluronan-binding/PICSI: a large randomized trial found fewer miscarriages but no statistically significant improvement in term live birth. For IMSI and microfluidic selection, randomized trials have generally been neutral. MACS, Zeta selection, birefringence, and zona-binding have encouraging individual studies, especially in selected male-factor populations, but the results have not been consistently replicated in large, well-powered trials.

All studies cited below are individual primary research reports—not reviews, guidelines, opinions, or editorials. Search is current through **July 17, 2026**.

## Other relevant primary studies

**Birefringence selection:** Gianaroli et al. randomized 231 couples. Overall fertilization and cleavage were similar, but clinical pregnancy, ongoing pregnancy, and implantation were higher in a severe-male-factor subgroup. Live-birth evidence remained limited. ([PubMed][15])

**Additional IMSI trial:** Balaban et al. randomized 168 cycles. Overall differences in implantation, clinical pregnancy, and live birth—43.7% versus 38.3%—were not statistically significant, although implantation improved in the severe-male-factor subgroup. ([RBMO Journal][16])

**Newest randomized sibling-oocyte microfluidic study:** Thanapongpibul et al., 2026, randomized oocytes from 55 couples rather than couples themselves. Fertilization improved, **82.3% vs 74.7%**, but blastocyst formation, good-quality blastocysts, and euploidy did not. It could not assess whether more couples ultimately had live births. ([PubMed][17])

**Large recent observational microfluidic study:** Balakier et al., 2026, compared 1,091 ZyMōt cycles with 1,176 density-gradient cycles and reported higher fertilization, blastulation, clinical pregnancy, and live birth with ZyMōt. Because sperm preparation was not randomized, differences in patients, treatment periods, laboratory protocols, or embryo-transfer selection could account for some or all of the association. ([PubMed][18])

## What the primary evidence means

**For routine IVF/ICSI:** None of these techniques has repeatedly shown that it increases live birth in large randomized trials.

**PICSI:** This has the most reliable evidence. It appears reasonable to describe it as **not improving overall live birth, with a possible reduction in miscarriage**.

**Microfluidic devices such as ZyMōt/FertileChip:** They often produce sperm with less measured DNA fragmentation, but randomized studies have generally found **similar pregnancy and live-birth outcomes**. A benefit specifically among men with markedly elevated sperm DNA fragmentation remains possible but unproven.

**IMSI:** Positive findings are mainly from older or selected severe-male-factor/subgroup studies. Trials in broader or first-cycle populations have been neutral.

**MACS and Zeta:** These may be worth investigating in carefully defined male-factor or high-DNA-fragmentation situations, but the evidence consists largely of small trials and inconsistent live-birth reporting.

When discussing an add-on with the IVF clinic, the most useful question is: **“What is the live-birth rate per started retrieval or per randomized couple in patients with our exact sperm diagnosis—not just DNA-fragmentation reduction, embryo quality, or pregnancy per transfer?”**
