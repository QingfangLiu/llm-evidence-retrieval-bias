## Primary comparative studies

I used a targeted search of PubMed-indexed records and publisher pages through **July 17, 2026**. I included human IVF/ICSI studies comparing an advanced sperm-selection procedure with conventional density-gradient centrifugation, swim-up, or routine visual selection by motility/morphology. Studies reporting only semen or DNA-fragmentation outcomes were omitted unless they also reported pregnancy, miscarriage, or live birth.

This is a study-finding map rather than a PRISMA systematic search. **All studies listed below are primary research reports; no reviews, guidelines, opinions, or editorials are included.**

### Overall signal

Taken together, the individual studies do **not show a consistent, replicated live-birth advantage** for any advanced sperm-selection method in general IVF/ICSI populations.

The most definitive study is the 2,772-couple HABSelect trial: PICSI did not significantly improve term live birth, although miscarriage was reduced. For IMSI and microfluidic selection, larger or broader randomized trials have generally been null, while several smaller single-center trials in selected populations have reported substantial benefits. MACS was ineffective in a randomized donor-oocyte trial, but selected high-sperm-DNA-fragmentation studies have been more favorable. ([PubMed][1])

## Important nonrandomized comparative studies

These are useful for signal detection but are more susceptible to selection, temporal, and treatment-policy confounding.

| Study                                                                                | Technique/design                                                                           | Findings                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **El Khattabi et al., 2013**, *Fertility and Sterility*. PMID 23548938               | Prospective nonrandomized IMSI versus ICSI study; 478 patients                             | In severe teratozoospermia, live birth was **38% with IMSI versus 20% with ICSI**. ## Evidence-synthesis considerations

The most important design and analysis issues are:

1. **Randomization unit:** Several studies randomized oocytes rather than couples, but subsequently reported participant-level pregnancy outcomes.
2. **Postrandomization selection:** Some analyses included only participants reaching embryo transfer, euploid transfer, or another downstream milestone.
3. **Outcome denominator:** Pregnancy or live birth per transfer can favor an intervention that changes the probability of reaching transfer; per randomized couple or retrieval is generally more informative.
4. **Selected populations:** Positive studies frequently involved severe teratozoospermia, high sperm DNA fragmentation, advanced maternal age, or prior treatment failure. Their applicability to unselected patients is uncertain.
5. **Small-study effects:** Several positive trials reported very large treatment effects from fewer than 50 transfers per arm, while the largest pragmatic and multicenter trials were predominantly null.
6. **Multiplicity:** Subgroup and intermediate embryology outcomes were often numerous, without clear adjustment for multiple testing.

For a core evidence table focused on live birth, the highest-priority studies are **Miller 2019, Leandri 2013, Shakeri 2026, Yetkinel 2019, Aydın 2022, Huong 2026, Romany 2014, Ganeva 2024, and Yousefi 2025**.
