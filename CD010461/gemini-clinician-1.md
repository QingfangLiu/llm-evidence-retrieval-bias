As a clinician navigating the rapidly expanding options for IVF and ICSI, weighing the true clinical benefit of advanced sperm selection against conventional methods is a critical but often frustrating task. When we strip away the systematic reviews and look strictly at individual primary studies, the data on advanced techniques—like PICSI, IMSI, MACS, and microfluidics—presents a nuanced picture. While they generally do not show sweeping improvements in overall live birth rates for unselected populations, they often demonstrate specific benefits, such as reduced miscarriage rates or improved outcomes for men with high DNA fragmentation.

Here is a breakdown of the primary evidence for the most common advanced sperm selection techniques.

## Physiological ICSI (PICSI)

PICSI uses hyaluronic acid (HA) binding to select mature sperm, as only mature sperm develop HA receptors. The largest randomized trial to date, the HABSelect trial, found that compared to standard ICSI, PICSI did not significantly increase full-term live birth rates (Miller et al., 2019). However, the trial did demonstrate a significant decrease in miscarriage rates in the PICSI group, suggesting a potential benefit in sustaining pregnancies rather than initiating them (Miller et al., 2019).

## Intracytoplasmic Morphologically Selected Sperm Injection (IMSI)

IMSI utilizes ultra-high magnification (typically 6,000x) to select sperm devoid of microscopic vacuoles or structural defects. A multicentric randomized controlled trial evaluating IMSI versus standard ICSI in couples undergoing their first ART cycle found no significant differences in implantation, clinical pregnancy, or live birth rates (Leandri et al., 2013). The study concluded that the routine use of IMSI in the first ART cycle does not offer a clinical advantage over conventional ICSI, meaning it may be better reserved for cases of recurrent implantation failure (Leandri et al., 2013).

## Magnetic-Activated Cell Sorting (MACS)

MACS filters out apoptotic sperm—which express externalized phosphatidylserine—using annexin V-conjugated magnetic beads. A retrospective study of patients with high sperm DNA fragmentation undergoing ICSI found that utilizing MACS after density-gradient centrifugation yielded lower miscarriage rates and a significant increase in live birth rates compared to centrifugation alone (Pacheco et al., 2020). Similarly, a matched cohort study observed that while basic embryological outcomes did not differ drastically, cumulative live birth rates were higher in patients utilizing MACS when elevated DNA fragmentation was a factor (Mei et al., 2021).

## Microfluidic Sperm Sorting

Microfluidics separates highly motile, structurally sound sperm without the mechanical stress or reactive oxygen species generation associated with traditional centrifugation. In a comparative study of an unselected IVF-ICSI population, patients utilizing microfluidic sperm preparation had similar fertilization rates to those using standard density gradient centrifugation; however, the microfluidic group experienced significantly better blastocyst formation and clinical pregnancy rates (Zaha et al., 2023).
