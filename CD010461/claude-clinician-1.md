Here are individual primary studies (RCTs and prospective/retrospective comparative studies) comparing advanced sperm selection techniques against standard preparation (density gradient centrifugation/swim-up or conventional ICSI) in IVF/ICSI. I've excluded reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, and commentaries as requested, organized by technique.

## PICSI (hyaluronan-binding selection)

**HABSelect trial** — Miller et al., *Lancet* 2019. Two-group, parallel, double-blind RCT across 16 UK IVF clinics, 2,752 couples randomized to PICSI vs standard ICSI. PICSI did not significantly increase the term live birth rate compared with standard ICSI, but miscarriage rates were significantly lower in the PICSI group. (This is the largest PICSI RCT to date and the first powered for live birth.)

**Worrilow et al., *Hum Reprod* 2013** — Multicenter, double-blind RCT (referenced in the CooperSurgical source above): reported significant improvement in clinical outcomes with hyaluronan-based sperm selection vs conventional ICSI. Worth pulling the full text directly given it conflicts somewhat with HABSelect.

**Worrilow et al. 2009 (ASRM abstract)** — 208 ICSI patients randomized to PICSI, ICSI control, or non-participatory arms. Overall clinical pregnancy rate was higher in the PICSI group than the ICSI control group (44.8% vs 37.9%, n=67 vs 58), and patients with a low hyaluronan-binding index showed a larger benefit from PICSI.

**Alegre et al., *Biomedicines* 2025** — Single-center, triple-blind RCT, 277 couples in an egg-donation program randomized to ICSI vs PICSI. Pregnancy rate was numerically higher with PICSI (74.04% vs 70.87%) and cumulative pregnancy rate after four transfers was significantly higher (88% vs 72%, p<0.05), though live birth rate was not significantly different between groups.

**Nasr-Esfahani et al. / abnormal SDF RCT (2021, PMID 34076869)** — 302 ICSI patients with abnormal sperm DNA fragmentation, randomized to DGC, testicular sperm, PICSI, or MACS. Pregnancy rate was highest in the PICSI group (69.6%) and lowest in the DGC group (51.4%, p=0.025); the testicular-sperm group had significantly lower cleavage and blastulation rates than PICSI, DGC, or MACS.

## MACS (magnetic-activated cell sorting)

**González-Ravina et al., *Cells* 2022** — Prospective, randomized, multicenter trial, 181 donor-IUI cycles (not ICSI, but relevant to sperm selection) comparing MACS+DGC vs DGC alone. No significant differences were found in gestation, live birth, or miscarriage rates between groups.

**PICSI vs. MACS RCT — Mostafa et al., *J Assist Reprod Genet* 2021** — Prospective RCT, 413 ICSI cases with abnormal SDF (>20.3% by TUNEL), randomized to PICSI or MACS. No significant differences were found between PICSI and MACS groups in pre-implantation embryological data, implantation, or clinical outcomes, with ongoing pregnancy rate as the primary endpoint.

**Gil Julia et al., *Biology* 2021 (retrospective, large sample)** — Retrospective, 46,807 standard vs 1,779 MACS-added ICSI cycles using autologous oocytes (Spanish IVIRMA clinics, 2008–2020). No significant differences were seen in pregnancy or live birth rates between groups; a modest increase in cumulative live birth rate was found only when measured per embryo transferred or per MII oocyte used.

**Romany et al. RCT 2017** (referenced within source 23) — cited as an earlier randomized study evaluating MACS's effect on obstetric/perinatal outcomes; worth retrieving directly for full data.

**MDPI 2020 retrospective cohort** (source 19) — Compared MACS vs standard preparation in patients with high SDF. The MACS group showed significantly lower miscarriage rates in autologous ICSI cycles, higher pregnancy rates in oocyte-donation cycles, and significantly increased live-birth rates in both autologous and oocyte-donation cycles.

## IMSI (high-magnification morphological selection)

**Leandri et al., *Andrology* 2013** — Multicentric RCT, 255 couples with severe male-factor infertility (first ART attempt), randomized to IMSI vs ICSI. IMSI did not provide significant improvement in implantation (24% vs 23%), clinical pregnancy (31% vs 33%), or live birth rates (27% vs 30%) compared with ICSI, regardless of baseline sperm DNA fragmentation, nuclear immaturity, or morphology.

**Antinori et al., *Reprod Biomed Online* 2008** — RCT, 446 couples with severe oligoasthenoteratozoospermia randomized to ICSI (n=219) vs IMSI (n=227). Original description found in source 18; full results would need direct retrieval, but this is a foundational IMSI RCT frequently cited in later comparative literature.

**Prospective randomized study — advanced maternal age (PMID 24103532)** — 66 couples (≥37 years) randomized to ICSI vs IMSI. IMSI cycles showed significantly higher implantation (12.1% vs 38.3%, p=0.026) and pregnancy rates, and IMSI was associated with a roughly 9-fold increase in the odds of pregnancy in this subgroup.

**Prospective randomized study — unexplained infertility, *Fertil Steril* 2012** — 72 couples randomized to ICSI (n=36) vs IMSI (n=36). IMSI resulted in higher implantation and pregnancy rates than conventional ICSI in couples with unexplained infertility, with roughly a 4-fold increase in pregnancy rate.

**Open prospective study, 2016–2017 (PMC8560014)** — 446 ICSI vs 79 IMSI cycles, one center. ICSI performed statistically better than IMSI for chemical pregnancy, clinical pregnancy, live birth, and fertilization rates (p<0.05) — notably a negative result for IMSI, and non-randomized (open prospective, unequal group sizes), so weight accordingly.

**Gatimel et al., *J Assist Reprod Genet* 2016** — Retrospective comparative study, 216 couples with two prior ICSI failures undergoing a third attempt, IMSI vs ICSI. IMSI did not significantly improve implantation (12% vs 10%), clinical pregnancy (23% vs 21%), or live birth rates (20% vs 19%) compared with ICSI.

**Repeated implantation failure study (PMC3156729)** — Comparative prospective study, IMSI vs ICSI in couples with prior implantation failure. No statistically significant differences were found in fertilization, implantation, or pregnancy rates, though miscarriage rate was numerically lower and live birth/ongoing pregnancy rates were numerically higher (nearly doubled) in the IMSI group.

**Repeated IVF/ICSI failure study, *Fertil Steril* 2012 (self-controlled)** — 75 couples after ≥2 failures, IMSI vs their own prior IVF/ICSI cycles. Top-quality embryo rate on day 2 was higher with IMSI (89.8% vs 79.8%, p=0.009), extended culture was more frequent (41.3% vs 26.7%, p=0.04), and mean blastocyst number was higher (1.5 vs 1.0, p=0.03). Note: this design uses within-couple historical comparison, not concurrent randomization.

## Microfluidic sperm sorting (e.g., ZyMōt)

**Retrospective cohort, 2022 (Rev Int Androl / ScienceDirect)** — 28 couples with elevated sperm DNA double-strand breaks, each couple's own first (conventional) vs second (microfluidic) ICSI cycle compared. Biochemical pregnancy rate increased 28.31%, clinical pregnancy rate increased 35.56%, and live births increased 35.29% with microfluidic sorting compared to the prior conventional cycle. Self-controlled design, small N.

**Comparative retrospective study, unselected IVF-ICSI population, 2023 (PMC10145657)** — 119 DGC vs 120 microfluidic (ZyMot) couples. No significant difference in fertilization rate (p=0.87), but blastocyst rate (p=0.046) and clinical pregnancy rate (p=0.049) were significantly better with microfluidic selection.

**RCT protocol/ongoing, NCT06005311** — Randomized, double-blind, quadruple-masked trial (Queen Mary Hospital/Kwong Wah Hospital) comparing microfluidic chip vs DGC, primary outcome cumulative live birth rate at 6 months. Currently recruiting — flag as not yet reporting results.

**RCT, microfluidic vs DGC for DFI reduction, NCT07004309 (Hanoi Medical University Hospital, 2023–2024)** — 119 couples randomized to microfluidic vs DGC. Primary outcomes were live birth rate and DNA fragmentation index reduction; secondary outcomes included motility, morphology, fertilization rate, embryo quality, and pregnancy outcomes. Check registry/publication for final reported results, as this may since have posted results.

**PICSI vs ZyMot ongoing RCT, NCT07240779 (Sunrise Fertility Center)** — Randomized, parallel-model interventional trial directly comparing PICSI and the ZyMot microfluidic device for fertilization rate, embryo quality, and clinical pregnancy. Status marked ACTUAL as of Nov 2025 — worth checking for posted results now.

## Additional relevant RCT (SDF-driven, second ejaculate vs PICSI)

**NCT04496232** — Prospective RCT, Ganin Fertility Center. Compares PICSI against a second-ejaculate strategy (versus a normal-SDF control arm) for managing abnormal sperm DNA fragmentation in ICSI patients, with density-gradient processing as the common baseline. Status: completed — check for published results.

**A few notes for your own critical appraisal:**
- Results are markedly inconsistent across techniques and even within technique (e.g., IMSI shows benefit in some small single-center RCTs, particularly in enriched subgroups like advanced maternal age or unexplained infertility, but not in larger/multicenter RCTs).
- The strongest single trial by sample size and design is HABSelect (PICSI, n=2,752) — negative for live birth, positive for miscarriage reduction.
- Many "positive" studies are retrospective, single-center, or self-controlled (couples as their own controls), which raises confounding/regression-to-the-mean concerns — I've flagged these explicitly above.
- Several relevant trials are still recruiting or newly completed (registry pages above) — worth checking ClinicalTrials.gov or PubMed directly for updated results before finalizing any synthesis, since some may have posted results after my search.
