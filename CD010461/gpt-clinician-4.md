## Bottom line

Across primary comparative studies available through **July 17, 2026**, no advanced sperm-selection method has consistently improved **live birth** over standard density-gradient centrifugation, swim-up, and routine morphology/motility selection in an unselected IVF/ICSI population.

The most reliable result is the 2,772-couple HABSelect trial: **PICSI did not significantly improve term live birth**. Microfluidic selection and IMSI also have several neutral randomized trials. Positive findings for MACS, zeta potential, microfluidics, birefringence, and cumulus-cell selection generally come from smaller, single-center studies or observational cohorts and have not been consistently replicated. ([PubMed][1])

## Hyaluronan binding/PICSI

| Primary study                                | Design and population                                                                   | Main findings                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Miller et al., HABSelect, Lancet 2019**    | Multicenter RCT; 2,772 couples undergoing ICSI with fresh transfer                      | Term live birth: **27.4% PICSI vs 25.2% standard ICSI**, OR 1.12, 95% CI 0.95–1.34; not significant. This is the largest and most decision-relevant trial. ([PubMed][1])                                                                                           |
| **Worrilow et al., Human Reproduction 2013** | Multicenter, double-blind RCT; 802 couples; stratified by hyaluronan-binding score      | No significant overall improvement in implantation or clinical pregnancy. In patients with initial HA binding ≤65%, pregnancy loss was lower with HA selection: **3.3% vs 15.1%**, although the trial stopped early and subgroup numbers were small. ([PubMed][2]) |
| **Majumdar & Majumdar, JARG 2013**           | Prospective RCT; 156 first-cycle patients with unexplained infertility and normal semen | Fertilization, good-quality embryos, and clinical pregnancy were essentially identical; pregnancy loss was numerically lower with PICSI but not significant. ([PubMed][3])                                                                                         |
| **Alegre et al., Biomedicines 2025**         | Prospective triple-blinded study; 277 donor-oocyte recipients                           | No increase in live birth; clinical outcomes were generally comparable. Cumulative pregnancy after four transfers favored PICSI, but this was not accompanied by a live-birth advantage. ([PMC][4])                                                                |

**Interpretation:** PICSI may have a pregnancy-loss signal in selected patients, particularly those with poor HA binding, but it has not demonstrated a clear live-birth benefit.

A particularly large benefit was reported after ≥2 previous failures, but live birth was not the prespecified primary outcome and subsequent trials have not consistently reproduced this result. | **De Vos et al., Human Reproduction 2013**  | Prospective randomized sibling-oocyte study; 350 ICSI cycles                              | Fertilization and embryo development were similar. ([PubMed][7])                                                                                                      |

## Magnetic-activated cell sorting, MACS

| Primary study                                          | Design and population                                                                    | Main findings                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Dirican et al., JARG 2008**                          | Comparative study; 196 oligoasthenozoospermic couples; MACS plus DGC vs DGC              | Higher cleavage and pregnancy rates were reported with MACS; implantation was only slightly higher. Allocation was not clearly described as randomized. ([PubMed][15])                                                                                |

| **Mei et al., Asian Journal of Andrology 2022**        | Small comparative study; approximately 80 first cycles with DFI ≥30%                     | No significant clinical-pregnancy or implantation difference. First-transfer live birth was **63.2% vs 53.9%** and cumulative live birth **79.5% vs 70.7%**, reported as nonsignificant trends; fewer transfers were needed with MACS. ([PubMed][17]) |
| **Gil Juliá et al., J Clin Med 2020**                  | Retrospective study; 724 high-SDF cycles                                                 | Lower miscarriage in autologous cycles and higher live birth in some autologous and donor-oocyte analyses were reported, but the nonrandomized design is vulnerable to treatment-selection bias. ([PubMed][18])                                       |
| **Gil Juliá et al., J Clin Med 2021**                  | Retrospective multicenter cohort; 46,807 standard vs 1,779 MACS autologous-oocyte cycles | No significant improvement in conventional pregnancy or live-birth rates. ([PubMed][20])                                                                                                 |

**Interpretation:** MACS may improve embryo or implantation measures in selected high-SDF or severe male-factor populations, but large unselected cohorts do not support routine use, and adequately powered live-birth RCTs are lacking.

## Zeta-potential selection

| Primary study                                      | Design and population                                          | Main findings                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Nasr-Esfahani et al., Int J Fertil Steril 2016** | Double-blind RCT; 203 male-factor ICSI cycles; DGC-zeta vs DGC | More top-quality embryos and a higher pregnancy rate with zeta selection. ## Other advanced methods

| Technique and study                                                           | Findings                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **FACS/YoPro1 sorting — de Geyter et al., JARG 2019**                         | Double-blind RCT of 104 male-factor couples. Live birth was numerically higher with FACS, **42.3% vs 34.6%**, but the prespecified improvement in cumulative ongoing pregnancy could not be demonstrated and the trial was stopped after interim analysis. ([PubMed][23]) |
| **Sperm-head birefringence — Gianaroli et al., Fertility and Sterility 2008** | Prospective randomized study, 112 intervention and 119 controls. Overall fertilization and cleavage were similar; pregnancy and implantation improvements were confined to severe male-factor/testicular-sperm subgroups. ([PubMed][24])                                  |
| **Cumulus-column selection — Tavalaee et al., 2021**                          | Single-blind clinical trial involving 150 male-factor couples. Higher fertilization, embryo quality, clinical pregnancy, and ongoing pregnancy were reported; no live-birth endpoint was provided. ([PubMed][25])                                                         |
| **Cumulus-column selection — Yousefi et al., 2025**                           | Randomized study of 88 cycles. Reported live birth was **72.7% vs 25.0%**. The exceptionally large estimate comes from only 44 patients per group and included 17 twin pregnancies, so independent replication with single-embryo transfer is essential. ([PMC][26])      |
| **Electrophoretic selection — Fleming et al., Human Reproduction 2008**       | Split-sample/split-cohort trial in 28 couples. Demonstrated that electrophoretically selected low-DNA-damage sperm could fertilize oocytes, but the study was too small and not designed to establish live-birth effectiveness. ([PubMed][27])                            |

## Practical evidence hierarchy

For counseling or add-on decisions, I would place the techniques in this order:

1. **PICSI:** best studied; no demonstrated overall live-birth benefit, possible reduction in pregnancy loss in selected patients.
2. **Microfluidics:** good laboratory rationale; predominantly neutral randomized clinical evidence, with conflicting small positive studies.
3. **IMSI:** conflicting early trials, with later and recent randomized studies largely neutral.
4. **MACS:** possible subgroup utility in high SDF, but insufficient randomized live-birth evidence and little benefit in large unselected cohorts.
5. **Zeta, birefringence, FACS and cumulus-cell selection:** promising but insufficiently replicated; cumulus-cell results in particular should be considered preliminary.

A recurrent problem is that many studies use **embryo quality, implantation, or pregnancy per transfer** rather than cumulative live birth per randomized couple. Sibling-oocyte designs are valuable for embryo outcomes but usually cannot provide an unbiased live-birth comparison because the embryos ultimately transferred are no longer randomized.
