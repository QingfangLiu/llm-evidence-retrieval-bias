## PICSI (hyaluronan-based selection)

- **HABSelect trial** (Lancet, 2019) — Largest RCT to date, 2,772 couples. PICSI did **not** significantly improve term live birth rate (27.4% vs 25.2%) but significantly reduced miscarriage rates. The term livebirth rate did not differ significantly between PICSI (27.4% [379/1381]) and ICSI (25.2% [346/1371]) groups (odds ratio 1.12, 95% CI 0.95–1.34; p=0.18).
- **Multicenter double-blind RCT** (Worrilow et al., 2013, PubMed 23203216) — Found improved implantation rate in a subgroup with lower hyaluronan-binding scores.
- **HABSelect mechanistic follow-up** (Human Reproduction, 2022) — found HA-based selection improved live birth outcomes among older couples, connected to underlying sperm DNA quality effects.
- **Korosi et al. 2017** (Eur Rev Med Pharmacol Sci) — RCT of PICSI with oral myo-inositol pretreatment in oligoasthenoteratozoospermic men.
- **Alegre et al. 2025** (Biomedicines) — Triple-blinded trial comparing PICSI vs standard ICSI on embryo quality, cumulative pregnancy and live birth rates.

## Head-to-head sperm-selection RCT (DGC vs Testicular vs PICSI vs MACS)

- **Hasanen et al., 2021** (PubMed 34076869) — 302 patients with abnormal sperm DNA fragmentation randomized to 4 arms. Testicular-sperm group had significantly lower cleavage and blastulation rates compared to PICSI, DGC, or MACS groups (p=0.001).

## MACS (magnetic-activated cell sorting)

- **Gil et al. RCT** (J Assist Reprod Genet, ~2013) — Comparing PICSI vs MACS in high-SDF patients; found no significant differences between PICSI and MACS groups in embryological or pregnancy outcomes.
- **Retrospective cohort, 724 ART cycles** (Cells, 2020, MDPI) — The MACS group showed significantly lower miscarriage rates in autologous ICSI cycles, higher pregnancy rates in oocyte-donation cycles, and increased live-birth rates in both autologous and oocyte-donation cycles.
- **Small RCT, 62 samples** (Clinical outcomes of MACS, PubMed) — Higher rate of high-quality embryos, pregnancy, and implantation with MACS-DGC vs DGC alone, though fertilization rates were not different.
- **Romany et al. 2014** (Fertil Steril) — Controlled/randomized trial of annexin-V sperm removal in oocyte-donation cycles; found no improvement in reproductive outcome in unselected males.
- **González-Ravina et al. 2022** (Cells, PMC9180176) — RCT of MACS in donor IUI (not ICSI); no significant differences in gestation, live birth, or miscarriage rates between MACS and control groups.
- **Obstetric/perinatal follow-up of MACS RCT offspring** (J Assist Reprod Genet, 2017, Springer)

## IMSI (high-magnification morphology selection)

- **Bartoov et al. 2003** (Fertil Steril) — Original report that pregnancy rates were higher with IMSI vs ICSI.
- **Antinori et al. 2008** (Reprod Biomed Online) — Prospective RCT, 446 couples with severe OAT; a comparison was made in terms of pregnancy, miscarriage and implantation rates between IMSI (n=227) and ICSI (n=219) groups.
- **Leandri et al. 2013** (Andrology) — Multicentric RCT, 255 first-ART-attempt couples; IMSI did not provide any significant improvement in the clinical outcomes compared with ICSI for implantation, clinical pregnancy, or live birth rates.
- **Braga et al. 2012** (Fertil Steril) — RCT in unexplained infertility (36 vs 36 cycles); IMSI resulted in higher implantation and pregnancy rates, with a 4-fold increase in pregnancy rate compared to ICSI.
- **Advanced maternal age RCT** (PubMed 24103532) — 33 vs 33 cycles; IMSI showed significantly higher implantation and pregnancy rates, with a 9-fold increase in clinical pregnancy rate in older couples.
- **Gatimel et al. 2016** (J Assist Reprod Genet) — RCT in patients with two prior ICSI failures; IMSI did not significantly improve implantation, clinical pregnancy, or live birth rates compared with ICSI.
- **Balaban et al. 2011** (Reprod Biomed Online) — Prospective randomized study of high-magnification sperm injection.
- **Setti et al.** — Randomized prospective study comparing IMSI/ICSI, with subgroup analysis by OAT criteria (fertilization rate higher with IMSI; implantation/pregnancy higher only in a specific OAT subgroup).
- **Open prospective cohort, 2016–2017** (PMC8560014) — 446 ICSI vs 79 IMSI cycles; here conventional ICSI was statistically better than IMSI for chemical pregnancy, clinical pregnancy, live birth, and fertilization rates.

## Microfluidic sperm sorting

- **Yetkinel et al. 2019** (J Assist Reprod Genet) — RCT, 122 couples with unexplained infertility, Fertile Chip® vs swim-up; clinical pregnancy rates were 48.3% vs 44.8% (p=0.35) and live birth rates were 38.3% vs 36.2% (p=0.48) — no significant difference.
- **Aydın et al. 2022** (Andrologia) — Prospective RCT of microfluidic chip technology in male infertility patients.
- **Okan/Akdeniz sibling-oocyte study** (PubMed 31434165) — Split sibling-oocyte design, swim-up vs Fertile Plus®; no statistically significant differences were found between groups in any laboratory or clinical outcome measured.
- **Quinn et al. 2018** (Human Reproduction) — Split-semen sample comparison of microfluidic sorting vs DGC/swim-up; reported reduced sperm DNA damage with microfluidics.
- **Parrella/paired cohort study** (J Assist Reprod Genet, PMC9365916) — Paired-cycle design (same patients, prior DGC cycle vs later microfluidics cycle); fertilization rates and euploidy rate per oocyte retrieved were significantly higher in the microfluidics group (8.5% vs 4.3%, p=0.04).
- **Amjadi et al. 2022** (PubMed 35650258) — Cohort study in patients with prior fertilization failure; microfluidic sperm sorting improved fertilization rate in patients with poor fertilization outcomes following ICSI.
- **Retrospective cohort, ZyMōt™/FertileChip®** (ScienceDirect, 2022) — 28 couples with elevated sperm DNA double-strand breaks; biochemical pregnancy rate increased 28.31%, clinical pregnancy rate increased 35.56%, and live births increased 35.29% with microfluidic sorting vs. conventional methods.
- **Retrospective cohort, FERTILE PLUS™** (PMC11111481, 2024) — provided tentative evidence that microfluidic sorting can improve blastocyst formation, utilization, and euploidy rates compared to DGC.
- **Ozaltin et al. 2023** (IMR Press) — Cohort comparing sperm-chip vs swim-up in men with normal semen analysis, 102 vs 111 patients.
- **Ongoing RCT, Hanoi Medical University** (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07004309, 2023–2024) — 119 couples, microfluidics vs DGC, primary outcomes live birth rate and DNA fragmentation reduction.
- **Ongoing/completed RCT** (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03085433) — Randomized trial of microfluidic sperm sorting vs standard processing for IVF/ICSI.

**Overall pattern across these primary studies:** results are mixed and technique-dependent. The largest, most rigorous RCTs (HABSelect for PICSI, Leandri for IMSI, Yetkinel for microfluidics) generally show **no significant improvement in live birth or clinical pregnancy rates** for unselected populations, though some show secondary benefits (e.g., lower miscarriage with PICSI). Smaller trials and studies enriched for specific subgroups (advanced maternal age, high sperm DNA fragmentation, prior fertilization failure, unexplained infertility) more often report benefits — suggesting these techniques may help specific patient subpopulations rather than IVF/ICSI patients generally.
