## Bottom line

**No advanced sperm-selection method has shown a consistent, replicated improvement in live birth over standard density-gradient/swim-up selection followed by conventional IVF/ICSI.** The largest randomized evidence is neutral for live birth. PICSI may reduce miscarriage without increasing term live birth, while isolated smaller trials report benefits from microfluidics, IMSI, zona adhesion, or MACS that have not been consistently reproduced. This is a synthesis of the individual primary studies below. ([PubMed][1])

I weighted outcomes in this order: live birth, ongoing pregnancy, clinical pregnancy, miscarriage, then laboratory outcomes. Patient-randomized trials are more informative than sibling-oocyte studies, because the latter generally do not randomize which embryo is transferred.

## 5. Other advanced selection approaches

### Zeta-potential selection

**Nasr Esfahani et al., 2016**, *Int J Fertil Steril*, PMID **27441060**: double-blind randomized trial of **203 male-factor ICSI cycles**. Zeta selection increased top-quality embryo and pregnancy measures compared with DGC alone. It remains an isolated study without strong multicenter replication of live-birth benefit. ([PubMed][23])

### Flow-cytometric removal of DNA-fragmented sperm

A prospective randomized double-blind study of **104 couples**, PMID **31463874**, compared flow sorting with swim-up. Cumulative ongoing pregnancy was 51.9% vs 46.2% and live birth 42.3% vs 34.6%; neither represented the prespecified clinically important improvement, and the trial was stopped after interim analysis for futility. ([PubMed][24])

### Sperm-head birefringence

**Gianaroli et al., 2008**, *Fertility and Sterility*, PMID **18023436**: prospective randomized comparison involving **231 couples**. No overall fertilization or cleavage benefit was found. Pregnancy and implantation appeared higher in selected severe male-factor subgroups, particularly immotile ejaculated or testicular sperm, but this subgroup result has limited confirmatory evidence. ([PubMed][25])

### Zona-pellucida adhesion/binding

| Study                                                            | Findings                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Casciani et al., 2014**, *Zygote*. PMID **23659189**           | Zona-bound sperm injection and conventional ICSI produced equivalent fertilization, pregnancy, implantation and take-home-baby outcomes. ([PubMed][26])                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| **Jin et al., 2016**, *J Assist Reprod Genet*. PMID **26941098** | Randomized **84 couples**. Higher embryo-quality measures, but clinical pregnancy 60.5% vs 47.6% was not statistically significant. ([PubMed][27])                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Ganeva et al., 2024**, *Andrology*. PMID **38225818**          | Prospective randomized study with **500 eligible couples**, but only the **368 who reached euploid frozen embryo transfer** were analyzed. Among those analyzed, live birth was 38.0% vs 25.9%, implantation 50.4% vs 37.0%, and miscarriage 11.3% vs 22.2%. Excluding 132 randomized participants before analysis compromises an intention-to-treat interpretation and may introduce selection bias. ([Wiley Online Library][28]) |

## Practical interpretation for IVF/ICSI counseling

1. **Routine use:** Current primary-study evidence does not establish that PICSI, IMSI, microfluidics, MACS or another advanced method routinely improves live birth.

2. **PICSI:** The most credible clinical signal is reduced miscarriage in HABSelect, but the absolute reduction did not produce a statistically significant increase in term live birth.

3. **Male-factor or high-DFI subgroups:** Several small studies report benefit, but subgroup definitions vary, effects are inconsistent, and large confirmatory patient-randomized trials are lacking.

4. **Laboratory improvement is not equivalent to clinical benefit:** Better motility, lower DNA-fragmentation indices, fertilization or embryo morphology may not improve ongoing pregnancy or live birth.

5. **Evidence remains technique- and population-specific:** A neutral result in unselected patients does not definitively exclude benefit in a narrowly defined subgroup, but such benefit should presently be described as unproven rather than expected.

## Studies not treated as valid efficacy evidence

An IMSI randomized article by **La Sala et al.** was subsequently retracted because of inadequate ethical oversight, so I excluded it from the evidence synthesis. ([PMC][29])

A trial comparing PICSI and MACS in patients with abnormal sperm DNA fragmentation, PMID **32772268**, now carries an **Expression of Concern**; I likewise did not use its results to support effectiveness. ([PubMed][30])
