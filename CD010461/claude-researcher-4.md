Here's a compiled set of primary studies (RCTs and prospective/retrospective comparative studies) comparing advanced sperm selection techniques against standard selection in IVF/ICSI. ## Hyaluronan-based selection (PICSI / HA-ICSI)

- **Miller et al. (HABSelect trial)** — *Lancet* 2019. Parallel-group RCT, 2772 couples, PICSI vs standard ICSI. Live birth rate did not differ significantly between PICSI (27.4%) and ICSI (25.2%) groups (odds ratio 1.12).
- **Novoselsky Persky et al.** — *Andrology* 2021. Sibling-oocyte design comparing conventional ICSI vs PICSI within the same cohort.
- **Alegre et al.** — *Biomedicines* 2025. Triple-blinded RCT in an oocyte-donation population. Pregnancy rate was higher in the PICSI group than the ICSI group (74.04% vs. 70.87%), and cumulative pregnancy rate was significantly higher in the PICSI group.
- **Korosi et al.** — *Eur Rev Med Pharmacol Sci* 2017. RCT of PICSI outcomes with oral myo-inositol pretreatment in oligoasthenoteratozoospermic men.

## IMSI (high-magnification morphology-selected sperm injection)

- **Antinori et al.** — *Reprod Biomed Online* 2008 (this is the classic prospective RCT described in your search results as "Intracytoplasmic morphologically selected sperm injection: a prospective randomized trial"). 446 couples with severe oligoasthenoteratozoospermia, randomized to ICSI (n=219) vs IMSI (n=227). IMSI resulted in a higher clinical pregnancy rate (39.2% versus 26.5%; P = 0.004) than ICSI when applied to severe male infertility cases.

## MACS (magnetic-activated cell sorting)

- **Romany et al.** — prospective RCT (cited under "Clinical outcomes of magnetic activated sperm sorting in infertile men candidate for ICSI," PubMed). 62 semen samples allocated to MACS combined with density gradient centrifugation (MACS-DGC, N=29) vs DGC alone (N=33); no significant difference in fertilization rates, but percentage of high-quality embryos, pregnancy and implantation rates were significantly higher in the MACS-DGC group.
- **Bartoov-type / Spanish multicenter trial** — *Cells* 2022 (donor IUI, not ICSI, but a primary RCT on MACS). Prospective randomized multicenter study of 181 donor-IUI treatments, MACS after density-gradient centrifugation vs DGC alone; no significant differences in gestation, live birth, or miscarriage rates were found.

## Microfluidic sperm sorting

- **Yetkinel et al.** — *J Assist Reprod Genet* 2019. Prospective RCT, 122 couples with unexplained infertility, microfluidic chip (Fertile Chip®) vs conventional swim-up. CPR in the study group and control group were 48.3% and 44.8% (p = 0.35) and LBR were 38.3% and 36.2% (p = 0.48); the microfluidic technique did not change fertilization, CPR, and LBR.
- **Ozcan et al.** — *J Obstet Gynaecol Res* 2021. Study of microfluidic sperm sorting vs standard selection in male-factor infertility (referenced in search results above).
- **Quinn et al.** — *Hum Reprod* 2018. Split-semen-sample comparison of microfluidic sorting vs density gradient centrifugation with swim-up.
- **Cavagnoli et al. (or similar Spanish group)** — cohort study, "Microfluidic sperm sorting improves ICSI outcomes in patients with increased values of Double-Strand Breaks in sperm DNA," *Andrologia*-type journal 2022. Retrospective cohort of 28 infertile couples, each performing a first ICSI cycle with conventional preparation and a second with the ZyMōt™ microfluidic device; clinical pregnancy rate increased 35.56% and live births increased 35.29% with microfluidic sorting.
- **NCT03085433** — "Sperm Selection by Microfluidic Separation Improves Embryo Quality," registered RCT (ClinicalTrials.gov), randomizing couples to microfluidic sorting vs standard density-gradient prep prior to IVF/ICSI.

## Cumulus-cell column selection

- **(Author names not fully resolved) — *Reproductive Sciences*-type journal 2024/2025.** "Sperm Selection Using Cumulus Cell Column Improves Sperm DNA Integrity, Embryo Morphokinetics, and Clinical Outcomes Following ICSI: A Randomized Clinical Trial." 88 ICSI cycles randomized to standard density-gradient centrifugation vs an added cumulus-cell-column (CCC) selection step; implantation (58% vs 28.4%), clinical pregnancy (77.3% vs 25%), and live birth rates (72.7% vs 25%) were all significantly higher in the CCC group.
