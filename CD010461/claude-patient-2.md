Let me also check for IMSI (intracytoplasmic morphologically selected sperm injection) RCTs to round this out.Here's a rundown of individual primary studies (RCTs and prospective/retrospective cohort studies) comparing advanced sperm selection techniques against standard sperm selection (typically density gradient centrifugation/swim-up) in IVF/ICSI. I've grouped them by technique.

## PICSI (hyaluronic acid–based selection)

- **HABSelect trial (Miller et al., *Lancet* 2019)** — The largest PICSI RCT to date, randomizing 2,772 couples 1:1 to PICSI vs standard ICSI. PICSI did not significantly increase the term livebirth rate compared with standard ICSI, but there was a significant decrease in miscarriage rates among couples in the PICSI group, with no differences in other outcomes.
- **HABSelect follow-up mechanistic analysis (West et al., *Hum Reprod* 2022)** — Reanalyzed the same trial cohort and found older women randomized to the PICSI arm had live birth rates equal to younger women, likely due to better avoidance of sperm with damaged DNA.
- **Parmegiani et al., *Fertil Steril* 2012** — Prospective RCT comparing two HA-binding devices (PICSI vs Sperm Slow) rather than against standard ICSI.
- **Korosi et al., *Eur Rev Med Pharmacol Sci* 2017** — Prospective RCT of PICSI outcomes after oral myo-inositol pretreatment in oligoasthenoteratozoospermic men.
- **Hasanen et al., *J Assist Reprod Genet* 2020** — Prospective randomized trial comparing PICSI vs MACS specifically in men with abnormal sperm DNA fragmentation.
- **Hozyen et al., *Reprod Sci* 2022 (four-arm RCT)** — 302 patients with abnormal sperm DNA fragmentation randomized to DGC, testicular sperm, PICSI, or MACS. The highest pregnancy rate was in the PICSI group (69.6%), the lowest in the DGC group (51.4%), a significant difference.

## Microfluidic sperm sorting (e.g., ZyMōt)

- **Yetkinel et al., *J Assist Reprod Genet* 2019** — Prospective RCT on microfluidic chip selection for unexplained infertility.
- **Ozcan et al., *J Obstet Gynaecol Res* 2021** — Study on microfluidic sorting in male-factor infertility IVF cycles.
- **Erdem et al./Ozaltin et al. (Acibadem Fulya Hospital), *Clin Exp Obstet Gynecol* 2023** — Randomized prospective cohort, 213 patients, comparing microfluidic chip (n=102) vs swim-up (n=111) in men with normal semen analysis.
- **Retrospective/case-control study (Oradea, Romania), *PMC* 2023** — 239 couples randomized 1:1 to microfluidic (ZyMot) vs density gradient. No significant difference in fertilization rate, but blastocyst rate (p=0.046) and clinical pregnancy (p=0.049) significantly favored the microfluidic group.
- **Lara-Cerrillo et al. (retrospective cohort), 2022** — 28 couples with elevated sperm DNA double-strand breaks, each serving as their own control across two ICSI cycles (conventional vs ZyMōt). Clinical outcomes were statistically better with microfluidic sorting: biochemical pregnancy rate up 28.3%, clinical pregnancy rate up 35.6%, live births up 35.3% versus the prior conventional cycle.
- **Yildiz & Yuksel, *J Assist Reprod Genet* 2019** — Microfluidic chips in patients with recurrent IVF failure.
- **Pujol et al.** — Microfluidic sorting and reduction of double-strand DNA fragmentation.
- **Kocur et al., *Andrology* 2022** — Examined whether microfluidic selection improves embryo ploidy.

## MACS (magnetic-activated cell sorting)

- **González-Ravina et al., *Cells* 2022** — Multicenter RCT, 181 donor IUI cycles (not ICSI), MACS+DGC vs DGC alone. No significant differences were found in gestation, live birth, or miscarriage rates between groups.
- **Iranian prospective RCT (Royan Institute), *Reprod Biomed Online* ~2018** — 62 semen samples, MACS-DGC (n=29) vs DGC alone (n=33) prior to ICSI. No significant difference in fertilization rates, but percentage of high-quality embryos, pregnancy, and implantation rates were significantly higher in the MACS-DGC group.
- **Hozyen et al. 2022 four-arm RCT** (see above) also included a MACS arm.
- **Romany et al., 2017 RCT** — Referenced as an early MACS-before-ICSI randomized trial (cited in the ScienceDirect retrospective safety study below).
- **Gil Juliá et al. 2021a and 2021b (retrospective cohorts)** — Looked at cumulative live birth rates with MACS in donor-oocyte and autologous-oocyte ICSI cycles.
- **Retrospective multicentre cohort, *Reprod Biomed Online* 2023** — Obstetric/perinatal safety outcomes of MACS before ICSI (2008–2020 cohort).
- **Retrospective study, *J Clin Med* 2020 (n=724)** — Large retrospective cohort on MACS in patients with high sperm DNA fragmentation, including a PGT-A subgroup.

## IMSI (high-magnification morphological selection)

- **Antinori et al., *Reprod Biomed Online* 2008** — Prospective RCT, 446 couples with severe oligoasthenoteratozoospermia randomized to ICSI (n=219) vs IMSI (n=227).
- **Leandri et al., *Andrology* 2013** — Multicentric RCT testing IMSI in the first ART cycle. IMSI showed no significant improvement over ICSI in implantation (24% vs 23%), clinical pregnancy (31% vs 33%), or live birth rates.
- **Moubasher et al., *Cureus* 2021** — Open prospective trial, 446 ICSI vs 79 IMSI cycles. ICSI was statistically better than IMSI for chemical pregnancy, clinical pregnancy, live birth, and fertilization rates.
- **Prospective comparative study, *Andrologie* 2013** — IMSI after repeated IVF/ICSI failure.
- **Retrospective study, *J Assist Reprod Genet* 2016** — 216 couples with two prior ICSI failures, IMSI vs conventional ICSI in a third attempt; found no benefit.
- **Repeated-implantation-failure cohort study, *PMC* ~2011** — 200 couples (100 IMSI vs 100 ICSI). IMSI showed no significant clinical improvement overall, though there were trends toward lower miscarriage and higher live birth rates.

## Other/newer techniques

- **Sperm selection using cumulus cell columns (CCC), RCT, 2024/2025** — 88 ICSI cycles, CCC vs standard DGC. CCC selection significantly reduced sperm DNA fragmentation and produced higher implantation, chemical pregnancy, clinical pregnancy, and live birth rates than controls.
- **Four-arm RCT abstract, *Hum Reprod* 2023 (P-007)** — Compared multiple advanced sperm selection techniques specifically for raised sperm DNA fragmentation index.

---

A few notes as you evaluate these: the largest, most rigorous trial (HABSelect, PICSI) found no live-birth benefit overall despite a miscarriage-rate reduction, while several smaller trials of MACS, microfluidic sorting, and cumulus-cell columns report positive effects, and IMSI trials are mostly negative or mixed. Study quality, sample size, and whether the population has elevated sperm DNA fragmentation seem to matter a lot for whether these techniques show benefit — worth reading full texts rather than relying on abstracts alone, especially since your own case has particulars that could make any given trial more or less applicable.
