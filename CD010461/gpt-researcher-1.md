## Scope

I searched for **human comparative primary studies** of sperm-selection add-ons used in IVF/ICSI, using conventional density-gradient centrifugation, swim-up, or embryologist selection by motility/morphology as the standard comparator. I prioritized live birth, ongoing pregnancy, clinical pregnancy, and miscarriage; embryo and fertilization outcomes are secondary.

The list below contains **primary studies only**—no systematic reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, opinions, or editorials. Searches were current through **July 17, 2026**.

## Most informative randomized studies

### Hyaluronan binding/PICSI

| Study                                             | Design and population                                                                                | Main findings                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Miller et al., HABSelect, 2019**; PMID 30712901 | Multicentre RCT; 2,772 couples undergoing ICSI; PICSI versus standard ICSI                           | The largest trial. Term live birth was **27.4% versus 25.2%**, OR 1.12, 95% CI 0.95–1.34, *P*=0.18. Miscarriage among clinical pregnancies was lower with PICSI, approximately **12.3% versus 19.6%**, but this did not produce a statistically significant live-birth benefit. ([PMC][1])              |
| **Worrilow et al., 2013**; PMID 23203216          | Multicentre, double-blind RCT; 802 enrolled; stratified by hyaluronan-binding score                  | No statistically significant overall improvement in implantation or clinical pregnancy. In the subgroup with low hyaluronan binding, pregnancy loss was lower with HA-selected sperm—**3.3% versus 15.1%** in one prespecified classification. Trial stopped early for financial reasons. ([PubMed][2]) |
| **Majumdar and Majumdar, 2013**; PMID 24085466    | Prospective randomized study; 156 first-cycle patients with unexplained infertility and normal semen | Fertilization, top-quality embryos, and clinical pregnancy were essentially identical. Live birth was **31.0% with PICSI versus 26.3% with ICSI**; the study was underpowered for that difference. ([PubMed][3])                                                                                        |
| **Van den Bergh et al., 2009**; PMID 20031019     | Randomized sibling-oocyte study; 407 oocytes from 44 patients                                        | No meaningful difference in fertilization, pronuclear score, cleavage, or embryo score between HA-bound and non-HA-bound sperm. Clinical outcomes could not be attributed separately because embryos from both groups could be transferred. ([PubMed][4])                                               |

**Interpretation:** PICSI has the strongest trial base. The large HABSelect trial argues against a clinically important improvement in live birth in an unselected ICSI population, although its miscarriage result remains a potentially relevant signal.

### Intracytoplasmic morphologically selected sperm injection—IMSI/MSOME

| Study                                       | Design and population                                                                       | Main findings                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Antinori et al., 2008**; PMID 18549694    | Prospective RCT; 446 couples with severe oligoasthenoteratozoospermia                       | Clinical pregnancy was higher with IMSI, **39.2% versus 26.5%**, particularly among couples with previous failed attempts. Live-birth reporting was incomplete at publication. ([PubMed][5])                                                                 |
| **Leandri et al., 2013**; PMID 23788532     | Multicentre RCT; 255 couples undergoing their first ART attempt for male-factor infertility | No benefit for implantation, clinical pregnancy, or live birth: implantation **24% versus 23%**, clinical pregnancy **31% versus 33%**, and live birth approximately **27% versus 30%** for IMSI and ICSI. ([PubMed][6])                                     |
| **Shakeri et al., 2026**; PMID 42270872     | Double-blind RCT; 100 couples with severe teratozoospermia                                  | No statistically significant differences in fertilization, clinical or ongoing pregnancy, live birth, or miscarriage. Reported live birth was **30.2% with ICSI versus 15.6% with IMSI**, but the difference was imprecise and nonsignificant. ([PubMed][7]) |
| **Oliveira et al., 2011**; PMID 21781299    | Comparative study; 200 couples with repeated implantation failure                           | No statistically significant difference in implantation or pregnancy. Live birth was **21% with IMSI versus 12% with ICSI**, described as a nonsignificant trend. ([PubMed][8])                                                                              |
| **El Khattabi et al., 2013**; PMID 23548938 | Prospective nonrandomized study; 478 patients                                               | In the severe-teratozoospermia subgroup, live birth was **38% with IMSI versus 20% with ICSI**. No benefit was found in patients with repeated implantation failure but without severe male factor: **21% versus 22%**. ([PubMed][9])                        |

**Interpretation:** IMSI findings are inconsistent. Positive results are concentrated in older, single-centre or nonrandomized studies and selected severe-teratozoospermia populations; multicentre and newer randomized trials have not confirmed a consistent benefit.

### Microfluidic sperm sorting

| Study                                                      | Design and population                                                                   | Main findings                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Yetkinel et al., 2019**; PMID 30542782                   | Prospective RCT; 122 couples with unexplained infertility; microfluidics versus swim-up | More grade-1 embryos, but no significant improvement in clinical pregnancy (**48.3% versus 44.8%**) or live birth (**38.3% versus 36.2%**). ([PubMed][10])                                                                                                               |
| **Quinn et al., 2022**; PMID 35522187                      | Pragmatic RCT; 386 patients; microfluidic device versus density-gradient centrifugation | No difference in cleavage-stage or blastocyst quality and no difference in clinical or ongoing pregnancy. The population was not enriched for high sperm DNA fragmentation. ([PubMed][11])                                                                               |
| **Prospective male-factor RCT, 2022**; PMID 35263457       | 128 male-factor ICSI cycles; Fertile Chip versus swim-up                                | Reported higher implantation, clinical pregnancy and live birth with the chip: live birth **46.8% versus 25.0%**. This positive single-centre result conflicts with the larger pragmatic trial. ([PubMed][12])                                                           |
| **Microfluidics versus DGC RCT, 2026**; PMID 41757843      | 119 IVF couples; live birth and DNA-fragmentation reduction were primary outcomes       | Microfluidics reduced DNA fragmentation more effectively, especially at higher baseline DFI, but did **not** significantly improve fertilization, pregnancy, ongoing pregnancy, or live birth. ([PubMed][13])                                                            |
| **Microfluidic sibling-oocyte study, 2019**; PMID 31434165 | Sibling oocytes from 81 patients; Fertile Plus versus swim-up                           | No significant differences in fertilization, embryo development, implantation, clinical pregnancy, or live birth. Clinical comparisons were based on a smaller transfer subgroup. ([PubMed][14])                                                                         |
| **Large ZyMōt cohort, 2026**; PMID 42116698                | Retrospective study; 1,091 ZyMōt and 1,176 DGC cycles                                   | Higher fertilization and day-5 blastulation and higher reported live birth after euploid embryo transfer in donor and autologous cycles. As a retrospective comparison, it remains vulnerable to selection, temporal and laboratory-practice confounding. ([PubMed][15]) |

**Interpretation:** Microfluidics consistently selects sperm with better motility or DNA-integrity measures, but randomized evidence has generally not shown better pregnancy or live-birth outcomes. A possible benefit in high-DFI or selected male-factor populations remains unconfirmed.

## MACS, FACS and surface-charge selection

### Magnetic-activated cell sorting—MACS

| Study                                              | Design and population                                                              | Main findings                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Dirican et al., 2008**; PMID 18810633            | Comparative ICSI study; 196 couples; MACS plus processing versus DGC               | Higher cleavage and pregnancy rates with MACS and a slightly higher implantation rate. Allocation was not described as randomized. ([PubMed][16])                                                                                   |
| **Troya et al., 2015**; PMID 27206090              | Prospective randomized three-arm study; 136 patients; MACS, PICSI or standard ICSI | Similar fertilization and embryo outcomes. Clinical pregnancy was **58.1% with MACS, 40.4% with PICSI and 27.3% with standard ICSI**, *P*=0.019. Live birth was not reported. ([PubMed][17])                                        |
| **Sánchez-Martín et al., 2020**; PMID 33302575     | Retrospective study; 724 high-SDF ART cycles; MACS+DGC versus DGC                  | MACS was associated with lower miscarriage in autologous cycles and higher live birth in some autologous and donor-oocyte analyses. Nonrandom allocation and multiple subgroup analyses limit causal interpretation. ([PubMed][18]) |
| **Mei et al., 2022**; PMID 34708718                | Comparative first-cycle study; approximately 80 high-DFI patients                  | No significant difference in clinical pregnancy or implantation after the first transfer. Live birth and cumulative live birth numerically favored MACS but were not statistically significant. ([PubMed][19])                      |
| **Large donor-oocyte cohort, 2022**; PMID 35184950 | Multicentre retrospective study; 40,157 standard cycles and 1,240 MACS cycles      | MACS did not improve cumulative live birth after adjustment and when outcomes were evaluated over successive embryo transfers or oocyte use. ([PubMed][20])                                                                         |

### Flow-cytometric removal of DNA-damaged sperm

**Rappa et al., 2019**, PMID 31463874, randomized 104 couples to standard swim-up or YoPro1 flow-cytometric sorting. Cumulative ongoing pregnancy and live birth were numerically higher after sorting—live birth **42.3% versus 34.6%**—but the planned improvement was not achieved and the trial stopped after its interim analysis. ([PubMed][21])

### Zeta-potential selection

| Study                                         | Design and population                                              | Main findings                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Nasr-Esfahani et al., 2016**; PMID 27441060 | Double-blind RCT; 203 male-factor ICSI cycles; DGC/Zeta versus DGC | Higher top-quality embryo and pregnancy rates; the study also reported substantially more live births with Zeta selection. It additionally found an altered sex ratio, an unexpected result requiring independent confirmation. ([PubMed][22]) |
| **Karimi et al., 2020**; PMID 31606966        | Randomized single-blind trial; 205 evaluable male-factor couples   | Clinical pregnancy was approximately **35% versus 20.7%**, and miscarriage **8.9% versus 19.4%**, favoring DGC/Zeta. Implantation was not significantly different and live birth was not reported. ([PubMed][23])                              |

**Interpretation:** Zeta selection has two positive randomized pregnancy studies, but neither constitutes a definitive, adequately powered live-birth replication.

## Other biologically based selection methods

### Sperm-head birefringence

**Gianaroli et al., 2008**, PMID 18023436, prospectively randomized 231 couples. Overall fertilization and cleavage were similar. Clinical pregnancy, ongoing pregnancy and implantation favored birefringence selection only in the most severe male-factor and testicular-sperm subgroups. ([PubMed][24])

**Ghosh et al., 2012**, PMID 22369032, studied 96 azoospermic or completely asthenozoospermic cycles. Birefringence selection did not significantly improve the azoospermia comparison but improved embryo grades and pregnancy in the complete-asthenozoospermia subgroup. ([PubMed][25])

### Zona-pellucida binding/adhesion

**Jin et al., 2016**, PMID 26941098, randomized 84 couples after previous failed IVF. Zona-bound sperm improved the proportions of high-quality and usable embryos, but clinical pregnancy—**60.5% versus 47.6%**—was not statistically different. ([PubMed][26])

**Kamenov et al., 2024**, PMID 38225818, randomly allocated 368 couples to sperm–zona adhesion or standard ICSI, followed by frozen transfer of euploid embryos. Implantation, clinical pregnancy and live birth favored zona adhesion; live birth was **38.0% versus 25.9%**, with miscarriage **11.3% versus 22.2%**. This is promising but needs independent replication because it uses a specialized, nonstandard zona-processing method. ([PubMed][27])

## Overall evidence pattern

The clearest finding is that **better laboratory sperm characteristics do not reliably translate into more live births**. PICSI has not improved live birth in the largest trial, although miscarriage was reduced. Larger randomized microfluidic trials are predominantly neutral. IMSI evidence is contradictory, with no consistent benefit in multicentre or recent randomized trials. MACS, Zeta and zona-based methods have positive individual studies, mainly in selected male-factor or high-DFI populations, but the positive live-birth findings have not yet been consistently replicated.

One relevant trial—**“PICSI vs. MACS for abnormal sperm DNA fragmentation ICSI cases”**, PMID 32772268—now carries an **expression of concern** in its PubMed record; I would not use it as dependable effectiveness evidence without resolution of the journal’s concerns. ([PubMed][28])
