## Scope

I treated **advanced therapy** as biologic therapy or an orally administered targeted small molecule. The synthesis below focuses on randomized primary studies in adults with moderate-to-severe, predominantly luminal Crohn’s disease that reported clinical remission, steroid-free remission, endoscopic outcomes, or randomized maintenance of response/remission.

I excluded systematic reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, opinions, editorials, observational cohorts, postoperative-only studies, and fistula-only studies. The list emphasizes pivotal, head-to-head, and informative negative trials rather than every dose-ranging study ever conducted.

## Main findings from the primary studies

### Anti-TNF therapies

**Infliximab has strong evidence for both induction and scheduled maintenance.** In the original randomized induction trial, a single infliximab infusion produced clinical remission at week 4 in 33% versus 4% with placebo; overall adverse-event rates were similar. In ACCENT I, among initial responders, week-30 remission was 39% with infliximab 5 mg/kg and 45% with 10 mg/kg, versus 21% after withdrawal to placebo. Serious-infection incidence was similar among groups. ([PubMed][1])

**Adalimumab also induces and maintains remission.** In anti-TNF-naive patients in CLASSIC-I, the 160/80-mg induction regimen produced week-4 remission in 36% versus 12% with placebo; injection-site reactions were more frequent with adalimumab, while overall adverse events were otherwise similar. In GAIN, involving patients intolerant of or no longer responsive to infliximab, week-4 remission was 21% versus 7%; two adalimumab and four placebo patients stopped because of adverse events, and serious infections occurred in no adalimumab patients versus four placebo patients. Among induction responders in CHARM, remission at week 56 was 36% with every-other-week and 41% with weekly adalimumab, versus 12% with placebo. ([PubMed][2])

**Certolizumab’s induction evidence is weaker than its maintenance evidence.** PRECiSE 1 found modest improvements in response but no statistically significant difference in remission; serious adverse events occurred in 10% versus 7%, and serious infections in 2% versus less than 1%. In PRECiSE 2, among induction responders, 63% maintained response through week 26 with certolizumab versus 36% with placebo. ([PubMed][3])

**Combination infliximab–azathioprine outperformed either monotherapy in biologic- and immunomodulator-naive patients.** In SONIC, week-26 corticosteroid-free remission was 56.8% with combination therapy, 44.4% with infliximab alone, and 30.0% with azathioprine alone. Serious infections occurred in 3.9%, 4.9%, and 5.6%, respectively. The study does not establish that combination therapy has the same benefit-risk profile in previously treated patients or with other biologics. ([PubMed][4])

### Anti-integrin therapies

**Vedolizumab has a comparatively modest and sometimes delayed induction effect, but clearer maintenance efficacy.** In GEMINI 2, week-6 remission was 14.5% versus 6.8% with placebo. Among induction responders, week-52 remission was 39.0% with every-8-week and 36.4% with every-4-week vedolizumab, versus 21.6% after withdrawal. In that trial, serious adverse events, infections, and serious infections were numerically more frequent with vedolizumab. In GEMINI 3, involving mostly patients with prior anti-TNF failure, the prespecified week-6 remission endpoint was negative—15.2% versus 12.1%—but remission at week 10 was 26.6% versus 12.1%, suggesting slower onset in this population. ([PubMed][5])

**Natalizumab produced mixed induction results.** ENACT-1 did not show statistically significant week-10 remission, whereas the later ENCORE trial in patients with elevated CRP found sustained remission through week 12 in 26% versus 16% with placebo, with similar short-term adverse-event frequencies. These efficacy trials were not large enough to characterize rare neurologic harms. ([PubMed][6])

**Etrolizumab is an informative negative induction trial.** In BERGAMOT, week-14 clinical remission was 33% with etrolizumab versus 29% with placebo, and endoscopic improvement was 27% versus 22%; neither difference was significant. Among induction responders, randomized maintenance favored continued etrolizumab at week 66 for clinical remission, 35% versus 24%, and endoscopic improvement, 24% versus 12%. Overall adverse-event frequencies were similar during maintenance. ([PubMed][7])

### IL-12/23 and selective IL-23 therapies

**Ustekinumab is effective for induction and maintenance.** UNITI-1 and UNITI-2 showed significantly higher week-6 clinical-response rates than placebo in both anti-TNF-failure and conventional-therapy-failure populations. Among responders in IM-UNITI, week-44 remission was 53.1% with dosing every eight weeks and 48.8% every 12 weeks, versus 35.9% after withdrawal; adverse-event rates were similar among groups. ([PubMed][8])

**Ustekinumab and adalimumab were similar in biologic-naive patients.** In the double-blind SEAVUE trial, week-52 remission was 65% with ustekinumab and 61% with adalimumab, a nonsignificant difference. Serious infections occurred in 2% and 3%, respectively, with no deaths. ([PubMed][9])

**Risankizumab has strong clinical and endoscopic induction and maintenance evidence.** In ADVANCE, week-12 CDAI remission was 45% with 600 mg versus 25% with placebo; in the biologic-failure MOTIVATE population, it was 42% versus 20%. Endoscopic-response differences were also substantial, and overall adverse-event incidence was similar among groups. In FORTIFY, continued risankizumab maintained clinical and endoscopic benefit better than withdrawal, with broadly similar adverse-event frequencies. ([PubMed][10])

**Direct comparative evidence favors risankizumab over ustekinumab after anti-TNF failure.** In SEQUENCE, risankizumab was noninferior for week-24 clinical remission—58.6% versus 39.5%—and superior for week-48 endoscopic remission—31.8% versus 16.2%. Adverse-event incidence appeared similar. Because the trial was open-label with blinded endpoint assessment, treatment discontinuation and subjective outcomes could still be affected by knowledge of assignment. ([PubMed][11])

**Mirikizumab was effective in a treat-through phase 3 study.** In VIVID-1, the composite of early symptomatic response and week-52 CDAI remission occurred in 45.4% with mirikizumab versus 19.6% with placebo; the corresponding endoscopic-response composite was 38.0% versus 9.0%. Serious adverse events occurred in 10.3% with mirikizumab, 10.7% with ustekinumab, and 17.1% with placebo. ([PubMed][12])

**Guselkumab produced large clinical and endoscopic effects in GALAXI-2 and GALAXI-3.** The composite of week-12 response and week-48 clinical remission occurred in approximately 47%–55% across guselkumab regimens versus 12%–13% with placebo. Serious adverse events occurred in 7%–11% with guselkumab, 12% with ustekinumab, and 15% with placebo; no deaths were reported. The trials also reported superiority to ustekinumab for several prespecified week-48 outcomes. ([PubMed][13])

### Oral JAK inhibitors

**Upadacitinib has the strongest phase 3 evidence among the oral targeted therapies identified.** In U-EXCEL and U-EXCEED, week-12 remission was 49.5% versus 29.1% and 38.9% versus 21.1%, respectively. Among induction responders in U-ENDURE, week-52 remission was 37.3% with 15 mg and 47.6% with 30 mg, versus 15.1% with placebo. Herpes zoster was more frequent with upadacitinib; hepatic abnormalities and neutropenia were more frequent with 30-mg maintenance, and gastrointestinal perforations occurred in several treated patients. The final, larger U-ENDURE analysis confirmed the maintenance benefit and reported no adjudicated major cardiovascular events or venous thromboembolic events during the trial, although it remained too small for precise estimates of rare risks. ([PubMed][14])

**Filgotinib produced inconsistent induction but positive maintenance results.** FITZROY found week-10 remission in 47% with filgotinib 200 mg versus 23% with placebo; serious treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 9% versus 4%. In the larger DIVERSITY program, the phase 3 induction studies did not meet both coprimary clinical and endoscopic endpoints. Among responders entering maintenance, however, week-58 clinical remission was 43.8% with continued 200 mg versus 26.4% after withdrawal, and endoscopic response was 30.4% versus 9.4%. No deaths were reported. ([PubMed][15])

**Tofacitinib was negative in Crohn’s disease.** In the phase IIb induction study, remission was 43.5% with 5 mg twice daily and 43.0% with 10 mg twice daily, versus an unusually high 36.7% placebo rate; neither comparison was significant. Maintenance also failed its primary endpoint. ([PubMed][16])

## Interpretation issues for an evidence synthesis

Most maintenance studies are **randomized-withdrawal trials**: only patients who first responded to active induction were randomized. Their maintenance remission percentages therefore should not be interpreted as remission among all patients who started treatment.

Earlier studies relied heavily on **CDAI remission**, whereas newer IL-23 and JAK programs generally required both clinical and endoscopic outcomes. CDAI remission can occur without mucosal healing and vice versa.

The trials are useful for common short-term adverse events, treatment discontinuation, and some serious infections, but they are generally **underpowered for uncommon malignancies, opportunistic infections, thromboembolism, major cardiovascular events, demyelination, or other rare harms**. Open-label extensions increase exposure time but lose randomized comparisons.
