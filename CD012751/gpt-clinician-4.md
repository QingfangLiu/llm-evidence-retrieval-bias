## Scope and how to interpret the evidence

The studies below are **individual primary trials or primary observational cohorts** in adults with moderate-to-severe luminal Crohn’s disease. I excluded systematic reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, committee statements, and editorials.

Most older trials defined clinical remission as **CDAI <150**. Newer trials often also used patient-reported symptom remission and endoscopic outcomes. Direct numerical comparisons across drugs are unreliable because trials differed in prior biologic exposure, disease severity, corticosteroid use, outcome definitions, and timing.

A particularly important design issue is that many maintenance trials were **responder-enriched withdrawal trials**: everyone received active induction, and only responders were randomized to continue treatment or switch to placebo. Their maintenance percentages therefore apply to patients who have already responded—not to everyone starting the drug.

## What direct comparative primary trials show

**Combination versus monotherapy.** SONIC provides strong evidence that, in appropriately selected biologic- and immunomodulator-naive patients, infliximab plus azathioprine produces more corticosteroid-free remission than either drug alone. The incremental benefit must be balanced against thiopurine toxicity and the higher observed lymphoma rate with combination exposure. ([PubMed][4])

**Ustekinumab versus adalimumab in biologic-naive disease.** In SEAVUE, week-52 clinical remission was **65% with ustekinumab versus 61% with adalimumab**, a nonsignificant difference. Serious infections occurred in 2% and 3%, respectively. ([PubMed][20])

**Risankizumab versus ustekinumab after anti-TNF failure.** In SEQUENCE, week-24 clinical remission was **58.6% versus 39.5%**, meeting noninferiority, and week-48 endoscopic remission was **31.8% versus 16.2%**, establishing superiority for risankizumab on the endoscopic endpoint. Adverse-event rates were broadly similar. ([PubMed][21])

**Newer IL-23 agents versus ustekinumab.** VIVID-1 found mirikizumab noninferior to ustekinumab for clinical remission but did not establish superiority on the prespecified endoscopic comparison. GALAXI 2/3 found greater pooled endoscopic response with guselkumab, without a clear statistically significant clinical-remission advantage. ([PubMed][18])

## Clinical interpretation

The primary studies support several conclusions:

1. **Most established biologic and advanced therapies improve induction and/or maintenance of remission compared with placebo**, but the magnitude cannot be validly ranked using separate trials.

2. **Prior exposure matters.** Absolute remission rates are generally lower in treatment-refractory populations. Vedolizumab may take longer to show benefit after anti-TNF failure, while risankizumab has direct comparative evidence over ustekinumab in that setting.

3. **Maintenance works only when the treatment is continued.** Across withdrawal trials, patients switched to placebo after responding consistently lost remission more often than those continuing therapy.

4. **The most distinctive trial-level safety considerations are** serious/opportunistic infection with systemic immunosuppression, the thiopurine-associated lymphoma contribution when used with anti-TNF therapy, and herpes zoster, laboratory abnormalities, and gastrointestinal perforation with upadacitinib. Rare malignancy, cardiovascular, thromboembolic, demyelinating, and opportunistic-infection risks cannot be reliably quantified from individual efficacy trials.

5. **Endoscopic benefit is increasingly important.** Risankizumab, upadacitinib, mirikizumab, and guselkumab all demonstrated substantial endoscopic effects, but differing endpoint definitions and trial designs preclude simple numerical comparisons.
