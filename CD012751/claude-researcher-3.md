## Primary studies on biologic/advanced therapies for inducing and maintaining remission in Crohn's disease

Below is a list of individual primary studies (randomized controlled trials) I identified on biologic and advanced (small-molecule) therapies for induction and/or maintenance of remission in Crohn's disease. Systematic reviews, meta-analyses, narrative reviews, guidelines, and editorials/comments have been excluded.

**Anti-TNF agents**

The ACCENT I trial randomized 573 patients who responded to a single infliximab infusion to placebo, or scheduled infliximab maintenance (5 mg/kg or 5→10 mg/kg) every 8 weeks through week 46, with co-primary endpoints of remission at week 30 and time to loss of response through week 54.

The CLASSIC I dose-ranging trial randomized 299 anti-TNF-naive patients with moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease to adalimumab (various doses) or placebo at weeks 0 and 2, and CLASSIC II followed CLASSIC I responders on adalimumab maintenance for up to 56 weeks. The CHARM trial gave open-label adalimumab induction, then randomized week-4 responders to placebo, adalimumab 40 mg every other week, or adalimumab 40 mg weekly through week 56, with remission at weeks 26 and 56 as co-primary endpoints. GAIN enrolled 325 patients with moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease who had lost response to or were intolerant of infliximab.

PRECiSE 1 was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial evaluating certolizumab pegol induction in 662 adults with moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease, stratified by baseline CRP. PRECiSE 2 was a 26-week trial of maintenance and withdrawal therapy with certolizumab pegol in patients who had responded to open-label induction. PRECiSE 3 was an open-label extension in which PRECiSE 2 completers received certolizumab pegol every 4 weeks.

The SONIC trial randomized 508 immunosuppressant- and biologic-naive adults with moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease to infliximab monotherapy, azathioprine monotherapy, or combination therapy, with corticosteroid-free remission at week 26 as the primary endpoint.

**Anti-integrin agent**

GEMINI 2 was an integrated induction and maintenance trial in which 368 patients with active Crohn's disease were randomly assigned to vedolizumab or placebo at weeks 0 and 2, and responders to induction were then re-randomized to placebo or vedolizumab maintenance every 4 or 8 weeks through week 52. VISIBLE 2 was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of a subcutaneous vedolizumab formulation as maintenance treatment; after open-label IV induction, week-6 responders were randomized 2:1 to subcutaneous vedolizumab or placebo every 2 weeks through week 50.

**Anti-IL-12/23 agent**

UNITI-1 enrolled 741 patients with prior non-response or intolerance to TNF antagonists, and UNITI-2 enrolled 628 patients in whom conventional therapy had failed; both were 8-week randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled induction trials of intravenous ustekinumab. Responders from both trials entered IM-UNITI, in which 397 patients were randomized to subcutaneous ustekinumab (every 8 or 12 weeks) or placebo.

**Anti-IL-23 agent**

ADVANCE and MOTIVATE were randomized, double-masked, placebo-controlled phase 3 induction trials of intravenous risankizumab (600 mg or 1200 mg) versus placebo in patients with moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease intolerant/refractory to conventional therapy (ADVANCE) or biologics (MOTIVATE). FORTIFY was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, maintenance withdrawal trial evaluating subcutaneous risankizumab in patients with clinical response to induction in ADVANCE or MOTIVATE.

**JAK inhibitor**

In the phase 3 induction trials U-EXCEL and U-EXCEED, patients with moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease were randomized 2:1 to upadacitinib 45 mg or placebo daily for 12 weeks; in U-ENDURE, 502 induction responders were randomized to upadacitinib 15 mg, 30 mg, or placebo for 52 weeks of maintenance.

**Head-to-head trial**

SEAVUE was a multicenter, double-blinded trial randomizing 386 biologic-naive patients with moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease 1:1 to adalimumab or ustekinumab monotherapy, with clinical remission at week 52 as the primary endpoint.
