## Bottom line

Across individual randomized trials, advanced therapies consistently outperform placebo for **inducing clinical remission, maintaining remission, and—particularly in newer studies—producing endoscopic improvement**. Absolute remission benefits are commonly around **15–30 percentage points**, although results cannot be compared directly because populations, prior treatment exposure, endpoints, and trial designs differ.

The strongest direct comparative evidence includes:

* **Infliximab plus azathioprine** outperforming either drug alone in treatment-naïve patients in SONIC.
* **Ustekinumab and adalimumab** producing similar one-year clinical remission in biologic-naïve patients in SEAVUE.
* **Risankizumab** outperforming ustekinumab for endoscopic remission after anti-TNF failure in SEQUENCE.
* **Guselkumab** outperforming placebo and, on several endoscopic outcomes, ustekinumab in GALAXI-2/3.

Safety differences between drugs are harder to quantify from these trials. Most lasted approximately one year and were not large enough to measure uncommon infections, malignancies, cardiovascular events, thrombosis, or other rare outcomes reliably.

### Special situations

**Fistulizing Crohn’s disease:** In a dedicated 94-person RCT, complete fistula closure occurred in **55% with infliximab 5 mg/kg versus 13% with placebo**. ACCENT II subsequently demonstrated a markedly longer time to loss of fistula response with maintenance infliximab—more than 40 weeks versus 14 weeks. ([PubMed][18])

**Natalizumab:** ENCORE demonstrated sustained remission in **26% versus 16%** during induction. In the earlier ENACT maintenance trial, remission through week 36 was **44% versus 26%** among responders. However, a participant in the open-label extension died from JC-virus-associated progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy, a uniquely consequential harm that substantially changes the benefit–harm balance. ([PubMed][19])

## How to interpret the numbers

1. **Maintenance percentages frequently apply only to induction responders.** They should not be read as the probability that every person beginning the drug will be in remission one year later.

2. **Withdrawal placebo is not the same as untreated placebo.** In many maintenance trials, patients first improved on the active drug and were then randomized either to continue it or stop it.

3. **Symptom remission is not synonymous with inflammation control.** CDAI can improve without endoscopic healing. Newer trials therefore give more weight to endoscopic response or remission.

4. **Prior treatment exposure matters.** Remission rates are generally lower in biologic-refractory populations than in biologic-naïve populations.

5. **Rare harms remain uncertain.** One-year RCTs are useful for common adverse events but much less informative about uncommon opportunistic infections, malignancy, thrombosis or long-latency toxicity.
