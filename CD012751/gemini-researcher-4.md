As an evidence-synthesis researcher, you need clean, unadulterated data from primary randomized controlled trials (RCTs) rather than relying on interpretations from existing systematic reviews or guidelines.

Below is a synthesis of the efficacy (induction and maintenance of remission) and safety profiles of key biologic and advanced treatments for moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease, drawn strictly from landmark phase 2 and 3 primary trials.

## IL-23 and IL-12/23 Inhibitors

This class has become foundational for both biologic-naive and biologic-experienced patients, generally demonstrating favorable safety profiles.

* **Risankizumab (Selective IL-23):** The phase 3 ADVANCE and MOTIVATE trials established the efficacy of risankizumab for induction therapy. ADVANCE included patients with either conventional or biologic failures, while MOTIVATE strictly evaluated bio-refractory patients. At week 12, both doses of risankizumab met coprimary endpoints, achieving significantly higher rates of clinical remission (CDAI) and endoscopic response compared to placebo. The incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events was similar between the risankizumab and placebo groups (D'Haens et al., 2022).
* **Guselkumab (Selective IL-23):** The phase 2 GALAXI-1 study evaluated three intravenous induction regimens. At week 12, all guselkumab regimens induced greater clinical and endoscopic improvements compared to placebo. Safety profiles, including adverse event rates, were generally similar across all treatment and placebo groups (Sandborn et al., 2022).
* **Ustekinumab (IL-12/23):** The phase 3 UNITI trials demonstrated rapid induction of remission. UNITI-1 evaluated patients who had failed TNF-antagonists, while UNITI-2 evaluated those who failed conventional therapies. In both cohorts, intravenous induction resulted in significantly higher clinical response rates at week 6 than placebo. Maintenance therapy sustained these benefits through week 44, with adverse events remaining comparable to placebo (Feagan et al., 2016).

## JAK Inhibitors (Small Molecules)

Unlike monoclonal antibodies, Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors are orally administered small molecules. They provide rapid onset but carry distinct safety considerations.

* **Upadacitinib:** The phase 3 U-EXCEL and U-EXCEED trials demonstrated robust induction efficacy. At week 12, upadacitinib (45 mg) yielded significantly higher clinical remission (49.5% and 38.9% in the respective trials) and endoscopic response rates compared to placebo. The U-ENDURE maintenance trial confirmed that lower doses (15 mg and 30 mg) sustained clinical remission and endoscopic healing at 52 weeks (Loftus et al., 2023).
* **Specific Harms:** The safety profile requires careful patient selection. In the maintenance trial, herpes zoster infections, hepatic disorders, and neutropenia occurred more frequently in the upadacitinib groups (particularly at 30 mg and 45 mg doses) than with placebo. Gastrointestinal perforations were rare but were noted slightly more frequently in the upadacitinib cohorts (Loftus et al., 2023).

## Anti-Integrin Therapy

Anti-integrins are gut-selective therapies designed to reduce systemic immunosuppression by specifically blocking the trafficking of lymphocytes to the gastrointestinal tract.

* **Vedolizumab (α4β7 integrin antagonist):** The GEMINI 2 trial evaluated vedolizumab for both induction and maintenance. At week 6, treated patients were significantly more likely to achieve clinical remission (14.5% vs. 6.8% for placebo). Among those who responded to induction, continued maintenance therapy resulted in significantly higher remission rates at 52 weeks (39.0% for 8-week dosing) compared to placebo (Sandborn et al., 2013).
* **Specific Harms:** While vedolizumab was developed to avoid the central nervous system risks of earlier non-selective integrins, the trial noted that vedolizumab was associated with higher overall rates of serious adverse events (24.4% vs. 15.3%), general infections (44.1% vs. 40.2%), and serious infections (5.5% vs. 3.0%) compared to placebo (Sandborn et al., 2013).
