## Overview by drug class

**Anti-TNF agents**
- Infliximab was first shown to induce clinical response in a short-term placebo-controlled trialA short-term study of chimeric monoclonal antibody cA2 to tumor necrosis factor alpha for Crohn's disease was published in the New England Journal of Medicine in 1997. Maintenance efficacy was established afterwardin the ACCENT I randomized trial, published in the Lancet in 2002. A separate trial tested infliximab for fistulizing Crohn's disease in a multicenter, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial of 306 adult patients with draining abdominal or perianal fistulas, evaluating infliximab given at weeks 0, 2, and 6, followed by randomization to placebo or ongoing infliximab through week 54. The comparative value of combination therapy was tested in a randomized, double-blind trial evaluating infliximab monotherapy, azathioprine monotherapy, and combination therapy in 508 adults with moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease who had not had prior immunosuppressive or biologic therapy. Withdrawal after sustained remission was studied in a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled withdrawal trial in patients in clinical, biochemical, and endoscopic remission after at least a year of infliximab maintenance, with 115 patients randomized 1:1 to continued infliximab or placebo for 48 weeks.
- Adalimumab was studied for induction in the CLASSIC-I trial, a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, dose-ranging trial of 299 anti-TNF-naive patients with moderate to severe Crohn's disease. Maintenance efficacy came from the CHARM trial, in which patients received open-label induction with adalimumab and week-4 responders were randomized to placebo, adalimumab every other week, or weekly adalimumab through week 56.
- Certolizumab pegol induction was tested in PRECiSE 1, a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 662 adults with moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease. Maintenance was tested in PRECiSE 2, a 26-week trial of maintenance and withdrawal therapy with certolizumab pegol in patients who had responded to open-label induction.

**Anti-integrin agents**
- Natalizumab was studied in the ENACT-1 induction trial and ENACT-2 maintenance trial, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled studies conducted at 142 centers. A follow-up induction trial in patients with elevated CRP was the ENCORE trial, in which 509 patients with moderately to severely active Crohn's disease and elevated CRP were randomized 1:1 to natalizumab or placebo.
- Vedolizumab was studied in GEMINI 2, a phase 3, randomized, parallel-group, double-blind, placebo-controlled study with separate induction and maintenance trials conducted at 285 centers in 39 countries, in which 368 patients were randomized to vedolizumab or placebo for induction, and 461 responders were randomized to placebo or vedolizumab every 8 or 4 weeks for maintenance. A subcutaneous maintenance formulation was tested in VISIBLE 2, a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial in which week-6 clinical responders to open-label IV induction were randomized 2:1 to subcutaneous vedolizumab or placebo every 2 weeks through week 50.

**IL-12/23 and IL-23 inhibitors**
- Ustekinumab was studied in UNITI-1 (741 patients with prior anti-TNF failure) and UNITI-2 (628 patients in whom conventional therapy had failed), followed by the IM-UNITI maintenance trial in which 397 induction responders were randomized to subcutaneous ustekinumab every 8 or 12 weeks or placebo.
- Risankizumab induction was tested in ADVANCE and MOTIVATE, randomised, double-masked, placebo-controlled phase 3 induction studies in which patients were randomly assigned to intravenous risankizumab (600 mg or 1200 mg) or placebo at weeks 0, 4, and 8. Maintenance was tested in FORTIFY, a phase 3, multicentre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, maintenance withdrawal study across 273 centres in 44 countries, enrolling patients who had responded to risankizumab in ADVANCE or MOTIVATE.

**JAK inhibitor**
- Upadacitinib was studied in two phase 3 induction trials, U-EXCEL and U-EXCEED, and the maintenance trial U-ENDURE, in which a total of 526, 495, and 502 patients respectively underwent randomization, with significantly higher clinical remission and endoscopic response rates for 45-mg upadacitinib versus placebo in both induction trials.
