Stepping up to a biologic or advanced targeted therapy is a significant milestone in managing Crohn’s disease. These medications are powerful because they don't just temporarily mask symptoms; they target specific pathways in the immune system to quiet the inflammation, allowing the bowel lining to heal.

Here is a breakdown of the benefits and harms based on the primary studies and clinical trials that originally established or evaluated these treatments for Crohn's disease.

## Benefits: Inducing and Maintaining Remission

The primary goal of advanced therapy is twofold: **induction** (getting you into remission quickly) and **maintenance** (keeping you there long-term without needing steroids).

* **Anti-TNF Therapies (Infliximab & Adalimumab):** The landmark ACCENT I trial demonstrated that among patients who initially responded to a single infusion of infliximab, 39% to 45% achieved sustained clinical remission by week 30 when they continued receiving treatments every 8 weeks, compared to just 21% on placebo (Hanauer et al., 2002). Similarly, the CHARM trial evaluated adalimumab (a self-injectable anti-TNF). For those who responded initially, about 40% maintained clinical remission at 26 weeks, and 36% to 41% remained in remission at a full year (56 weeks), compared to 12% on placebo (Colombel et al., 2007). Both drugs also proved highly effective at helping patients successfully taper off corticosteroids.
* **Anti-Integrin Therapies (Vedolizumab):** Vedolizumab is designed to be "gut-selective," meaning it primarily targets the immune response in the gastrointestinal tract rather than the whole body. In the GEMINI 2 trial cohort, vedolizumab was significantly superior to placebo for inducing clinical remission at week 6 and maintaining that remission through week 52 in patients with moderate-to-severe disease (Banerjee et al., 2021).
* **IL-23 Inhibitors (Risankizumab):** This newer class of biologics targets a specific inflammatory protein. A post-hoc analysis of the pivotal ADVANCE, MOTIVATE, and FORTIFY trials found that risankizumab induced rapid symptom improvement as early as week 4. By week 12 of induction, 42% to 45% of patients (depending on the trial and dose) achieved strict clinical remission, outperforming placebo significantly. Furthermore, these early symptomatic improvements strongly correlated with long-term endoscopic healing—meaning the actual ulcers in the bowel were resolving (Colombel et al., 2023).
* **JAK Inhibitors (Upadacitinib):** Unlike biologics (which are large proteins administered via injection or IV), JAK inhibitors are oral small molecules. A recent UK multicentre real-world cohort study evaluated upadacitinib in patients with highly refractory Crohn's disease (meaning they had already failed multiple other advanced therapies). Despite treating a very difficult-to-manage group, the study observed clinical remission rates of 64% at week 12, 48% at week 24, and 38% at one year (Elford et al., 2024).

## Harms and Side Effects

Because advanced therapies alter the immune system, they carry specific safety considerations. The risks vary depending on how broadly the medication affects your immune response.

* **Infections:** The most consistent risk across systemic biologics (like anti-TNFs) is an increased susceptibility to infections. In ACCENT I, the incidence of serious infections was notable but generally similar between the infliximab and placebo groups during the trial (Hanauer et al., 2002). In the risankizumab trials, the drug showed minimal immunogenicity (meaning the body rarely forms antibodies against the drug that would stop it from working), but a small number of serious infections were still reported across thousands of patients (Colombel et al., 2023).
* **Drug-Specific Risks:** The oral JAK inhibitor upadacitinib carries a known, specific risk for herpes zoster (shingles) reactivation. While it is highly effective, patients considering JAK inhibitors are usually advised to receive the shingles vaccine beforehand. However, in the real-world cohort, it was generally well-tolerated with high patient persistence (Elford et al., 2024).
* **Injection/Infusion Reactions:** Adalimumab is known to occasionally cause injection-site reactions (pain, redness, or swelling where the shot is given). However, in the CHARM trial, these were generally mild, and withdrawal from the study due to an injection-site reaction was exceptionally rare (Colombel et al., 2007).
