Navigating treatment decisions for Crohn's disease requires looking at the raw, primary trial data to accurately weigh efficacy against safety profiles. Here are the core benefits and harms from the landmark Phase 3 clinical trials for four distinct classes of advanced therapies.

## Upadacitinib (JAK1 Inhibitor)

Upadacitinib is a small-molecule targeted therapy that works systemically by inhibiting the Janus kinase (JAK) pathway.

* **Benefits:** In the U-EXCEL and U-EXCEED induction trials, 45 mg of daily upadacitinib achieved clinical remission in 39–50% of patients at week 12, significantly outperforming placebo (21–29%). During the U-ENDURE maintenance phase, a 30 mg daily dose sustained clinical remission in 48% of patients at week 52. The drug also demonstrated rapid and significant improvements in endoscopic response (Loftus et al., 2023).
* **Harms:** Because it modulates the immune system broadly via the JAK pathway, specific safety signals emerged. Herpes zoster infections, hepatic disorders, and neutropenia were more frequent at the 30 mg maintenance dose compared to placebo. Gastrointestinal perforations were also reported in a small number of patients receiving the drug (Loftus et al., 2023).

## Risankizumab (Anti-IL-23)

Risankizumab is a monoclonal antibody that targets the p19 subunit of interleukin-23, a cytokine heavily implicated in intestinal inflammation.

* **Benefits:** The ADVANCE and MOTIVATE trials demonstrated that intravenous risankizumab successfully met coprimary endpoints for clinical remission and endoscopic response. At week 12, clinical remission rates were 40–45% compared to 20–25% for placebo, even in populations that had previously failed other biologics (D'Haens et al., 2022).
* **Harms:** The safety profile was highly favorable. The overall incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events was similar between the risankizumab and placebo groups during the induction phase, with very low immunogenicity and few serious infections (D'Haens et al., 2022).

## Ustekinumab (Anti-IL-12/23)

Ustekinumab targets the shared p40 subunit of both IL-12 and IL-23.

* **Benefits:** In the UNITI-1 and UNITI-2 trials, a single intravenous, weight-based induction dose yielded significantly higher clinical response rates at week 6 (up to 34.3%) compared to placebo (21.5%). This held true for both biologic-naïve patients and those who had previously failed anti-TNF therapies. Subsequent subcutaneous maintenance therapy effectively sustained this remission over 52 weeks (Feagan et al., 2016).
* **Harms:** Ustekinumab was well-tolerated, showing an adverse event profile comparable to placebo. The trials identified low rates of severe infections and no disproportionate safety or malignancy signals during the study period (Feagan et al., 2016).

## Vedolizumab (Anti-α4β7 Integrin)

Vedolizumab uses a gut-selective mechanism, blocking the α4β7 integrin to prevent inflammatory white blood cells from entering the gastrointestinal tract.

* **Benefits:** The GEMINI 2 trial showed that at week 6, 14.5% of patients achieved clinical remission versus 6.8% on placebo. While induction response can sometimes be slower than systemic biologics, patients who responded to induction and continued maintenance therapy were significantly more likely to sustain clinical remission and corticosteroid-free remission at week 52 (Sandborn et al., 2013).
* **Harms:** Overall adverse events were generally mild and comparable to placebo (e.g., headache, nasopharyngitis). Crucially, because its mechanism confines lymphocyte suppression to the gut, it avoids the risk of progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML)—a severe brain infection associated with older, non-selective integrin inhibitors (Sandborn et al., 2013).
