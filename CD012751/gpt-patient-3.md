## Scope

I prioritized **randomized primary trials in adults with moderately to severely active luminal Crohn’s disease**, published through July 2026. I excluded systematic reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, editorials, committee statements and narrative reviews. Postoperative prevention, pediatric Crohn’s disease and fistula-specific trials are outside this summary.

“Clinical remission” usually means a Crohn’s Disease Activity Index, or CDAI, below 150. It is symptom-based and is **not equivalent to endoscopic healing**. Newer studies increasingly include endoscopic response or remission.

## Evidence from individual trials

### Anti-TNF treatments

**Infliximab**

In the original 108-patient induction trial, one infliximab infusion produced clinical remission at week 4 in **33% versus 4%** with placebo—an absolute difference of 29 percentage points. Clinical response occurred in 65% versus 17%. Overall adverse-event rates were similar, although infusion reactions and isolated serious infections occurred. ([PubMed][1])

ACCENT I enrolled 573 patients, all of whom initially received infliximab; the maintenance comparison was restricted to initial responders. At week 30, remission occurred in **39% with continued 5-mg/kg infliximab and 45% with the 10-mg/kg strategy, versus 21% after withdrawal to placebo**. Median time to loss of response was 38 weeks or longer than 54 weeks with maintenance, versus 19 weeks after withdrawal. Serious-infection rates were reported as similar among groups. ([PubMed][2])

**Adalimumab**

In anti-TNF-naïve patients in CLASSIC-I, the currently used high-dose induction regimen produced week-4 remission in **36% versus 12%** with placebo. Overall adverse-event frequencies were similar, but injection-site reactions were more common with adalimumab. ([PubMed][3])

GAIN studied patients who had lost response to or could not tolerate infliximab. Week-4 remission occurred in **21% with adalimumab versus 7% with placebo**, showing that a second anti-TNF can work after failure of the first. ([PMC][4])

In CHARM, among patients who initially responded to open-label adalimumab, remission at week 56 occurred in **36% with every-other-week dosing and 41% with weekly dosing, versus 12% after withdrawal to placebo**. No significant efficacy advantage was shown for weekly over every-other-week administration. ([PubMed][5])

**Certolizumab pegol**

PRECISE 1 produced a modest improvement in clinical response, but **remission was not significantly different from placebo** at the principal induction assessments. Serious adverse events occurred in 10% versus 7%, and serious infections in 2% versus less than 1%. ([New England Journal of Medicine][6])

PRECISE 2 then randomized patients who had responded to induction. At week 26, remission was maintained in **48% with certolizumab versus 29% after withdrawal to placebo**. Serious infections occurred in 3% versus less than 1%, including one case of pulmonary tuberculosis in a certolizumab recipient also taking azathioprine. ([New England Journal of Medicine][7])

### Integrin-directed treatments

**Vedolizumab**

In GEMINI 2, week-6 clinical remission occurred in **14.5% with vedolizumab versus 6.8% with placebo**, although the other co-primary induction outcome—CDAI-100 response—was not significantly different. Among induction responders, week-52 remission was **39.0% with dosing every eight weeks and 36.4% every four weeks, versus 21.6% after withdrawal to placebo**. In this trial, serious adverse events were 24.4% versus 15.3%, infections 44.1% versus 40.2%, and serious infections 5.5% versus 3.0%. ([New England Journal of Medicine][8])

GEMINI 3 illustrates vedolizumab’s sometimes slower onset after anti-TNF failure. Remission at week 6 was not significantly different—15.2% versus 12.1%—but at week 10 it was **26.6% versus 12.1%**. Adverse-event results were similar between groups. ([PubMed][9])

**Natalizumab**

In ENCORE, sustained remission through week 12 occurred in **26% with natalizumab versus 16% with placebo**, with similar short-term adverse-event frequencies. ([PubMed][10])

In ENACT, the overall induction remission difference was not statistically significant, but among natalizumab responders, continued maintenance produced sustained remission through week 36 in **44% versus 26% after withdrawal**. Critically, a patient in the open-label extension died from JC-virus-associated progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy, or PML. This rare but potentially fatal risk sharply distinguishes natalizumab from gut-selective vedolizumab. ([PubMed][11])

### Interleukin-directed biologics

**Ustekinumab: IL-12/23 p40**

UNITI-1 enrolled patients with anti-TNF failure; week-8 remission with the approximately 6-mg/kg induction dose was **20.9% versus 7.3%** with placebo. In UNITI-2, which primarily enrolled patients whose conventional therapy had failed, remission was **40.2% versus 19.6%**. Among ustekinumab induction responders in IM-UNITI, week-44 remission was **53.1% with every-eight-week dosing and 48.8% every 12 weeks, versus 35.9% after withdrawal**. Adverse-event rates were similar among groups within the trials. ([New England Journal of Medicine][12])

**Risankizumab: selective IL-23 p19**

In ADVANCE, week-12 CDAI remission was **45% and 42% with the two risankizumab doses versus 25% with placebo**; endoscopic response was 40% and 32% versus 12%. In the more treatment-refractory MOTIVATE population, remission was **42% and 40% versus 20%**, and endoscopic response 29% and 34% versus 11%. Overall treatment-emergent adverse-event frequencies were similar. One death in a risankizumab group was considered unrelated to treatment. ([PubMed][13])

In FORTIFY, among induction responders, the 360-mg maintenance regimen produced week-52 CDAI remission in **52% versus 41% after withdrawal** and endoscopic response in **47% versus 22%**. Overall adverse-event rates were similar across groups. ([PubMed][14])

**Mirikizumab: selective IL-23 p19**

VIVID-1 used “treat-through” composite endpoints. Clinical response at week 12 followed by endoscopic response at week 52 occurred in **38.0% with mirikizumab versus 9.0% with placebo**. Clinical response at week 12 followed by CDAI remission at week 52 occurred in **45.4% versus 19.6%**. Serious adverse events occurred in 10.3% with mirikizumab, 10.7% with ustekinumab and 17.1% with placebo. None of the reported deaths was considered treatment related. ([PubMed][15])

**Guselkumab: selective IL-23 p19**

GALAXI-2 and GALAXI-3 also used treat-through composite outcomes. Clinical response at week 12 followed by clinical remission at week 48 occurred in approximately **47%–55% with guselkumab versus 12%–13% with placebo**. Response followed by endoscopic response occurred in approximately **34%–39% versus 5%–6%**. Serious adverse events occurred in 7%–11% across the guselkumab regimens, 12% with ustekinumab and 15% with placebo; no deaths were reported. ([PubMed][16])

### Oral advanced treatment

**Upadacitinib: selective JAK inhibitor**

In U-EXCEL and U-EXCEED, week-12 clinical remission occurred in **49.5% versus 29.1%** and **38.9% versus 21.1%**, respectively, with upadacitinib versus placebo. Endoscopic response was **45.5% versus 13.1%** and **34.6% versus 3.5%**.

Among induction responders in U-ENDURE, week-52 remission was **37.3% with 15 mg and 47.6% with 30 mg, versus 15.1% after withdrawal**; endoscopic response was **27.6%, 40.1% and 7.3%**, respectively. Herpes zoster was more frequent with the higher induction and maintenance doses. Hepatic abnormalities and neutropenia were more frequent with 30-mg maintenance. Gastrointestinal perforations occurred in four induction patients and one patient in each maintenance-dose group. ([PubMed][17])

## Direct comparative evidence

**Infliximab plus azathioprine versus either alone — SONIC:** In patients naïve to both biologics and immunomodulators, corticosteroid-free remission at week 26 was **56.8% with combination therapy, 44.4% with infliximab alone and 30.0% with azathioprine alone**. Mucosal healing occurred in 43.9%, 30.1% and 16.5%, respectively. Serious infections occurred in 3.9%, 4.9% and 5.6%. This establishes an efficacy advantage for starting infliximab with azathioprine in this particular population, but the trial was too short and small to settle uncommon long-term thiopurine harms. ([New England Journal of Medicine][18])

**Ustekinumab versus adalimumab — SEAVUE:** In biologic-naïve patients receiving monotherapy, week-52 remission was **65% with ustekinumab versus 61% with adalimumab**, a nonsignificant difference. Serious infections occurred in 2% and 3%, respectively. ([PubMed][19])

**Risankizumab versus ustekinumab — SEQUENCE:** In patients with previous anti-TNF failure or intolerance, week-24 clinical remission was **58.6% with risankizumab versus 39.5% with ustekinumab**, meeting the prespecified noninferiority criterion. At week 48, risankizumab was superior for endoscopic remission: **31.8% versus 16.2%**. Overall adverse-event incidence was similar; serious adverse events were 10.3% versus 17.4%, with much of the difference attributable to worsening Crohn’s disease. ([New England Journal of Medicine][20])

## Important qualifications

1. **Maintenance percentages are often enriched.** ACCENT I, CHARM, PRECISE 2, GEMINI 2, IM-UNITI, FORTIFY and U-ENDURE rerandomized people who had already responded to induction. Their maintenance remission rates should not be interpreted as the probability that every person starting the medication will be in remission one year later. ([PubMed][2])

2. **Cross-trial percentages are not rankings.** Populations differed substantially in prior biologic failures, disease severity, placebo response, duration and outcome definitions. Treat-through composite endpoints from VIVID and GALAXI are particularly different from responder-withdrawal maintenance trials.

3. **Randomized trials characterize common, short-term harms better than rare or delayed harms.** Most biologic trials lasted roughly one year and enrolled hundreds rather than tens of thousands of patients. They cannot precisely estimate uncommon malignancies, opportunistic infections or other events with long latency.

4. The most distinctive trial-level harm signals were **PML with natalizumab**, **herpes zoster, laboratory abnormalities and gastrointestinal perforation with upadacitinib**, infections including occasional tuberculosis with anti-TNF therapy, and the higher serious-adverse-event rate observed with vedolizumab in GEMINI 2. ([PubMed][11])

Overall, the primary trials show that several mechanistically different treatments can induce and maintain remission. The most relevant choice depends heavily on previous biologic exposure, need for rapid symptom control, objective endoscopic disease, fistulas or strictures, infection history, age and cardiovascular or thrombotic risk, pregnancy plans, and willingness to use an infusion, injection or oral treatment.
