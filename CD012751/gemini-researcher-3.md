To help with your evidence synthesis, I’ve bypassed the systematic reviews, meta-analyses, and clinical guidelines to pull the landmark, primary randomized controlled trials (RCTs) that established the efficacy and safety profiles for the major biologic and small-molecule therapies in Crohn’s disease.

Here is a breakdown of the core primary studies detailing the benefits (clinical and endoscopic remission) and harms (adverse event profiles) of these advanced treatments.

### Anti-TNF Therapies

**Infliximab (ACCENT I Trial)**
The ACCENT I trial was the pivotal study that shifted the paradigm of biologic use from episodic to scheduled maintenance therapy.

* **Benefits:** Hanauer et al. (2002) demonstrated that patients who responded to a single induction dose of infliximab were significantly more likely to achieve and maintain clinical remission at weeks 30 and 54 if they continued receiving scheduled 8-weekly infusions, compared to those receiving placebo. Scheduled maintenance also facilitated corticosteroid tapering and delayed the loss of response.
* **Harms:** The incidence of serious infections and development of anti-drug antibodies were notable, though the overall safety profile was consistent with expected immunosuppression.

### Anti-Integrin Therapies

**Vedolizumab (GEMINI 2 Trial)**
Vedolizumab utilizes a gut-selective mechanism by targeting the α4β7 integrin, effectively preventing lymphocytes from trafficking into the gut mucosa.

* **Benefits:** Sandborn et al. (2013) evaluated this in the GEMINI 2 induction and maintenance trials. At week 6, patients receiving vedolizumab were significantly more likely to achieve clinical remission than those on placebo. Among responders, every-8-week and every-4-week maintenance dosing sustained clinical remission at week 52.
* **Harms:** Because it does not cause systemic immunosuppression, vedolizumab avoided the widespread infectious complications seen with systemic agents. Adverse events were largely limited to nasopharyngitis, headache, and arthralgia.

### Anti-IL-12/23 Inhibitors

**Ustekinumab (UNITI Trials)**
Ustekinumab targets the p40 subunit shared by both interleukin-12 and interleukin-23. The clinical program consisted of two induction trials (UNITI-1 for anti-TNF failures, UNITI-2 for conventional therapy failures) and a maintenance trial (IM-UNITI).

* **Benefits:** Feagan et al. (2016) reported that intravenous induction generated significantly higher clinical response rates at week 6 compared to placebo across both refractory populations. In the maintenance phase, subcutaneous dosing every 8 or 12 weeks effectively maintained remission at week 44.
* **Harms:** The rate of adverse events, including serious infections and malignancies, was comparable between the ustekinumab and placebo groups, establishing a highly favorable safety profile for long-term use.

### Anti-IL-23 Specific Inhibitors

**Risankizumab (ADVANCE, MOTIVATE, and FORTIFY Trials)**
Risankizumab selectively targets the p19 subunit of IL-23, isolating the inflammatory pathway without affecting IL-12.

* **Benefits:** The ADVANCE and MOTIVATE induction trials showed that intravenous risankizumab (600 mg or 1200 mg) achieved both co-primary endpoints—clinical remission and endoscopic response—by week 12 (D'Haens et al., 2022). The FORTIFY maintenance withdrawal study confirmed these benefits hold long-term, with 360 mg subcutaneous dosing significantly outperforming placebo for clinical remission and endoscopic healing at 52 weeks (Ferrante et al., 2022).
* **Harms:** Maintenance treatment was well tolerated, with worsening Crohn's disease, headache, and arthralgia being the most common adverse events. Serious infection rates did not significantly differ from placebo.

### Small Molecule / JAK Inhibitors

**Upadacitinib (U-EXCEL, U-EXCEED, and U-ENDURE Trials)**
Upadacitinib is an oral, reversible, and selective Janus kinase 1 (JAK1) inhibitor.

* **Benefits:** Evaluated in the U-EXCEL and U-EXCEED induction trials, 45 mg daily dosing resulted in rapid clinical remission and endoscopic response by week 12 compared to placebo. In the U-ENDURE maintenance trial, both 15 mg and 30 mg daily doses maintained clinical remission and endoscopic response at week 52 (Loftus et al., 2023).
* **Harms:** Due to its mechanism of action, upadacitinib carries a distinct safety profile. The trials recorded dose-dependent increases in adverse events such as herpes zoster infections, hepatic disorders, and neutropenia, which were most pronounced in the 30 mg maintenance cohort.
