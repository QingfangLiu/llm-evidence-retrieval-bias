## Summary of findings

Randomized controlled trials over the past ~25 years have established several biologic and small-molecule "advanced" therapies as effective for inducing and/or maintaining remission in moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease, while also identifying consistent safety signals.

**Anti-TNF agents** (infliximab, adalimumab, certolizumab pegol) were the first class studied. In ACCENT I, patients who responded to a single infliximab infusion were more likely to sustain remission if given repeat infliximab infusions every 8 weeks than if switched to placebo, with remission rates of 39–45% versus 21% at week 30. The CHARM trial similarly found that adalimumab, given every other week or weekly, was significantly more effective than placebo for maintaining remission through week 56. Certolizumab pegol showed a modest benefit on response rates in PRECISE 1 but did not significantly improve remission, while PRECiSE 2 found 63% of certolizumab-treated responders maintained a 100-point response versus 36% on placebo. The SONIC trial directly compared infliximab, azathioprine, and their combination in 508 treatment-naive patients, and found combination therapy produced higher corticosteroid-free remission (56.8%) than either infliximab (44.4%) or azathioprine (30.0%) alone, establishing combination therapy as more effective than monotherapy.

**Gut-selective and IL-12/23 agents** followed. In GEMINI 2, vedolizumab-treated patients were more likely than placebo patients to be in remission at week 6, and responders who continued vedolizumab were more likely to be in remission at week 52; adverse events were somewhat more common with vedolizumab. The UNITI-1 and UNITI-2 induction trials and the IM-UNITI maintenance trial established ustekinumab's efficacy in both anti-TNF-experienced (UNITI-1) and conventional-therapy-failure (UNITI-2) populations.

**Newer IL-23 and JAK-inhibitor agents** have since outperformed placebo in later trials. In the ADVANCE and MOTIVATE induction trials, risankizumab was effective and well tolerated in patients with prior inadequate response to conventional or biologic therapy, and subcutaneous risankizumab was confirmed safe and effective for maintenance in the FORTIFY trial. Head-to-head data are also emerging: the SEQUENCE trial directly compared risankizumab with ustekinumab in patients who had failed anti-TNF therapy, finding lower rates of Crohn's-related and all-cause hospitalization with risankizumab. For the oral JAK inhibitor upadacitinib, the U-EXCEL and U-EXCEED induction trials and U-ENDURE maintenance trial found significantly higher clinical remission and endoscopic response rates with 45-mg upadacitinib versus placebo at week 12, and with 15-mg or 30-mg upadacitinib versus placebo at week 52, though herpes zoster infections, hepatic disorders, and neutropenia occurred more frequently in upadacitinib-treated patients.

Across trials, common harms reported include infections (including serious infections and herpes zoster), infusion or injection-site reactions, and in some agents (e.g., upadacitinib) hepatic and hematologic effects — though absolute rates and comparative safety vary by drug class and require reading the full safety sections of each trial.
