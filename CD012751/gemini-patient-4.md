Living with Crohn’s is tough, and making the call to start advanced therapies can feel like a heavy decision. It makes complete sense that you want to look at the hard data—specifically the primary clinical trials—before moving forward.

I've bypassed the reviews and opinions to pull the landmark Phase 3 trials that established the efficacy and safety of four major classes of advanced Crohn's treatments. In these studies, **induction** refers to the initial phase of getting the disease under control, while **maintenance** refers to keeping it there over the long term.

## 1. Infliximab (Anti-TNF)

**Primary Trial:** ACCENT I (2002)
Infliximab was one of the first major biologic therapies for Crohn's, neutralizing a key inflammatory protein called Tumor Necrosis Factor (TNF).

* **Benefits:** In this trial, patients who responded to an initial dose were given maintenance doses every 8 weeks. By week 30, **39% to 45%** of those on maintenance infliximab were in clinical remission, compared to just 21% on placebo. It also significantly improved the ability to stop taking corticosteroids.
* **Harms:** The primary risk was a higher incidence of infections due to immune suppression. The trial noted that serious infections were consistent with previous data on the drug.

## 2. Ustekinumab (Anti-IL-12/23)

**Primary Trials:** UNITI-1, UNITI-2, and IM-UNITI (2016)
This biologic targets two different inflammatory pathways (Interleukin 12 and 23).

* **Benefits:** The induction trials showed that roughly **34%** of patients achieved a clinical response by week 6, significantly outperforming the placebo group (21.5%). Patients who continued into the maintenance phase had significantly higher rates of sustained remission at week 44 compared to placebo.
* **Harms:** Overall adverse events were remarkably similar to the placebo group in these specific trials. However, as an immunomodulator, there is a baseline, class-wide risk of common infections like nasopharyngitis or upper respiratory tract infections.

## 3. Risankizumab (Anti-IL-23)

**Primary Trials:** ADVANCE and MOTIVATE (2022)
A newer biologic that specifically targets the IL-23 pathway, offering a more narrowed immune blockade.

* **Benefits:** At week 12 of induction, **40% to 45%** of patients achieved clinical remission (versus 20-25% on placebo). Crucially, the trial also looked at endoscopic healing (actual visible healing of the gut lining), showing significant improvements over placebo.
* **Harms:** The overall incidence of side effects during induction was similar to placebo. Standard biologic risks apply, but the narrowed mechanism aims to preserve more normal immune function than older, broader drugs.

## 4. Upadacitinib (JAK Inhibitor)

**Primary Trials:** U-EXCEL, U-EXCEED, and U-ENDURE (2023)
Unlike the biologics above, this is an oral small-molecule drug that works inside the cell to block inflammatory signaling (Janus Kinase pathways).

* **Benefits:** It acts incredibly fast. At 12 weeks, **39% to 49.5%** of patients were in clinical remission, compared to 21-29% on placebo. At 52 weeks of maintenance, up to **47.6%** maintained remission.
* **Harms:** The trials flagged specific mechanism-related risks. Herpes zoster (shingles) occurred more frequently. Higher doses also showed increased rates of hepatic (liver) disorders and neutropenia (low white blood cell counts). Rare gastrointestinal perforations were also observed.
