Navigating advanced therapies for Crohn's disease requires balancing the robust efficacy of these agents in achieving clinical and endoscopic remission against their specific safety profiles. By looking strictly at the landmark, phase 3 primary randomized controlled trials for these drugs, we can clearly delineate the benefits and harms of each class to help guide treatment decisions.

## Anti-Tumor Necrosis Factor (Anti-TNF)

**Infliximab**
The SONIC trial established the efficacy of infliximab, highlighting the value of combination therapy over conventional monotherapy.

* **Benefits:** Infliximab combined with azathioprine achieved significantly higher rates of corticosteroid-free clinical remission and mucosal healing at week 26 compared to either azathioprine or infliximab used alone (Colombel et al., 2010).
* **Harms:** Serious infections and infusion reactions are the primary risks. The trial confirmed that opportunistic infections, while relatively uncommon, are a recognized risk of systemic anti-TNF therapy, alongside the risk of developing anti-drug antibodies (Colombel et al., 2010).

## Anti-Integrin Therapy

**Vedolizumab**
The GEMINI 2 trial evaluated vedolizumab, a gut-selective $\alpha_4\beta_7$ integrin antagonist.

* **Benefits:** Patients receiving vedolizumab were significantly more likely to achieve clinical remission at week 6 and maintain it through week 52 compared to the placebo group. The agent proved effective even in patients who had previously failed anti-TNF therapies (Sandborn et al., 2013).
* **Harms:** Because its mechanism is gut-selective, systemic immunosuppression risks are minimized. The trial reported headache, nasopharyngitis, and joint pain as common adverse events, but serious infection rates remained comparable to those in the placebo group (Sandborn et al., 2013).

## Interleukin (IL) Inhibitors

**Ustekinumab (IL-12/23)**
The UNITI-1, UNITI-2, and IM-UNITI trials evaluated ustekinumab in patients with moderately to severely active disease.

* **Benefits:** Intravenous induction resulted in significantly higher clinical response rates by week 6 compared to placebo, regardless of prior anti-TNF failure. Transitioning to subcutaneous maintenance therapy successfully sustained clinical remission through week 44 (Feagan et al., 2016).
* **Harms:** The overall adverse event rate, including serious infections and malignancies, was similar to placebo, establishing a favorable safety profile with very low immunogenicity (Feagan et al., 2016).

**Risankizumab (Selective IL-23)**
The ADVANCE and MOTIVATE trials investigated risankizumab, which specifically targets the p19 subunit of IL-23.

* **Benefits:** Risankizumab met all co-primary endpoints at week 12, demonstrating robust clinical remission and deep endoscopic response rates compared to placebo, establishing it as a highly effective induction agent (D'Haens et al., 2022).
* **Harms:** The drug was well tolerated during induction, with the overall incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events (such as upper respiratory infections and headache) remaining comparable between the treatment and placebo groups (D'Haens et al., 2022).

## Janus Kinase (JAK) Inhibitors

**Upadacitinib**
The U-EXCEL, U-EXCEED, and U-ENDURE phase 3 trials introduced this oral, reversible small-molecule JAK inhibitor to the Crohn's landscape.

* **Benefits:** Upadacitinib induction and maintenance therapy proved highly superior to placebo for both clinical remission and endoscopic response. A significant proportion of patients achieved target mucosal and clinical outcomes as early as week 12 and sustained them at week 52 (Loftus et al., 2023).
* **Harms:** The safety profile reflects its broad systemic mechanism. The trials reported an increased incidence of herpes zoster infections, hepatic disorders, and neutropenia compared to placebo. Notably, gastrointestinal perforations occurred in a small number of patients receiving the drug (Loftus et al., 2023).
