Here is a synthesis of pivotal primary clinical trials—specifically focusing on the induction and maintenance of remission—for the major classes of biologic and advanced therapies used in Crohn's disease. I have explicitly excluded systematic reviews and meta-analyses, drawing the efficacy (benefits) and safety (harms) data directly from foundational and recent Phase 2/3 randomized controlled trials.

## Anti-TNFα Therapies

**Adalimumab**

* **Benefits (Maintenance):** In the landmark CHARM trial, adalimumab demonstrated significant efficacy for maintaining clinical remission. Among patients who responded to induction, 40% (every other week) and 47% (weekly) achieved clinical remission at 26 weeks, and 36% and 41% at 56 weeks, respectively, compared to 17% and 12% for placebo (Colombel et al., 2007).
* **Harms:** The safety profile was consistent with standard anti-TNF risks. Interestingly, the placebo group experienced a higher rate of treatment discontinuation due to adverse events (13.4%) than the adalimumab groups (4.7%–6.9%) (Colombel et al., 2007).

## Anti-Integrin Therapies (Gut-Selective)

**Vedolizumab**

* **Benefits (Induction & Maintenance):** The GEMINI 2 trial established vedolizumab's efficacy, showing that 14.5% of patients achieved clinical remission at week 6 (vs. 6.8% for placebo). Among induction responders, those who continued maintenance vedolizumab were significantly more likely to maintain remission at week 52 compared to those switched to placebo (Sandborn et al., 2013).
* **Harms:** Because it selectively targets the α4β7 integrin in the gut, vedolizumab avoids widespread systemic immunosuppression. Adverse events were comparable to placebo, offering a highly favorable safety profile for maintenance therapy (Sandborn et al., 2013).

## Anti-IL-12/23 Therapies

**Ustekinumab**

* **Benefits (Induction & Maintenance):** The UNITI-1 and UNITI-2 induction trials showed that a single intravenous dose (130 mg or ~6 mg/kg) yielded clinical response rates around 34% at week 6 (vs. 21.5% for placebo). Subsequent maintenance therapy successfully sustained these responses over time (Feagan et al., 2016).
* **Harms:** Ustekinumab demonstrated a robust safety profile across the trials, with adverse event rates—including infections—similar to those observed in the placebo groups (Feagan et al., 2016).

## Anti-IL-23p19 Therapies

These newer biologics specifically target the p19 subunit of IL-23, offering more refined mechanisms than the earlier dual IL-12/23 inhibitors.

* **Risankizumab:** In a post hoc analysis of the ADVANCE, MOTIVATE, and FORTIFY Phase 3 studies, intravenous induction provided early and significant symptom relief (reductions in stool frequency and abdominal pain) as early as week 1. This early symptom improvement was highly predictive of achieving both clinical and endoscopic endpoints at 12 and 52 weeks (Colombel et al., 2023).
* **Guselkumab:** The Phase 2 GALAXI-1 trial evaluated multiple dosing regimens against placebo. At 12 weeks, all guselkumab doses induced significantly greater rates of clinical remission (45.9%–57.4% vs. 16.4% for placebo) and endoscopic responses. Safety event rates were similar across all treatment groups (Sandborn et al., 2022).
* **Mirikizumab:** The Phase 3 VIVID-1 study evaluated mirikizumab across different baseline disease locations. Mirikizumab met co-primary endpoints for clinical and endoscopic remission over 52 weeks in colonic and ileocolonic disease, outperforming placebo significantly and maintaining improvements up to one year (Barnes et al., 2024).
