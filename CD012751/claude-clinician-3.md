## Anti-TNF agents

**Infliximab**
- **ACCENT I** (Hanauer et al., *Lancet* 2002) — 573 patients received induction infliximab 5 mg/kg at week 0; week-2 responders were randomized to placebo, infliximab 5 mg/kg, or 5 mg/kg→10 mg/kg maintenance q8w through week 46. Scheduled maintenance significantly increased remission at week 30 (39–45% vs 21%) and prolonged time to loss of response.
- **SONIC** (Colombel et al., *NEJM* 2010) — 508 immunomodulator/biologic-naïve patients randomized to infliximab monotherapy, azathioprine monotherapy, or combination therapy. Combination therapy produced higher corticosteroid-free remission at week 26 than either monotherapy.

**Adalimumab**
- **CLASSIC I** (induction) and **CHARM** (Colombel et al., *Gastroenterology* 2007) — CHARM randomized 778 induction responders to placebo, adalimumab 40 mg eow, or 40 mg weekly through week 56; remission at weeks 26 and 56 was significantly higher with adalimumab (36–41% vs 12%).

**Certolizumab pegol**
- **PRECiSE 1** (Sandborn et al., *NEJM* 2007) — induction/maintenance placebo-controlled trial.
- **PRECiSE 2** (Schreiber et al., *NEJM* 2007) — 428 induction responders randomized to certolizumab 400 mg or placebo q4w through week 26; maintenance response/remission rates were significantly higher with active drug.

## Anti-integrin

**Vedolizumab**
- **GEMINI 2** (Sandborn et al., *NEJM* 2013) — integrated induction/maintenance trial; 368 patients randomized to vedolizumab or placebo for induction (cohort 1); 461 induction responders randomized to placebo or vedolizumab q4w/q8w for 52-week maintenance. Week-52 remission: 39.0% (q8w) and 36.4% (q4w) vs 21.6% (placebo).
- **VISIBLE 2** (2022, *J Crohns Colitis*) — subcutaneous vedolizumab maintenance RCT; 48.0% remission vs 34.3% with placebo at week 52.

## Anti-IL-12/23

**Ustekinumab**
- **UNITI-1** and **UNITI-2** (induction) and **IM-UNITI** (maintenance) — Feagan et al., *NEJM* 2016. UNITI-1 (741 anti-TNF failures) and UNITI-2 (628 conventional-therapy failures) established induction efficacy; IM-UNITI randomized 397 responders to ustekinumab q8w, q12w, or placebo, with week-44 remission significantly favoring active treatment.
- Earlier phase 2b trial (Sandborn et al., *NEJM* 2012) — anti-TNF-refractory patients, dose-ranging induction/maintenance.

## Anti-IL-23 (selective p19)

**Risankizumab**
- **ADVANCE** and **MOTIVATE** (induction, D'Haens et al., *Lancet* 2022) — placebo-controlled induction trials in conventional-therapy failures (ADVANCE, n=850) and biologic-experienced patients (MOTIVATE, n=569); risankizumab significantly improved clinical remission and endoscopic response at week 12.
- **FORTIFY** (maintenance, Ferrante et al., *Lancet* 2022) — withdrawal-design maintenance trial in ADVANCE/MOTIVATE responders; subcutaneous risankizumab maintained remission significantly better than withdrawal to placebo.

## Head-to-head comparative trials

- **SEAVUE** (Sands et al., *Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol* 2022) — 386 biologic-naïve patients randomized 1:1 to ustekinumab vs adalimumab (labeled dosing, no forced dose escalation) through 1 year; no significant difference in clinical or endoscopic remission between agents.
- **SEQUENCE** (Peyrin-Biroulet, Dubinsky et al., presented UEGW/DDW 2023–2024; published *Am J Gastroenterol* 2024) — 527 anti-TNF-experienced patients randomized to risankizumab vs ustekinumab; risankizumab non-inferior for week-24 clinical remission and superior for week-48 endoscopic remission.
