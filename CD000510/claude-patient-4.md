I'll research primary studies comparing prophylactic versus selective (rescue) surfactant administration in preterm infants at risk for respiratory distress syndrome (RDS).Here's what the primary-study literature shows on prophylactic vs. selective (rescue) surfactant for preterm infants at risk of RDS, based on individual randomized controlled trials (not reviews/meta-analyses).

## What "prophylactic" vs "selective" means in these trials

- **Prophylactic**: surfactant given shortly after birth (often within minutes to ~30 min), before RDS is clinically confirmed, to infants judged at high risk based on gestational age.
- **Selective/rescue**: surfactant withheld until the infant develops clinical/radiographic signs of RDS or meets an oxygen-requirement threshold (e.g., FiO₂ >0.30–0.40), often while being supported on CPAP.

## General pattern across the trials

**Potential benefits of prophylactic dosing** (seen in some earlier trials, especially before routine CPAP/less-invasive delivery was standard): an initial improvement in respiratory status and a decrease in the incidence of RDS, and pooled analyses of these trials found reduced pneumothorax, pulmonary interstitial emphysema, mortality, and death or bronchopulmonary dysplasia associated with prophylactic administration.

**Potential harms/drawbacks**: prophylactic dosing requires intubating and treating infants who would never have gone on to develop RDS — meaningful over-treatment, especially now that CPAP can stabilize many infants without any surfactant. This is why more recent trials (conducted in the CPAP era) generally favor a selective/early-rescue approach over routine prophylaxis, since stabilization using CPAP with surfactant given only to infants who develop breathing problems may be as or more effective than the more aggressive prophylactic approach.

**Timing still matters within "selective" treatment**: several trials look at whether even the selective approach should be triggered earlier. One narrative synthesis of these RCTs notes initial trials of prophylactic surfactant vs. placebo showed reduced mortality (RR 0.60) and air leaks (RR 0.40) favoring surfactant, but that no RCTs have directly compared prophylactic treatment to early (<2–3 hours) selective treatment — only observational cohorts, which showed similar mortality between prophylactic and early-selective approaches, with morbidity differences too inconsistent to interpret given observational design limitations.

## Notes on individual trials

- **Kendig et al. (NEJM 1991)** — directly randomized infants <30 weeks to prophylactic vs. rescue surfactant.
- **Dunn, Shennan, Zyack, Possmayer (Pediatrics 1991)** — RCT of bovine surfactant, prophylaxis vs. treatment, in infants <30 weeks.
- **Kattwinkel et al. (Pediatrics)** — found prophylactic calf lung surfactant extract more effective than early treatment of RDS in neonates 29–32 weeks' gestation.
- **Bevilacqua, Chernev, Parmigiani et al.** — Italian-Bulgarian RCT of prophylaxis vs. rescue treatment.
- **OSIRIS Collaborative Group (Lancet 1992)** — large multicenter RCT of early vs. delayed synthetic surfactant administration.
- **Sandri et al., CURPAP Study Group (Pediatrics 2010)** — prophylactic or early selective surfactant combined with nCPAP in very preterm infants, a modern-era trial directly testing whether prophylaxis adds benefit over early selective dosing when CPAP is used from birth.
- **Dunn, Kaempf, de Klerk et al., Vermont Oxford Network DRM Study Group (Pediatrics 2011)** — randomized trial comparing 3 approaches to initial respiratory management of preterm neonates (prophylactic surfactant vs. INSURE vs. CPAP-only).
- **Göpel, Rausch et al., pro.LISA trial** — a contemporary RCT (published as a protocol in *Trials*, 2023, with results reporting ongoing/pending) comparing prophylactic vs. selective "less invasive surfactant administration" (LISA), with the selective group receiving surfactant if FiO₂ exceeds 0.30 — reflects how the question is still being actively re-tested with modern minimally-invasive delivery techniques.

## Bottom line for a parent

The historical trial evidence (pre-CPAP era) leaned toward prophylactic surfactant reducing mortality and air-leak complications, but the tradeoff is unnecessary intubation/treatment of infants who wouldn't have developed RDS. In the modern era of routine CPAP and less-invasive surfactant delivery, most newer trials (CURPAP, Vermont Oxford DRM trial, pro.LISA) are testing whether **early selective treatment guided by CPAP + oxygen threshold** achieves similar outcomes without over-treating — and current practice at most centers has shifted toward selective/early-rescue dosing for that reason. Your neonatologist can tell you which approach your NICU uses and why, based on your baby's specific gestational age and clinical picture, since the risk-benefit balance shifts with gestational age.
