**Studies incorporating CPAP-based selective strategies (surfactant "if FiO2 threshold met" vs. prophylactic/early intubation):**
Trials from the early 2000s compared routine delivery-room intubation (with prophylactic surfactant) to first-intention CPAP in preterm infants <30 weeks' gestation, in an era of more common maternal steroid use, where most infants randomized to delivery-room intubation received prophylactic surfactant and about half of the CPAP-arm infants required surfactant later for evolving RDS. **Retrospective/observational comparison (not randomized — flagging separately since you asked for primary studies but not necessarily RCT-only):**
Retrospective analysis comparing premature infants (23+0 to 26+6 weeks) receiving 200 mg/kg surfactant within five minutes after birth (prophylactic group, N=31) with infants who received surfactant therapy for established RDS (selective group, N=34); prophylactic therapy significantly decreased need for mechanical ventilation (74 vs. 171 hours per patient).

**Modern trial on prophylactic "less invasive surfactant administration" (LISA):**
The pro.LISA randomized controlled trial: preterm infants 25+0–28+6 weeks breathing spontaneously on CPAP with FiO2 ≤30% in the first hour of life are randomized to prophylactic LISA surfactant (100–200 mg/kg) versus continued CPAP with surfactant given only if FiO2 exceeds 0.30 (selective/guideline-based control), with follow-up to age 5 years and spirometric FEV1 z-score as primary outcome.
