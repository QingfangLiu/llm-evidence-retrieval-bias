## Summary of findings across studies

**Classic prophylactic vs. rescue/delayed-selective trials (pre-CPAP era, intubated infants):**
- The OSIRIS trial randomly allocated 2,690 infants at high risk of RDS to early administration or delayed selective administration of a synthetic surfactant, finding a 16% lower risk of death or oxygen dependence at the expected date of delivery with early administration.
- Dunn et al. randomized 182 neonates under 30 weeks' gestation to control, early (at-birth) surfactant, or late (<6 hours) surfactant, allowing infants in the late group to avoid treatment if they met defined respiratory criteria.
- Kattwinkel et al. (1993, *Pediatrics* 92:90–98) found prophylactic calf lung surfactant extract more effective than early treatment in neonates 29–32 weeks' gestation.
- Egberts et al. (1993, *Pediatrics* 92:768–774) compared prophylaxis vs. rescue treatment with Curosurf in neonates <30 weeks.
- Bevilacqua et al. (1996, *J Perinat Med* 24:609–620) compared prophylactic vs. rescue treatment with modified porcine surfactant.

**Modern trials (CPAP era, comparing prophylactic surfactant vs. early CPAP with selective/rescue surfactant):**
- Sandri et al. (CURPAP trial) randomized 208 infants born at 25–28 weeks to prophylactic surfactant or nCPAP with early selective surfactant, finding no significant difference in need for mechanical ventilation in the first 5 days of life (31.4% vs. 33.0%).
- Rojas et al. 2009 (*Pediatrics* 123:137–142) — Colombian Neonatal Research Network trial of very early surfactant without mandatory ventilation vs. CPAP alone.
- The pro.LISA trial protocol describes an ongoing RCT randomizing infants 25–29 weeks on CPAP to prophylactic less-invasive surfactant administration (LISA) vs. continued CPAP with surfactant given if FiO₂ exceeds 30%, with 5-year spirometry follow-up as the primary outcome.
