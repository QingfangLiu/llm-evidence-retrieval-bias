**What the trials generally show:**

The earliest trials (Kendig 1991, Bevilacqua 1996, Egberts 1997, Dunn 1991) tested prophylactic (surfactant given at birth, before RDS developed) against rescue/selective surfactant (given only after RDS signs appeared) in an era of routine intubation, and generally found prophylactic dosing reduced mortality, pneumothorax, and pulmonary interstitial emphysema, particularly in infants <30 weeks' gestation.

Later trials (Sandri/CURPAP 2010, Rojas 2009, Dunn/Vermont Oxford 2011, Finer/SUPPORT 2010) were conducted in the CPAP era, where many "control" infants avoided intubation altogether. These generally found no advantage — and sometimes a disadvantage — to prophylactic surfactant compared with early stabilization on CPAP with selective/early-rescue surfactant, reflecting a shift in practice away from routine prophylactic intubation.
