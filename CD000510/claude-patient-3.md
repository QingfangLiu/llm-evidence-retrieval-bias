Here's an overview of the individual randomized controlled trials (primary studies) comparing **prophylactic** (surfactant given at/shortly after birth, before signs of RDS) versus **selective/rescue** surfactant (given only after RDS develops) in preterm infants. I've excluded reviews, meta-analyses, and guidelines as requested.

## What the trials generally found

**Classic prophylactic-vs-rescue trials (1986–1993, pre-CPAP era):** These trials, done before nasal CPAP was standard practice, generally found that prophylactic surfactant reduced pneumothorax, pulmonary interstitial emphysema, and mortality compared with rescue treatment given after RDS was established. The tradeoff was that many infants without evolving RDS were unnecessarily intubated and treated.

**Modern trials (2009–2011, CPAP era):** Once nasal CPAP became routine initial management, trials shifted to comparing prophylactic/very-early surfactant against a strategy of stabilizing infants on CPAP and giving surfactant selectively only if they failed CPAP. These more recent trials generally found **no benefit** (and sometimes harm, e.g., more chronic lung disease) from prophylactic surfactant when CPAP was used as initial support, supporting a shift toward selective use.

## Primary studies

1. **Merritt TA, Hallman M, Bloom BT, et al.** Prophylactic treatment of very premature infants with human surfactant. *N Engl J Med.* 1986;315(13):785–790.

2. **Merritt TA, Hallman M, Berry C, et al.** Randomized, placebo-controlled trial of human surfactant given at birth versus rescue administration in very low birth weight infants with lung immaturity. *J Pediatr.* 1991;118(4):581–594.

3. **Vaucher YE, Harker L, Merritt TA, et al.** School-age follow-up of prophylactic versus rescue surfactant trial: pulmonary, neurodevelopmental, and educational outcomes. *J Pediatr.* 1993;122(1):126–132. (long-term follow-up of trial #2)

4. **Dunn MS, Shennan AT, Zayack D, Possmayer F.** Bovine surfactant replacement therapy in neonates of less than 30 weeks' gestation: a randomized controlled trial of prophylaxis versus treatment. *Pediatrics.* 1991;87(3):377–386.

5. **Bevilacqua G, Chernev T, Parmigiani S, et al.** Use of surfactant for prophylaxis versus rescue treatment of respiratory distress syndrome: experience from an Italian-Bulgarian trial. *J Perinat Med.* 1996;24(1):1–12.

6. **Kattwinkel J, Bloom BT, Delmore P, et al.** Prophylactic administration of calf lung surfactant extract is more effective than delayed treatment in neonates <29 weeks' gestation. *Pediatrics.* 1993;92(1):90–98.

7. **Rojas MA, Lozano JM, Rojas MX, et al.; Colombian Neonatal Research Network.** Very early surfactant without mandatory ventilation in premature infants treated with early continuous positive airway pressure: a randomized, controlled trial. *Pediatrics.* 2009;123(1):137–142.

8. **Sandri F, Plavka R, Ancora G, et al.; CURPAP Study Group.** Prophylactic or early selective surfactant combined with nCPAP in very preterm infants. *Pediatrics.* 2010;125(6):e1402–e1409.

9. **Dunn MS, Kaempf J, de Klerk A, de Klerk R, Reilly M, Howard D, Ferrelli K, O'Conor J, Soll RF; Vermont Oxford Network DRM Study Group.** Randomized trial comparing 3 approaches to the initial respiratory management of preterm neonates. *Pediatrics.* 2011;128(5):e1069–e1076.

A note: a newer German trial, **pro.LISA** (Göpel W, et al., registered DRKS00028086, protocol published in *Trials* 2023;24:612), is directly testing prophylactic vs. selective *less invasive* surfactant administration (LISA) with 5-year follow-up, but as of my most recent information its primary results have not yet been published — only the study protocol is available. You may want to check for a completed results paper if you're looking at the most current evidence.
