## Summary of findings

The primary RCT evidence is generally supportive but modest in magnitude:

- A pooled analysis of five hip-OA RCTs found a small treatment effect for pain (SMD −0.38), but no significant benefit for self-reported physical function — though this synthesis itself is a meta-analysis, the individual trials it drew on are primary studies.
- The landmark **Bennell et al. 2014 JAMA trial** was a placebo-controlled RCT of 102 people with hip OA; participants had hip pain of at least 40/100 on a VAS and radiographically confirmed hip OA, and were randomized to 12 weeks of active physical therapy (education, manual therapy, home exercise, gait aid) versus sham treatment (inactive ultrasound and inert gel), with pain and WOMAC physical function as primary outcomes.
- Older Dutch and Nordic trials (van Baar 1998, Tak 2005, Fransen 2001) established that exercise therapy for hip OA is effective at decreasing pain and improving function, though few of these studies compared different exercise types or dose-response effects head-to-head.
- More recent work has tested exercise combined with other approaches, e.g., the 2025 **Annals of Internal Medicine trial** comparing a very-low-calorie weight loss diet plus exercise versus exercise alone, with 6-month change in hip pain severity as the primary outcome.

## Primary studies (individual RCTs — no reviews/guidelines)

1. **Bennell KL, Egerton T, Martin J, et al.** *Effect of physical therapy on pain and function in patients with hip osteoarthritis: a randomized clinical trial.* JAMA. 2014;311(19):1987-97. PMID: 24846036

2. **Hall M, Hinman RS, Knox G, et al.** *Efficacy of a very-low-calorie weight loss diet plus exercise compared with exercise alone on hip osteoarthritis pain: a randomized controlled trial.* Ann Intern Med. 2025;178(9):1227-1237.

3. **van Baar ME, Dekker J, Oostendorp RA, et al.** *The effectiveness of exercise therapy in patients with osteoarthritis of the hip or knee: a randomized clinical trial.* J Rheumatol. 1998;25(12):2432-9.

4. **Tak E, Staats P, Van Hespen A, Hopman-Rock M.** *The effects of an exercise program for older adults with osteoarthritis of the hip.* J Rheumatol. 2005;32(6):1106-13.

5. **Fransen M, Crosbie J, Edmonds J.** *Physical therapy is effective for patients with osteoarthritis of the knee: a randomized controlled clinical trial* — Fransen also published a comparable 2001 hip/knee exercise RCT; check J Rheumatol 2001;28(1):156-64 for the parallel hip-inclusive trial.

6. **Krauss I, et al.** (Hip School trial) — results paper following the published protocol (Krauss I, Steinhilber B, Haupt G, et al., BMC Musculoskelet Disord. 2011;12:270, protocol; look for the subsequent outcomes publication).

7. **Bieler T, Siersma V, Magnusson SP, et al.** *In hip osteoarthritis, Nordic Walking is superior to strength training and home-based exercise for improving function.* Scand J Med Sci Sports. 2017;27(8):873-886.

8. **Villadsen A, Overgaard S, Holsgaard-Larsen A, Christensen R, Roos EM.** *Immediate efficacy of neuromuscular exercise in patients with severe osteoarthritis of the hip or knee: a secondary analysis from a randomized controlled trial.* J Rheumatol. 2014;41(7):1385-94. PMID: 24931956

9. **Fernandes L, Storheim K, Sandvik L, Nordsletten L, Risberg MA.** *Efficacy of patient education and supervised exercise vs patient education alone in patients with hip osteoarthritis: a single blind randomized clinical trial.* Osteoarthritis Cartilage. 2010;18(10):1237-43.

10. **Fukumoto Y, et al.** (2014) — trial comparing high- vs low-velocity strengthening exercise in hip OA, reporting functional performance benefits favoring high-velocity training with no difference in pain (cited in Østerås et al. pilot study above; search "Fukumoto 2014 hip osteoarthritis high velocity exercise" for full citation).

11. **Østerås N, et al.** *Effects of medical exercise therapy in patients with hip osteoarthritis: a randomized controlled trial with six months follow-up. A pilot study.* (Musculoskeletal Science and Practice, ~2016).

12. **Abbott JH, Robertson MC, Chapple C, et al. (MOA Trial Team).** *Manual therapy, exercise therapy, or both, in addition to usual care, for osteoarthritis of the hip or knee: a randomized controlled trial, 1: clinical effectiveness.* Osteoarthritis Cartilage. 2013;21(4):525-534.

13. **Foley A, Halbert J, Hewitt T, Crotty M.** *Does hydrotherapy improve strength and physical function in patients with osteoarthritis – a randomised controlled trial comparing a gym-based and a hydrotherapy-based strengthening programme.* Ann Rheum Dis. 2003;62:1162-1167 (includes a land-based/gym arm as comparator).

A few notes: several of the newer trials I found (GHOst, PHOENIX, Better Hip) are currently only published as **protocols**, not completed results — I've excluded those from the numbered list since they aren't yet primary outcome data, but flagged them below in case you want to track them once results are published:

- GHOst trial (gluteal exercise vs sham) — protocol in PMC6149073
- PHOENIX trial (aerobic + strengthening vs strengthening alone) — protocol in PMC9014787
- Better Hip trial (telehealth exercise + weight loss) — protocol, BMC Musculoskelet Disord 2024
