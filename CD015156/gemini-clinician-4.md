For active children and adolescents, lower-limb apophysitis—most commonly Sever's disease (calcaneal apophysitis) and Osgood-Schlatter disease (tibial tubercle apophysitis)—can be a persistent source of pain. While these conditions are often self-limiting and traditionally managed with a "wait-and-see" approach, recent primary studies offer insight into active interventions that can accelerate pain relief and a return to sport.

Here is a breakdown of the evidence from individual primary studies, specifically excluding reviews and guidelines.

## Sever's Disease (Calcaneal Apophysitis)

### Orthotics, Heel Raises, and Insoles

Biomechanical offloading is a standard frontline treatment, but specific types of inserts yield different timelines for relief.

* **Heel Raises vs. Orthoses:** In a 12-month randomized controlled trial (RCT), simple heel raises (which slightly plantarflex the ankle to reduce Achilles traction) demonstrated a clear short-term advantage over prefabricated foot orthoses at the two-month mark (James et al., 2016). However, by 12 months, both interventions resulted in equal improvement, indicating that heel raises are a cost-effective choice for early, acute management (James et al., 2016).
* **Heel Cup Insoles:** The use of heel cup insoles without any other modifications to physical activity or immobilization provides significant pain relief during sports (Perhamre et al., 2011). This suggests that young athletes can safely continue their sports with appropriate insole cushioning rather than requiring complete rest.

### Taping and Kinesiotherapy

* **Kinesio Taping:** Kinesiotherapy combined with Kinesio taping has been shown to significantly outperform placebo taping in reducing pain and improving functional scores in male athletes with Sever's disease (Kuyucu et al., 2017). This indicates that localized tension-relieving strategies over the Achilles and plantar fascia can offer functional benefits.

## Osgood-Schlatter Disease (Tibial Tubercle Apophysitis)

### Injection Therapies

For recalcitrant Osgood-Schlatter disease that fails to improve with rest or standard physical therapy, regenerative injection therapies have shown strong outcomes in clinical trials.

* **Hyperosmolar Dextrose (Prolotherapy):** A double-blind RCT demonstrated that ultrasound-guided 12.5% hyperosmolar dextrose injections significantly outperformed saline injections (Wu et al., 2021). Patients receiving dextrose showed much faster and more substantial improvements in their Victorian Institute of Sport Assessment (VISA) scores at 3, 6, and 12 months, facilitating a rapid return to asymptomatic sports (Wu et al., 2021).
* **Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP):** For prolonged cases, the application of leukocyte-rich platelet-rich plasma (LR-PRP) has also been investigated. The effectiveness of this regenerative approach is heavily dependent on the duration of the condition, though it provides a viable nonsurgical option for adolescents with chronic, unrelenting tibial tubercle pain (Guszczyn et al., 2024).
