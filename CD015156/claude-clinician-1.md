Here's a synthesis of individual primary studies (RCTs and comparative/cohort trials) on nonsurgical treatment of lower-limb apophysitis, organized by condition. Reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, and opinion pieces are excluded except where cited incidentally to locate a primary study.

## Osgood-Schlatter disease (tibial tuberosity apophysitis)

**Exercise-based self-management vs. usual care** — The SOGOOD trial, a pragmatic, triple-blinded RCT of 130 adolescents (ages 10–16) with long-standing symptoms (mean duration >15 months), compared 3 months of a structured self-management program (exercise, education, activity modification) against usual care. Self-management was not found to be superior to usual care, though both groups showed meaningful within-group improvement, and no safety issues occurred. This suggests structured exercise programs may not outperform standard advice-based care, though both approaches led to improvement.

**Injection therapies** — Two double-blind RCTs tested hyperosmolar dextrose (prolotherapy) injections against saline:
- A 70-patient trial found the dextrose group showed greater improvement in VISA-Patella scores from baseline to 3 months than the saline group, injected under ultrasound guidance.
- A separate prospective double-blind trial in 36 boys (44 knees) comparing dextrose vs. saline injection found no significant difference between groups from pre-injection to final follow-up, though VISA scores rose significantly in both groups over time, with no adverse effects.
These two trials conflict, and a related Pediatrics-published trial (Topol et al. 2011) also examined hyperosmolar dextrose injection for recalcitrant OSD.

**Extracorporeal shockwave therapy (ESWT)** — Mostly small case series/retrospective cohorts rather than controlled trials:
- A retrospective follow-up of 14 patients (16 knees) with recalcitrant OSD treated with radial ESWT reported outcomes via the VISA-P-G questionnaire at long-term follow-up.
- A retrospective case series of 22 athletes (15 OSD, 7 Sever's) receiving low-energy focused ESWT found most patients returned to prior activity levels within 2–4 weeks, with no adverse events or recurrence at 3 months.
- A comparative pre/post study of 40 OSD patients allocated to interferential therapy vs. shockwave therapy (both added to a conventional program) evaluated pain outcomes over 8 weeks of thrice-weekly treatment.

## Calcaneal apophysitis (Sever's disease)

This condition has the strongest comparative-trial evidence base of the apophysitides.

**Three-arm comparative RCT (wait-and-see vs. orthotic vs. exercise)** — Wiegerinck et al. (J Pediatr Orthop, 2016) randomized 98 children (ages 8–15) to a wait-and-see protocol, a heel-raise inlay (ViscoHeel), or supervised eccentric-exercise physiotherapy for 10 weeks. All three treatment modalities produced a clinically relevant and statistically significant reduction in heel pain, with no significant differences found between the regimens.

**Factorial RCT of footwear and orthoses** — James, Williams & Haines (Br J Sports Med, 2016) randomized 124 children (ages 8–14) in a 2×2 factorial design to heel raise vs. prefabricated orthoses, and current footwear vs. new athletic shoes, with follow-up to 12 months. At 2 months there was a relative advantage of heel raises over prefabricated orthoses, but by 12 months no treatment showed an advantage over another, suggesting treatment choice can reasonably defer to cost and preference beyond the short term.

**Custom orthoses vs. heel-lifts** — A CONSORT-compliant RCT of 208 children compared custom-made polypropylene foot orthoses against off-the-shelf heel-lifts over 12 weeks. Both groups improved, but the custom-orthoses group showed significantly greater increases in pain-pressure threshold (53.4%) and greater VAS pain reduction (−68.6%) than the heel-lift group.

**Insole/heel-cup studies (Perhamre et al.)** — A series of Scandinavian trials:
- A randomized crossover trial comparing two insole alternatives ("treat it with a heel cup") for Sever's injury.
- A companion trial ("Sever's injury: treatment with insoles provides effective pain relief") reporting effective pain relief with insole treatment.
- A biomechanical follow-up study showing a heel cup improved heel-pad thickness, peak pressure, and pain outcomes.

**Bracing comparison** — Sweeney et al. randomized 43 young barefoot athletes (mostly gymnasts) to two brace types (Tuli's Cheetah heel cup vs. Tuli's X Brace) over 3 months, measuring the OxAFQ-C physical score and VAS pain during rest, ADLs, and sport.

## Summary for practice

Across both conditions, the pattern is consistent: **most conservative interventions (rest/activity modification, heel lifts, orthoses, eccentric exercise) improve pain over weeks to months, but head-to-head trials generally fail to show one modality is clearly superior to another** — including in the largest and most rigorous trials (Wiegerinck, James/Williams/Haines, SOGOOD). Injection and shockwave therapies have much thinner and more heterogeneous evidence, with conflicting RCT results for dextrose prolotherapy in OSD and only uncontrolled case series for ESWT.
