## Calcaneal apophysitis (Sever's disease)

- **Wiegerinck et al. (2016)** — Single-blind RCT (n=98) comparing wait-and-see, heel-raise inlay, and supervised eccentric calf-strengthening physical therapy over 10 weeks. All three treatments produced a clinically relevant, statistically significant reduction in heel pain, with no significant difference between regimens.

- **James, Williams & Haines (2016)** — 2×2 factorial RCT (n=124) testing heel raise vs. prefabricated orthoses, crossed with footwear replacement vs. no replacement, over 12 months. Heel raises showed significant short-term (1–2 month) improvement in the physical domain of the Oxford Ankle-Foot Questionnaire versus prefabricated orthoses, but no significant differences remained at 6 or 12 months for any outcome.

- **Alfaro-Santafé et al. (2021)** — CONSORT-compliant RCT (n=208) comparing custom-made polypropylene foot orthoses vs. off-the-shelf heel-lifts over 12 weeks. The custom-orthoses group showed greater improvement in pressure-pain threshold and VAS pain scores than the heel-lift group, though both groups improved.

- **Sweeney et al.** — RCT comparing two heel-cup/brace designs (Tuli's Cheetah heel cup vs. Tuli's X Brace) in 43 barefoot athletes aged 7–14 (mostly gymnasts), over a 3-month period.

## Osgood-Schlatter disease (tibial tuberosity apophysitis)

- **Nakase et al. (2020)** — Prospective randomized double-blind study of hyperosmolar dextrose injection vs. placebo (saline) injection in adolescents refractory to conservative treatment. No significant difference was found between dextrose and saline groups in VISA scores from pre-injection to final follow-up, though both groups improved significantly over time.

- **Wu, Tu & Tu (2022)** — Double-blind RCT of hyperosmolar dextrose injection for OSD (*Arch Orthop Trauma Surg*).

- **Topol, Podesta & Reeves (2011)** — Hyperosmolar dextrose injection trial for recalcitrant OSD, published in *Pediatrics*.

- **Guszczyn et al. (2023)** — Prospective cohort of 152 children with chronic OSD treated with leukocyte-rich platelet-rich plasma (LR-PRP) added to standard conservative therapy. 75% of subjects were satisfied with treatment results, and 72% returned to full physical activity, with a significant decrease in median VAS pain score after treatment.

- **Guszczyn et al. (2024)** — Follow-up study, *J Clin Med*, examining effectiveness of LR-PRP for OSD stratified by disease duration.

- **Danneberg (2017)** — Small case series (2 patients) using autologous-conditioned plasma for treatment-resistant OSD (*Joints*).

- **Outcomes of PRP vs. Dextrose 10% Prolotherapy for OSD** — Retrospective cohort (n=40, non-randomized) comparing single PRP injection vs. triple dextrose prolotherapy series, measuring pain (VAS) and function (Lysholm) at baseline, 4, 8, and 12 weeks, with return-to-sport time and satisfaction as secondary outcomes.

- **SOGOOD trial (2025)** — Pragmatic, triple-blinded RCT (n=130, ages 10–16) comparing a structured self-management approach (exercise, education, activity modification) vs. usual care over 3 months, with the primary outcome being the KOOS-child 'Sport/play' subscale at 5 months.

- **Lohrer et al.** — Retrospective case series/cohort (n=14 adolescents, 16 knees) evaluating safety and effectiveness of radial extracorporeal shock wave therapy for recalcitrant Osgood-Schlatter disease.

- **Focused ESWT for youth apophyseal injuries** — Retrospective case series (n=22: 15 OSD, 7 Sever's disease) evaluating low-energy focused ESWT, with time to return to prior activity level of 2 weeks in most patients and no reported adverse events or recurrence at 3 months.

## Combined / multiple apophysitis sites

- **Comparison of Three Treatments for Lower Extremity Apophysitis (NCT01826071)** — Completed RCT (Ann & Robert H. Lurie Children's Hospital of Chicago) comparing a home exercise program of active elongation exercises vs. static stretching vs. symptomatic treatment (ice, NSAIDs/acetaminophen, activity modification) across Osgood-Schlatter, Sinding-Larsen-Johansson, and Sever's disease. (Results not yet located in indexed literature — may be unpublished beyond the registry.)

- **Iontophoresis with Dexamethasone and Physical Therapy for Apophysitis of the Knee (NCT03606980)** — Phase II three-arm RCT comparing iontophoresis with dexamethasone sodium phosphate plus physical therapy, iontophoresis with placebo plus physical therapy, and physical therapy alone for pediatric knee apophysitis (n=23 per the Cochrane summary of this trial).
