## Osgood-Schlatter disease (tibial tuberosity apophysitis)

**Injection therapies (RCTs):**
- A double-blind RCT in Chinese adolescents found that dextrose injection with local anaesthetic was superior to usual care or local anaesthetic alone across three-armed trial designs (Wu, Tu & Tu, 2022).
- Conversely, a separate Japanese prospective randomized double-blind study assigned 44 knees in 36 boys, whose symptoms hadn't responded to over a month of conventional conservative therapy, to blinded injection of lidocaine with saline versus dextrose, finding no significant difference in VISA scores between the dextrose and saline groups, though both groups improved significantly from baseline (Nakase et al., 2016 — "no superiority" trial).

**Regenerative/orthobiologic injections (single-arm/cohort):**
- A large case series assessed pain and knee function in 152 children with chronic OSD before and after leukocyte-rich PRP injection alongside conservative therapy, using VAS, Tegner, Lysholm, and KOOS scales, reporting 75% patient satisfaction and 72% return to full activity (Guszczyn et al., 2023).
- A retrospective comparative cohort study of 40 adolescents compared a single PRP injection to a series of three dextrose prolotherapy injections in refractory OSD, finding PRP produced greater short-term pain reduction and faster return to sport (2024–2025).

**Exercise / activity-modification / self-management:**
- A prospective cohort study of 51 adolescents tested a 12-week intervention combining an activity ladder for load/pain management, knee-strengthening exercises, and graded return to sport, with global rating of change as the primary outcome (Rathleff et al., 2020).
- A retrospective cohort of 78 patients compared local treatment alone versus local treatment plus quadriceps-release manual therapy, finding the combined group recovered significantly faster with fewer treatment sessions (2025).
- A retrospective cohort of Russian youth soccer players compared a combined conservative protocol (main group) against a control group, finding both groups improved in pain and function, but the main group showed significantly greater reduction in ultrasound-detected inflammatory signs at 3 months (Bezuglov et al., 2020).
- A prospective RCT (SOGOOD trial) randomized 130 adolescents aged 10–16 to 3 months of usual care versus a self-management approach (exercise, education, activity modification), measuring the KOOS-child Sport/play subscale at 5 months (published results, Rathleff/Krommes group, 2025).

**Natural history / long-term outcome studies (relevant as comparator/no-specific-treatment data):**
- An 18-patient, 14-control comparative cohort study evaluated sonographic characteristics, jump performance, and strength/endurance two years after OSD diagnosis (Kaya et al., 2013).

## Sever's disease (calcaneal apophysitis)

**RCTs comparing orthoses/insoles/heel devices:**
- A CONSORT-compliant RCT randomized 208 children aged 9–12 to custom-made polypropylene foot orthoses versus off-the-shelf heel-lifts, measuring algometry and VAS over 12 weeks, finding a 53.4% increase in pain threshold and 68.6% VAS reduction favoring custom orthoses (Alfaro-Santafé et al., 2021).
- A randomized crossover trial compared a heel cup with a heel-lift insole, finding the heel cup preferred by more than 75% of boys when given an active choice, with high activity maintained throughout (Perhamre, Lundin, Norlin & Klässbo, 2011, Scand J Med Sci Sports).
- A companion Perhamre et al. (2011) RCT, "Sever's injury: treatment with insoles provides effective pain relief," and a related 2012 study on heel-pad thickness/peak pressure (same author group) are also primary trials in this literature.
- The largest trial to date, a 12-month randomised comparative efficacy trial in 124 children using a 2×2 factorial design (heel raise vs. prefabricated orthoses; new footwear vs. no footwear change), with the Oxford Ankle Foot Questionnaire as primary outcome (James, Williams & Haines, published as Br J Sports Med 2016;50:1268-1275).
- A three-arm pragmatic RCT compared a wait-and-see protocol, a heel-raise inlay, and supervised eccentric exercise physiotherapy over 10 weeks in children aged 8–15, concluding all three treatments produced clinically relevant, statistically significant reductions in heel pain with no significant differences between them (Wiegerinck et al., J Pediatr Orthop 2016).
- A randomized clinical trial compared two braces (Tuli's Cheetah heel cup vs. Tuli's X Brace) in 43 barefoot athletes aged 7–14, mostly gymnasts, over a 3-month study period (Sweeney et al., J Athl Train 2023).
