## Primary Studies on Nonsurgical Treatment of Pediatric Lower-Limb Apophysitis

**Knee (Osgood-Schlatter disease)**

- **Exercise/activity modification RCT:** A pragmatic, assessor-blinded RCT (n=130, ages 10–16) compared a self-management program (exercise, education, activity modification) to usual care over 3 months. Self-management was not found to be superior to usual care, though both groups showed meaningful within-group improvement, and more than one-third of patients did not reach an acceptable symptom state.

- **Activity modification/knee-strengthening cohort study:** A prospective case series (n=51, ages 10–14) tested a 12-week program combining an activity ladder to manage patellar tendon loading and pain, knee-strengthening exercises, and a gradual return to sport, as an alternative to passive rest-based approaches.

- **Dextrose (prolotherapy) injection RCT #1:** Double-blind RCT (n=70) comparing 12.5% hyperosmolar dextrose injection vs. saline injection under ultrasound guidance; the dextrose group showed greater improvement in VISA-Patella score from baseline to 3 months than the saline group (Wu, Tu & Tu, 2022).

- **Dextrose injection RCT #2 (conflicting result):** A separate prospective, randomized, double-blind study found no superiority of dextrose injections over placebo injections for Osgood-Schlatter disease (Nakase et al., 2020).

- **Dextrose injection vs. usual care (earlier trial):** Girls aged 9–15 and boys aged 10–17 were randomly assigned to therapist-supervised usual care or double-blind injection of 1% lidocaine with or without 12.5% dextrose, given monthly for 3 months (Topol, Podesta & Reeves, Pediatrics, 2011).

- **Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) cohort study:** A study of 152 children with chronic OSD treated with leukocyte-rich PRP alongside standard conservative care found 75% of subjects were satisfied with results and 72% returned to full physical activity (Guszczyn et al., 2023).

- **PRP vs. dextrose prolotherapy (retrospective cohort):** In 40 adolescents with refractory OSD, PRP showed better short-term outcomes than dextrose prolotherapy, including greater pain reduction and faster return to sports (Thapa et al., 2025).

**Heel (Sever's disease / calcaneal apophysitis)**

- **Custom orthoses vs. heel-lifts RCT:** A CONSORT-compliant randomized trial (n=208, ages 9–12) found custom-made foot orthoses produced a 53.4% increase in pain-pressure threshold and a 68.6% decrease in VAS pain compared with off-the-shelf heel-lifts, though both groups improved.

- **Heel raises vs. prefabricated orthoses (protocol/trial):** A factorial 2×2 RCT (n=124, ages 8–14) compared heel raises against prefabricated orthoses for posterior heel pain associated with calcaneal apophysitis.

**Hip (iliac crest / ASIS / AIIS apophysitis)**

- **Overuse injury case series:** A retrospective review of 21 cases of overuse injury to the iliac crest apophysis in adolescent runners found all cases resolved with 4 to 6 weeks of rest (Clancy & Foltz).

- **AIIS avulsion case series:** A case series of 7 adolescent soccer players with rectus femoris/AIIS apophyseal injury, initially treated non-operatively; all but one recovered after a period of relative rest, with a median return to sport of 2.5 months and high functional scores at follow-up.
