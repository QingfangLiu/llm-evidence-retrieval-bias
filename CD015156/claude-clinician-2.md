## Summary of findings

**Osgood-Schlatter disease (OSD)** has the most RCT-level evidence, concentrated on injections. The one trial testing a self-management/exercise approach (SOGOOD, 2025) found self-management was not superior to usual care, though both groups showed meaningful within-group improvement, with only 55–63% reaching an acceptable symptom state at 5 months. Dextrose (prolotherapy) injection trials are conflicting: the original Topol 2011 study found dextrose injection over the apophysis and patellar tendon origin was safe and resulted in more rapid and frequent achievement of unaltered and asymptomatic sport than usual care, but two later placebo-controlled trials disagreed — Nakase 2020 concluded they were unable to show efficacy of dextrose injections compared to saline, while Wu 2022 found the dextrose group did outperform saline. PRP has only single-arm/uncontrolled data so far, with Guszczyn's studies reporting 75% of subjects satisfied and 72% returning to full physical activity after LR-PRP. ESWT evidence is limited to small case series/pilot studies (Lohrer, and a mixed OSD/Sever's case series), not yet RCTs.

**Sever's disease (calcaneal apophysitis)** has more comparative RCTs, mostly on orthotics/footwear. Wiegerinck's 3-arm trial is the strongest: wait-and-see, a heel raise inlay, and physical therapy each resulted in a clinically relevant, statistically significant reduction of heel pain, with no significant difference between the three regimens. James's 12-month factorial trial similarly found treatment choice may come down to cost, patient preference and clinician judgment rather than superiority of one orthotic/footwear approach. Perhamre's crossover trial found a custom heel cup outperformed a heel wedge reducing the odds of pain to a fifth compared with the wedge, and the heel cup was preferred by more than 75% of the boys. Kinesio taping improved function but not pain more than sham taping.

**Sinding-Larsen-Johansson syndrome and iliac apophysitis** — I found essentially no controlled primary treatment studies; the literature here is limited to case reports/series and narrative summaries, reflecting a genuine evidence gap.
