## Summary of findings

**Osgood-Schlatter disease (tibial tuberosity apophysitis):** Primary evidence is sparse and skewed toward injection therapies. Two placebo-controlled RCTs of hyperosmolar dextrose injection reached opposite conclusions one trial of 70 patients found dextrose outperformed saline injection on VISA-Patella scores at 3 months, while a separate Japanese trial found no superiority over placebo. Retrospective/case-series data suggest platelet-rich plasma improved knee pain and function in 152 children with chronic OSD, with 75% patient satisfaction and 72% returning to full activity and outperformed dextrose prolotherapy in a small retrospective comparison. For active/exercise-based management, a 2020 prospective cohort study of 51 adolescents using an activity-modification and knee-strengthening program showed improvement at 12 weeks measured by global rating of change, and the SOGOOD RCT (Denmark) is now testing this self-management approach against usual care in 130 adolescents. A large retrospective cohort in youth soccer players also supports early return-to-sport conservative protocols over immobilization.

**Sever's disease / calcaneal apophysitis (heel apophysitis):** This condition has more comparative RCT evidence. Orthoses/heel-lift trials are the best studied: a 208-patient CONSORT-compliant RCT found custom-made foot orthoses produced greater pain reduction than off-the-shelf heel-lifts over 12 weeks; an earlier 124-patient factorial RCT compared heel raises vs. prefabricated orthoses and footwear prescription, finding modest short-term benefit for heel raises. A three-arm RCT (Wiegerinck et al., 98 children) compared wait-and-see, heel-raise inlays, and supervised eccentric-exercise physiotherapy over 10 weeks, finding all three roughly equivalent. Two Swedish RCTs (Perhamre et al.) showed a custom heel cup reduced pain by ~80% compared to a heel wedge without needing activity restriction. A brace-comparison RCT in barefoot athletes and a kinesio-taping vs. sham RCT rounded out the evidence, along with a 12-month factorial trial of footwear/orthoses.
