## Summary

Evidence for nonsurgical treatment of lower-limb apophysitis in children/adolescents is concentrated on two conditions — **Osgood-Schlatter disease (OSD)** and **calcaneal apophysitis (Sever's disease)** — and remains sparse and methodologically limited overall. Nearly all rigorous comparative studies are recent (2010s–2020s), sample sizes are modest, and the evidence base for **Sinding-Larsen-Johansson syndrome** consists almost entirely of case reports/case series rather than controlled trials, so I found no qualifying primary comparative study for it.

**For OSD**, the two completed RCTs both test dextrose (prolotherapy) injections against saline, with conflicting results; a self-management (exercise/education/activity-modification) trial versus usual care has now completed (with a separate published protocol); and a few smaller trials/case series test taping-plus-exercise, ESWT, and interferential therapy.

**For Sever's disease**, orthoses/heel-lift comparisons dominate (several by the same Perhamre group), alongside a wait-and-see vs. orthosis vs. physical therapy pragmatic trial, a kinesiotherapy/taping RCT, and a brace-comparison RCT — these represent most of the field's Level 1–2 evidence, per the systematic reviews I cross-checked.

I did not include the systematic reviews, scoping reviews, or narrative summaries that surfaced in search (e.g., the OSD conservative-treatment systematic review, the Sever's disease systematic reviews, the SLJ scoping review) — they're listed only because they helped me locate primary studies, per your exclusion criteria.
