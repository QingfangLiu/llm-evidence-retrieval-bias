## Summary of findings by condition

**Osgood-Schlatter disease (tibial tuberosity apophysitis)**
Injection-based therapies have the strongest trial-level evidence: two RCTs found ultrasound-guided hyperosmolar dextrose (prolotherapy) injections outperformed saline, and a separate double-blind RCT compared lidocaine/dextrose injection combinations. A large RCT (SOGOOD trial, Denmark) tested a structured self-management program (exercise + education + activity modification) against usual care, addressing a long-standing evidence gap for conservative/exercise-based care. Regenerative injectables (leukocyte-rich PRP, PRP vs. dextrose prolotherapy) show promising results in cohort studies, especially in acute-phase disease, though evidence is smaller and less controlled. Extracorporeal shockwave therapy (ESWT) has support from case series and one RCT-style comparison against interferential therapy, generally showing safety and symptom improvement in recalcitrant cases. Kinesiotaping combined with strengthening/stretching is being tested in an ongoing registered trial.

**Calcaneal apophysitis (Sever's disease)** — this condition has the most primary-study depth:
- The Wiegerinck et al. RCT (three-arm: wait-and-see vs. heel-raise inlay vs. supervised eccentric exercise) found all three approaches produced comparable improvement at 3 months — no approach was clearly superior.
- The James et al. 12-month factorial RCT (heel-raise vs. prefabricated orthoses × footwear replacement vs. no replacement) similarly found no relative advantage of one treatment over another, aside from footwear preference effects.
- Alfaro-Santafé et al.'s CONSORT RCT found custom orthoses outperformed off-the-shelf heel lifts.
- Perhamre et al.'s crossover RCTs found a heel cup outperformed a heel wedge, with high preference and continued sports participation.
- Sweeney et al.'s RCT compared two bracing options in barefoot athletes (gymnasts).
- ESWT case series (Lohrer & Nauck; Shafshak & Amer) report safety and pain relief in recalcitrant cases.

**Sinding-Larsen-Johansson syndrome (distal patellar apophysitis)** — despite an extensive search, I could not locate dedicated primary intervention studies (RCTs, cohort studies, or case series) isolating SLJ treatment efficacy; existing literature here is dominated by scoping/narrative reviews and patient-education material, representing a genuine evidence gap.
