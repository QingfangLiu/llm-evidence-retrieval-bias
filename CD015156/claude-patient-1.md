## Knee (Osgood-Schlatter disease / Sinding-Larsen-Johansson)

**Exercise / activity-modification approaches**
- Rathleff MS et al. *Activity Modification and Knee Strengthening for Osgood-Schlatter Disease: A Prospective Cohort Study.* Sports Health. 2020. (n=51 case series; 12-week activity ladder + strengthening program)
- Krommes K et al. *Effect of a self-management approach versus usual care for Osgood-Schlatter: A randomized clinical trial including 130 adolescents.* (SOGOOD trial results; self-management exercise/education/activity modification vs. usual care)
- Retrospective cohort study on quadriceps release therapy as adjunct to local treatment (n=78), 2024–2025, PMC12158620.

**Injection therapies**
- Topol GA, Podesta LA, Reeves KD, et al. *Hyperosmolar dextrose injection for recalcitrant Osgood-Schlatter disease.* Pediatrics. 2011;128(5):e1121-8. (RCT: dextrose vs. lidocaine vs. usual care)
- Nakase J, Oshima T, et al. *No Superiority of Dextrose Injections over Placebo Injections for Osgood-Schlatter Disease: A Prospective Randomized Double-Blind Study.* Arch Orthop Trauma Surg. 2020;140:197-202.
- Wu Z, Tu X, Tu Z. *Hyperosmolar dextrose injection for Osgood-Schlatter disease: a double-blind, randomized controlled trial.* Arch Orthop Trauma Surg. 2022;142(9):2279-85. (n=70)
- Nakase J et al. *A Novel Injective Approach for Osgood-Schlatter Disease: A Prospective Randomized Double-blind Study.* (lidocaine/dextrose vs. saline, n=44 knees)
- Guszczyn T, Kulesza M, Kicman A, et al. *Enhanced Pain Relief and Function Improvement in Children with Osgood-Schlatter Disease: LR-PRP as a Complementary Treatment to Standard Conservative Therapy.* Med Sci Monit. 2023;29:e941523.
- Maciąg G et al. *The Effectiveness of Treating Osgood-Schlatter Disease with Leukocyte-Rich Platelet-Rich Plasma (LR-PRP) Depending on the Duration of the Disease.* J Clin Med. 2024;13(14):4220.
- Thapa R, Shanthappa AH, Chandrashekar S, Agrawal A. *Outcomes of Platelet-Rich Plasma Versus Dextrose 10% Prolotherapy in the Treatment of Osgood-Schlatter Disease: A Retrospective Study.* Cureus. 2025.

## Foot (Sever's disease / calcaneal apophysitis)

- Wiegerinck JI, Zwiers R, Sierevelt IN, et al. *Treatment of Calcaneal Apophysitis: Wait and See Versus Orthotic Device Versus Physical Therapy: A Pragmatic Therapeutic Randomized Clinical Trial.* J Pediatr Orthop. 2016;36(2):152-7. (n=101; wait-and-see vs. heel raise vs. eccentric exercise)
- James AM, Williams CM, Haines TP. *Effectiveness of footwear and foot orthoses for calcaneal apophysitis: a 12-month factorial randomised trial.* Br J Sports Med. 2016;50(20):1268-75. (n=124; 2×2 factorial: heel raise vs. prefabricated orthoses × footwear replacement vs. none)
- Alfaro-Santafé J, Gómez-Bernal A, Lanuza-Cerzócimo C, et al. *Effectiveness of Custom-Made Foot Orthoses vs. Heel-Lifts in Children with Calcaneal Apophysitis (Sever's Disease): A CONSORT-Compliant Randomized Trial.* Children (Basel). 2021;8(11):963. (n=208)
- Sweeney EA et al. Randomized clinical trial comparing two heel braces (Tuli's Cheetah heel cup vs. Tuli's X Brace) for Sever's disease in 43 barefoot athletes (gymnasts).

## Hip (apophysitis/apophyseal injuries — ASIS, AIIS, iliac crest, ischial tuberosity)

Controlled trials specifically on hip apophysitis (as opposed to acute avulsion fractures) are essentially absent in the literature; available nonsurgical primary evidence is mostly case series on closely related apophyseal avulsion injuries managed conservatively:
- Case series (n=5), *Anterior Inferior Iliac Spine Avulsion Fracture: A Series of 5 Cases*, treated nonoperatively.
- Case series (n=7), *Apophyseal Avulsion of the Rectus Femoris Tendon Origin in Adolescent Soccer Players* — nonoperative management outcomes (WOMAC/Tegner scores, return to sport).

**A note on what I found:** There's substantially more high-quality primary research (including several RCTs) on the knee and foot than on the hip, where true chronic apophysitis is rarely studied in controlled trials — most hip literature concerns acute avulsion fractures rather than overuse apophysitis, and management there leans more heavily on case series. If your child's pain is specifically in the hip, that evidence gap is worth knowing about when discussing options with their clinician.
