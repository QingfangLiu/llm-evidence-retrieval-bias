I have enough well-documented primary studies now. Here's a curated list of individual primary studies (all randomized controlled trials, none of them reviews/guidelines/editorials) examining vitamin B12 supplementation vs. placebo, no supplementation, or the same supplement minus B12, in children under 12:

## Growth, neurodevelopment, hemoglobin

**1. Strand et al., 2020 — "Effects of vitamin B12 supplementation on neurodevelopment and growth in Nepalese Infants: A randomized controlled trial"** (*PLOS Medicine*)
600 marginally stunted 6–11-month-old infants in Nepal, randomized 1:1 to 2 μg vitamin B12 or placebo daily for 12 months. Children were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to 2 μg of vitamin B12, corresponding to approximately 2 to 3 recommended daily allowances, or a placebo daily for 12 months. The trial found vitamin B12 supplementation resulted in an improved metabolic response but did not affect neurodevelopment, growth, or hemoglobin concentration.

**2. Strand et al., 2017 — study protocol for the above trial** (*Trials*), PMID referenced as 28431557 — companion protocol paper describing the same design.

## Motor/cognitive development (India)

**3. Kvestad et al., 2015 — "Vitamin B12 and Folic Acid Improve Gross Motor and Problem-Solving Skills in Young North Indian Children: A Randomized Placebo-Controlled Trial"** (*PLOS One*)
Children aged six to 30 months received supplement with placebo or vitamin B12 and/or folic acid for six months in a 1:1:1:1 factorial design; development was measured in 422 children using the Ages and Stages Questionnaire. Compared to placebo, children who received both vitamin B12 and folic acid showed higher gross motor and problem-solving scores, and vitamin B12 alone significantly improved gross motor scores.

**4. Kvestad et al., 2023 — 6-year follow-up of the same cohort — "Vitamin B12 and/or folic acid supplementation on linear growth"** (long-term growth outcomes; found a small, non-significant effect of vitamin B12 on linear growth and no effect of folic acid at 6-year follow-up).

**5. Related follow-up — "Daily Folic Acid and/or Vitamin B12 Supplementation Between 6 and 30 Months of Age and Cardiometabolic Risk Markers After 6–7 Years"** — same original trial cohort, examining cardiometabolic markers (leptin, adiponectin, homocysteine) at 6–7 year follow-up.

## Cognitive performance (preschool, Netherlands)

**6. Louwman/van Dusseldorp et al. — "Three-month B vitamin supplementation in pre-school children affects folate status and homocysteine, but not cognitive performance"** (*European Journal of Nutrition*)
250 low-folate-status kindergarten children were randomized in a double-blind trial to receive daily a sachet containing folic acid, B2, B6, and B12, or calcium only, for 3 months, with verbal IQ, short-term memory and processing speed as primary outcomes.

## Disease-specific populations

**7. Nigerian nephrotic syndrome trial — "Vitamin B Supplementation and Homocysteine Reduction in Nigerian Children with Nephrotic Syndrome: A Randomized Controlled Trial"**
48 children with nephrotic syndrome and elevated homocysteine were randomized to daily folic acid, B6, and B12 supplementation or placebo for six months; the intervention group showed significant homocysteine reduction versus minimal change in controls.

## Complementary feeding / cluster-randomized

**8. Sheng et al., 2019 — "Effects of dietary intervention on vitamin B12 status and cognitive level of 18-month-old toddlers in high-poverty areas: a cluster-randomized controlled trial"** (*BMC Pediatrics*) — infants randomized by village cluster to pork, fortified cereal, or control complementary foods, assessing B12 status and cognition.

## Micronutrient combination trial including B12

**9. "A Randomized, Double-Blind Study Assessing Changes in Cognitive Function in Indian School Children Receiving a Combination of Bacopa monnieri and Micronutrient Supplementation vs. Placebo"** — 300 Indian school children aged 7–12 randomized to a fortified beverage (Bacopa + multiple micronutrients including B12) versus a non-fortified control, with cognition assessed via CANTAB. (Note: multi-ingredient — isolates B12 less cleanly.)

## Registered/ongoing trial

**10. NCT06100146 — "Effectiveness of Fortification With Folic Acid and Vitamin B12 Among Teenage Girls"** — school-attending teenage girls randomized to fortified vs. unfortified flour for six months, targeting folate/B12 deficiency, anemia, and cognitive/depressive outcomes. (partly outside your <12 age range, but relevant if adolescents are of interest — flag if you want it excluded)

A few notes for your own appraisal:
- The **Kvestad/Strand North India trial family** and the **Nepal Strand trial** are the two most methodologically strong, directly relevant RCTs (large samples, placebo-controlled, B12 given directly to children under 12, using both B12-alone and B12-vs-same-supplement-without-B12 arms).
- Several of these are **multi-nutrient trials** (B12 + folic acid ± other B vitamins) rather than B12-only, so isolating B12's independent effect requires attention to whether they report single-nutrient arms (Kvestad 2015 does, via its factorial design).
