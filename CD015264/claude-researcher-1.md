## Large RCTs (India – North Indian children, 6–30 months)
A single factorial-design RCT (~1000 children, New Delhi) underlies several linked publications, each reporting different outcomes:

- A randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind trial in 1000 North Indian children aged 6 to 35 months, providing twice the RDA of folic acid and/or vitamin B-12, or placebo, daily for 6 months, in a 1:1:1:1 factorial design — growth outcomes (Strand et al., *Pediatrics*, 2015; PMID 25802345).
- The same cohort's development was measured in 422 children using the Ages and Stages Questionnaire 3rd ed. at the end of the six-month intervention, finding that combined B12 + folic acid produced higher gross motor and problem-solving scores versus placebo (Kvestad et al., *PLoS ONE*, 2015; PMID 26098427).
- Hemoglobin, folate, B12 and homocysteine were measured in 262 children from the same trial; mean Hb decreased across all four groups and supplementation did not improve Hb (Kumar et al., *Clinical Nutrition*, 2017).
- Folic acid and vitamin B-12 supplementation and common infections in 6–30-mo-old children in India: a randomized placebo-controlled trial (Taneja et al., *Am J Clin Nutr*, 2013; PMID 23902779).
- A 6-year follow-up measured height in 791 of the original children (mean age 7.4 years) and found a small, non-significant effect of vitamin B12 on linear growth (Manapurath/Strand et al., *Br J Nutr*, 2023).
- A follow-up after 6–7 years examined cardiometabolic risk markers (homocysteine, leptin, adiponectin) in 791 of the original children (Manapurath et al., *J Nutr*, 2023).

## RCTs in Nepal (marginally stunted infants, 6–11 months)
- A community-based, individually randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in Bhaktapur, Nepal enrolled 600 marginally stunted 6–11-month-old infants (2015–2017), randomized 1:1 to 2 µg vitamin B12 or placebo daily for 12 months, on top of a multi-micronutrient background (both arms). The trial found no effects on Bayley-III neurodevelopment scores, growth, or hemoglobin concentration, though metabolic markers (homocysteine, MMA) improved (Strand/Ulak et al., *PLoS Medicine*, 2020; trial protocol PMID 28431557).
- A related follow-up: "The effect of infant vitamin B12 supplementation on neurodevelopment: a follow-up of a randomised placebo-controlled trial in Nepal" (*Br J Nutr*, 2022).

## Norwegian infant trials
- A double-blind RCT in 79 infants under 8 months with biochemical signs of impaired cobalamin function randomized to a single intramuscular 400 µg hydroxycobalamin injection (n=42) or sham injection (n=37), showing improved Alberta Infant Motor Scale scores and reduced regurgitation at 1 month versus sham (Torsvik et al., *Am J Clin Nutr*, 2013; PMID 24025626). Note: intramuscular route, not oral.
- Motor development related to duration of exclusive breastfeeding, B vitamin status and B12 supplementation in infants with a birth weight between 2000–3000 g: results from a randomized intervention trial (Torsvik et al., *BMC Pediatr*, 2015; PMID 26678525).

## Other settings
- A single-blind RCT at University College Hospital, Ibadan, Nigeria randomized 48 children with nephrotic syndrome and elevated homocysteine to daily folic acid + vitamin B6 + vitamin B12 or placebo for six months, finding a significant homocysteine reduction in the intervention group (12.8 to 6.9 µmol/L) versus minimal change with placebo (2025, *PMC12129721* — condition-specific population, not general pediatric nutrition).
