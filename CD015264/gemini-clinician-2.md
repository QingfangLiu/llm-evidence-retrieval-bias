Sorting through the mixed data on pediatric vitamin B12 supplementation can be challenging, especially when separating targeted clinical treatments from general population interventions.

Based strictly on primary randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and their follow-ups, the evidence suggests that while B12 supplementation does not reliably boost neurodevelopment or growth in the general population, it may offer targeted benefits for children with baseline deficiencies or specific conditions like Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD).

Here is a breakdown of the primary literature comparing vitamin B12 supplementation to placebo in children under 12.

## Neurodevelopment and Cognition

Large-scale RCTs examining early childhood development have yielded conflicting results regarding the benefit of widespread B12 supplementation.

* **Modest localized improvements:** A factorial RCT involving North Indian children (6–30 months) found that supplementing vitamin B12 and folic acid for six months slightly improved gross motor and problem-solving skills compared to a placebo. Notably, vitamin B12 alone had a significant independent effect on improving gross motor scores (Kvestad et al., 2015).
* **No general cognitive benefit:** Conversely, a highly powered RCT in 600 Nepalese infants (6–11 months) demonstrated that while daily supplementation of 2 µg of vitamin B12 for an entire year successfully improved metabolic B12 status, it produced zero significant improvements in neurodevelopment, hemoglobin concentrations, or physical growth compared to placebo (Strand et al., 2020).
* **Long-term follow-up:** When the Nepalese cohort was reassessed between 30 and 47 months of age, the lack of cognitive benefit persisted. Preschool cognitive function, measured via the WPPSI-IV full-scale IQ, showed no difference between the children who received infant B12 supplementation and those who received the placebo (Ulak et al., 2022).

## Physical Growth

The data indicates that B12 is not a universal growth enhancer, but it may help children whose growth is restricted by an existing subclinical deficiency.

* **Subgroup benefits only:** Researchers evaluated the North Indian cohort six years post-intervention (at ages 6–9 years). While they found no overall long-term effect of early childhood B12 supplementation on linear growth, subgroup analyses revealed a positive effect on height-for-age z-scores exclusively among children who began the trial stunted or with a true baseline cobalamin deficiency (Taneja et al., 2022).

## Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD)

There is emerging support for methyl B12 supplementation in ASD, driven by its role as a metabolic cofactor for cellular methylation rather than general nutritional support.

* **Symptom improvement:** An 8-week RCT evaluating subcutaneous methyl B12 injections in children with ASD (mean age 5.3 years) reported statistically significant improvements in clinician-rated symptoms via the Clinical Global Impressions-Improvement (CGI-I) score. This clinical improvement directly correlated with increases in cellular methylation capacity, though parent-rated behavioral scales did not capture the same significant changes (Hendren et al., 2016).
