## Verified primary comparative studies

I found **at least six unique randomized cohorts** in which vitamin B12 was given directly to children younger than 12. Publications from the same randomized cohort are grouped together to prevent double-counting.

### 1. North India factorial trial — young children

**Population/intervention:** 1,000 children aged 6–30 months were randomized in a 2×2 factorial design to daily vitamin B12 1.8 µg, folic acid 150 µg, both, or placebo for six months. Trial registration: NCT00717730.

**Primary reports:**

* **Taneja et al., 2013, American Journal of Clinical Nutrition.**
  *Folic acid and vitamin B-12 supplementation and common infections in 6–30-mo-old children in India: a randomized placebo-controlled trial.*
  Vitamin B12 improved biochemical B12 status but did not reduce diarrhea or lower respiratory tract infections. PMID 23902779; DOI 10.3945/ajcn.113.059592. ([PubMed][1])

* **Strand et al., 2015, Pediatrics.**
  *Vitamin B-12, folic acid, and growth in 6- to 30-month-old children: a randomized controlled trial.*
  B12 produced a small improvement in weight-for-age z score, approximately 0.07 SD, with larger apparent effects among children who were wasted, underweight, or stunted at baseline. PMID 25802345; DOI 10.1542/peds.2014-1848. ([PubMed][2])

* **Kvestad et al., 2015, PLOS One.**
  *Vitamin B12 and Folic Acid Improve Gross Motor and Problem-Solving Skills in Young North Indian Children: A Randomized Placebo-Controlled Trial.*
  In a 422-child developmental substudy, combined B12 plus folic acid improved gross-motor and problem-solving scores relative to placebo. The publication did not establish that B12 alone caused these effects. DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0129915. ([PMC][3])

* **Kumar et al., 2017, Clinical Nutrition.**
  *Supplementation of vitamin B12 or folic acid on hemoglobin concentration in children 6–36 months of age: a randomized placebo controlled trial.*
  In the hematology substudy, supplementation did not improve hemoglobin concentration or substantially reduce anemia. PMID 27486122; DOI 10.1016/j.clnu.2016.07.002. ([PubMed][4])

* **Kvestad et al., 2020, Pediatrics.**
  *Vitamin B12, Folate, and Cognition in 6- to 9-Year-Olds: A Randomized Controlled Trial.*
  Follow-up of 791 original participants approximately six years later found no benefit for general intelligence or other cognitive outcomes. ([PubMed][5])

* **Manapurath et al., 2023, Journal of Nutrition.**
  *Daily Folic Acid and/or Vitamin B12 Supplementation Between 6 and 30 Months of Age and Cardiometabolic Risk Markers After 6–7 Years.*
  Combined B12 plus folic acid lowered later homocysteine, but there was no broad, consistent improvement across cardiometabolic outcomes. DOI 10.1016/j.tjnut.2023.03.003. ([PubMed][6])

**Study-level interpretation:** Clear biochemical efficacy, a small possible growth benefit, and a short-term developmental signal for the combined B12–folate arm; no consistent benefit for infections, anemia, or later cognition.

### 2. Nepal infant trial — oral B12 added to an otherwise identical micronutrient supplement

**Population/intervention:** 600 marginally stunted infants aged 6–11 months received either vitamin B12 2 µg/day or placebo for 12 months. Both groups received the same 15 other vitamins and minerals, making this a particularly clean B12-specific contrast. Trial registration: NCT02272842.

**Primary reports:**

* **Strand et al., 2020, PLOS Medicine.**
  *Effects of vitamin B12 supplementation on neurodevelopment and growth in Nepalese infants: a randomized controlled trial.*
  Supplementation markedly improved B12-related metabolic markers, including methylmalonic acid and homocysteine, but did not improve Bayley developmental scores, growth, or hemoglobin. PMID 33259482. ([PubMed][7])

* **Kvestad et al., 2022/2023, British Journal of Nutrition.**
  *The effect of infant vitamin B12 supplementation on neurodevelopment: a follow-up of a randomised placebo-controlled trial in Nepal.*
  Follow-up at approximately 30–35 and 42–47 months found no effect on full-scale IQ or other neurodevelopmental outcomes. ([PubMed][8])

* **Ulak et al., 2024, Journal of Nutrition.**
  *The Effect of Vitamin B12 Supplementation on Leukocyte Telomere Length in Mildly Stunted Nepalese Children.*
  No effect on leukocyte telomere length was detected. ([PubMed][9])

* **Strand et al., 2026, Journal of Nutrition.**
  *The Effect of Daily Vitamin B-12 Supplementation on the Incidence of Common Childhood Infections in Nepal.*
  The intervention did not reduce any of 20 prespecified infection outcomes or healthcare visits; the overall infection incidence-rate ratio was 0.99. DOI 10.1016/j.tjnut.2026.101534. ([PubMed][10])

**Study-level interpretation:** Strong improvement in biochemical B12 status, but no demonstrated benefit for growth, anemia, development, later cognition, infections, or telomere length.

### 3. Guatemala infant complementary-feeding trial

**Allen et al., 2007, FASEB Journal conference abstract.**
*Vitamin B12 Status and Development of Young Guatemalan Children: Effects of Beef and B12 Supplements.*

**Population/intervention:** 304 infants were randomized at 12 months to supervised beef, a B12-fortified fruit-and-vegetable supplement, or an otherwise comparable unfortified fruit-and-vegetable supplement, five days weekly until 21 months.

**Results:** The B12-fortified supplement increased plasma B12 numerically, but no treatment effects were reported for growth or developmental outcomes. The report is available only as a conference abstract, substantially limiting assessment of methods and risk of bias. DOI 10.1096/fasebj.21.5.A681. ([ResearchGate][11])

## Direct B12 trials using intramuscular supplementation

These meet the broad intervention/comparator question but represent different clinical settings from routine oral population supplementation.

### 4. Norway — healthy young infants

**Bjørke-Monsen et al., 2008, Pediatrics.**
*Common metabolic profile in infants indicating impaired cobalamin status responds to cobalamin supplementation.*

A total of 107 healthy six-week-old infants were randomized to a single 400-µg intramuscular cobalamin injection or no intervention. At four months, treated infants had substantially higher serum B12 and lower methylmalonic acid and homocysteine. Functional health or developmental benefits were not evaluated as primary outcomes. PMID 18595990; DOI 10.1542/peds.2007-2716. ([PubMed][12])

### 5. Norway — symptomatic infants with impaired B12 function

**Torsvik et al., 2013, American Journal of Clinical Nutrition.**
*Cobalamin supplementation improves motor development and regurgitations in infants: results from a randomized intervention study.*

Seventy-nine infants younger than eight months with feeding or developmental symptoms and biochemical evidence of impaired B12 function received 400 µg intramuscular hydroxycobalamin or a sham injection. At one month, B12 improved metabolic markers, Alberta Infant Motor Scale scores, and regurgitation. This was a selected, symptomatic population rather than a general-child supplementation trial. PMID 24025626; DOI 10.3945/ajcn.113.061549. ([PubMed][13])

### 6. United States — preterm infants

**Worthington-White et al., 1994, American Journal of Clinical Nutrition.**
*Premature infants require additional folate and vitamin B-12 to reduce the severity of the anemia of prematurity.*

A factorial trial randomized 184 preterm infants weighing under 1,800 g to folate, monthly intramuscular B12 100 µg, both, or neither for four months. B12-treated groups had higher hemoglobin at approximately 10–12 weeks. Generalizability is principally to anemia management in preterm infants. PMID 7985636; DOI 10.1093/ajcn/60.6.930. ([PubMed][14])

## Overall pattern

Across the **oral, community-based trials**, vitamin B12 consistently improved biochemical or metabolic indicators of B12 status. However, it did **not consistently improve clinically important outcomes**, including linear growth, anemia, infections, neurodevelopment, or later cognition. The main possible exceptions were:

* A small weight-for-age improvement in the North India factorial trial.
* Short-term developmental benefits from **combined B12 plus folic acid**, not clearly B12 alone.
* Motor and feeding improvements after high-dose intramuscular treatment in symptomatic, biochemically impaired infants.
* Improved hemoglobin in a specialized preterm-infant trial.

The India publications represent **one randomized cohort**, and the Nepal publications represent **one randomized cohort**; they should not be counted as separate trials in a synthesis.
