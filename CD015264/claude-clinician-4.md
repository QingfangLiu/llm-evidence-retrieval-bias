Here are individual primary studies (RCTs) on oral or parenteral vitamin B12 supplementation in children under 12, compared with placebo, no supplementation, or the same regimen minus B12. I've excluded systematic reviews, meta-analyses, narrative reviews, guidelines, and editorials — the Cochrane protocol/review that surfaced is mentioned only as a pointer, not included as a primary source.

**Growth and development (RCTs, North India — Strand/Taneja/Kumar group)**

1. **Strand TA, et al. "Vitamin B-12, Folic Acid, and Growth in 6- to 30-Month-Old Children: A Randomized Controlled Trial." *Pediatrics* 2015;135(4):e918–e926.** 1000 children, 6–35 months, 2×2 factorial, double-blind, placebo-controlled; B12 alone increased mean weight-for-age z-score (0.07, 95% CI 0.01–0.13); effects were larger in wasted/underweight/stunted subgroups.

2. **Kvestad I, Taneja S, Kumar T, et al. "Vitamin B12 and Folic Acid Improve Gross Motor and Problem-Solving Skills in Young North Indian Children: A Randomized Placebo-Controlled Trial." *PLoS One* 2015;10(6):e0129915.** Same cohort/design; 422 children assessed with Ages & Stages Questionnaire; combined B12+folic acid improved gross motor and problem-solving scores; B12 alone improved gross motor scores only.

3. **Kvestad I, et al. "Vitamin B12 and/or folic acid supplementation on linear growth: a 6-year follow-up study... North India." *Br J Nutr* 2022.** Follow-up of the same trial at age 6–9 years (n=791); no long-term effect of prior B12/folic acid supplementation on height.

4. **Follow-up cardiometabolic study: "Daily Folic Acid and/or Vitamin B12 Supplementation Between 6 and 30 Months of Age and Cardiometabolic Risk Markers After 6–7 Years."** Same trial, follow-up at 6–7 years (n=791); examined homocysteine, leptin, adiponectin.

5. **"Supplementation of vitamin B12 or folic acid on hemoglobin concentration in children 6–36 months of age: A randomized placebo controlled trial." *Clin Nutr* 2016.** Same parent trial, subset n=262; neither B12 nor folic acid improved hemoglobin at 6 months.

**Nepal (Strand/Chandyo/Hysing group)**

6. **Strand TA, Ulak M, Hysing M, et al. "Effects of vitamin B12 supplementation on neurodevelopment and growth in Nepalese Infants: A randomized controlled trial." *PLoS Medicine* 2020;17(10):e1003430.** 600 marginally stunted infants (6–11 months), individually randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, daily B12 for 12 months; improved metabolic B12 status but no effect on neurodevelopment, growth, or hemoglobin.

7. **Study protocol paper (Kvestad et al., *Trials* 2017)** describing the design of the above trial — a protocol, included here only for completeness since it documents planned primary/secondary outcomes.

8. **Follow-up: "The effect of infant vitamin B12 supplementation on neurodevelopment: a follow-up of a randomised placebo-controlled trial in Nepal." *Br J Nutr* 2022.** Longer-term neurodevelopmental follow-up of the same Nepal cohort.

**Norway (Torsvik/Bjørke-Monsen group) — infants with biochemical B12 insufficiency**

9. **Torsvik I, Ueland PM, Markestad T, Bjørke-Monsen AL. "Cobalamin supplementation improves motor development and regurgitations in infants: results from a randomized intervention study." *Am J Clin Nutr* 2013;98(5):1233–1240.** Infants <8 months (n=79) with biochemical B12 insufficiency; double-blind RCT of intramuscular hydroxocobalamin vs. sham injection; improved motor scores (AIMS) and reduced regurgitation.

10. **Torsvik IK, Ueland PM, Markestad T, Midttun O, Bjørke-Monsen AL. "Motor development related to duration of exclusive breastfeeding, B vitamin status and B12 supplementation in infants with a birth weight between 2000–3000 g." *BMC Pediatr* 2015;15:218.** Low-birth-weight infants, subset randomized (n=32) to IM hydroxocobalamin vs. sham; improved AIMS and ASQ gross motor scores.

**Other comparator/population trials**

11. **Nigerian study: "Vitamin B Supplementation and Homocysteine Reduction in Nigerian Children with Nephrotic Syndrome: A Randomized Controlled Trial."** 48 children with nephrotic syndrome and elevated homocysteine; single-blind RCT of combined folic acid/B6/B12 vs. placebo for 6 months; significant homocysteine reduction.

12. **"Effects of dietary intervention on vitamin B12 status and cognitive level of 18-month-old toddlers in high-poverty areas: a cluster-randomized controlled trial."** Cluster-RCT in toddlers; dietary/B12 intervention effects on status and cognition.

A few notes for your review:
- Most pediatric B12 RCTs cluster around infants/toddlers (6 months–3 years) in South Asia and Norway; I did not find RCTs specifically isolating school-age children (5–12 years) as the primary population — most "school-age" data come from the North Indian trial's 6–9-year follow-up rather than de novo enrollment at that age.
- Several trials use vitamin B12 alone vs. placebo (Strand 2015, PLoS Medicine 2020, Torsvik 2013/2015), which best match your "same supplement without B12" comparator; others use B12 as one arm of a folic acid factorial design.
