Primary research on vitamin B12 supplementation in children under 12 years of age presents mixed findings. While there are some short-term developmental and lasting metabolic benefits—particularly when B12 is combined with folic acid or administered to specific clinical subpopulations—long-term cognitive improvements over placebo groups have largely not been observed in general pediatric populations.

## Short-Term Cognitive and Motor Development

In young children at risk for deficiency, vitamin B12's effects appear most pronounced when paired with other essential nutrients. A randomized controlled trial (RCT) in North India evaluated daily oral supplementation over six months in children aged 6 to 30 months (Kvestad et al., 2015). The study found:

* **Combined Supplementation:** Children who received both vitamin B12 and folic acid demonstrated significantly improved gross motor and problem-solving skills compared to the placebo group.
* **B12 Alone:** Supplementation with vitamin B12 alone only yielded a significant improvement in gross motor scores. It had no observable impact on communication, fine motor skills, or personal-social development compared to the placebo.

## Long-Term Effects on Neurodevelopment

Despite short-term gains, follow-up evaluations of early-childhood RCTs indicate that these developmental benefits may not persist into later childhood in general populations.

* **Indian Cohort:** When researchers reassessed the same North Indian cohort at 6 to 9 years of age, they found no significant differences in cognitive outcomes (such as general IQ, verbal scales, or working memory) between the children who received early B12 and folic acid interventions and those who received a placebo (Kvestad et al., 2020).
* **Nepalese Cohort:** An independent RCT in Nepal tracked infants (initially 6 to 11 months old) who received daily vitamin B12 supplements for an entire year. By the time the children reached 42 to 47 months of age, the supplementation had yielded no significant effect on preschool cognitive function or other neurodevelopmental outcomes compared to the placebo group (Ulak et al., 2022).

## Cardiometabolic and Nutritional Markers

While long-term cognitive gains are difficult to isolate, early supplementation can leave lasting impacts on metabolic health. A 6- to 7-year follow-up study of the North Indian cohort measured cardiometabolic risk markers and found that early combined supplementation with vitamin B12 and folic acid resulted in significantly lower total homocysteine concentrations later in childhood (Manapurath et al., 2023). Additionally, vitamin B12 supplementation was associated with an improved leptin-adiponectin ratio in specific nutritional subgroups, indicating persistent metabolic advantages (Manapurath et al., 2023).

## Impacts on Specific Subpopulations

Vitamin B12 may provide substantial, targeted benefits for children with clinical diagnoses that affect cellular methylation. For example, an 8-week RCT evaluating subcutaneous injections of methyl B12 in 57 children diagnosed with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) found that the active intervention significantly improved clinician-rated ASD symptoms compared to a saline placebo (Hendren et al., 2016). These clinical improvements correlated directly with measurable improvements in the children's cellular methylation capacity.
