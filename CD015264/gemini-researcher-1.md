Here is a targeted synthesis of recent primary studies (specifically randomized, placebo-controlled trials and their longitudinal follow-ups) investigating the effects of vitamin B12 supplementation on the health and development of children under 12.

Because severe vitamin B12 deficiency is heavily tied to dietary intake of animal products, the most robust primary trials in this demographic are clustered in regions with prevalent plant-based diets and high baseline risks of deficiency, such as North India and Nepal.

The primary literature is divided into three main outcome categories:

## Neurodevelopment and Cognitive Function

The evidence regarding cognitive and motor development is mixed and appears to depend on the co-administration of folic acid and the baseline deficiency status of the child.

* **Synergistic benefits:** A factorial-design randomized controlled trial (RCT) in North Indian children aged 6 to 30 months found that supplementation with *both* vitamin B12 and folic acid for six months significantly improved gross motor functioning and problem-solving skills compared to placebo (Kvestad et al., 2015). Supplementation of vitamin B12 alone only improved gross motor scores. The cognitive benefits were most pronounced in children who were already stunted or had elevated baseline plasma homocysteine (a marker of B12/folate deficiency).
* **Null findings:** Conversely, a follow-up of a double-blind RCT in Nepalese infants (who received 2 µg of daily B12 from 6–11 months of age for one year) found no significant differences in full-scale intelligence quotient (FSIQ) or other neurodevelopmental outcomes when the children were evaluated at 30 to 47 months of age (Ulak et al., 2022).

## Physical and Linear Growth

For physical growth parameters, primary data suggests that blanket supplementation does not reliably drive population-wide height increases, but targeted supplementation in deficient subgroups yields measurable improvements.

* **Catch-up growth:** A 6-year follow-up of the North Indian RCT evaluated linear growth when the children reached 6 to 9 years of age. While the overall population-level effect of early childhood B12 supplementation on height-for-age z-scores (HAZ) was small and non-significant, researchers observed a significant subgroup effect: children who were clinically deficient at baseline (<200 pmol/L) and received B12 saw a +0.34 HAZ improvement compared to the placebo group (Taneja et al., 2022).

## Cardiometabolic and Nutritional Biomarkers

Supplementation during the critical early childhood window appears to leave a lasting footprint on metabolic health.

* **Persistent metabolic effects:** A second longitudinal follow-up of the North Indian cohort examined cardiometabolic risk markers at 6 to 7 years of age. Children who had received combined B12 and folic acid supplementation maintained persistently lower plasma homocysteine concentrations six years after the intervention ended compared to the placebo group (Manapurath et al., 2023). Furthermore, early B12 supplementation was associated with a lower leptin-adiponectin ratio in certain nutritional subgroups, indicating a potential long-term cardioprotective benefit.
